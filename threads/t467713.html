I enjoyed that Stephen Colbert bit, thank you for that.On the subject, censorship is something I consider a danger. Not necessarily censorship of specific words, which doesn't immediately is the danger, but once you start censoring larger bits of education material (read: historical items, forcing ,,e.g. pro-governmental, pro-consumerism, whatever you can come up with,, perspectives onto children, etc.) it could end up corrupting the very essence of the entire educational system. The essence which I consider to be factual, scientifically proven material which does not influence children in a specific way, but leaves them open for their own interpretation (which would be limited by the scientifically proven material anyway). Freedom of thought, in a way. In my country of residence (The Netherlands) the history material we get at high schools, and in which will be in our final exams, is determined by the government in the slightest detail - so they made sure several aspects of our history are censored (or 'left out of the material'), and our history in general is told mostly in a pro-governmental perspective. Obviously they cannot teach us everything, for all history would be quite an elaborate subject to test and teach, but still. I don't have any reference, and if I did, it would be Dutch so I can't really back these statements up, but it's a rather common acknowledged fact.I do believe I strayed from the subject slightly. It's been a long day.In a nutshell, I think censorship is a danger to freedom of speech (obviously) and also freedom of thought, especially in educative (which should be factual and unmodified) material.
