[center][/center]Name: Playstation 3 (PS3)Release Dates: North America- Friday, November 17, 2006 Japan- Saturday, November 11, 2006 Europe- Friday, March 23, 2007 Australia/New Zealand- Friday, March 23, 2007Latest Firmware Update: 2.20 [March 25, 2008]General SummaryThe Playstation 3 is one of the latest video game consoles and is part of the seventh generation, a position it shares with Microsoft's Xbox360 and Nintendo's Wii. The most noticeable feature of the PS3 is the price which is the result of all the hardware and software features built into the system. When released, the PS3 came in two different setups: a basic system or a premium system. The basic system includes a 20 GB upgradeable hard drive, a Bluetooth controller, a Blu-ray drive, an HDMI port, and an Ethernet cable. This basic system has been discontinued in the U.S. and Canada and has yet to be released in PAL countries. The premium system adds more to the basic setup in the form of a 60 GB upgradeable hard drive, Wi-Fi capabilities built-in, flash card readers, and chrome trim. As of now, the 20GB and 60GB version of the PS3 have been reduced in price and are discontinued. Sony released the 40GB version of the PS3 with neither backwards compatibility nor Super Audio CD support. Instead, an 80GB version replaces the old premium system that has software emulation instead of the Emotion Engine for backwards compatibility. The 80GB version was released in August 2007 and uses software emulation for PS2 compatability. It is also the only version of the PS3 in production that has a flash card reader.In terms of games, the Playstation 3 had the most playable titles at launch due to its backward compatibility. This allowed the PS3 to play games from its predecessors, the Playstation and Playstation 2. This compatibility is only guaranteed for the games that followed the original systems' technical requirements checklist. The PS3 also supports devices such as the EyeToy camera and the SOCOM Headset. In addition to the backward compatibility, the PS3 is also able to utilize software emulation for the original Playstation and Playstation 2 games.The graphical user interface of the PS3 is the XrossMediaBar. It gives the player nine menu options: Users, Settings, Photo, Music, Video, Game, Network, the PlayStation Network, and Friends. Through this interface, the user can rip CDs and play music, movies, and videos. There is also support for USB keyboards and webcams. Sony announced earlier in the year that the in-game XMB menu music to be play and game messages or invites to be received without needing to exit the game.The PS3 is also able to download and use firmware updates from the Playstation Network. Look here for the latest firmware version: Firmware 2.20 New firmware updates will allow more functionality and uses for the PS3.
