An interesting debate we have going so far. I would like to throw a thought of mine in here. Just because something reacts to something (ie a plant produces a sound, a scent to attract insects, or grows due to music) doesn't necessarily mean that it is showing emotion. There are many actions that we humans, who everyone here seems to agree have emotion, perform independent of emotion. For example, if a doctor is checking your reflexes with a reflex hammer, most people's legs will naturally kick. It's not because the person was angry at the doctor for hitting them. To develop this thought in a different direction, I'll pick an object that I believe we can agree has no emotions. Say I take a basketball and decide to bounce it on the ground. The ball is going to react to this by bouncing back up in the air again, a simple cause and effect action. I propose that stating that a plant reacts to external influences may simply be another, albeit more complicated, cause and effect action. Finding a way to measure emotions seems to have too many possible flaws in it. Instead, I would rather ask the question is it possible that it feels emotions? From what I understand of emotions, they are different chemical states in the brain. Happiness caused by endorphins being released into the brain, etc. In short, they are a very complex process occuring in the nervous system. Plants, however, have no central nervous system. Therefore, even if they can react to things plants would be incapable of having emotions as we understand them.
