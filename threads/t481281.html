May 15th

38,000 feet over the Pacific Ocean

 

Once upon a time, Mac was the editor and owner of a newspaper. Were they happy times? Not always. Were they financially safe times? Never. But Mac was able to use that paper as a force of social good, of change. People were living better, more informed lives, because of the work he and his people did. He was living the dream, following his ideals, and he got good sleep.

 

Until the day his paper hit a slump that he couldn't shake. Until the day he sold out. The day he found out how much his soul was worth, and just how low it was.

 

So now he was on a plane, sharing business seats with one of those fat, sweaty, loud people who seem to exist solely to take up room. The fat, sweaty man was currently asleep, snoring louder than the engine, slumping towards Mac, threatening to engulf him in waves of greasy, greasy blubber.

 

And he's probably a Republican, Mac thought. He tried not to be judgmental, but everyone had their limits.

 

"Excuse me, flight attendant?" he asked as one was walking down the aisle. "How much longer to Singapore?"

 

"A few more hours, sir," she said, with an institutional smile.

 

"Thank you," Mac said, and tried to return to his thoughts.

 

There was a low squawk from the intercoms as one of the pilots addressed the cabin. "This is your captain speaking," he said. "We are currently, ah, cruising at a speed of about 175 knots, and we are currently on track to, ah, arrive on time to Singapore. We just picked up on our radars, what looks like an aircraft on a, ah, an intercept course. Nothing to be alarmed about, probably just someone curious about us, but we'll try and raise them on the horn, see if we can't get them out of our hair. So if you look out your window and you see something unusual, ah, that's what that's all about. Thank you."

 

Mac only realized that the fat man next to him was awake when he felt warm breath on his neck. He turned his head, and got a face full of ugly.

 

"Hey, don' I  recognize yew?" the fat man said.

 

"I doubt it," Mac said.

 

"Yew sure? I coulda swear you was on the news'r sumthin."

 

"I just have one of those faces," Mac said, with a smile.

 

"I guess," said the fat man. "Wouldja budge on over? I gotta go drop a Texas choclit pie, if ya know whaddi mean."

 

The fat man somehow managed to climb over Mac and into the aisle. Mac breathed in, and out, trying to enjoy the few minutes of peace. He looked out the window.

 

He saw another plane, a two-seater biwing job. Something was tied down on top, something metal. The pilot was wearing a long, red scarf that flapped in the wind. The passenger, he was waving. And then Mac realized that the passenger was waving at him.

 

"Oh dear," Mac said.

 

He got out of his seat, was running towards the front of the plane, to the door.

 

"Excuse me, sir, but I need you to return to your-" a flight attendant began to say.

 

"Do you have parachutes?" Mac said.

 

"Excuse me?"

 

"Parachutes! The things you use to-"

 

"I'm aware of the existence of parachutes, sir, but if you expect me to let you jump out of this plane mid-fight, you are in for a surprise, mister."

 

"You don't understand," said Mac. He grabbed her by the shoulders, tried to shake sense into her. "He's after me! He found me, and now he's here to-"

 

There was a sound, like a motor revving, from outside. Then the sound of metal shearing. Something crashed through the fuselage, in the rear, where Mac had been sitting. Someone was sitting on...

 

"Is that a motorcycle?" the flight attendant said, as if it wasn't obvious.

 

"Vroom, vroom, bitc'es!" said motorbike.

 

"Mac, you sumbitch," said sleight, holding a bat, throttling the engine, working motorbike in the aisle. "Best stand down, if you know what's good for you!"

 

"What?" asked Mac. The wind coming through the huge hole was ripping the words out of the air.

 

"I said stand down!"

 

"What?"

 

"Stand down!"

 

"What?"

 

"I said -- ah, hell with it!" said sleight. He gunned motorbike, and started ripping his way up the plane.

 

Mac was out of time. He grabbed the handle, wrenched it up, and shoved against the door. It popped opened like a cork, and Mac fell.

The Eastern Jungle

 

It was a trick Mac learned as a cub reporter, back in his college days when he was cracking down on corruption in the faculty. If at any time, while perusing secret files or setting up secret bugs, he noticed that campus security was catching onto him, he could leap through any window, on any floor, and by ducking and rolling just as he hit ground he could survive any fall.

 

Falling out of a plane was a little different. It only meant the timing was a bit tricky.

 

All the downward energy was immediately transferred to horizontal velocity, and Mac went a'tumbling.

 

Through bush and past trees, he rolled and rolled, breaking branches and skipping off boulders. He glanced off the trunk of a mighty tree, and he felt something in his left ankle crunch. He couldn't do anything about it except stay as limp as he could. At last, with a few final, stomach wrenching somersaults, he came to rest. He laid on the ground for either five minutes or five days, his eyes shut, trying to convince himself that he really was stopped.

 

At last he could look around with sight cleared of double vision, and up and down were themselves again. He got up, slowly, his body made up of bruises and (probably) fractured bones. He touched his ankle. It was swollen already, like someone had stuffed tennis balls in a tube sock and then beat it with a lead pipe.

 

He crawled over to the trunk of a tree, grabbing a nice sized broken branch along the way, and then lifted himself up, bracing his back against it, trying to keep the weight off the ankle. The branch made for a decent make-shift crutch, for the moment. He hobbled off with the gait of an eighty-year-old.

 

Questions, questions, questions pestered him like flies, and he did his best to swat them away. Was he on Zombie Island? Maybe. Did sleight think he was really dead? Probably. Was there help on this island? Too soon to say. Was his ankle broken, or was it just a really bad sprang? He hoped it was a sprain.

 

Presently he came upon a building, an old and decrepit hut next to an abandoned garden, walled off by a short stone fence. Mac stumbled up to the door of the hut. It was open, and hung loss on its hinges.

 

His eyes adjusted to the dark, and he saw an old bed, a cold hearth, and shelves with broken pots upon them. He made for the bed, when someone spoke from a shadowed corner.

 

"Crikey, mate, but you look right buggered."

 

Mac turned to look. In the corner, sitting on the floor, with his pack between his knees, was a man. In one hand, he held a knife, long and serrated.

 

"and if'n you want that nasty ankle looked at, I'd say you're bloody well lucked out. Any doctor with sense left a long time ago. Hell, anybody with the brains God gave a cogar cleared out a long time ago."

 

"And why is that?" asked Mac.

 

"Well, it's a mystery, ain't it?" said the man. "Welcome to Zombie Island, mate."
