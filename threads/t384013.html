It's not that Sony isn't paying attention to the PSP. They make new firmware for it all the time, they add features, and they try hard. The problem is that game companies do not want to develop games for their hardware. Piracy is a high concern for most developers. The PlayStation Portable is easily the easiest device to load pirated games. It's not homebrew that's inherently bad, it just the fact that you can load a direct iso rip just like the game was in the drive, and you can rip an iso with the click of the button right to your memory card, issues start to come up with developers. Sony could always make games themselves, but this is where another issue pops up:Sony is just not as dedicated to game development. Nintendo is all about games. Sega was all about games. When either company didn't have as much third-party support for their respective consoles, the only options were to let it die, or deliver content. Nintendo has always delivered the highest quality games for years, so even when consoles like Gamecube or N64 had less third party support than PlayStation, Nintendo has been fine. They can still make games, they can still sell them. They do these things well.Sony, on the other hand, has never been in a trailing position like this. They need to push their handheld with quality software, not awesome firmware features. They need to make people who don't know how to install custom firmware want a PSP. Sure, you can use means to pirate Nintendo DS games, but the major audience for that console includes old people, young people, moms, kids, etc. People who just don't know how to pirate. PSP has basically only tied to appeal to hardcore gamers. People who have no trouble with pirating.Sony also needs to combat piracy (whereas the above paragraph describes just sidestepping it). You do not do this by making "yet-another-secure-firmware" that you need to run the latest games. All this is doing is making the system less appealing to new audiences, thus hurting you more than helping you (since it doesn't help you... like, at all). *Instead* Sony needs to embrace their online game download service. People download because of speed and convenience. I want to try a new game, but I do not want to go up to GameStop or Wal-mart to buy one. With the online service, I wouldn't have to. Right now it only has demos and some older games. Sony needs to bring their entire library to the internet. People usually go for what's more convenient over what's cheaper. At the moment, piracy is both cheaper and more convenient. If Sony makes their online service better, then just like how people have found iTunes and similar services to be easier than torrenting or using piracy sites, even if it's cheaper, people will start using Sony's service. Since they don't need to design packaging, or manufacture labels, boxes, discs, or transport games to stores, games can potentially be cheaper, and thus make them even more appealing. If the general success of Valve's Steam is any indication that such a system works, I think this is Sony's best bet for keeping the system alive and kicking.
