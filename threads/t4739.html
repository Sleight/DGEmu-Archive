NNogusielktJan 17 2006, 11:27 PM
The reason to me is simple. Games and movies are based upon two different ideas. Movies are meant to bring in someone who hasn't heard anything about it, bring them up to speed quickly, and finish within 2 hours. In movies, every conflict is important, because of both the limited time AND because the first rule of writing is "Don't [display] anything unless it is important". Movies have been using the same tactics for 50 years to get done what they need to get done. Therefore, movies tend to bend things to their needs.Games are another story. Games are meant to be played for a long time (most of them anyhow). While many games will assume you know nothing and at least offer you a tutorial, those are usually reserved for strategy games (which usually work on a sliding scale anyhow). Games can waste as much time as they want developing any character, building up tension, or anything else for that matter. Games have been changing ever since their creation, even if it does take longer than we would like.The reason why 99% of games based on movies and movies based on games suck, is because they work for different agendas. Games need to fill time with mindless combat, collecting, or puzzles. Movies can't afford to do that. Movies have to rework stories to fit within time constraints, games need to rework stories to fit within gameplay aspects. Both of them are made by a bunch of humans who, more than likely, don't have enough of an idea as to what the movie to game or game to movie has to be. Each person brings in their own ideas of what they think is cool, and when an individual is given authority, mistakes can be made easily....That and they haven't made any cool conversions, like Zelda, Metroid, or Metal Gear.
