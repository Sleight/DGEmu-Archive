TTehpengwanMay 17 2011, 02:17 AM
This is a really tricky, and in a way, a somewhat depressing debate. The bottom line is, going into space basically does nothing for our society that we can make use of in the immediate future. The most common argument for space exploration is that it boosted the development of technology such as the computer. And then there's satellites and whatnot.But the space age is arguably past, and we aren't netting as much benefit out of 'wasting' trillions of dollars to examine a few rocks on alien worlds. At home, there's always something more immediate to spend money on that will benefit us more (or that the government chooses to prioritize) in the span of a couple months than space travel would in a century. Why land on a rock in the sky when all we get is some geological information? Why spend a quadrillion dollars and a hundred fifty years building a moon colony when it would be infinitely easier and cheaper to industrialize and maintain our nation's deserts? In fact, if we ever hope to build colonies on other planets, we should figure out how to maintain a stable living zone away from a source of water beforehand.Classically, the reason why we haven't colonized other planets is that 'we're not ready.' The truth of the matter is though, is that we don't seem to want to put the effort into doing so, and as a result, we might never be ready. Not now, not in a couple millennia, and even at the end of Earth, not even then--though at that point it would be too late.When it comes down to it, going into space these days is an incredibly stupid economic decision to make compared to basically everything else you could do with those funds (except of course, for fighting pointless wars), and yet the irony is that it is ultimately our only hope.Still, the end of our planet is, optimistically speaking, perhaps a couple billion years away. Space travel may not be likely, but a billion years is a lot of time for probability to go awry.
