MmouseORIGINAL POSTFeb 01 2014, 04:26 AM

What I mean is, say you have a game with some big revelation somewhere toward the middle or end. Then, the next time you play through it, you pick up on all sorts of subtle things that you didn't notice before because you didn't know (or weren't sure) about the big twist.

 

Probably best to use spoilers. Some examples:

 

Shadow of the Colossus.

 

I played through this game several times, and I noticed a few things each time. Beyond just the fact that Wander gets progressively more haggard and worn as you get closer to the end, I also noticed things about the colossi. For a lot of them, it's almost like they're fighting you out of self-defense rather than aggression. Some of them don't even attack until you make the first move - such as the bird colossus, or the first colossus. In all cases, they don't attack you until you enter their territory.

 

Spec Ops: The Line.

 

This one's kind of obvious for anyone who has played through it. The squad is noticeably angrier and more worn out as the game goes on. Instead of giving out formal squad commands, the main character starts yelling and swearing. The melee takedowns get more brutal. I'm also told, though I did not notice this and may have to play through again, that if you look back at trees after you pass by, sometimes they go from alive to dead.

 

Final Fantasy X.

 

Okay, so this one wasn't necessarily a twist, but there's the whole thing about Tidus not knowing that Yuna is supposed to die when she defeats Sin. It's hinted at on several occasions, and each time Tidus tries to get everyone excited about beating Sin, I'm all, "He doesn't know!"

KKHRSFeb 01 2014, 05:08 AM

This isn't a game I played recently, but I do like the way they handled it.

 

Dragon Quest 5 (actual spoilers in here. Like pretty much the entire plot of the game)

So, DQ5 is in the Zenithian trilogy of the DQ games (DQ 4, 5, 6). In the previous game you eventually get to play as the main character who is the chosen hero, the only one that is able to wield the Zenithian Sword and the Zenithian armor. All Dragon Quest games use the same archetypical main character design, they're either from a special bloodline or just have some special power which makes them more special than everyone else and therefor are capable of things that normal people can't do (which is the most common trope in all stories of any culture or whatever). However, in Dragon Quest 5 you play as the prince (later king) of a random kingdom. You start as a child and follow the MC through childhood, adolescence and adulthood. As a teenager you find the Zenith Sword. Immediately you're thinking "oh well, I'm the super special main character so obviously I can equip this and I'll be really strong." Wrong. The MC can't equip it. So at this point you're thinking "Wait a minute, if the MAIN CHARACTER isn't the super special chosen one, then who the hell is?" Well eventually as you progress through the game's story you marry a girl of your choice (1 of 3 choices in the DS version and either the childhood friend or random girl in the original version). Regardless of your choice, you eventually find out through talking to the girl's father (applies for all 3 girls, though 2 of them are sisters so they have the same "father") that they aren't the girl's true father. It turns out that all 3 girls are orphans from Zenithia (hence why the DS version's subtitle is Hand of the Heavenly Bride). Further along the game's plot you impregnate your wife (giggity) and she eventually gives birth to twins, a boy and a girl. A few years pass and the kids get to be about 8 I think and join you on your journey to fight the big bad baddest bad guy. As it turns out, your 8yo son is actually the chosen one and can equip all of the Zenithian gear. So, while you're the main character of the game, who's in his 20's and has been through a lot of hardships (watching your dad die, being a slave, fighting demons and shit), the true hero is your 8yo dipshit son who spent his whole life chilling casually in a castle.

 

So, what exactly is the plot twist? Not only is the MC not the super special hero, he's literally just some random dude.The gene for the legendary hero that can wield the Zenithian gear belongs to any one of the potential Zenithian brides. They could (theoretically) marry any guy and poop out a babby that could wear the armor and stuff. The main character's sole role is to be a baby maker, past that all he is is a secondary goon for the real main hero, his 8yo son. I considered this a pretty big plot twist because by nature you expect the MC to be the "chosen one" but this game takes it a step further and takes place before the chosen one is even born. During my first playthrough when they revealed the MC wasn't the hero I was really confused and wondering who the actual hero was. I thought it was pretty creative, especially for a JRPG to make the MC essentially a generic party member.

 

Oh oh oh, there's also a part of the game that involves time travel. You go back in time to talk to your childhood self. In the very beginning of the game during the childhood arc you see a weird guy and have to talk to him. It turns out it was you all along and when you're an adult you get to re-do the cutscene but from a different perspective. I always liked that part because when you're a kid and talk to the adult version of yourself he tells you to love and cherish your dad, which is foreshadowing the death of your father which happens shortly after that scene.
