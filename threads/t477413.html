Anyone played "Shattered Horizon" and/or "Lead and Gold"?

In the first one, you play team against team, either in deathmatch or with particular goals to achieve - standard shooter fare, except YOU ARE IN SPACE. There's no gravity, no fixed directional orientation. You can mount to surfaces with artificial gravity, but why do that when you can boost toward the enemy line, cut off your guidance system and glide in undetected for a face stab? I have yet to see any game attempt the same concept.

In the second one, you play one of a motley band of Wild West gunslingers and use teamwork to complete tasks, such as robbing the bank (or protecting it) or good ol' Capture the Flag. When it first came out, it had something of a TF2 vibe, but while it had the same setup of specialized classes and teamwork as a central mechanic, the atmosphere set it apart.

So what of these games now?

Neither game has much of a community anymore. The powers that be see no reason to create new content for them because servers are empty. "Shattered Horizon" introduced fairly decent AI to play against, but it's not the same.

The studio that released "Shattered Horizon", FutureMark, was acquired by Rovio, so the best one can hope for there is zero-gravity Angry Birds. The game will likely amount to nothing more than a benchmark.

Fatshark, which released "Lead and Gold", was told by its publisher (Paradox Interactive) that it isn't interested in investing further in the game. Fortunately, Fatshark owns rights to the intellectual property, so they have the ability to release a sequel - and they might have that chance if "Krater" and "War of the Roses" do well enough.

Of course, it's silly to think that just because I enjoyed a game, it must therefore deserve to be successful and well-loved. And I'm just as guilty as anyone else for the failure of those games - I didn't stick with them, either, and there were reasons. Still, I can't help but feel that if these games had received more exposure, or if the publishers put more effort into helping them succeed, things could have been different.
