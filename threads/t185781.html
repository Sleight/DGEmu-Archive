KKiririn-shiORIGINAL POSTNov 27 2006, 07:50 AM
The two pinned topics in the staff forum are both messy and outdated. I would like to propose a revision of the guidelines with current concerns. Below is my proposition, all comments and suggestions are welcome. It takes into accounts of course all relevant features from Teck's and Saady's guides. (What is in red are just comment and will be removed of course).________________This guide is essentially intended to unify the different way of modding so that we have all the same policy as to how we mod. We shouldn't appear totally disorganized in the eyes of regular members and should speak with one single voice.1. What You Should KnowThere are two essential aspects of moderating your sections: "forum rules" and "basic knowledge sections you are modding".Rules: A moderator is similar to an officer as your primary responsibility is to enforce the board rules as well as answer members' questions regarding them. Therefore, you MUST know the rules inside out so to speak. If you find there are ambiguities or are unsure of something yourself, be sure you find out the facts immediately. In addition, be sure to check the Rules and Guidelines forum regularly for any updates/alterations. In general this item should not be a major concern since you (and the other staff) have already proven to be exceptional rule-abiding members.Basic knowledge (not applicable to global moderators): In general, bmods (baby moderators) are either assigned sections according to their domain of competence and/or interest or you may have been asked which forums you want to cover. Nevertheless, be sure to become knowledgeable of that section's main topic. For example, if you are unfamiliar with a certain console (and you have that section), try to learn the basics like the most popular games, most common features, frequently asked questions, etc. When you are going to mod some board, make sure to read/understand at least the pinned topics from that section.Don't appear like a fool in the eyes of members especially if you want them to respect you for your position.2. Moderator Powers (certain powers are only available in your assigned sections)Editing: You have the ability to edit members posts. This is designed for adding a "moderating-type" comment such as a verbal warning or a reason for closing a topic (more on closing later in this guide). Do not alter the actual content of the post except in case of spam or offensive content/pictures. Lastly, always identify yourself so not to make it look as if the member typed it himself/herself. Leaving identification will also allow the member to contact you if they have questions concerning why their post was edited or closed. Do not use this tool to insult or make fun of someone.NOTE: Don't hesitate to edit the title when it is not explicit of the content.Closing: This is the most basic tool and as you know, it alters a thread so that members can no longer reply. Simply put, close any thread that violates the rules (exception: topics in the wrong section as those can simply be moved instead). For example, some threads you may close include (and of course reasons are not limited to this): Random X vs Y topics (i.e. those without a reasonable explanation for their creation within the thread's content)Obvious spam topics and/or threads that can not possibly start a proper discussion Plagirization of review/cheat/walkthrough/etc without purposeLastly, ALWAYS provide a reason why you close a topic (unless it's blatantly a spam thread with contents like "sdjklfhsldfhlksdfh;;;;"). Your explanation can be brief like "Pointless thread" or "Done before". It is often fastidious to do it but at least try the best you can to follow this guideline. See what other moderators have done if you need examples.NOTE: Moderators (and up) can reply and/or edit topics in locked threads so don't bother re-opening and closing a thread if you need to make a change.You can either edit the first post or reply directly. In case where you just edit it, please make sure to leave your name so that the topic starter knows who to complain at.Opening: If by any chance, you encounter a topic closed without any obvious reason and you believe that it is a mistake, reopen the thread and mention why you did it (this situation is uncommon though). Pinning: The current rule is still standing whereby no bmods/smods can pin a topic without the permission from a sentinel or admin (and in most cases, you may lack the access rights to do so in general). Post the thread you want to pin in the correct section and PM a sentinel/admin to get his/her approval. If you are unsure whether members should see it before it is pinned, just set it as invisible. Eligible threads for pinning cover topics that only concern "general information" on the specific section as opposed to a thread about a specific game, question, etc.Unpinning: Same as above, (i.e. permission from a sentinel/admin is required).Moving: When a topic is in the wrong forum, move it to the correct section. Whether you wish to leave a link in the original forum is up to your discretion. However, special cases are the "Spam Center" and "the Dump". Move a topic in the Spam Center only if you think the topic deserves to remain open but is not suited in regular forums. If it is just a pointless thread (as mentioned in the first point), just close it. Move a topic to the Dump if it is a common dupe thread. Try to avoid moving non obvious ones though but instead close it, post the link to the original thread, and leave it where it is.Merging: When members are opening a bunch of dupe/pointless threads, merge them all, close the resulting thread, and warn him/her while providing the link to this specific thread. This will make the warn log tracking easier (NOTE: If a member floods your section, follow the same steps as mentioned above but in addition, suspend the member and report them for ban in the "bannings" forum). Lastly, merge threads that show evidence of the member clicking "New Topic" instead of "Add Reply". How to merge threads properly: When you are reading topic A, choose merge and paste topic B's URL into the merge option, topic A will overwrite topic B's topic ID. Just make sure you don't merge an older topic into a newer in case there might be links to the old one.Splitting: This function is rarely used but still available to you. When a member posts a completely unrelated reply in a thread, if you find it interesting or worth to answer, split the reply to create another thread. It happens sometimes in the Support and Suggestion forum when members sometimes ask for support in the pinned threads whereas it has nothing to do with their purpose. Otherwise, just edit the post with a message like "irrelevant" or if it is too anoying, delete it. Also, splitting can be used to correct the error of improper merging.Deleting: This particular action is not recommend so please use it as a last resort. However, one exception is for a flooder who posts garbage like "asdhjklkj;;;" (though if they are threads, it's better touse the "merge and close" tactic mentioned above).Also, when a member reports minor rule breaking posts that are from more than about one week (use your personal appreciation), delete the post rather than take punitive actions like point removing or warning.Setting as Invisible: Do this when you are unsure of what to do with a thread that can be potentially problematic if others see it. For example, if a member opens a heated debate on religion, better make it invisible so to be sure whether it is authorized. Also, as said above, you may also set a topic to invisible if you want it pinned and wish for a sentinel/admin to review it before members see the thread.Setting as Visible: This is more common since all members put under mod preview will have their topic/post invisible. Once you take the appropriate actions, set the thread back to visible.3. Warns and Point DeductionPoint Deduction: This is a relatively new and very handy tool. Do it for every minor rules infractions such as overly short posts (i.e. less than one full sentence), overuse of smilies, TYPING ALL IN CAPS, dupe threads, etc. The post will automatically be edited to indicated that points have been penalized and will also state whom deducted them in case the member wishes to question that particular moderator.Positive Warns: With the availability of the point deduction/award feature, warn issuing should be reserved for more severe cases. Unlike before, do not issue a positive warn each time a member breaks a rule, especially if it is minor. However, warn them when they break major rules like posting insults/offending content/pictures, if they intentionally post spam, or if they repeatedly break minor rules. The reason being is that positive warns are permanently attached to that particular member's profile and greatly affect their GP>DP conversion rate whereas the 10 GPs lost in a deduction can be compensated by two posts or one thread. Lastly, give members a chance to redeem themselves. Unless they are relentlessly flooding for GPs, do not warn them multiple times but rather give them a chance to view their warn log so they can correct their actions. Point Awarding (for global moderators and above only): Not often used but you are welcome to do so when you see a high quality post like a good comment on a discussion, a helpful reply to solve a problem, or when a member writes a useful guide. Try to edit the post with a message like "POINTS were awarded for this post" in green if possible.Negative Warns: All the cases where you should give negative warns are described in the warn FAQ. However just a precision on the warn earned by reports: give them only if the member makes many useful reports. A bunch of reports for one word posts is not worthy of a decrease. However members making reports for dupe threads with link to the original should be awarded if they do it regularly. The exact number of such reports is up to your discretion but do check the warn log before issuing it to ensure that another moderator has not done so already. Basically one week should be an appropriate time span between two negative warns for reports.4. TaggingThis idea was proposed initially but seldom applied due to the incredible number of threads. However it is never too late to begin and if possible, re-tag old topics as well. Please take a look at the NDS section for an illustration of some tags. For example, here are the tag I propose:[OFFICIAL]: For all official topic. There should be one single official topic for each game, movie, and console. If you have time, try to post the basic information like the title, developer, and release date. Make sure to only tag the thread with the most replies or the most recently active. Just use common sense.: Use this tag when the original problem is solved. By "original" I mean "not asked before" (otherwise just close it). Solved topics should not be closed under normal circumstances.: For spam/dupe/pointless topics you closed. I don't think it is essential so no need to do it each time.[GAMES]: For all topics like 'best RPG games' or 'first game', etc (if you have a better idea for this label, feel free to share it). These compensate for the 3 character limitation on the search function (which prevents you from using words like GPS, NDS, GBA, etc)[MOVIES]: Same thing as above but for movies.[ANIMES]: Same thing as above but for anime.: Same thing as above but for book.NOTE: the three last ones are quite uncommon.5. MiscellaneousAttitude: NEVER insult a member no matter how "dumb" or "n00bish" they may seem. This rule applies to the forum, PMs, and the warn log. You don't know who these members so you have no right to attack them personally. Doing so will not be tolerated.The Dump: Follow the instructions for moving threads to the dump. Do not manually delete topics therein but instead use the "update dump feature" written by Firebat and atomicknight. It can be found here.Ban: Before reporting a member for a ban, read the two pinned topics in the banning subforum. For smods, think carefully before banning a member (as to whether they actually deserve it). For delicate cases or if you are unsure, report and leave the decision to sentinels/admins.Thanks for your attention.DGEmu Administration.
