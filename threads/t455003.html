I personally don't think it's ideal to be a vegetarian and I disagree with your statement that there's no downsides to being vegetarian. We evolved our massive, energy hungry brains first through eating meat, then through eating cooked food. Certainly, there are some benefits to being a vegetarian but they're not as big a benefit as some vegetarians would like to make out.For example, vegetarians state that it's better for the environment. By going vegetarian, you're not eating meat. Therefore, we aren't wasting agricultural land growing food for animals and wasting more land to graze the animals. Thing is, it all depends on where you graze the animal. There are some locations where you can do nothing but graze animals, for example, in quite large swathes of Australia or in the New York state where much of the land wasn't suitable for growing crops anyway.In fact, some studies have shown that eating a diet that still has meat (but only a little) is far more efficient (in environmental terms) than being vegetarian.http://www.newscientist.com/blog/shortshar...-brain-and.htmlIt's probably because what people fail to take into account is quality of agricultural land and the need for protein. Vegetables aren't an efficient source of protein, so you need to grow more veg like soy, thus taking up more land which could have been left natural.
