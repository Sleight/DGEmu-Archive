I'm not talking here about your legacy to the world, I'm interested in how your own family will remember you.Think about how much you know about your brothers/sisters (if you have them). Maybe you know more than you want to about them!What about your parents?Your grandparents?Getting a bit fuzzy here maybe, but go further back.Your great-grandparents?Your great-great-grandparents? Go this far, four generations back, and most likely you don't even know their names, never mind what they were like and how they lived their lives. Do you think that the ease with which we can record our day to day lives will change things for your descendants?Or will it all just evaporate, and they will know as little about you as you do about your predecessors?What sort of things would you like them to know, and do you think there's anything you can do about that? And does it matter to you?

Yes, I think it matters that my offspring knows who I was, what I did and what motivated me to do just that. Not personally, obviously, for I hope I'll be long gone by the time my grand-grand children walk this earth, but considering my own interest in where I came from (as it is undoubtedly part of what I am now; genetically) I would very much like my offspring to find out, especially with the modern ease of technological prowess, easily everything there is to know about me, preferably written by myself. An autobiography. They should obviously be skeptical (call it scientific) in reading it, as I wrote it myself which automatically comes with a sense of self glorification (unless I become known to a point where others will write about me, in which case they'll have a required reference which should be included in the biography also).I think something like a minimalistic (auto-)biography should be written by/for everybody, to promote knowledge about the state of mind of your ancestors/predecessors (for whatever purpose), or for general progress in accurate historical reference. Whether this will be made 'public' should be a choice left to the person it's about, perhaps only to be released to family of a selected grade/type/level. It should be treated much as medical dossiers, for in a sense, it is a psychological documentation of a person anyway.Personally I would like my offspring to know much about my political perspective/opinion(s), activities such as personal hobbies and sports played. Also (and mostly) I would like them to know about my profession, education and achievements. I will most likely write an autobiography someday, and I'll include everything I've just listed.I know quite a bit about my parents, grandparents and grand-grandparents for they've been active in quite a few public/governmental activities/projects (they were mostly architects, engineers and philosophers) which were documented in great detail. However, that hardly tells me anything about their personality, and I never had the honor to read anything written by them (except my parents). Or read a biography.
