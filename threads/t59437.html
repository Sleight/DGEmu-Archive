SScathelockDec 02 2004, 09:23 AM
I think enough insults have been made to this game that people get the point. To attempt to sum it up for people who haven't played it, who are still trying to figure out whether or not it's worth it, here's my two cents:Runescape is a slow-moving game. Leveling up takes a long, long time, and the fighting is more or less go lick on an enemy, and whoever has the better stats and armor has the better chance of winning. If you die, oh well, lose some stuff, go back to the bank and buy more stuff. It does get sort of monotonous, since once you get to high levels, you often won't be using the skills you had before. For example, you can smith bronze daggers when you're at a low smithing level, but once you're able to smith mithril at, say, 30 levels higher, you probably won't ever touch bronze again. The exception would be magic, since there are various types. If you want to prevent an enemy from running away, you'll use a low-level entrapment spell repeatedly, even if you're already 25 magic levels higher than what's required to cast it.The major pros of Runescape are that, as has been emphasized, there is a completely free version of it that gives you the basic gameplay idea close enough to the members' version. There's a fair variety of items (even though you can't really tell by the graphics) to obtain, and the skills are fun to learn about, since for each category, you can do more things if you're at a higher level. e.g. if you're at a high enough fishing level, you can catch lobsters or swordfish, which heal the most health if you eat them. Of course, you need to be able to cook whatever you catch before you can eat it, and that requires a cooking level :D Also, since it's obviously an online multiplayer game, it can be amusing trying to buy/sell your goods. Lots of negotiation goes on, and you can make a lot of allies and friends through trading. Just don't try to rip off anyone and be a jerk - there's a reporting system, even though its effectiveness is in question.The quests are decent. Since there's no main storyline, you have to go hunt around for stuff. If you decide that you do like the game enough, I recommend you spend the time to actually try and figure out some of the puzzles, instead of asking someone else to help you (which admittedly would be faster). Like I said, the gamplay can be kind of slow, but otherwise, the quests would be pretty much equal to the side stories you'd find in an RPG or something.Basically, the bottom line is this: the game takes patience for upgrading your character, and you have to enjoy small, very random storylines, as well as interacting with random people, to enjoy the game. If you get lost, ask around, people will help. Also check out the actual site (others have already put it up, but for convenience's sake, it's just www.runescape.com, and there's an online game manual) which explains some stuff. If you've tried it, and you're willing to pay for this type of game, maybe you should look at other, more advanced stuff. I hear World of Warcraft is going to kick @$$Uh, no more bashing on this thread, please - we get the point, and we understand why you find it boring or sucky or whatever (yeah, yeah, graphics suck, takes too long to get into x_X). Don't use up any more space after you've gotten it out of your system.Happy Runescaping to anyone who decides to try it :P
