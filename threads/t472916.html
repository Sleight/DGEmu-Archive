This is really interesting because from having taken Materials Engineering [why, I don't know] a few semester ago....engineers have studied the heat treatment of steel for 2 centuries, all of the specific states and microstructures have been well documented and tested since forever, and yet there is still a new discovery.For those who may have taken a course with steel microstructures [milo?]:According to another article, the reason why this heat treatment process creates a stronger steel than the martensite standard is that while carbides usually dissolve during austenitization and annealing, the flash bainite process heats the ferrite extremely quickly and forgoes annealing altogether, so the carbides never completely dissolve and are present in the final microstructure after cooling to martensite, which gives it new properties. I guess the reason why this was never discovered was because a century ago, there was no technology to heat the steel so quickly. And then since the annealing process for manipulating hardness/tensile strength/ductility/etc was already "mastered", no one was doing research with very fast processesSo...uneducated serendipity whooWhat the article doesn't say is that while it is 7% stronger than commerical standards, there are already methods to make steels with several times more tensile strength [though sacrificing ductility]The important part is that the process is shorter and requires less energy, cheaper manufacturing = cheaper steel products in the future?
