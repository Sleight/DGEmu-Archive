Heya Folks, This is what's planned in the dgRPG so far, this is as detailed as I can think of right now. dgRPG is goign to be a pure fully D&D based RPG (random numbers will replace the dices). alright i'm starting..Note: source of information is www.Wizards.com - 80% of the information on this page is taken from there, the original ideas and changes to the origianl D&D are from this very community.Character Selection and Game BeginningAlright this section is about the very beginning of the game - registration and so, and also about classes and their features.Character Stats - I'm starting with this because next sections are somewhat based on this. Well your character qualities are:Strength which will determine the damage you make with melee weapons and thrown weapons and the weight of equipments and items you can carry.Dexterity - measures hand-eye coordination, agility, reflexes, and balance, gives bonuses to armor class (more below) and ranged weapons and thieving chances.Intelligence - Intelligence measures how well a character learns and reasons. It determines how many languages a beginning character can speak, how fast skill points accumulate, how many spells a wizard can cast and the amount of magic points one can accumulate.Magic Power - Determines strength and duration of spells and bonuses magic resistance.Vitality - Determines one's maximum hit points and stamina and reduces rest time.Character Status VariablesHP - Hit points, go down when being damaged and up upon recovery - when it reaches zero you die and wait a certain amount of time (we'll set it later) before you're being resurrected (with reduced money and inventory or reduced vitality)MP - Magic Points or Mana Points or whatever - let's you cast spells - think of it as magic dust - when you run out of it u can't do magic :D .Attack Rate - (Equipment and strength) simply determines the amount of damage you'll do to a certain creature (will vary from creature to creature depending on their armor class)Armor Class - (Equipment and Dexterity) Determines your ability to avoid being hit in combat, from non-magical items.Magic Resistance - (Equipment and Magic Power) determines your ability to avoid being hit by a spell - direct damage or mind effect.Experience - determines how experienced you are - basically needed for leveling up.Note: Spells also have the ability to change one's stats but their effect is temporairly only.Character Creation - Alrighty so you've registered to the (future-to-be) dgRPG.com site, you login using your username and pass for the first time and kaboom u hit the character creation page. where you basically choose your gender, pick an avatar, choose your alignment (9 are planned, vary from Chaotic Evil to Lawful Good, but it will change during the gameplay as you interact good or bad with the system - more details in a minute) and choose your class (more details below) and your initial statistics (more details below).Character's Alignment - Right now the only non-original idea for alignments is the normal 9 alignment every RPG has - lawful good, neutral good, chaotic good, lawful neutral, neutral, chaotic neutral, lawful evil, neutral evil, and chaotic evil.Alignment changes the character's choice of options - a chaotic evil character won't be able to pet a baby and a lawful good character won't be able to steal, some classes won't go with some alignments and some doors and quests won't open to some people aligned in some way (heh) but in general the effect of a character's alignment won't change the gameplay to the extreme :( Selecting a Class - This is a pretty important step in the game, class is a one time selection only (unlike in the IBRPG, you will not be able to change classes, once u choose your class it's final).Each class will feature an information page with full details about the class and pros and cons plus a set of bonuses.Choosing Initial Statistics - Each character starts with 50 quality points which he can split between his different qualities - basically each quality must have at least 3 quality points so that leaves the user only 35 points to spread between the 5 qualities when random split will be available and each class will have a recommeneded way to share qualities.The restrictions are 3 points minimum for each quality and 20 points maximum. Some classes will also have restrictions - for example a Wizard will require an intelligence and spell power of at least 12.ClassesEach class has a set of skills, spells, equipment and items one can only use if belonging to the that class, also some shops, resting houses and more will provide certain classes some benefits and certain terrains will provide certain bonuses for certain classes - a magician will be have increased magic power in holy lands and druids will have quicker rests and more MP in the woods (examples).Right now we have the basic classes minus some crappy ones:Barbarian: The barbarian is a ferocious warrior. Barbarians use fury and instinct to bring down foes. don't expect to use much magic with this class but sure will satisfy those who are in the game for melee slaughter.Cleric: Masters divine magic and are able to fight face to face, they are sworn to act in accordance with their deities’ wishes (limits them with spells).Druids: Druids cast divine spells much the same as clerics do, though they get their spells from the power of nature, not from deities. Their spells are mostly nature and animals related. an experienced druid has the ability to take the shapes of animals (temporairly).Fighter: A fighter is a warrior with exceptional combat capability. Whether they are questing knights, conquering overlords, king’s champions, elite foot soldiers, hardened mercenaries, bandit kings, or simply adventurers, all fighters earn their living with their weapons. Fighters have the best all-around fighting capabilities of all the character classes. They are familiar with all standard weapons and armors, but most develop particular specialities with experience.Ranger: A skilled hunter, stalker, and warrior of the wilderness. Rangers frequently serve as woodland protectors, aiding those who live in or travel through the forests. They have skills that allow them to survive in the wilderness, to find their prey, and to avoid detection. Finally, experienced rangers have such ties to nature that they can actually draw on natural power to cast divine spells, much as druids do.Paladin: Paladins are champions of justice and destroyers of evil. Divine power protects them, warding off harm, defending against disease, healing wounds, and guarding against fear. Paladins can direct this divine power to heal others. In addition, each paladin eventually gains the service of a mighty mount (a creature that serves the paladin), who is intelligent and magically protected. Paladins must be lawful good, and they lose their divine powers if they deviate from that alignment.Roughe: Rogues can be diplomats, spies, thugs, thieves, scouts or any other profession involving trickery and skill at sneaking. In combat, a rogue can deliver extra damage with a sneak attack. The higher the rogue’s level, the more damage the sneak attack can inflict. Though rogues are not spellcasters themselves, they can figure out magic well enough to cast spells from scrolls, activate wands, and use just about any other magic item.Sorcerer: Sorcerers use no books, no mentors, and no theories. They develop basic powers at puberty. Sorcerers know fewer spells than wizards do, and they acquire new spells more slowly. In addition, they cannot specialize in certain schools of magic the way wizards can. But sorcerers can cast their spells more often than wizards can, and they need not select and prepare them ahead of time. They also have more time to learn fighting skills and are proficient with simple weapons.Wizards: A wizard is a potent spellcaster schooled in the arcane arts. To a wizard, nothing is as important as learning and imrpoving spells. Like casters of divine spells, wizards must prepare their spells in advance, though they do so by studying their spellbooks rather than meditation and praying. Wizards are uniquely capable of creating magic items, from simple scrolls to powerful staves. To do so, however, they must invest some part of themselves in the process.SkillsSkill is a talent that a character acquires and improves through training.Few D&D Terms to know for this section:Skil Rank: A number indicating how much training or experience a character has with a given skill. Skill rank is incorporated into the skill modifier, which in turn improves the chance of success for skill checks with that skill.Skill Modifier: The bonus or penalty associated with a particular skill. Skill modifier = skill rank + miscellaneous modifiers. (Miscellaneous modifiers include armor check penalty, situational modifiers, and so forth.) Skill modifiers apply to skill checks made by characters in the course of using the corresponding skills.Skill Check: A check relating to use of a skill. The chances of being able to use it, if u wish.Class Skill: A skill to which characters of a particular class have easier access than characters of other classes. Characters may buy class skills at a rate of 1 rank per skill point, as opposed to 1/2 rank per skill point for cross-class skills. The maximum rank for a class skill is 3 + character level.Cross-Class Skills: Skills not attachd with any class - skills all characters can use.Skill Points: The currency for 'buying' skills. Gained when leveling up.dgRPG's Skills Panel: The right side of the screen will be dedicated to a quick panel and will be the space for all panels - the skills panel will too open there. The skills panel will have 2 modes - the Use mode and the Acquire mode, use mode is for using the skills and acquire mode is for buying new skills - the acquire panel will only be avaible when the character has skills points left, and the panel will have "Use | Acquire" buttons on top to change from Use mode to Acquire mode and vice versa.Gaining Skills: At each level, a character gains skill points and spends them to 'buy' skill ranks. Each skill point buys 1 rank in a class skill or 1/2 rank in a cross-class skill. When having skill points left the skills panel, when opened, will feature all available to 'buy' skills and let u pick which ones u want. (it will also flash and annoy you about having skill points left :( )Using Skills: If the skill is in the quick panel then you can just click it on the quick panel and it will be put to use. Otherwise u need to open the skills panel (doh there'll be a button for that). Now if u got skill points left u'll need to switch to USE mode by clicking the button on the skills panel top and then choose the skill u want to use, if not just click on the skill u want to use and tada, you're done.SpellsDefinitions:Spell: A one-time magical effect. The two primary categories of spells are arcane and divine. Clerics, druids, paladins, and rangers cast divine spells, while wizards and sorcerers cast arcane spells. Spells are further grouped into eight schools of magic.School of Magic: A group of related spells that work in similar ways. The eight schools of magic available to spellcasters are abjuration, conjuration, divination, enchantment, evocation, illusion, necromancy, and transmutation.Spell Immunity: A creature with spell immunity avoids the effects of spells and spell-like abilities that directly affect it. This works exactly like spell resistance, except that it cannot be overcome. Sometimes spell immunity is conditional or applies to only spells of a certain kind or level. Spells that do not allow spell resistance are not affected by spell immunity. Spell immunity is usually an extraordinary ability.Spell Level: A number from 0 to 9 that indicates the general power of a spell. (but we already knew that didn't we :( )Spell Preperation: Part of the spellcasting process for wizards, clerics, paladins, rangers, and druids. Preparing a spell requires careful reading from a spellbook (for wizards) or devout prayers or meditation (for divine spellcasters). The character actually casts the first and lengthiest part of the spell during the preparation phase, leaving only the very end for completion at another time. To use a prepared spell, the character finishes the casting with the appropriate spell components -- a few special words, some complex gestures, a specific item, or a combination of the three. A prepared spell is used up once cast and cannot be cast again until the spellcaster prepares it again. Sorcerers need not prepare their spells.Note: in dgRPG preperation of spells will take 20 minutes per spell and the character could chooes to prepare the spells manually or add it to the rest time.Spell Slot: The "space" in a spellcaster's mind dedicated to holding a spell of a particular spell level. A spellcaster has enough spell slots to accommodate an entire day's allotment of spells. Spellcasters who must prepare their spells in advance generally fill their spell slots during the preparation period, though a few slots can be left open for spells prepared later in the day. A spellcaster can always opt to fill a higher-level spell slot with a lower-level spell, if desired.Domain: A granted power and a set of nine divine spells (one each of 1st through 9th level) themed around a particular concept and associated with one or more deities. The available domains are: Air, Animal, Chaos, Death, Destruction, Earth, Evil, Fire, Good, Healing, Knowledge, Law, Luck, Magic, Plant, Protection, Strength, Sun, Travel, Trickery, War, and Water - i'm not sure how many of those we'll feature in dgRPG.
