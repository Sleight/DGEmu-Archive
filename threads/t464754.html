NNova²Mar 20 2011, 01:48 AM
That is a good question. I too am fond of the old-school nostalgia and style of the old cartridge based console systems, and wished they would bring them back. I wish they would bring them back not only because they were durable enough to withstand the abuse of children and the idea is cool, but because flash-based technology has improved allot in the speed and space departments, in fact I feel that it has improved enough that there is no real advantages for using a disc over a cartridge. You can store the same and even more data than on any type of disc out on the market, the speed has been increased to be able to read/write faster than any other disc media, and no longer need to have a separate card or battery backup to save our game-progress, if needed they could easily expand the memory banks of the cartridge to fit bigger or smaller games on them, we have also greatly improved the durability of the slots/sockets for the connections to the pins, and did I mention the fact that it would be far more durable medium to stand up to the abuse of kids? To me it is harder to find an excuse to not bring cartridge based games back.Right now the largest commercial disc medium available is a Dual Layer Blu-Ray disc which can hold up to 50GB, the largest commercial thumb-drive I have seen holds 256GB, the largest commercial SSD drive I have seen was 2TB, and the largest SD card I have seen (SDXC) was 64GB. Some of the larger games I have seen so far are around 10 to 15GB. So it is not like we are talking about an insane amount of space needed to pack a game into flash-based memory on a cartridge, for the Nintendo Wii we are looking at a single-layer DVD which holds 4.7GB, and not every game uses all 4.7GB. When we look at the reality of things, back when the Nintendo Game Cube came out a CD was the better way of doing things at the time (they could have done it better like they did with the discs to the Sony PSP), it was cheap and delivered allot of space for the game at the time. But now the technology for flash-based medium has surpassed any disc-based medium we have today, on all levels accept cost, but you should still be capable of producing a reasonably priced cartridge for a console game in today’s world. I feel that it is not so much that discs are the modern way of doing things as it is the cheaper way of doing things. I could see that with future consoles it is a possibility to see cartridge based games return, due to their advantages over discs. However with the current trend I see it being more like the console being hooked to the internet (or some similar network) were not only do you link up with other players to play multiplayer, but you also have the option to purchase any existing game in the library and download them straight to your console. Similar concepts have already been put into motion with existing console systems.Optical discs are becoming an old out-dated technology for computers and consoles, actually outdated just in general. We all know the CD-ROM came out in the early 80s, the DVD came out in the mid-90s, and the Blu Ray came out commercially around the mid 00’s, and they were all based off of each other, however the CD ROM was based off of the original optical disc which was invented in around 1959. And the roots of flashed-based storages, like EEPROM is from the 1970s, so discs are more “Space-Race Technology” than “Space-Age Technology” when compared to cartridges. LOLAnother thing you have to look at is the psychological aspect of it too. Most of us are fond of the cartridge based consoles because we grew up on them and have allot of fond memories from that era in our lives, and this fondness takes on a thrill of its own in our subconscious when we think about picking up a game cartridge and sliding it into the slot the console.Sorry for getting a little out there and off topic, but it is an interesting discussion and argument with discs vs cartridges for consoles.
