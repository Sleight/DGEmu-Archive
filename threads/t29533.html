The following computer specifics constitute the minimum system requirements for proper emulation of the PlayStation (PSX) console:
- Pentium 200MMX or Compatible
- 64MB of RAM
- 16x CD-Rom
- Windows 9x
- DirectX 7.0

The following constitutes the minimum recommended system requirements for faster, more responsive, and otherwise better emulation:
- Pentium II 500 or Faster
- 256MB of RAM
- DirectX or OpenGL Compatible Graphic Card (for example: NVidia GeForce 2)
- 32x CD-Rom- Windows 2000 or XP
- DirectX 8

To begin, you will need an emulator (the program which runs the game). We recommend you download ePSXe.

NOTE: Download version 1.52 rather than 1.60; as 1.60 has terrible compatibility with Squaresoft games.

The following steps will guide you in configuring your emulator, and beginning play of your first game:
I. Download the emulator. If the above noted link does not work, go to Zophar's Domain to acquire a copy.
II. Extract the emulator. It is important that you do not open it yet.
III. Download a BIOS file (SCPH1001). You may acquire such from:
Uploaded.net
DepositFile.com
Or DGEmu's DLC Forum > Playstation Emulator with BIOs (Windows and Linux)
IV. Place the downloaded BIOS file into the "Bios" folder that was created when you extracted and installed your emulator.
V. Download a video plugin. You may acquire the latest version at Pete's Domain. It is recommended that you download more than one type, if only to test which is most compatible with your computer. Place the downloaded files into your "Plugins" folder.
NOTE: Version 2.6 is best if you are running a good video card, otherwise get 1.75.
VI. Start up the emulator; then follow the directions in the Configuration Wizard, selecting the files you downloaded when prompted.
VII. Configure the video plugin to optimize performance (fullscreen/window, resolution, detail). Do NOT forget to configure the input plugin as well, or you will be unable to play.
VIII. Download a PlayStation game (ISO). See the "PlayStation ISOs - Sites/Torrents" thread for a list of where to obtain such.
IX. Go to Configure>Video, and check "Use FPS Limit", then click "Auto Detect FPS Limit".
X. Click File, Load, select the ISO, and you are good to go.

Specific Game Notes
- Chrono Cross runs best when using Pete's D3D Driver 1.75 with ePSXe version 1.52. You must also configure your video plugin to enable "Odd/Even Hack".
- Tales of Destiny 2 runs best on Pete's Software Plugin 1.54; a copy of which you may download here.

Frequently Asked Questions/Troubleshooting Guide
Q1. When I unzip ePSXe I see files that say "Delete Me" in the folders the program creates; is it safe to delete them?
A1. Yes, you may safely delete all of said files.

Q2. My computer runs on the Macintosh operating system, is there a good PlayStation emulator for it?
A2. Your best bet would be to make use of either "PCSX" or "The Pi". Sadly, neither has anything approaching the quality of Windows emulators, PlayStation emulation for the Mac being sorely outdated and overlooked. You may obtain a copy of either of the aforementioned emulators at Zophar's Domain.

Q3. When I start-up ePSXe, a little DOS windows comes up as well; is that supposed to be there? Is it a bug? Can I close it?
A3. No, it is neither a bug, nor should you close it. The emulator requires that window to be up to fully function; do not close it.

Q4. My file comes with an extension that says ".7z", what is that, and how do I open it?
A4. "7z" is shorthand for 7zip, a file compression scheme whose popularity is catching on. Much like other zipped files, you will need an unzipper to "open" it. We recommend you make use of WinRAR, as it can unzip "zip", "7zip", "cab", "gzip", "rar", and other archived file types. You may obtain a copy of the program at the WinRAR homepage.

Q5. All I have are "torrent" files, so how do I make an iso out of them?
A5. Torrents are files used to download iso's, namely by using the BitTorrent program, or one of it's offshoots.

Q6. I continue to receive an error from my emulator that says "no WGL extension", what does that mean, and how do I fix it?
A6. This error crops up most often with those people making use of Pete's OpenGL plugins, who are running the emulator on a video card that is either outdated or simply non-compatible with the plugin. The simplest means of circumventing this error is to make use of Pete's 1.75 plugins; or else, consider purchasing a new video card. Occassionally with video cards from the GeForce 4 series, simply disabling any shader functions will solve the problem.

Q7. I keep getting a missing render-texture extension error, what am I doing wrong?
A7. You are using Pete's OpenGL2, there are a few flaws with it and inconsistences so try switching to either Pete's OpenGL or Direct3D plugins

Q8. I get an error saying "The DSOUND.dll file can not start" what happened?
A8. Problem: The DSOUND.DLL file cannot start. Check the file to determine the problem.Reason: Your DirectX is out of date and thus, incompatible with the software you are attempting to run.
Solution: Download the latest release of DirectX

Q9. I can't save using the memory card or save states, why not?
A9. Most likely the folder that the save states or memory cards are in are set on read-only, you need to turn this attribute off

Q10. Can I play ps1 backup games on a ps2 using swap magic?
A10. No. They are PS2 Boot Discs... for PSX/PSOne back-ups use the GameShark 2/Action Replay 2 original game discs to swap with and boot them up.

Bbasf11214Feb 20 2007, 02:09 AM
Codes only modify the memory addresses of the game that is being emulated. Unless you abuse the user database (or press ctrl-c while the game is running), it is impossible for PEC to use codes for different games at the same time.This is what happens when you do mess up in the user database:1. You will get graphical and/or sound glitches.2. The game will crash, but the emulator will still emulate the inevitable frozen screen.3. Your save file will not be affected.4. Your ISO image or physical media (CD) will not be affected.5. Your computer will not be affected, unless you used settings for the video/sound plugin that are too high for your computer to handle, but then that's you, not PEC, not the plugin, you.6. PEC will work even after you crash the game.If you activate too many codes at the same time, you can sometimes crash the emulator. You can avoid that by lowering the refresh rate of codes (configure the PEC plugin). If your computer is old (4~6 years), then you should lower the refresh rate of the codes to between 10 and 20. If your computer is REALLY old (7+), then you shouldn't be trying to emulate PSX (though you can still emulate GBA and SNES with good speeds).
