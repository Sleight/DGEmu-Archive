Okay, let's start in.In an RTS game, what really bugs me is lack of unit differentiation. Yeah, yeah, I know that it's good to have analogous units - but I mean like Advance Wars, where every group has the same Tech Tree - EVEN BLACK HOLE. I liked Civilization 2 where you could be fighting against a guy who still was using Musketeers while you were using Armors. I like being able to know that my guys are different. It's just me. I mean, even the first RTS for PC, Dune II had unit differentiation. Not much, mind you - but the Harkonnen didn't get Trikes and so were using Quads when everyone else was using trikes. Ordos Raider Trikes moved faster and had less defense than Atreides Trikes, and so on. In fact, the Saudakar don't have any units aside from the teams of three with rocket launchers. And that really made it fun.Sidescrollers: Bad graphics. Dumb enemies. Easy bosses or bosses who can only be hit at certain times because you can't get to them most of the time. If have no problem with bosses who go out of your attack range for a short time then come back, like Wario in Mario World 2. Games that provide one of these two things: 1. needless challenge that does not benefit the player either in storyline or game terms, or 2. provide no challenge whatsoever aside from the occasional "Boss Battle". Metroid Fusion at times seems to have both of these. Does anyone else remember the nonsense of "don't get the plasma missles, so you can talk with the mystery guy that then has ZERO effect on the storyline"? I do. And one general thing - bad glitches that hang up the game when you're not trying to do anything out of the ordinary.RPG games have a whole different set of problems. Unskippable storyline - this sucks hard. Bad graphics glitches, like obviously unshaded polygons there for no reason whatsoever. Character design...this bothers me a lot. For instance, I loved Castlevania : Harmony of Dissonance because Juste was cool. Final Fantasy Tactics Advance - same thing, Marche, Montblanc, and the rest were kickass. But Final Fantasy IV...Ugh. That main character was a pansy! He was a Pansy Dragoon. You know what I mean. Oh, and another element of character design. Is it so hard to make my character look just a little different than others of his class but not always be himself? Yeah. That bugs me. Oh, but one thing I love is when the characters are like, "what in the kaleidoscopic hell is going on here?" when the game throws something completely messed-up at you. Like, let's talk Harmony of Dissonance again. When Juste first encounters the shopkeeper, he's like...WTF? and then just goes on about his business, shaking his head in confusion at the man's logic. I also hate games that give a character huge amounts of either impossible or improbable history - for this I use Metroid Zero Mission as my example - what was up with that scene with the baby Samus seeing the painting on the wall about her Power Suit and having scratched a drawing in the wall which she then remembered? And then that whole idea getting stolen from Metroid and used in a really crappy movie, Underworld Evolution. Okay. FPS now. Firstly, weapon design. I know. Every game since DOOM seems to feel this need for a chainsaw somewhere. You don't need it, people, there are better things to do with your life. Second. I understand that every game needs a pistol. Gotta have a basic-level weapon. But could we please stop making it always look the same? Thank you! Now, UT has some interesting stuff, which is good. Odd guns, different races, different gametypes. It's a good game for that. Now, let's talk honestly. Ragdoll physics - do we really, really need it? I just want to kill and let the bodies fall where they may. Useless "taunts" that only get you killed - this pisses me off no end. Whenever I see someone "taunting", especially on UT, I take my lightning gun/sniper rifle/Zoom Instagib and on all the machines on the LAN, you hear "HEAD SHOT". Oh, and I hate messed-up targeting methods, like in Nightfire for GBA (don't tell me, I know it's an FPS on handheld. But to target, you had to like...hold down three buttons and the D-Pad, and even then you weren't sure you were on the guy). Kill.Zone and Wolf3dGBA are good examples of how an FPS should work. Simple, direct methods of targeting. D-Pad. In Kill.Zone, you use L&R to strafe, select to switch weapons, etc. Though when I first emulated Wolf3dGBA and DOOMGBA, I was having trouble, because I'd hit the 2 key and expect my pistol and go 'oops, it's not PC'. Then I'd quit out, go play the DOS version, and beat the GBA version again. I'm sick like that.Oh, and one thing I really, really hate is when companies and people have huge amounts of story they can't pack into the game, and so release sourcebooks...IN JAPAN. Someone already mentioned Zero's three "Heroic Deaths". Here's another set of gripes. I want English versions of the sourcebooks and Remastered Tracks. Is it so wrong? I mean, I'm a fan of the game, I'm as big a fan as any, and I'd like to know the background story - the fact that Zero was meant to be the main character of MMX for instance. Yeah, he was. That's why his appearance in Power Fighters was so important. You were going to be fighting as a Wily number, not a Light number. Oh, well.If I missed anything, feel free to tell me.EDIT: Oh, I just realized another thing. In any game where there're main characters, especially military ones, I hate the self-doubting nonsense. Patton said it best, "No poor bastard ever won a war by dying for his country. He won by making other poor bastards die for their country." I don't care that my character isn't a paragon of goodness or that he's on an unassaultable moral high-ground. If he can kill, and he feels he has to kill, just let him kill, dammit!
