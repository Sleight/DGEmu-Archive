While we can easily look at the world around us and observe cultures which can be extremely contrasting at times, those who pursue said holy grail, argue that these are only superficial differences. Indeed they have proposed that in order to understand what human nature is exactly, we must strip back the interference of 'civilization'. But if we were to strip it back, what exactly would we find? On one hand, we have had the view that human nature is quite dark. This can be demonstrated by the 'State of Nature', which proposed that without a State to protect us from human nature society would devolve. It was held that the devolution would see a world where it was all against all. Even more contemporary thinkers such as Steven Pinker hold this to be true. As seen by his assertion that a State of Anarchy would encourage an environment where one felt compelled to use pre-emptive strikes, in order to take a threat out before they could take you out. This was also supported in the fictional book, 'Lord of the Flies'. Where throughout the book we were shown a group of children, who slowly devolved back to human nature.In contrast to this, we have had the idea of the noble savage. It is through this that it was proposed that in direct contrast to the State of Nature that human nature is inherently positive. Such a view holds that it is civilization which corrupts us. Commonly this view romanticises tribal peoples and hunter gatherers. This can be demonstrated by numerous scholars who have declared hunter gatherer societies to be free of violence, oppression and all the traits in our society which are commonly assigned negative traits. A good example of this exists in the fictional story of Tarzan. A boy who is raised outside of the evil influences of society which are evil. It is portrayed that without such influences he grows up to become pure. Well much more pure than those in civilization anyway.Please note: This thread is no longer Debate of the Week!
