FFakey1974Jun 28 2010, 09:23 AM
This topic caught my eye as I was browsing through the forums. In short, Fibromyalgia is a medically recognized disease/syndrome. Unfortunately, the problem with fibromyalgia is that there is no objective test/labs/studies that can give a definitive diagnosis, which for many medical professionals is hard to deal with. The diagnosis of fibromyalgia is very subjective and based pretty much on patient's testimony. This can lead to people taking advantage of physicians to seek drugs (as unlikely hero pointed out). The other problem with the diagnosis is that it can be a "trash bag" diagnosis, meaning that if the doctor can't figure out what's wrong, they label the patient as having fibromyalgia. Before being diagnosed with fibromyalgia, other diagnises should be ruled out. Sometimes patients might have CPRS (Complex Regional Pain Syndrome) which presents with similar symptoms and can sometimes be treated. (Note: there are two types of CPRS. . . I can't remember the differences off the top of my head). Abdominal or Pelvic pain may be due adhesions or fibroids. If you have fibromyalgia, it's important to be seen by a specialist who has a history of seeing patients with fibromyalgia (i.e. usually a pain doctor). Other physicians who don't deal regularly with these type of patients can be overwhelmed by these type of patients. Fibromyalgia patients may need high level of narcotics because they've built up a tolerance to them over time, and a regular doctor (i.e. internist or family practicioner) might not feel comfortable prescribing that much medication. Also there are medications other than narcotics that a pain specialist can prescribe. In addition, fibromyalgia can become a vicious cycle: you need more meds, the doctor doesn't prescribe quite enough, you come back needing more meds, he increases the amount a little, you come back again frustrated that it's not enough, doctor starts viewing you as a whiner/drug seeker/problem patient, you become more frustrated and angry, etc, etc. (that's one of the reasons to see a specialist. . . they're more comfortable prescribing high doses of meds and using different treatment regimens).Last point: a holistic approach is probably the best for fibromyalgia, i.e. drugs aren't the only thing. Acupuncture, biofeedback, and other "non-traditional" treatment modalities can often be of help.
