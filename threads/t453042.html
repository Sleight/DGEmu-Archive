Lately a lot of media attention has seemed to be on plus sized models, especially recently because of a photoshoot in V magazine, http://www.stylelist.com/2010/01/04/plus-s...-fashion-shoot/ . There has been a lot of support, as well as backlash from people who say that it's not right for the media to promote "being fat" as a good thing because it is unhealthy, as well as people who have nothing better to say than that skinny women look better. I personally believe that the backlash is because of a skewed view of what such shoots are about. I highly doubt it is to promote obesity, but rather to promote being comfortable in your own skin. I happen to think the models in the shoot are gorgeous by the way. I do not think that being overweight or obese is a good thing, but there is a difference between that and having curves. It seems like any time a model bigger than a size 1 is featured in a fashion magazine it turns into a skinny vs fat war. It really should not be that way. Every body is different. Being skinny does not mean that you are healthy either. Every body is different. Two women could eat and exercise the same but be different weights, since individual bodies tend to want to settle at different weights. That's why there are people who eat nothing but junk and don't gain a pound, and others live a healthy lifestyle and are not rail thin, and why those "extra 5 lbs" are the hardest to lose for most people who try to lose weight. What I'm trying to say is that health should come before size. If the fashion industry is really concerned about improving their body image standards than they should hire some medium sized girls too! And represent the full spectrum rather than just two extremes. They are taking a step in the right direction though.
