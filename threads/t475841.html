KKirie MotobaMar 29 2012, 05:30 AM
Ok, before anyone gets into overclocking...Intel Core i5 and i7 processors all support TurboBoost (AMD processors also support something similar).It's a technology that will auto increment the clock of your cores and adjust the voltage automatically and disable cores that are not used depending on what is running(That's why when you shop for a computer, it says Core i7 2.8GHz Turboboost to 3.4GHz)Second, overclocking is not a simple thing of flipping a number in the bios like what BR did, that will result into failed booting, BSOD at random, etc.Overclocking is an art of adjusting the bus speeds, multiplier, voltages, memory timings accordingly while taking into consideration the temperature max that your computer is running at, and a lot of testing.Successful overclock involves having:- Better cooling units, whether monstrous air coolers or liquid cooling, as the processor will heat up- High performance RAM that is able to maintain the bus/voltage changes- A good power supply giving up enough amp on rail so that your computer doesn't randomly failIs it really useful and necessary? Not really. Increasing the clock just to gain like 3 frames per second while shortening your computer lifespan is not that wise.Also, badly programmed emulator is just simply badly programmed. If it has bugs or unoptimized coding, no matter what beast of a computer you have, you can't run it properly since it's the application at fault.Other note: Computers that tend to run very sluggish can be caused by overheating: When the processor reaches a certain temperature, the system will try to lower the clock speed and voltage in an attempt to cool down the processor. If it doesn't succeed, your computer just shuts off.
