I don't think it is a matter of quantity but rather an assessment of quality. Schools, (in the states I've lived in), base their version of homework off of the term "busy work". Take math for example, you receive the math lesson, and then what is your assignment? To do every single problem on the page. This is based off the theory that repetitiveness is what teaches. Basically, if something is repeated enough it will get drilled into you to do it automatically. While this is true for things that you use on a constant basis (due to the continual repetition of said subject after the fact anyways,) this unfortunately does not work for things that you cease using upon you leaving the school system, as this theory is reliant on the sole assumption that the human brain is capable of memorizing everything it gets taught. Two things can be considered when thinking along these lines:1. We can try to find a different approach to teaching the things we deem important in schools. Maybe use a hands on method or showing how the knowledge learned in schools can actually apply to a real life setting.2. If we can't apply the knowledge learned in real life setting, than for the most part there really is no point to teach it. In this case, we should change the curriculum to reflect that which we determine most important in life and life's settings.
