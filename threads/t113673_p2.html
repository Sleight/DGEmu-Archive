We die because it's functional, from evolution's point of view. We live not only as humans on the Earth, but as a symbiose with many many other forms of life. Without their deaths, we couldn't live. Of course it's a much more complicated theory, and there are other factors such as the environment. The only purpose of life is to stay alive. The only way to do that is to evolve as a species and to adapt as well as possible to environmental changes. When a new "diverse" generation has been formed, the older generation is no longer needed for the group to stay alive, and they even have lesser chances of living through an environmental change, in that case they would just parasite on the food and make it much harder for the new diverse generation to live through. That's why I think dying is functional to some extent. Of course whilst making this diversity, a very weak case can be formed, which would tragically die earlier in life than we would have wanted him/her to, that's the price life pays to attain the bigger goal: survive
