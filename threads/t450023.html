Some of you may be familiar with the concept of endless return, and I think Nietzsche gives an interesting view on this subject.For those of you not familiar with his work, I'll give a short summary. In Die fröhliche Wissenschaft(The Gay Science) and Also sprach Zurahastra(Thus spoke Zurahastra), Nietzsche argues that there is a finite amount of matter, but an infiinte amount of time and space. Since we do not know when time started, he says that there is only one logical conclusion: seeing there is only a finite amount of matter, there is also a finite amount of patterns to be formed by said matter. Therefore, everything that can possibly happen has already happened previously in time and it will be happen again later in time. We humans are trapped in an endless cycle. No matter what you, as a person, try or do, it is ultimately futile because it has already happened and it will repeat itself countless times. After all, it is impossible to stop this cycle of matter forming and reforming.I will ignore the scientific accuracy of these statements of the lack thereof (after all, space is finite and we know when time started... this was unknown information in Nietzsche's time) since I am convinced this isn't supposed to be interpreted in a literal sense, but in a metaphorical one. Nietzsche himself says it better than I can:
Die fröhliche Wissenschaft wrote on §341:
What, if some day or night a demon were to steal after you into your loneliest loneliness and say to you: 'This life as you now live it and have lived it, you will have to live once more and innumerable times more' ... Would you not throw yourself down and gnash your teeth and curse the demon who spoke thus? Or have you once experienced a tremendous moment when you would have answered him: 'You are a god and never have I heard anything more divine.'
What he wants to say is: how far does your love for life go? Knowing that you will have no impact on any turn of events, will you take all of the events that happen in your life with happiness? It is easy to be happy with the happy moments like marrying, becoming a father/mother and so on... but this is hypocrisy according to him. Why would you love only the "good" moments in life when all moments are unchangeable anyway? He calls this amor fati, the love of fate. The debate I am trying to start here is one question: how far does the love for life go? Will you take the subjectively good moments as the good moments in life, or will you live a happy life all the time because you accept fate as is is? According to Nietzsche you can only be truly happy if you love fate, and in extension all of life. What about the people of DGEmu? Carpe diem or carpe diem et nocem?
