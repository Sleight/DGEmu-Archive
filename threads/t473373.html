There's no stoping piracy.Granted, it's probably not as big a deal as some developers make it out to be. It's simple for us, but for the average person pirating anything without an emulator is pretty confusing. The jargon is dense and the results aren't always the easiest thing to work with. There's supplementary things you have to do (such as updating cIOS for Wii or upgrading the random ass CFW for PSP) plus you generally have to shy away from the internet capabilities, which are pretty important these days for non-Nintendo stuff. Then there's external HDDs, DVD drives that won't work with pirated games, the size of the games, availability and reliability of places people get them (I don't meet many people that can use torrents believe it or not). People see these things are hassles compared to just coughing up some dough and sticking the game in.For me, I pirate because of quality. I don't want to pay full price for a game when there are similar games that are better. Why would I want to pay for any platformer other than Mario? Not to mention the rate at which games come out. There's no way to give them all your time and it's easier to just buy the great ones and throw the rest on some hard drive to play when you're bored.Credit to Steam though. It's made me buy games I never would have bought.
