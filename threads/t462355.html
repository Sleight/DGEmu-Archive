Ever wish you could be a merchant in an RPG?Now you can.*Buy! Sell! Exploit the market!*Tons of items to do the above with*Customise your shop and draw in the suckers customers*Legend of Zelda styled dungeoning action*8 adventurers to work like horses*6 progressively longer dungeons to raid and pillage*Explore the town of Pensee!*Endless Mode and Survival Mode awaits you after the main gameRecette, a girl whose father had abandoned wandered off in the hopes of being an adventure, was one day approach by the fairy named Tear. Turns out the father had acculumated quite the debt - and now that the debt had been passed onto Recette, she could either pay it off or sleep on the streets. Tear offered her one more option, however: she could open an item shop and earn the money she needed over the course of five weeks.And that's where their story begin.The main goal of the game is to earn enough pix (the game's currency) to pay off the amount due for each week's debt installment. To do that, Recette must make good use of her shop - selling everything from books to food to equipments, and later on buying stuff off customers look to sell. Haggling plays a vital part in these transactions - too low a price and you might cut the installment's deadline too finely, too high a price and you'll have an angry customer storming out of the shop. By striking the right balance, you'll have customers that come back regularly... and with more px in their pockets to spend. You can also customise the shop to appeal to certain customer types, and gain merchant levels to help you get better at... merchanting?"Hold up!" you say. "But where does Recette get stuff to sell?" Besides the chances to buy off from customers, a merchant's guild and market is also available for getting new stock. However, the most profitable and lucrative source are, of course, dungeons.Dungeoning comprises the other half of Recetttear, but neither Recette nor Tear get their hands dirty - instead, the Adventurer's Guild allows Recette to hire an adventurer (duh) to exploit explore the depths of different dungeons, scouring for treasure chests along the way. A variety of monsters and traps serves to obstruct the path to the loot, as well as a selection of bosses. It's all very remiscent of Legend of Zelda's gameplay, and although it starts to get repetitive after a while (palette swap, anyone?) it still lasts long enough to be interesting. You start off with only a single adventerer at first, but up to 8 different characters can be unlocked to select from.It's not always work, either. Besides plot-related cutscenes, Recette can also go out into the town of Pensee to meet the townsfolks. The styles of these scenes are very much standard visual novel affair, and the artwork is quite well done. What really stands out, however, is the writing - Carpe Fulgur did a good job with the localisation, and players will find the jokes and references very appealing indeed. After completing the game once, Endless Mode and Survival Mode are unlocked - Endless Mode allows the player to continue on with their current game and also provides the chance to wrap up the story, while Survival Mode pits you against infinitely amounts of incrementing weekly installments to pay off. And even if you get a game over in your game, don't worry - you get to start another loop on the same save file, which places you back at Day 1 with most of your stuff intact.All in all, not bad for 15 bucks and three guys for the localisation team. Certainly, it might get repetitive after a while, and the anime-heavy style might be a turnoff for some; but if you are a fan of old-school gameplay, or would like to know a little more about the daily life and woes of an RPG merchant, give it a shot.Capitalism, Ho!Available on Steam, Impulse, and GamersGate. Yeah yeah, I know you guys probably won't actually buy it, but consider anyway if you like it - Carpe Fulgur could use the funding, being a newcomer to the industry and all, and now that they worked with EasyGamesStation they might start on some of their other stuff. Chantelise, damnit!
