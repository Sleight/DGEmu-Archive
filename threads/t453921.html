Franz Boas completely changed the way that cultures and societies are perceived and understood. Case in point, while he never identified it as such, he is widely attributed with the creation of Cultural Relativism. The concept of cultural relativism was quite closely related to his other key concept, namely Historical Particularism although like Cultural Relativism he did not identify it as such. Indeed Historical Particularism argued that each culture comes as a result of its own unique history. Such a position was further expanded when applied to cultural traits, which were expressed as gaining their meaning and value relative to the culture they reside in. In essence, it represents a rejection of attempts to explain cultures and more specifically cultural traits in a universalistic sense (A good example would be its rejection of 19th century evolutionism). Such a stance is not without its critics however. There are those who criticize Cultural Relativism as being illogical and self contradicting. It has also been criticized as supporting and/or providing justification for such things as the treatment of women in other cultures.So, what are the strengths and/or weaknesses of Cultural Relativism?Please note: All links in this post lead to text. HHuang Fei HongFeb 07 2010, 12:11 PM
For those of you who are lost, here's little background on what we're actually talking about here: Sociocultural evolution.I believe that there's another angle to approaching anthropological studies in cultural development that people haven't discussed here. Personally, just stating that different cultures develop differently isn't a huge revelation in itself, but it's understanding what gives rise to differences that is the real challenge.As per my usual stance, cultures and civilizations are akin to organisms and can be considered species unto themselves. Mechanisms that would apply to speciation in biology would apply to divergence in cultures.In addition there should be two types of observable development that a civilization undergoes.One results mostly from environmental factors and a people's adaptive efforts to make use of resources. This involved differences between what's available to a country like China (marshes, rice, soy, bamboo, silkworms, silt filled rivers, Pacific shores to the east, water buffalo) and a country like Ireland (potatoes, grassy plains, North Atlantic shores surrounding the island, and I wish I had a better list of what else).The other class of observable development results from interaction between cultures after the initial periods of isolation from its contemporaries. This includes everything from all out conflict to simple communication.Now, whereas neither of these are in opposition to reddeath's views, they are clear directional tangents.Within these two forms of development, however, there are definitive supportive arguments for ideas of generalized progression and cultural convergence; both of which, supporters Cultural Relativism and Historical Particularism would not be happy to see.For example, we see independent, but corresponding developments of movable type, metallurgy (which relate to very obvious phases of human development ), animal husbandry, generalized weapon design, mounted combat, games of war, games of chance, sports (specifically those from many cultures that, while individual, all highly resemble international football [or as Americans would say, soccer]), etc. around the world. This points to clear generalizations of progression.With interaction between cultures, we see cross-contamination patterns that are akin to the idea of "keeping up with the Jones". Modern cultural confluence targets include the United States of America (Coca Cola, McDonald's, movies/Hollywood, Michael Jackson, Internet) and Japan (Anime, gothic lolitas, J-rock, etc. you're online, just look around). With the widened availability of global travel and communication, particularism is slowly losing relevancy. Even with early contamination we see some immediate adoptions of "superior" elements from one culture to another. With Native Americans we see the immediate assimilation of horses and the infamous combination of "fire water" and "boomsticks".So in conclusion, there ARE phases to humanity and there are generalized progressions to an archetypal style of superiority. Now of course, this doesn't apply to every single aspect, but it does seem to apply more to the technologically oriented advances, games/sports, and vices.
