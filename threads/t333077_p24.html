To say that "Science is just theories" is probably the most common fallacy perturbing reason today. It is not just theories, and to try to state such a thing in such a matter-of-fact manner is insulting to science as a branch of reason and research. The 'theories' of science are the same as those of commonplace ideas. I may come to some sort of a conclusion as to how apples decompose like the following: gnomes nibble at them until they disappear. That's a theory, but it is not a scientific one.What sets a scientific theory apart from such commonplace theories, like the ones of gnomes or God-fictions, is that it has a very strict set of criteria it must first meet before it can even be considered to be considered as a scientific theory. Firstly, the hypothesis must be specific. It cannot be akin to the following: the sky is blue because light makes it that way. It has to be more along the lines of this: the sky is blue, in the day time, because the sun's light rays are disrupted by particles in the atmosphere, and only the blue rays in the spectrum of light carry through to our eyes. Secondly, a vast amount of data has to be both gathered and examined. This data comes from experiments that are designed not prove the hypothesis correct, but to prove it incorrect. Only after scientists have exhausted all known manners of testing the theory in ways that it might fail, can it prove itself inherently correct. Thirdly, this new theory has to be able to predict the outcome of an empirical situation. It has to be able to use its calculations in ways that predict the outcomes of situations before they actually occur, and if these outcomes all prove correct, then this theory can be deemed correct and applied to real world scenarios.Again, a commonplace theory does meet all this expectation, and therefore cannot be held to the same level as scientific theories.
