Processor is fine.

HS: Totally overkill, you can use stock cooler if no overclock, or go with Cooler Master Hyper 212 EVO if that's the one you were talking about.

RAM: Look into G.Skill Ripjaws. They're often cheaper than Kingston, and perform as well / better.

GPU: Quite the expensive card, but will work wonders.

Case: Fractal Design is really nice. I've built 3 machines with them, never disappointed. Core series are extremely nice, best bang for buck. I'm using a Define case for server, stylish match in my living room (not good for heavy duty though).

Mobo: Good choice. I personal would go with an equivalent Asus Z97 chipset mobo, but that's gonna cost you more for sure. The Gigabyte works wonders though.

PSU: Personally would ditch Antec and go with a Corsair TX or HX series (I think they're called builder nowadays). And if you've got cash to flock, Seasonic, it's like the mother golden egg of power supplies, AAA quality.

Monitor: BenQ is decent. Asus screens are decent also (those are the ones I use). In Canada, Samsung has been selling dirt cheap screen, IPS 24" for like 120$ CAD instead of 240$. Grab those for sure if they're ridding their stock in Australia.

SSD: Intel are on the expensive side, but they're been more than stable. Better priced and better performing, Samsung Pro drives are the way to go.

HDD: Drop the Seagate, go WD Red for storage (avoid green, it will anger you), or regular use, WD Blue. WD Black if you got cash to waste.

 

Core i7, not needed, unless you're really into massive stuff. I haven't even needed it either on my end.

Good speakers: IF anyone is still selling Logitech X-230 (2.1 speakers) or X-530 (5.1 speakers), get those, most powerful for a good price. All newer models don't sound as great, but they still do the job perfectly fine though.

If you get accustomed to 2 screens, it's like a drug, you'd never want to go back to 1 screen.

The amount of work / procrastination that can be done simultaneously is awesome.

 

Also:

 

WD Green: Eco friendly drives, slow speeds, constantly shutting off, parking head, and turning on when you need it. Basically, you always constantly wait like 5 seconds for it to spin up on first access after a bit.

WD Red: Meant for NAS usage, perfect drives for RAID and mass data storage without the slowness of the Green. It has also been proven that they can be even more energy efficient.

WD Red Pro: Same as above, but sturdier, even more features for RAID, much more expensive.

WD Purple: Meant for surveillance camera system (and in reality, any system that is constantly writing 24/7 to the drive). These are meant for extremely high write abuse devices.

WD Blue: The traditional hard drive, balance of speed, performance, etc. This is meant to have Windows installed on these (Unlike Green and Red where you might suffer some performance issues for Windows).

WD Black: For the enthusiast that want large storage without compromise on performance, high speeds, etc. These are good for installing Windows as well as games and stuff to have them load quicker. Problem is the price on these are harsh.
