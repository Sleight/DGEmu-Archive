XXiamDec 12 2006, 06:47 AM
Because I am seriously bored, I will go through all of them and my arguments regarding them. I apologize in advance for my rants.The Legend of ZeldaAlthough incredibly fun and personally rewarding once I finally beat it (I had never beaten a retro NES game, the only NES game I remember beating was Kirby's Adventure), its graphics were very primitive. however, this was given the time it was created, and the overall gameplay is incredibly fun. It set a new standard for games, and is an undeniable classic. No true Zelda fan could ever badmouth this one.Zelda II: The Adventure of LinkWhile innovative for the series, it is regretably the black sheep of the family. I have never beaten it, and honestly couldn't even get past the third temple. It's also very confusing, and even though you can talk to people, you have to use the B button. You might as well be stabbing them, huh? At least you can jump without a Roc's Feather.A Link to the PastI will not deny it, I love this game. It too is a classic, and while returning to its roots of the original Zelda game, it brought a deeper story, and new items which would change the entire series. Aside from the bottle which allowed us to keep fairies nearby in case we were accidentally killed, so we may be resurrected automatically (a beautiful strategy), this game brought us the ultimate weapon of the Hero: The Master Sword. Nowadays, we cannot possibly imagine Link without it.Link's AwakeningI love this game. I was introduced to the series through this one, and although it has very little to do with Ganon OR Zelda (besides a shadow of Agahnim and Link mistaking Marin for Zelda), it is still a very solid game with a fun (if not bizarre) story. it also introduces the long-winded Kaepora Gaebora, if I'm not mistaken.Ocarina of TimeMany people believe this game to be the DEFINITIVE game of the series. I feel it too constricting, but that's just me. The fairy Navi, while annoying, helps the transition to 3D by allowing an innovative targeting system, and also gives advice on what to do next (trust me, sometimes I did have no idea what to do). Also, we are allowed to see Ganon's background, and we learn that he is not some disturbing monster, but simply a cruel and ambitious Gerudo (whose given name is actually Ganondorf). Also, the Ocarina of Time itself is a pleasing twist to a previously established item in the series. While the Ocarina first appeared for allowing you transportation, it allows so much more in this game. You'll want to keep it equipped at all times.Majora's MaskThough bizarre and difficult to save and just pick it up for a small bit of play before going somewhere, Majora's Mask is probably one of the most interesting of the series. It has nothing to do with Ganon, and Zelda is only seen in a flashback, but it is incredibly fun, and the time AND mask factors of the game make it incredibly fun. I have an interest in masks and possible magical properties, so I find this one very much worth owning.Oracle of Ages & Oracle of SeasonsThough I haven't beaten Seasons yet, I enjoy these games a lot. The linking is a bit of a pain, and it's hard to keep up with Bipin and Blossom's son, but otherwise these games are pretty fun. As long as you don't pick Moosh, because he's horribly difficult to get over those large stretches of holes. But he is fun to slam down on people. But the dynamics of the two games are fun, changing seasons to get to new places in OoS, or travelling back and forth through time to meet different people in OoA. And you even get to see cameos by various characters, such as Tingle (ugh) and the Zoras of OoT and MM (ocean-living Zoras, who are much different from their freshwater counterparts).Four SwordsNow, technically this is not a full game. It was an attatchmen to the GBA re-release of A Link to the Past. However, if you get friends to play with, this is actually very fun - though short. It forces you to manage teamwork (while also being a little competetive) and brings forward a compelling new character. Even if he is a little cliché. Come on, kidnapping pretty girls? What gives?The Wind WakerYou can complain about the cel-shading all you want, but it is a BEAUTIFUL game. And you can complain about the sailing all you want, but it is very realistic in that respect. And you can complain about the storyline, but um... I'll hafta punch you in the face. Let's face it, I love this game, and the gameplay had never been better. Seriously, play Ocarina of Time, then play The Wind Waker. The controls are so much smoother, and the targeting and camera movement is so much easier to control. And if you ever reached the final battle, you will agree that it is amazingly beautiful. What's more, any scene with Ganondorf has shown how much he has grown. And I don't mean wider. No longer is he a single-minded villain, a two-dimensional monster. He has progressed so much, and he spouts philosophy and determination. He gives his reasons for wanting Hyrule, and by the end, you almost feel sympathetic for him. This is a truly beautiful game, in all forms of the word.Four Swords AdventuresNow, I'll admit it wasn't as good as The Wind Waker, but it does a LOT for the Zelda series. It doesn't have as much depth or as much of a "wandering" feeling as the other games, but it does have a helluva story. Not only do you have to deal with Vaati, but a shadow version of yourself, which can eventually split into four just like you did... and by the end, you realize there is a horrible mastermind to the entire thing. If you look at the story, as well as the map of Hyrule, youll see many similarities between this and A Link to the Past. Presumably, it is a prequel leading up to it.The Minish CapThis game. Is. Amazing. It introduces Vaati as a non-ugly one-eyed beast, searching for a "Light Force" to give him great power. It also introduces a new twist to the story, suggesting that the Master Sword and the Triforce itself were given to humans by tiny thumb-sized people known as the Picori - who call themselves the Minish. Along with the introduction of the Minish, you are given a new element to the story by having the ability to shrink to their size, with the help of a character I love, Ezlo - basically, your hat, and the title character of this edition of the Zelda series. His advice is less annoying as Navi, and he has more character than her. But not as much as Tatl, from Majora's Mask. She was a real fireball, eh?Twilight Princess...Sorry, I haven't played this one. No opinion can be given yet.Again, I apologize for the ranting.
