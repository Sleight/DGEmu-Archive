RRaikizenJul 12 2009, 08:47 PM
I do not believe there is such a thing as "True Love" and I hardly believe in "Love" myself. There are a few key reasons to why I don't believe in love:1) Almost everything a human does can be related to Instincts. People are always commenting how love makes you do "crazy" things, when in all actuality this can easily be explained on a subconcious level. Ever seen a peacock flash it's tale feathers in the zoo? Then surely you've noticed that sometimes one of them will dance, maybe warble, or even fly around to get the attention of the female over the others. These things aren't "crazy" as many people exclaim. They are simple a way of assuring your victory over someone else.Example #1: A man proposes to a woman. The woman accepts. Another man comes along and proposes in either the same, or a less flashy fashion. Almost guaranteed the woman will say no. Now, a man comes along spelling out his love and devotion through sky writing and asking her to marry him on nation television. His show of "Love" is so far above the other mans that he must surely love her more than him, right?Example #2: Say the woman chooses the less qualified man who proposed in a similar fashion to the first one. Surely it must be because the two of them are meant to be together, right? "True Love". Well, on a subconcious level that the woman may not be aware of, she chose him because for breeding purposes she found him more compatible.2) "Love" is one of those words that is extremely hard to define. Brother/Sisterly love, Parental Love, Spousal Love, the love between a man and his dog, etc. Are all of these things love? Are none of them? Who's to say that they are just not affection of such a degree that is frightens some people so they call it "love"? "Love" is such a hard thing to define that I can't see anyone being able to say it and truly mean it.3) It's so easy to say "I love you." Everyone uses it all the time for a vast array of things. Does it really mean all of those things? Doesn't it defeat the purpose of "Love" really, when people just casually banter it about? Wouldn't it in all reality be harder to say, "I would give my up my very life to make sure you are safe from harm," and truly mean it then to just say "I love you."?These are just my opinions on the matter. I've been with plenty of women that I can honestly say I was very, and truly, affectionate about. I cannot say though that I have ever experienced what everyone describes "Love" to be. To me: You just get comfortable with a person. You enjoy having them around enough to keep them around with you for a while. "Love" has been known to fade in time. All that really means is that having that person around is no longer interesting.
