someone wrote:
from what i have heard blue-ray is currently beating hd-dvd in sales
In terms of market share, they're about the same, last time I saw the financial reports. In terms of sales *velocity*, Bluray has a 3:1 advantage over HD-DVD.
someone wrote:
i have also heard blu-ray has given manufacturers many problems
You're confused. Bluray discs themselves cost more to make because of the thinness of the protective layer, which means it requires more precision and potentially some exotic materials (which isn't a problem per se -- it's the price of admission). HD-DVD has the same thickness as DVD, so it requires the same precision as regular DVDs. That said, a difference in cost of disc production is totally trivial. Even if it costs 10x as much to make a Bluray disc than an HD-DVD, and that difference is passed on to the consumer, the price difference between the movies would still be less than one dollar. The cost of what you're paying for is the content on the disc, not the media itself.As for the players, the problem wasn't a manufacturing issue -- it was a parts supply issue. Specifically, the supply of blue-violet laser diodes (which, btw is the same diode for both Bluray and HD-DVD). There were only a few manufacturers of these diodes, and Sony was monopolizing their production yields for the early production runs of PS3. This also inflated the street price of the diodes massively. As of mid-April, though, Sony and Nichia started running their foundries at near full capacity and Sony additionally put 75% of their yields on the market, so the bulk street price of the laser diodes went down from $78.25 a piece to $8.70 a piece.That also means that both types of players can be produced in higher volume now.
someone wrote:
also i have read there are people already working on a format that uses ultra violet which goes far beyond blu-ray
There are about 20-odd new formats currently being worked on in some labs of some company somewhere in the world right now. When all is said and done, though, it really has no impact for Bluray or HD-DVD because the time from research to viability to market is a minimum of 10 years. One of the holy grail technologies out there is the holographic media which uses reference wave interference fields to store multiple bits on the same point. That allows them to get higher than Bluray capacity on business card sized media. That has been in research for over 12 years, and it still isn't market viable because the media stability is so poor.
