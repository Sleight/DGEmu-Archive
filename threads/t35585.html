DDeviantFeb 22 2005, 05:41 AM
If you are going to cut out your own pics for a project, never use the magic wand. Use the eraser only until your control improves and then upgrade to the polygon losso (I find it the best).When doing the actual cutting, zoom in as close as you can so that you can see the individual pixels of the pic's outline and try to cut off the outline too. Many times the bg will blend with the pixels of the outline, so leaving the outline can keep some of the color of the bg too. Removing it will give a nice clean render.Also, with the polygon lasso tool, select small amounts at a time. It'll avoid choppy lines.

The most precise way i've found to cut out an image is the pen tool. Select the pen tool and change it to "Paths" (you'll see the button once you select the pen tool). Then trace around your render by clicking points on all around it. Then right click and select "Make Selection".This method is a little more time consuming than using the polygonal lasso, but the results are much better. And if you mess up, you dont have to start all over. It is also great for getting rid of pesky small areas that you dont want.

Layer masks are the most customizable way of cutting out or blending different pics. You can draw on them and use filters like a normal layer, but it's in black and white. White = shown, black = hidden. You can switch foreground and background colors by pressing "X". "[" and "]" decrease and increase the brush size. When cutting out an area using the brush on a mask, sometimes it's better to switch colors, put a big blob of the background, then switch back and refine with a smaller brush. If you put a layer underneath of contrasting color you can see exactly where the edges are.
1
