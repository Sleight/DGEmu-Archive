EEndymion_MallornDec 17 2010, 07:50 AM
There are actually several reasons, honestly. At least for the way I see it.1. "Money, Dear Boy" - Mario is, if not the single best-selling game franchise in the world, certainly close to the top. Universal character recognition - even Dennis Hopper and Bob Hoskins (otherwise skilled actors) attached themselves to this series in a movie they both hate because they needed the money! Mario = $$$2. Those four games set out a lot of what we considered to be "standard 2D platforming" for many years - only to have 3D try and take over. Heart containers, world-affecting actions, the whole nine; those were in SMB2. Each one provided a gameplay base - and if it wasn't possible to put them into a system, then scrap the whole thing - because it couldn't handle simple, basic gameplay.3. Nostalgia - closely tied to #1, but this time on a creative end. Super Mario 64 had been an example of what 3D platforming was to become. Luigi's Mansion, Super Mario Sunshine, etc. are all great games. But they're the new ones, there's none of the home comforts to them. You can't just look at that and say, "Yeah, I remember when we thought that might not sell so well." It's a reminder to the designers of yore that they hit something that worked, and to today's designers to remind them: there is a history here. Don't fear it.4. Age - Let's start with Super Mario Bros.; rather than SMB2. Released for NES in 1985(!), fully 25 years ago now. Rereleased in 1993 on the SNES (Super Mario All-Stars), and 1999 on Gameboy Color (Super Mario Bros. Deluxe - which includes the original J version of SMB2 under the title "For Super Players".) Note that while the 1993 version was on a back-compatible system, the move to handheld put it in to the reach of many gamers who might have otherwise not had direct contact with what might just be the most influential (and certainly one of the most iconic) platformers of all time. Classic NES in 2004 rounds it out - and that sucked compared to the GBC release of Deluxe.SMB2 / Super Mario USA: Released 1986 for NES, rereleased 1993 for SNES. Then released on GBA for a new generation in 2001. Realize for a moment what that means. In 2001, there were kids exposed to the original Mario for whom 64 had been released before they were born.SMB3: 1988 NES/1993 SNES/2003 GBA. See SMB2World: 1990 SNES/2004 GBASMW2 (Yoshi's Island): Featuring a different style of play altogether, controlling the Yoshis, protecting Baby Mario and a vastly different art style. Released in 1995 for console, 2002 for GBA, sequel in 2006 for DS.Yes. Almost two entire generations got to see these games, and play them. If I had been a decade older, they'd have been the games I saw when I was in my teens. As it was, I grew up with them - and with Mario is Missing!. I could be confident as a parent (and my parents could be confident as grandparents, for the record), that these games are safe for children and for families. There is nothing objectionable to Mario. He is, overall, safe. You do have to let the kids watch TMNT so they get over the turtle-stomping urges... but hey, that's a small price to pay!So that's why.
