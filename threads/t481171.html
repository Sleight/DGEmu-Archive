I mentioned this earlier, it's mostly the perfect motion blur in movies. In video games, a single frame is a static image, and when it have motion blur it's simulated by a shitty algorithm simple enough to be applied 30(or what ever frame rate the game runs at) time in a single second.

With movies motion blur isn't simulated, it happen naturally from the exposure of films while shooting, a single frame is never a static image, it's a representation of the motion that happened during the 0.041 second in which the frame was taken. So a 24fps movie looks smoother than a 24fps game because a single frame from the movie have way more information about the motion happening than a single frame from the game.
