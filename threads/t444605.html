VVerdantAug 07 2009, 02:29 AM
"Are there actually arguments pro-Latin?" Speaking as a Classics masters candidate currently teaching Latin at a university, I'll say there are uses for Latin and Greek. Not only are the points already made here --- Latin as the basis for many of our words, Latin as the origin of some of the most commonly spoken world languages --- extremely important, but there are yet more.People think in language. It's not something most people ponder until they try to start thinking in another one. An English-speaker learning French has to restructure how his mind organizes the parts of speech. The greater the difference between languages, the greater the gap to be bridged. Latin and Greek, being far more alien than French, force the learner to revolutionize the mind and develop new ways of thinking. People seem to agree that there's value in "Thinking outside the box;" well, learning a very different language provides an entirely new vehicle of thought, and hence entirely new ways of approaching problems.Then there's the whole fact that some of the greatest works of literature the world has ever seen and continually attempts to recreate can't fully be appreciated outside their own languages...
