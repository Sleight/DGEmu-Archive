The Intel HD 3000 is more than sufficient for basically anything besides gaming and serious rendering where you'd want something like CUDA.There's a huge market for external GPUs that's just waiting to be tapped into. Any gamer can tell you that the eGPU setup Sony made could have been ideal with a high-end card, because laptops generally have to be plugged in to game anyway, otherwise they tend to throttle back the card. And of course, buying a new laptop every 3 years because the graphics card is outdated when the CPU is still good enough is infuriating.There's already the ViDock, which can also be made on a DIY level for cheaper, but it requires your computer to have the uncommon ExpressCard slot, which also only transfers data at 2.5Gb/s. The Light Peak port in the Vaio Z transfers data at 10Gb/s, which is still nowhere near PCIe x16 2.0's 64Gb/s, but it gives you more leeway for higher-end cards.So what if they'd need to make the enclosure a little bigger, add in a fan and a heatsink, etc?I really think that if Sony were to ensure that Light Peak would be present in Vaio Z laptops for at least 2 more iterations, and if they were to make the dock hold a desktop graphics card, it would satisfy the need for a lightweight computer with a powerful CPU that also gains a very powerful GPU when you're near outlets. No DIY ViDock can currently match the bandwidth of the Light Peak port in the Vaio Z.
