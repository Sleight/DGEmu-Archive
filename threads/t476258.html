Anyways, the jokes on him.

By commercial standards, 16GB is 16 x 1000MB to dumb it down for the consumer.
By technology standard, 1GB is not 1000MB, but 1024MB
So in reality, you would be getting really around 15.625GB of storage space on your USB stick.

Assuming we put a file of 1024MB (1GB) on the USB stick, you would end up with 14.625GB of space, not 15GB.

PLUS, in Windows Explorer, the drive will still show up as 15.625GB for that FAT32 partition, with 1GB used.

So you sir, suck at faking a 15GB USB stick.

The smart person would instead use the disk drive management, delete the whote partition from the drive, and then create a new FAT32 partition of exactly 15360MB, and leave the balance not allocated.
That will simulate your 15GB stick, and will show up as such in "My Computer"
