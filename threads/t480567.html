KKirie MotobaFeb 18 2014, 04:48 PM

Well, after reading up on your mobo:

You have 8 SATA2 ports on your Intel controller.

You have 2 SATA3 ports on your Marvell controller. The Marvel controller support Hotplug, meaning by default, it is hot-swappable (and the reason why they appear with the safely remove option).

 

The hotplug is not a capability of a hard disk, but of the controller.

If you were smart, you'd have plugged those disks into the SATA3 ports to take advantage of the higher speeds and set them all to AHCI in the bios. (Only in AHCI that hotplug is available also)

 

To disable the Hotplug:

Windows key + R

Type "devmgmt.msc" without the quotes in the Run box and hit ok.

Expand "Storage Controllers" and find your Marvell 6G controller.

Right-click > Properties

Move to the Policies tab.

Uncheck "Enable Safely Remove Disk".

Apply, OK, you may need to restart your computer for changes to apply.

 

One thing you can do to help optimize your drives also:

In Device Manager, expand "Disk drives".

For each hard disk listed, right-click > Properties.

Move to the Policies tab.

Make sure both "Enable write caching on the device" and "Turn off Windows write-cache buffer flushing on the device" are both checked.

Give a better performance to your hard disk, though the second option will increase your chances of data loss if you lose power.

KKirie MotobaFeb 19 2014, 03:40 PM

This is your mobo, right? (On Newegg)

 

Your motherboard SATA connectors are color coded blue and white in pairs on the side of the motherboard.

Read near the connectors (In that image at the top, the text seems to be on the left side of the white connectors), it should say something like:

 

SATA6g_0-1

SATA_0-1

SATA_2-3

SATA_4-5

SATA_6-7

 

Is your disk connected on the 6G port? It should normally.

 

Secondly, IDE Controller in your device manager??

When you enter your bios settings, you should have 2 configurables:

- The "high-speed" SATA ports (or whatever they call it)

- The onboard SATA ports

Both of them should be set to AHCI, not IDE (or Legacy as some bios calls it). Careful with this though as swapping this can make your operating system not boot anymore...

 

If you don't have the policies tab, that's because the device item doesn't have any configurations available for it.
