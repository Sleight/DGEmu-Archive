LLedah WolfJun 07 2007, 08:12 PM
It's a well-rounded franchise. There are lots of games in the series, and the Mystery Dungeon and Ranger games were inventive if nothing else. Still, Diamond feels remarkably like Silver and Gold. It's a tried-and-true system that makes lots of money, but it's time to move on. The series is great, and the developers stick to what they know works. The only problem with that is that there isn't anything new. So many game series have had a working system, and blew it because they didn't want that system to get old. The Mana series, the Tomb Raider series, the Tenchu series... but there's that fine line where the developers have to recognize when to stop. Diamond and Pearl sold amazingly well, and so, probably, will the next game in the series, but, yes, the games are getting old. Saphire and Ruby fell apart a little because they took so much away from them after Gold and Silver. Gold and Silver had the day/night system, the huge after-game mountain with Red at the top, and 16 badges, as opposed to the traditional 8. It had the capability to travel between regions, also something that we haven't seen since. Gold and Silver were quite possibly the best in the series. The franchise was still new at that point, but gave the players of R/B/G a wave of nostalgia by taking them back to Kanto. They were huge, sprawling games, great to play. Then the developers took out so much. The travel, the challenge, the day/night system... I think that the games that were the least recognized and the most mediocre were FireRed and LeafGreen, though. They were, and still are, the only games in the series to be remakes of a game where the gameplay system is no different than any other. We'd seen it all before, to a higher level than anything else. Plus, they were released in Ruby and Saphire's shadow, and didn't get as much recognition. So, between timing and re-cooking, yes, it's time to stop making the games. Or, if they want to continue the franchise, they should try changing storylines from game to game. Ranger and Dungeon were a nice break away from the standard. The franchise is still solid, which is rare in games these days. They could still get players out of it, but they need to move away from the age-old method.
