Because of the Internet's nature, much of what we do in terms of homework has to be reconsidered and revamped. For example, mathematics homework would have to be changed because there are literally millions of online calculators for every branch of math. So online homework will have to force a student to understand school topics more conceptually, rather than rote memorisation. That's a great plus point, because students will start to really learn about those topics, rather than regurgitate what they were told or what's written in their notes.The access to the information on the web also allows students to easily search for additional information that may not have been provided by teachers, or simply give students an opportunity to add to their own knowledge. Quench their thirst for knowledge, the way they want to, not force-feeding them what a school syllabus says. That way, they have their own motivations to explore and search for things to learn. I believe self-motivated learning is one of the best ways for a student to grow.There's also the ease of typing instead of writing. Editting becomes much easier as well. It allows students to fret less about time, about tidiness, about formatting, about legibility, because that's easily settled with some highlighting and a few clicks. That being said, it's much too easy to simply copy & paste chunks of text, and plagiarism cases will result. Nonetheless, as suggested by many, the Internet offers many cross-referencing tools to assist teachers in this matter. Accountability for homework is also made simple. A quick message or email from students to teachers will update them on the status of their homework. Lack of a reply from students could imply that they've never even attempted it. And it is entirely impossible to have no communication at all with the teacher, even if the student lacks Internet access. For students who have difficulties accessing the Internet because of their circumstances, they should reflect this to their teachers, who should accomodate them and not penalise them.I guess most of the effectiveness must result from the student's own attitudes as well. If a student truly wants to learn, then the Internet and online homework becomes a great vehicle to bring this student along in his growth. A student with a bad attitude will never be able to use the Internet well. He will be distracted by games, entertainment, etc, he will plagiarise, he will copy from his friends, and he might even adopt one of those pay-per-essay services that exist. Ultimately, it's the student who chooses whether to gain or lose out, not the teacher who offers the homework.In the end, I'm coming from a point of view that focuses on a student's personal learning, rather than his academic grades. Finals and end-year papers should still be done with traditional pen-and-paper, for all the integrity and accountability issues, sure. That's also the only way to truly test a student regarding a semester's work (although the exact methods of marking and assessment can still be flawed at times, but it's hard to quantify true knowledge). Still, I believe online homework is a great and innovative way to start educating people, but it simply requires the right approach to achieve the good results.
