Statistically women do tend to do significantly better than men on the English portion of the SATs, and men tend to do significantly better on the maths and science portions. By "significantly" we mean that we can reasonably conclude that these differences do not arise by a sampling error, i.e. a statistical sample that by pure chance happens to be very different from the population it seeks to measure. However, this says nothing for what we call "effect size" in stats. The actual difference between sexes is very small, and so there's a lot of overlap with each sex's bell curves. Moreover, it appears the difference is getting smaller, probably as a result of decreasing faith in the "nature" account for the disparity, the idea that these differences are naturally born in males in females.There is currently no known way by which to test whether one sex is "naturally" smarter than the other. Cognitive and developmental psychologists, who tend to take the "nature" side of the "nature vs. nurture" debate, have been touting their beloved I.Q. tests for decades as the ultimate proof that intelligence is determined by genetics, even using these instruments to give the eugenics movement the justification it needed to sterilize and/or exterminate "inferior" races. Behaviorists and social psychologists, who take the "nurture" side, generally tended to ignore scientific questions about intelligence, with the notable exception of the Psychological Behaviorists like Arthur Staats, who came up with the idea of a "Basic Behavioral Repertoire" -- a nexus of learned associations supported by a biological structure -- to answer the infamous question of just what intelligence is (in psychology the answer has always jokingly been, "Whatever the intelligence tests measure"). Staats was fond of pointing out the underdetermination of the data in his criticisms: just because you can measure something that you think is intelligence doesn't at all mean that you've proved intelligence is genetic. Or natural.

CChristlicher SoldatJun 24 2008, 07:41 PM
For pity's sake, stop swallowing all this nativist genetic determinism that pop psychology feeds us. The question is much, much more complicated than that, as we can easily see from the bidrectionality of structure and function in embryology. Chick embryos display spontaneous movement before muscle and skeletal development is complete. Such movement obviously stems form the maturation of the underlying structures, in this case bones, muscle, and nervous tissue. When embryonic chicks are given a drug to temporarily paralyze them for as little as one to two days, deformation of the joints of the legs, toes, and neck develop, which in turn affect the subsequent movement of the limbs. In other words, the spotaneous activity (function) of the skeletal structures is necessary for the proper development and functioning of the joints.We can observe similar phenomena in psychology. As an undergrad, I had the chance to work under Dr. Edward Taub, a world-famous neuroscientist who has used behaviorist principles to train paralytic stroke patients to use their disabled limbs again through simple exercises. It works like this: in your brain, there is a strip of neocortex gray matter in the back (caudal) end of your frontal lobe that contains brain cells (neurons) which represent all the motor functions in your body. There are zones for your arms, your feet, your genitals, your face, you name it. And when you move any of the muscles in those limbs, we see that neuronal activity in these zones occurs at the same time. Now, with somebody paralyzed by a stroke, the representational zone for the paralyzed limb actually begins to shrink; neurons that once represented one's right arm, for example, are being recruited to perform other functions for other muscles. And the smaller these zones become, the more effortful it is to use their corresponding limb. However, once a stroke patient goes through Taub's intense training program, we observe that these cortical representation zones begin expanding again, and as they expand movement becomes easier for the patient. Think about that for a moment. In both cases, there is no direct change to the structure. No surgical procedures to restore lost connections between neurons. We simply altered the environment, either by drug or by contingencies of reward, to inhibit or promote function, and as an indirect result the structure changed. And that structure will in turn affect function. The chick will never be able to move properly when it reaches adulthood, and Taub's stroke patients can move their arms more easily as a result of his therapy. As a creationist, I find in this research a vindication of the holistic view of man reflected in the Old Testament. But were I an evolutionist, I might comment that function-structure bidrectionality should have been common sense to the scientific community from the start, because organisms ostensibly evolve to be suited to a very particular set of environmental inputs, the absence of which we would expect to send their biological developments into a tailspin. Yet a science biased towards nativism or genetic determinism is, despite 40 years of research like I've just described, still reluctant to accept what Canadian psychiatrist Norman Doidge has called the "neuroplastic revolution" -- the idea that our environments create ongoing changes in our brain structures not just across generations through natural selection, but within generations through culture.And as this relates to the question of whether women are "naturally" smarter than men, we see again the futility of trying to answer the question with our current methodologies. Because intelligence tests that are typically interpreted from a genetic determinist perspective can now not only be interpreted also from an empiricist, environmental determinist perspective, but a genetic-environmental interactionist perspective as well. And once you understand the full implications of the neuroplastic interactionist position, you begin to realize how blurred together the categories of "nature" and "nurture" have become, and how arbitrary they are in the first place. The only question that can be practical is whether, given a particular set of environmental inputs, women tend to be smarter than men. But that isn't interesting. That doesn't satisfy our zeal for victory in the battle of the sexes. We want to prove the genetic component one way or the other so we can smirk with the knowledge that one sex is intrinsically better than the other. As always and everywhere, our science is conditional upon our politics.
