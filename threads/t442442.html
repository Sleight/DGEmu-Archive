Rreddeath26ORIGINAL POSTJun 25 2009, 11:15 PM
There have been recent moves by the U.S government to address financial issues which university students face. This includes easing up the application process for financial assistance as well as possible incentives for universities which limit price increases. For some people this will be a great move, as university students face quite a high level of financial burden. However it does raise the question as to whether education is a public or private good (or perhaps a combination)? If it is a public good then it makes perfect sense that tax payers $$ would be used to provide citizens with higher levels of education. After all a society with higher qualified and skilled citizens is sure to benefit from this. On the other hand if it is a private good, it would make sense for the students themselves to meet the financial burdens. People on this side might argue that as the student will (likely) get an increased income as a result of their qualification, this is to their benefit. Or perhaps it is both a public good and private good whereby both gain benefit and should subsequently share the burden? So the question I am posing is, whether Education is a public or private good, and how should the burden be distributed? While I have used the situation of U.S.A and university as an example, I am not wishing to limit it to said situation. Feel free to bring primary, high school and other countries into the discussion.

You really don't seem to get what a public good is so I'll try to define it here. It just means that the benefit of a particular good cannot be limited to the person receiving the good, by the very nature of the object. I mentioned air, the pollution of which is a public cost (to various extents depending on the pollutant), since you can't control the flow of air. So unless the owner of a coal power plant has a tax levied against him or her, the cost is subsidized by the public. Education doesn't just benefit the individual that receives the education, anyone living in an educated society benefits. Businesses benefit from having an educated work pool, if education is pegged to earning potential, crime is reduced due to smoothed wealth distribution, government is improved by having an informed public base. The benefits of public goods extend beyond the use of a particular amount of money invested into a particular individual. It's like the use of vaccines which provide herd immunity only after reaching a certain point, or the limiting of greenhouse gases which only works if it's reduced beyond a certain point.

If you have problems with the legal implementation of something, that's not a commentary on the intrinsic nature of that object. I can have lots of problems with US foreign policy without saying that defense or collective bargaining (diplomacy) isn't a public good.
