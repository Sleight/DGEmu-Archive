djnforce9' post='382970 wrote on Mar 28 2005, 06:35 PM:
It all depends on you and what you believe. Roms and the "real thing" both have their advantages and here's my list when contemplating your question.Advantages:Roms1. They're Free!! If you get a game you don't particularly like, your money isn't wasted. Just delete it and try another one.2. Space saver. You can fit hundreds of Roms on a CD wheras a cartridge can only hold a single game (not including pirated material and flash cards because that's esentially Roms anyhow).3. Emulators for these Roms includes image enhancers and additional features like savestates and movie playback which aren't on the original hardware. If you have the right sound card, you can even add some reverb or other effects to your games. For me, that REALLY improved older systems like on NES and GAMEBOY4. Endless life span. Old systems will not last for eternity, but Roms can be re-transferred infinitely and hence will never be lost because of natural decay. You won't see too many working NES machines around in 100 years or so, but it's quite possible that NES roms will still exist (though whether they are played is for another discussion)5. You don't need to buy a console for each type of ROM. Instead, you can download an emulator for each which is much easier as you are not consuming living room space with more hardware. Secondly, some emulators can use multiple types of Roms such as Gens which covers GG/SMS/Genesis/32x/SegaCD. There's no such thing as a console that plays more than 1 type of game (except maybe GBA which plays GBA,GBC, and GB). 5.2. Might I also add that without emulation it wouldn't be possible have GOODSETS. Imagine MAME for example. If you had all the originals not only would it take like 3 stadiums to fit them all, the maintencance on these would be far overwhelming. Forget MAME though, even all 900 or so NES cartridges would really fill a room.6. Roms allow hackers to translate games to english allowing you to play ones not even available in your country nor your language (unless you're Japanese of course which most of us here aren't). Just think of great games like Tales of Phantasia and the first Star Ocean. Secondly, if you're bored of playing the same game, such hackers can also alter it to create more challenge.Real Thing:1. These are completely legal.2. The game plays perfect and with full speeds all the times. No emulator is 100% true to the original and hence has its quirks.3. Computers are FAR MORE expensive than consoles. You may even end up spending less buying the system and several carts/CDs/etc if you're just using a comuter for ROMS.4. Portable games lose their "portableness" on a computer. You can't take a desktop PC on the bus with you to play and labtops are still far bigger than a gameboy advance.5. No controller limitations. It's generally difficult to play an N64 game on the keyboard. ESPECIALLY Super Smash Bros. This could also be a disadvantage though if you don't like the original controls (e.g. Megaman Anniversery collection, but that's not playable yet on any emulator).5.2. Some games (especially Nintendo DS and GBA) have additional hardware that need to be faked in order to emulate where as you can play them the way they are meant to be if you have the real thing. Boktai is the best example. What fun is it without the solar sensor?6. For consoles, you can play the game on a MUCH bigger screen potentially. Many would think a computer Monitor is just too small. This applies to audio systems. Newer consoles come with AV ports to hook into a stereo system. Though, you can do this with PC as well, why would you have like a Dolby 5.1 system connected with such a small screen anyway?EDIT:7. Multiplay:It's MUCH better to set up multiplayer games on the actual consoles. On a computer, you could use LAN, but few emus support it and performance is always rather poor for some reason. Otherwise, you'll have to figure out how to stuff 2 or more pairs of hands onto one keyboard which results in both of you not having your optimal control setup. Either that or have several joysticks which is a pain as well to set up if they aren't compatible as there is only one joystick port on a PC (unless you get additional hardware which costs money too).There are therefore reasons to try both and I've concluded that it's just up to personal preference (Real thing had more advantages, but IMO, they are outweighted by certain emulation pluses). Just look at the advantages and decide which set is most appealing to you and then choose that route. I obviously chose roms since they are free and I love stuffing like 20 PSX ISOs on a DVD or entire goodsets for many systems on a single CD. Plus I don't have most consoles anyway.
That pretty much sums it up right there. However since I need sprites for my MUGEN I tend to go to ROMS for that purpose, otherwise its the real deal
