(Now I'm aware there's an "Online Homework" topic somewhere here but this is different.)With the development of computers and internet, many people have been looking for ways to do things more efficiently. What they would learn in school, they would learn online. They would have their lessons uploaded on the net. This means kids would stay at home or go to a friends house or whatever and learn their lessons by sitting on the computer.Now my the focal point of this debate is: Would this OVERALL be a better approach to teaching? Try to look at both the actual effect on the standard education and also the bigger picture (eg the Government.)Here are just SOME points which you may want to consider:Will it be a better way for the students to actually learn and understand the lesson? But what if a student has a question and does not understand something?Will it be better for the students' character? They won't meet friends at school, won't have much influence from teachers. But then, they may be kept out of influence of bad children. But then they may get bad influence elsewhere (outside gangs.)Will it be good for the students' confidence and independence levels? Stuffing themselves with crisps and chocolates. They may not get enough physical activity. Or maybe you would argue that there wouldn't be much difference. Due to time available, kids may play more sports with each other. Would you worry about the radiation and stuff emitted from computers and long hours of exposure to it all?Will it be good for the parents? They don't need to drop them off to school. However, they will go to work and their children at home alone. How will they be looked after? What will they be doing on the internet? Maybe porn, or maybe meeting shady people online.Will it be good for the economy and the government? The Government wont have to finance schools and teachers. LLizasLilProtegeJan 11 2010, 07:04 PM
You know, I wouldn't be too surprised if there did come a point when this becomes the norm for all children by which to earn their education. As for now though, I think that, while for some children this could work, for others, there's just no way in hell they'd stay on task to get their work completed. Think of how many parents have to work full time. This would leave the children essentially to their own devices....you know there'll always be the kids who do nothing but screw off all day. Hell, even in traditional schools, you've still got those kids. But if they were left at home, on their own, to do whatever they please....you just know they wouldn't get their work done. Their school productivity would drop substantially. Now, as for the points you brought up, because they're essentially the same ones I wanted to cover: Will it be a better way for the students to actually learn and understand the lesson? That's really hard to say. I believe that, while on the one hand, this would enable the children to work at their own pace as well as have more resources at their disposal from which to work, this would also raise a problem with just where they'd be finding their research information and ensuring the quality of it. In a classroom setting, all the students have the same textbooks. If this were online and they were able to research the subjects from anywhere, you might get some kids learning from Wikipedia while others are learning from highly inaccurate sites and others working from pages uploaded by the government itself.... I think there would need to be some quality control in that aspect. Additionally, in a classroom setting, the student has the ability to ask the instructor for help. If this were done online and the lessons were filmed, sure, they *could* email the teacher or something, but how would this aid the rest of the students? There's a lot of times when an interesting point has been raised by a single student asking a question that gets a lengthy answer, but if only that child gets to hear the answer, how is this fair to the rest?Will it be better for the students' character?This is always one of the biggest concerns raised by parents when considering taking their children out of a traditional school environment and transferring them to something else, like home study or independant study (yes, there's a difference). You don't have the interaction with other students. For some of us, the only interaction we got with other children while growing up happened at school. I personally wasn't allowed to go over to anybody's houses or vice versa (I'm 25 and I've only *just* begun to earn this privilege from my parents). Taking away that aspect would become a huge hinderance towards social development, I think. Although you wouldn't have to worry about your child dealing with school bullies, how will they ever learn to fend for themselves in the real world as adults?Will it be good for the students' confidence and independence levels?Yes and no. For the students who actually do the work on their own, complete it, and get good grades, I'd imagine this would become quite a confidence booster. But for the kids who slack off and fall behind, I believe this would make it all the easier for them to lose all motivation towards trying to learn and just give up after a while. Will it be good for the students' health?This one, I feel is semi-comparable to a normal schoolday. The majority of the day is spent sitting down in desks at school, correct? Sticking a computer in front of them at said desk wouldn't make much of a difference. I wouldn't worry *too* much about radiation levels from the screens - I've spent most of my life behind a computer and never had a problem. Radiation problems are rare, few, and far between when it comes to computers. The bigger issue here is physical activity. It's quite easy to tell whether a student has been doing their classwork or not, but how would you really prove whether or not they did physical activity for a set amount of time a day to make up for P.E. credits? You couldn't. And a lot of people would lie about it, I think. The junk food debate in here is a good one; at home, there's pretty much that unlimited access to food at all hours. While you're at school, you don't have this as an option - you can't eat in class. I'm sure not all students would just eat all day, but the overweight kids would likely have issues with that.Will it be good for the parents?I don't think this would be good at all. While yes, they wouldn't have to take off of work to pick up and drop off their kids to and from school, there'd be the problem of them being unsupervised all day, and a child should NEVER be left unsupervised. Ever. The fact that they'd have the internet access for all those hours would scare me too, given how many predators are out there. Also, what if the child has an emergency? There'd be nobody around to help them. And then there's the children who're in abusive homes....Teachers nowadays are trained to look out for the signs of abuse and report it if they see it. If there's nobody looking out for these kids, they may never get out of their situations. Also, being home 24/7 like that would make them a prime abuse target at all times. There'd be no escape.Will it be good for the economy and the government?I think that financially for the government, it would save a whole hell of a lot of money to do this, but at what social and moral costs? Yes, it would lay off a lot of teachers, but look at how much they make a year. They could do better and find better jobs, trust me. A teacher's salary is a joke, so I'm not too concerned about that part.
