gt79erighov7b87' post='4870466 wrote on Sep 26 2010, 07:38 PM:
Any major changes?Spare no detail
Tiles are hexagonal now but surprisingly that doesn't make too much of a difference.Combat is completely revamped. You can only have one unit on a tile (including cities) so it's a lot more tactical (and no more stacks of death, thank god). The combat also is a lot less random and the combat odds screen is a lot more informative and correct. Cities now have their own defence rating and they're pretty powerful - you'll need at least 4 or 5 units to take a city. Cities defend themselves even without a unit in garrison, and they can attack one unit within your borders a turn.Culture now has a rating both per city and for your entire civilization. Culture makes your city expand just like Civ 4, but only one tile per expansion and not just around the city like in Civ 4 - it specifically targets rescources and other good tiles. You can also buy tiles for your city.The civ-wide culture stat is for upgrading policies. Instead of just 5 different policies for government and stuff, you get an entire tree you can upgrade with culture. There are 10 trees but some are mutually exclusive.Religion and spying are gone.City states have been added. They're like a civ you can befriend or conquer, but they'll only ever be one city. If you ally them you get some bonuses (like increased food or culture production) but their disposition to you drops every turn so you'll have to continually give them money to keep the bonuses.
