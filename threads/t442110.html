Rreddeath26ORIGINAL POSTJun 22 2009, 11:36 PM
At the moment New Zealand is getting closer towards a referendum on the question, “Should a smack as part of good parental correction be a criminal offence in New Zealand?”. There are arguments on both sides of this debate. Those in favour of the change argue that a light smack does not equate to child abuse. But rather it can be used as appropriate correct measure to prevent negative behaviour from escalating. They are also arguing that by criminalizing good parents, you are undermining the role that the family plays in society. Those against the referendum, argue that a smack constitutes child abuse. Pointing out that if you were to smack an adult, it would not be allowed. Furthermore they argue it is both subjective and can be damaging to the child. So what are the thoughts of the people here?Vote No-http://voteno.org.nz/index.htmVote yes-http://yesvote.org.nz/tag/anti-smacking-referendum/For pure interest I have included the change to the act which sparked the referendum.
"Change in Act" wrote:
Domestic discipline(1) Every parent of a child and, subject to subsection (3) of this section, every person in the place of the parent of a child is justified in using force by way of correction towards the child, if the force used is reasonable in the circumstances.(2) The reasonableness of the force used is a question of fact.The new section 59 says:Parental control(1) Every parent of a child and every person in the place of a parent of the child is justified in using force if the force used is reasonable in the circumstances and is for the purpose of— (a) preventing or minimising harm to the child or another person; or (b) preventing the child from engaging or continuing to engage in conduct that amounts to a criminal offence; or © preventing the child from engaging or continuing to engage in offensive or disruptive behaviour; or (d) performing the normal daily tasks that are incidental to good care and parenting.(2) Nothing in subsection (1) or in any rule of common law justifies the use of force for the purpose of correction.(3) Subsection (2) prevails over subsection (1).(4) To avoid doubt it is affirmed that police have the discretion not to prosecute complaints against parents of any child, or those standing in place of any child, in relation to an offence involving the use of force against a child where the offence is considered to be so inconsequential that there is no public interest in pursuing a prosecution.

Just to be clear: How does hitting your child teach them when to shut up? Even if it does shut him/her up, does it teach him/her anything? I live in a typical Asian family, and my parents are Christians (i.e. they believe in "spare the rod, spoil the child"). But from my honest experience, I can safely say that out of all the times where I was beaten/caned, I did not feel that I learnt anything, other than fear, that is. I'm always quite shocked, and disgusted, when parents say that they hit their child because they deserve it. Kids deserve love, they also deserve teaching, advice and guidance. But never in any humane world, do they deserve pain or hurt or abuse. Please note the difference that you hitting your child is not what he/she deserves; he/she deserves to know that he/she is in the wrong and that something needs to be done about it to change it.For example; if your kid says a bad word, I think there are more effective and less abusive methods to deal with it. For example; you could fine him/her whenever they say a bad word, or not let him/she go over the so-and-so's house after school for that week. There are often better ways to deal with our children, but often hitting them seems the best/easiest thing to do, because, well, we're angry and we feel that hitting them would smack the badness out of them. But often, when my parents hit me, all I could think was how much I hated them, and how stupid they were hitting me; walls after walls came up around me to "defend" myself from my parents.There is one thing that should ever be in the motivation of teaching one's child, and that is love - to want to correct and guide your child because you care, and do not want him/her to err anymore. Now this is not to say, that you should let your kids do whatever they want, or ever give them the idea that because you do not hit them, they can do whatever they want. Make sure your punishments are always in proportion to the misdeed/misbehavior, and that while it should not hurt physically, its should cause at least a pinch into the things he/she has to give up because of his/her actions.While the "treatment" differs by age, for young kids, go for simpler punishments such as not allowing him/her to play with the toys for an hour or to put them in the naughty corner (although I think this is quite useless for most hyperactive kids). There is less reasoning involved, but the idea is to enforce discipline through the dos and do-nots (but never underestimate a child's capacity to understand). As they grow older, more reasoning and teaching can come into play; sometimes a good talk can be just as effective.I think the key in the entire bringing up of a child, is to be consistent. From year 0, you must lay down the expectations of appropriate behavior and right morals, and enforce it throughout. Also, important, is the idea of being a role model. If you tell your kids they shouldn't be swearing or smoking, you jolly well shouldn't either (saying that you're an adult is NOT a good reason). Kids ALWAYS learn from their parents, so do note that they WILL take after you, be it good or bad.
