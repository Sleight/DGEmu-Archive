Having read "The Hunger Games" and watched "Battle Royale", I would say that the former takes the main plot point of the latter and inserts a (this will probably annoy some people) somewhat "Twilight"-esque dramatic element through a leading female who can't (or refuses to) choose between two suitors who are seemingly equally worthy of her love. The main difference between this series and the other, though, is that the leading female actually has some depth and takes action, though she is a bit of a Mary Sue. Beyond the first book, it does get into the whole government authoritarian regime more in depth, though it is portrayed from this character's perspective, which is one of a not-entirely-willing pawn in a grand scheme to which she is not entirely privy. All that said, the books are fairly well-written and make good use of cliffhangers and other literary devices to keep the reader interested from start to finish. I guess what annoys me most about it is when people embrace something such as this with such fervor and proclaim it to be unique when, really, it's using a combination of concepts that other artists have used to great effect before.
