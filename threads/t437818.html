Wikipedia wrote:
Fable: The Lost Chapters is an expanded and re released version of Fable. It was released for Xbox and Windows PC platforms in September 2005. The Lost Chapters features all the content found in the original Fable, as well as additional new content such as new monsters, weapons, alignment based spells, items, armor, towns, buildings, and expressions, as well as the ability to give children objects. The story receives further augmentation in the form of nine new areas and sixteen additional quests. Characters such as Briar Rose and Scythe, who played only minor roles in the original game, are now given more importance and are included in certain main and side quests. Other character-based augmentations include the antagonist, Jack of Blades' voice sounding deeper, harsher and more demonic; or being able to uncover (and resolve) the murder mystery behind Lady Grey's sister.

It's quite simple: The parts really count for one archived file, broken up to make transferring the whole program online easier. Once every part is downloaded, click on the first part, and extract the ISO. It -should- extract with no problems.As to actually running the game, look to 48henny's nice post on how to do so (for Windows users, anyway). I'd like to add another tutorial, should anyone who uses the Linux distro "Ubuntu" (like myself)1) Open the Add/Remove program, search for the GMount-ISO program. Install it2) Open GMount-ISO, and locate your iso file. Then, locate your CD drive (For me, and most others, it'll be CDrom or CDROM0).3) Mount with the "Mount" button. Like a Mac computer, whenever something is "Mounted" (Like drives, CD's, etc), it'll show up on the desktop. Alternatively, you can also go to the "Computer" folder, and find the CD drive therein.4) Inside the drive should be some kind of installer program. These tend to stand out, as they are obviously named ("Setup.exe is a popular one. "Autorun.exe" might help as well, too)EDIT: Whoops, forgot an important part of installing games for Linux: You'll need WINE to run Windows games. Basically, it's a compatability layer that somewhat emulates Windows and DirectX for games and other programs. It's possible that Play on Linux will work too, but I've yet to check if Fable: The Lost Chapters is compatableAnd enjoy! I don't know how many linux users frequent this site, but might as well get it out there. As for you Mac users, well, you are outta luck.
