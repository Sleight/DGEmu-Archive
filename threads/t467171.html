My maternal grandmother wouldn't be able to identify a computer if it landed in her lap. She needs help setting her voice mail on her touch-tone house phone. My paternal grandfather, on the other hand, spends most of his free time surfing the internet looking for interesting articles to send to his friends and family. Grandma never held a technically inclined job. Grandad owned his own pharmacy and probably learned computers to improve his business. My father is an IT professional. He knows networking and hardware very well. He also thinks itunes is a website, not a program. He doesn't really know how to use a computer for casual use, his expertise remains in fixing computers. My mother was a programmer and has no problem using casual programs. I don't think the computer intelligence is as dependent on age as it is on the individual. I think the best attitude is to be cautious at first, until you can judge how much they know. Start assuming they don't even know (based on a true incident) that a computer requires electricity to work - if the power in the building is down, the computer won't work. Go up from there.
