ima sim racing fan,so lemme 'enlighten' you. ^^Both games have decent collection of cars,although GT4's 700+ easilly dwarfs Forza's 260+,so it would seem that GT wins this round.However,GT has been famous for adding in some rather 'useless' vehicles into its database.eg:the Daihatsu Midget which is a freaking 3 wheeled van that cant move faster than 60km/h, and some of those antique 1800s cars that look like electric trolleys.On top of that,GT has too many cars of the same make.(who would need to experiment with more than 4 different MX-5s when 1 particular make would do the job just fine?)However,both have certain cars which the other doesnt have.Forza was fortunate to secure the Ferarri licenses,making it possible to blast into the game with an Enzo,while GT had always prided itself on accquiring prototype concept cars like the then Evo 9, the Nike car...GT has better car details too,with the emmision of backblast from the exhaust pipes,and a special Porsche mechanism that automatically raises or lowers the Porsche's rear spoiller if the car ravels too fast and becomes unstable..Forza didnt manage to implement both.And downforce (usually toyed around when a spoiller is installed) is more accuratly mapped in GT than in Forza.therefore in terms of cars,GT wins for its large collection,while Forza shines in securing cars which you REALLY want to try your hand out at.now for racing physics.GT's car control is as close to the real thing as it gets.(probably too close for comfort).At points, its so unforgiving that only a perfect race will do,no questions asked.Forza on the other hand,is more forgiving in car handling.In fact,it is sometimes mistaken for being somewhat arcadey since the cars have a tendency to turn TOO easilly (resulting in spinouts). The understeer of the 4WDs in Forza is grossly overdone though,forcing you to brake much earlier than compared to the same 4WD car in GT because of this.Drafting is present in both games, although Forza's drafting physics seems a little harder cause the gain in speed when drafting is slightly slower than that of GT's.on to the details now.GT's environment details are laughable.The tracks are too shiny to look real,running in the grass doesnt seem to affect anything more than your speed,tire temperatures dont seem to be taken into consideration when negotiating races.there's no damage of any sort,not even engine or cosmetic damages.Paint doesnt even get scrapped off when brushing the wall,and tires do not leave burnout tracks.Even more ridiculous is that the car can still coast along at about 15 mph when the fuel tank is empty. :rolleyes:Forza on the other hand,seemed to have focused most of its efforts on environment variables. Real time damage in the game's main selling point. Tire and engine wear is calculated at every simple point of the race just like what actually happens in a race.Just after doing about 3 laps in a race should cause your car to start slipping and sliding around because the tire's grip has all but worn off.Novices who often overrev their cars to redline before the start of a race will find their engine significantly damaged enough to reduce the car's performance.Collisions and scrapes are well displayed by dents and scratches to the cars body along with compromised performance.A nasty collision will completly k.o your car engine,leaving you to crawl at a snail's pace of about 20km/h with a damaged steering which constantly drifts your car left or right constantly. drive into the gravel/grass to try and chip past the AI will get your cars stuck in there,greatly reducing your control ability and limiting your speed severly.(a 150-70 km/h drop iis a sure way to destroy a tight race). Most well exhibited in Laguna Seca,where most people in GT often try to cheat through the infamous cockscrew turn by roaring though the gravel.In forza however,try that and watch your car lose almost all its speed AND spin around.woo hoo. ^^now about the tracks.Both games boast actual tracks and fictional tracks,although GT crealy wins in this aspect,it has almost twice the number of available tracks(both real and fictional).And GT's rendering of real-life tracks is more accurate as well,example being Laguna Seca and NurbrigringIn Laguna Seca,the track prior to the downslope leading to the tight hairpin is supposed to be an upslope track.In Forza it had been toned down to a very gently upslope,almost level.The Nurbrigrirg circuit in Forza is MUCH MUCH more wider than the actual racetrack,making navigation much easier.
