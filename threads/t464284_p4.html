MNX1024' post='3678395 wrote on Nov 21 2007, 05:50 PM:
I don't want to sound like a newb, but, does the R4 support any memory unit that are above 4gb?
Short answer: The largest microSD capacity you can use is 2GB.Long answer: It technically supports up to 4GB because that's the theoretical maximum size the SD specification supports... but 4GB microSD cards don't really exist. It's not feasible to manufacture them at that capacity using the old technology, so the memory companies instead started producing 4GB microSDHC cards (HC= High Capacity). The HC cards retain the same form factor so that SDHC devices can still use older SD cards, but it doesn't work in reverse; standard SD devices like the R4 can't understand the new addressing method used by HC and are therefore incompatible. So, you may be able to find 4 & 8GB cards for sale, but they are HC versions and will not work in the R4.

I'm afraid you've been misinformed... The new side-loading R4 does not support SDHC.The entire purpose of the new R4 design was to eliminate the spring, due to its high failure rate. No functional hardware was changed, so it could not have gained SDHC support that way. The R4 team has said that they are looking into making the R4 support SDHC via firmware, in which case it would also work for the older R4. They even boldly claimed that the next firmware update will have SDHC support... yet here we are later, v1.12 is out, and no such support has been added. The only change was to fix saving in Worms 2: Open Warfare.It may not even be possible to add SDHC support via firmware if hardware turns out to be the limiting factor, so you would banking on hope and prayer if you purchase it now with the intent of using SDHC. The Supercard One was in a similar boat- they tried to add SDHC compatibility via firmware, but it turned out to be impossible... so they came up with a new hardware design and released it as the SC One: SDHC Edition.

DGEmu checks all rom uploads.FAT and FAT16 are the same thing as far as flash carts are concerned. FAT16 is an older and simpler filesystem, that tends to "waste" a little more space on the card, but some troublesome roms may perform a little better due to the lower overhead. FAT32 is a more modern filesystem, and is actually required on cards over 2GB since FAT16 is limited to that size.
