KKirie MotobaJun 21 2013, 07:05 PM

WD Red's are optimised for NAS, but they can be used outside that context as a regular drive.

 

Configuring a RAID 1 means the following:

- Changing your bios HD settings from IDE or AHCI to RAID. This will cause your drives to not boot anymore and possibly reinstall stuff, or simply purchase a RAID card and use that. If you have slightly better mobos, you might be able to set which SATA ports can be used as RAID, and the rest as classic IDE / AHCI.

- Setup the RAID1 into the new RAID utility. All data from both drives that will be in RAID will be lost.

- Once setup, you will see in Windows that the available size for the RAID is equivalent to 1 drive. That is normal as it's mirroring.

 

Advantage of RAID1: If 1 drive fails, your system will not be affected as it'll continue to run from the secondary drive.
