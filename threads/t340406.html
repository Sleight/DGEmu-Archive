TTHE GRAND GALACTIC INQUISITORDec 25 2007, 07:42 AM
The impression of it as a "first-person puzzle comedy", as you say, is pretty accurate. It's unique in many ways. It's an FPS, but the "shooting" isn't with bullets, and it's not a matter of taking out a crowd of enemies with guns before your life bar hits zero (in fact, there is no life bar; rather, if you get shot a whole bunch in a short period of time, fall into a pit of deadly water, have something fall on you, get hit by a ball of light, etc., you die). It's certainly a puzzle game, and each new puzzle does force you to consider new strategies and build upon previous ones. The "comedy" bit is definitely true - you'd have to be pretty stiff not to find any humor in the wacky computer's random statements, especially toward the end of the game when all Hell starts breaking loose.It has a storyline, though if you're playing Portal independently with no knowledge or experience with any of the Half-Life games, the overall plot might be a little fuzzy. These seemingly disjointed puzzles that you go through are testing simulations of a new device produced by a company called Aperture Science (their history alone is one big joke), which is a rival of Black Mesa, a major entity in the first Half-Life game. Word is, Episode 3 of Half-Life 2 will involve Gordon Freeman, the main character, paying a visit to the Aperture Science Labs, which can only mean one thing: pure awesomeness.Oh, and the cake is a lie. It's also a brilliant humor bit that has turned into an inside joke for anyone who has played the game, just like the Weighted Companion Cube and GLaDOS' creepy robot quips.
