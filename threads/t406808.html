yo Sepulchure.Well I see another hopeful wordslinger. And as a fellow word slinger (wicked), let me tell you a few pointers.1. If your really going for the big S.K. (Stephen king) make your work more disturbing with some realism. A lot of people would read about a dead girl with her head cut off, but try the same girl whom has been kept alive while these monsters are slowly eating her guts. She screams and screams and there is our hero whom will not be able to do anything (Either he is bound or just seeing a vision or a recording on tv). Wanna make that more disturbing? Make the girl coo with pleasure while being done that. 2. The unknown is your friend. Keep the readers glued to the pages by not introducing some monsters or the scare man with the knife at the start. Though some horror genre tries to do this, but you'll only have an action/horror genre and not a suspense/horror genre which I think you are aiming at. Try leaving a few foreshadows, like a messy trail of food from the fridge towards the basement or three dead cats found inside the closet. 3. Close the deal on who your hero is as quickly as three sentence like "My name is John Copax. I'm a marshal down by the county jail. I think something in my house is going to kill me". Well something like that. The point is to establish his name, his occupation or what is he doing with his life and what is the plot... I mean the problem your hero could be facing which could be the main story of your... uhm, story.Well those are just a few of my pointers. Hope this might help and I'll be around to look at how your doing.Oh and if anyone thinks I'm sick, I'm not. I'm just friggin wicked.
