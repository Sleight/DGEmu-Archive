It's really hard to say whether fantasy should just be for kids or for adults. As we all know, there's fantasy of the "easy" kind, where a bit of magic is enough to expel evil forever and win the quidditch game, and a somewhat "harder" type, where fantasy leads to the creation and destruction of worlds, to new languages to be learned by the connoiseur, and badly-shaven rangers turning out to be the king after all once the whole trilogy has been turned into a film.The problem is, of course, that it never is that easy to tell where the one ends and the other begins. Nor where either would be the aproppriate one. For every kid trying to learn each family tree of the Numenoreans by heart, there is an adult whose favorite holiday would be spent in Hogwarts.Far be it from me to judge which one would be better or to compare the qualities of "A Elbereth Gilthoniel" against "Repello Muggletum". Fantasy is not always escapism, nor always nerdy and as loserlike as stuffing your trousers into your socks while carrying the teacher's/boss's suitcase. It is so much more...Fantasy is a way to open your mind and be aware that there may be more than we can perceive with our senses. Fantasy allows us to share our imagination and the wonders it may conjure, connecting mental universes. In a nutshell, fantasy is the hyperdrive of our brains.That's why I would recommend fantasy for any age group (G).
