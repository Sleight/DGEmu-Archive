Anyway, I believe that killing someone in self-defense is quite justified, and if pressed I believe I'd be able to logically defend its ethics quite well. To be brief, the most vital question is whether the person attacking you intends to cause you permanent harm and has brought to bear the force capable of doing so. I don't mean a potentially permanent scar on your finger; if they are going for your head or throat, or your torso with a knife, equal and surpassing force in response is ethically justifiable, even arguably socially mandated --- why should someone intending to maim or murder another person without provocation achieve their ends? The result is that the population becomes proportionally worse; better to put them down in some fashion, to disable them permanently in some fashion, lest they try it again.
