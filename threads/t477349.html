MmouseORIGINAL POSTAug 04 2012, 04:14 PM
I don't know how to rant like Calvin Luther, but I can put words in front of other words and end them with some form of punctuation.

Truth be told, most of my favorite RPGs are those that do not sacrifice story for gameplay or vice versa. What I mean is, while it's great for a game to have a unique concept or to use a concept in an innovative way, it helps if the action is wrapped up in a compelling narrative.

For reasons I cannot identify, I picked up "Fable III" during the Steam summer sales. I put up with the annoying dog (I don't care if you found a useless gem and want me to dig it up, so stop barking) and the repetitious sidequests, the dumbening pathfinder, and all the other half-baked mechanics, and I saved the kingdom and all that. I never felt attached to any of the main characters, so the story didn't intrigue me that much. Now, the game is all, "You've won! But there's still quests to do! Gnomes, books, flowers and keys to find! Don't you want to know what you'll get, what we've hidden in that 50-key chest?"

No. The answer is no. Evil is vanquished. Why would I need more money, or stronger attacks, or better weapons? So I can go around and be the balverine janitor, cleaning up the kingdom's ever-constant lupine problem? Bollocks to that. It might be a fun social experiment to marry enough NPCs to have a spouse in every house that I already own, but hell if I'm going to go through the monotonous effort to do that.

It's like in "Dark Cloud", the first (and good) one, where beating the game unlocks a 100-floor dungeon. When you beat the dungeon, you are given the strongest (or was it second-strongest?) weapon in the game. But what's the point? You've beaten every level, defeated every boss. Bragging rights? Please.

And I understand "Fable III" has various multiplayer aspects, where I could go to the worlds of other players, help them fight baddies, marry them and spawn virtual babies, etc. No thanks, not for me.

And I get that some players (including a few regulars here) enjoy things like completion runs, optimization, time trials and that sort of thing. Again, not for me.

I don't mind if a game just ends, as long as it ends well. For instance, compared to "Fable III", I'd much rather play "Breath of Fire: Dragon Quarter" - a game that is generally considered to be terrible because of a gameplay mechanic that forces the player to start over several times to beat it - because it has a compelling story that serves as a nice payoff for sticking with the game until the end.

I'm willing to continue playing a game as long as there is more story to be unraveled. It doesn't even matter if it's something small, like the short clips that play every time one beats "The Binding of Isaac". Granted, while I understand that there are new quests unlocked in "Fable III" after beating it, I'm not feeling too tempted to play through those because I doubt the narrative reward will be worth it.

And, of course, my complaints are mainly with straight-up RPGs. Shootan and fightan games, world builders and MMORPGs are enjoyable (or not enjoyable) for different reasons.
