EEngyFeb 07 2008, 06:29 AM
It's totally worth it if you have a lot of disposable income, enjoy music games, and want to add some more fake plastic instruments to your resume. Harmonix/MTV have been really dedicated to putting out songs consistently and have promised 200+ songs this year (3 per week consistently so far this year, which means they're going to be putting out even heavier releases in the future).If you're an achievement whore, the achievements are pretty reasonable... drums get ridiculous toward the end, so you're going to have to be coordinated, dedicated, or recruiting a friend to work the bass pedal for you if you want that 1000. Band World Tour gives you a lot to do, and if you really want to test your limits, you can try vocals and guitar at the same time.Really more of a party game... if you always have people over at your place, it's a no-brainer. Money well spent.On its own merits, it's an incredible game. Of course, it's impossible not to turn this into the GH/Rock Band comparison.If you're a solo gamer who just wants another Guitar Hero, the guitar isn't going to be as hard as Guitar Hero 3, but you're also getting better notecharts (no 3-fret chords where there should be one-finger power chords, no playing organ/piano/percussion sections on the guitar, etc), and you don't have to worry about lame boss battles. As the game focuses more on co-op you don't have to worry about every single online game being the hardest available song (hey, who wants to play Dragonforce again!?!), and you don't have to worry about people trying to screw you over by switching to a lower difficulty and spamming the button to face off on Slow Ride.And the timing window's way tighter on HO/POs, so you can't cheese your way through solos the way you can with GH3.Plus, since I've become involved in the politics of the Rock Band/GH rivalry, I don't want to give Neversoft/Red Octane/Activision any more of my money after the way they're handling relations with Harmonix after buying the rights to Guitar Hero 3. They honestly referred to Rock Band as an imitator. Pfft. It's a culmination of everything Harmonix had done to date, and then some. You can't just buy the name of a series and then take credit for its success.A local pub started out recruiting my brother and me to bring in Guitar Hero 3 every Tuesday, and the only people who ever wanted to play were the two of us and the staff. Somebody else started bringing in Rock Band a few weeks later, and now Tuesdays are ridiculously packed in there. That say something.
