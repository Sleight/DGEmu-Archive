BBoondock2005Jan 15 2005, 05:13 PM
This is really trying to argue apples and oranges, both have great qualities about them so it just comes down to personal preference. I liked KH for many reasons and at the same time FFX had great qualities about it as well. KH was one of those games that everyone thought would be awful; Square mixing with Disney? What were they thinking? But in truth it turned out very very good. The story line was engaging, the battle system was interesting and kept my attention, and the quirky mini-games gave me something to do in case I got bored. At the same time though, KH lacked character depth and growth. In the game, you had your base good guy versus the big evil. The only character that really grew in the game was Rikku. I've mentioned that the battle system was interesting and engaging but the ability to only control a single character did get old after a while. I was looking forward to sending in Donald or Goofy to their certain doom ya know? At the same time though, FFX had its ups and downs too. While a great game, there were just a few things that needed work. The storyline, as in most FF games was very intriguing and highly enjoyable, the characters were simply amazing, and the mini-games provided hours of entertainment within themselves (Who didn't like a good blitz-ball game?). My main beef with FFX and most FF is the battle system. Random battles just get old and boring. While the versatility of characters is nice in a battle, it doesn't overcome the sense of dread that looms over every last one of those random battles. The worst part was that you couldn’t just avoid the battles because you needed to level up. As I said earlier, this is really just arguing apples and oranges and this is why: KH focused on an engaging story and a fun battle system for the player while FFX put its emphasis on character growth. It was like Oscar Wilde's "The Picture of Dorian Grey," the game mainly focused on the growth of the characters and the relationships they form with each other. Too many people expected FFX to be some ground-breaking story, but that was not what, I believe, the good people at Square were looking for. So, in conclusion, it just comes down to what you are looking for. Are you looking for a game with great characters, mini-games, and still a decent story? Than pick up the hit FFX. If you are looking for a fun and engaging battle system, a marvelous story, and characters that are as static as an unplugged television, than go with a copy of KH. I personally have them both and like them both the same, just for different reasons.-Bear Rossman aka The Boondock Saint
