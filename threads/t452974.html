Ddarkknight014Jan 11 2010, 03:12 AM
The Steam service refutes two of the above points.First of all, piracy is a lot more of a pain in the hole with Steam, because every Steam account with the game is kept track of, and if a Steam account is detected as playing a game not tied to the account, it's much much easier to put a stop to it by simply disabling the account. The way Steam tracks this information is far superior to any DRM precautions put in place with physical copies of a game that don't require activation through Steam.Secondly, with every digital distribution method, there are ways of recovering what's been lost, except save files. For simplicity, I'll use Steam again; Because the games that you buy are tied to a specific account, and that account is kept track of on Steam's servers, and they give you an invoice when you buy a game, and everything else that can prove you bought the game in lieu of the Steam servers (such as a bank statement), it's more or less impossible to actually permanently not be able to play a game that you've bought.This is another point I'd like to make; discs and physical media can be lost, stolen, damaged, or any number of other things can go wrong, whereas digital media is next to foolproof.As for being a collector, well, it's a lot like collecting stamps. I don't understand the point of collecting obsolete media, nor the point of wanting to keep all of that extra junk around in space that could be used for other stuff, but to each his own.
