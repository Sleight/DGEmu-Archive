There is now a Raspberry Pi 2.  It's basically the same hardware as the Pi B+ but it now has 1GB of RAM and a quad core cortexA7 processor(ARMV7) clocked at 900 MHZ.  Doesn't seem like much of an upgrade, but going from a single core Arm11 (ARMV6) and 512(Model B/+) or 256 MB(Model A/+) of RAM it's a night and day difference.  but here's the real interesting part to me. Apparently Microsoft has decided to get in on the game this time and is offering a version of Windows 10 for this new Raspberry Pi system.  it's only available to Developers who sign up and is the "Internet of Things" version, whatever that means.  But apparently because of the newer processor, Ubuntu will likely be easily runnable on it.  So yeah... finally a better desktop experience available in Raspian and otherwise.

DdeelunaFeb 03 2015, 02:41 AM
The fact that someone would put their time, energy and *relatively low amount* of money together for an unimpressive project is a matter of perspective.

Most Cellphones can't control a robot without voiding a warrantee, run a real linux OS (Android can't directly run Linux desktop programs), or in the case of this new one, Windows 10 and does this all while running at 5V 1.8A. let alone connect to external hard drives without special equipment. That and I spent:

1TB hard drive: $60 (but it was something I already had)
powered 7port USB 2.0 hub: $15 (also something I had laying around)
Raspberry Pi Model B: $35
USB Micro cable: $2 (once again... I had this)
8GB SD card: $10 (I stockpile these due to camera)
total cost beyond what I normally had/buy 35 bucks

and the amount of time to put it all together was about 13 minutes total. The longest job was putting the files to serve in the first place on the hard drive. A little router fiddling and I can turn it into a cloud server as well, though that wouldn't be very useful due to IP address being dynamic.

Yeah it's not that impressive overall, but the idea that it can be made so cheaply (the hard drive being the most expensive part) and still be able to do so much more. Time really isn't that big of an issue. Now in the case of the arcade cabinets... those people spent days to put the cabinet together and a hell of a lot more time programming and fiddling with the settings to make it work right.
