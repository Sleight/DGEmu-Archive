It isn't American standard for normal use in homes, it is standard for industrial and heavy use home equipment. Lots of electric ovens, dish washers, washing machines, dryers, and air conditioners run on 220 lines. It uses a different plug designs so you don't plug gear that needs one into the other and fry anything. In this case it is a locking plug spec named NEMA L6-30 and it looks like thishttp://www.signalandpower.com/products/38But IIRC the gear in the rack isn't actually run off of 220. The the 220 line go into a power distributor about midway up the rack on the right side and then feeds out a bunch of 110 lines. Kinda like a gigantic version of a normal household power strip only it carries a much higher total amperage. It needs the extra power for all the motors in that many drives.
