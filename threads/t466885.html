BBlueNinjaTigerORIGINAL POSTFeb 15 2011, 12:20 AM
Firstly, although this is the debate section, I do not post this with the intent of a black and white, right or wrong debate. This is an idea I have for revamping the education system. I hope those who comment on this do so with the intent of improving the idea and moving it towards reality. I think everyone can agree that our current system can be improved.In our current system, everyone is given a letter/percentage grade: A, B, C, D, or F. Those who make a C or above "pass" and move on. Sometimes those who continue to make F's are shoved forward anyways, especially in the low learning level students. Many students begin college ill-prepared. Most expectations are age based, and students move up because of their age, not understanding. Thus you have kid's starting college in remedial algebra as well as Calculus III.I propose this: no student "completes" a subject until he or she has fully mastered it. It's make an A or keep studying. It is a case of not allowing failure, not in the sense of coddling students' self esteem by simply not giving F's, but instead by ensuring they truly learn what they need to learn rather than just getting a general understanding.In this system, we would have to leave behind the age based system of grade level. After all, what is the point of labeling students as 10th, 11th, and 12th grades if we have students in 10th grade taking Pre-Calculus and students in 12th grade taking remedial algebra? Students would graduate High School at a certain knowledge level. For example, having mastered Precalc, high school physics 1 and 2, chemistry, world/us hist, government, economics, world geography, and a certain level of reading analysis and composition. Of course, we would first have to establish that standard, but states do that every year anyways, as they constantly try to improve their education system.The main goal of this system is not improving the level of education, but improving the level of understanding on the taught subjects.
