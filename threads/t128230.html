Why does dgemu stores unplayable roms?
EEricChizMay 27 2006, 05:32 AM
DGEmu stores complete sets (some are rather dated sets, but complete sets nonetheless).In a complete Goodset, there are proper dumps, hacked dumps, bad dumps, translated dumps, patched dumps, etc etc etc. The average person would only go after [!] releases, but to some people who enjoy collecting full sets, they are provided as well.Of course, when space is an issue, such as the N64 set, many of the known bad dumps were removed and not uploaded to the file server. The GoodN64 set is, after all, roughly 20GB when compressed in individual ZIP files.Of course, this could be solved with GoodMerging, merging all of the same type of rom into the same zip file, generally bringing the combined filesize way down. This would be good for both the casual gamer and the rom collector. However, for example, this would combine both Ocarina of Time and Master Quest, and the cost for download would be raised from 25.5 each to roughly 50DPs. There's also a thing that emulators have problems with opening a zip file with more than one rom in it.Of course, the filesize could be solved with using a higher compression format, like 7z or UHA. However, how many people do you think really understand the archival process (or even what 'archive' means)? Don't doubt the senselessness of some of the users of the internet. If ZIP support wasn't built right into WinXP, there would be people wondering how to use the file. (nonetheless, there are people who ask away anyways). That, and most emulators don't support RAR, 7Z, UHA, or such compression methods.*coughs* I've gone off on a rant. Anyways, the answer is, there are non working roms because non working roms exist.
