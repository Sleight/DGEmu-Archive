I used to be really bad at drawing, but I'm half decent now (Or at least decent enough to get art requests alot).For one, practice. If you don't want to make time to practice, then you'll never get better. If you're lazy, you don't deserve drawing talent anyways. You don't need to practice even an hour a day, though. If you're willing to doodle a bit and spend 30 minutes at a time trying, like I did, you'll get better, but those 30 minutes should be really focused.Start with tracing. This usually trains your hand to make smooth movements.Then try "sight copying", which is just when you look at a picture and copy it without tracing.Get some learn-to books, take up art lessons, and pay attention to perspective to improve a bit more. You also need to draw on your own a bit.Hope that helps a bit... ^^;

RragingoldmanAug 01 2006, 02:55 PM
I do not believe in the saying "practice practice practice".Sure, practice is key to perfection, but just practicing alone isn't going to magically make you an artist. It's like, for example, somebody wants to be a pro dancer, and for hours straight for a year, he does nothing but practice dance moves locked up in his room. Do you see the problem in this? There is definately more than just practice for your growth. That is, if you're aiming for a direction. (cuz if you just want to creatively freestyle draw, which isn't a bad thing, just keep practicing)My tip to you is to first "know how you learn." I personally learn better when I see other people apply their skills, rather than having them teach their skills. I find videos of artists drawing, and with an open mind, I emulate their styles and techniques. This doesn't necesarily mean that I am being a copycat. I'm just emulating their techniques in order to get a feel for them, and understand more deeply the concept of what their doing.My second tip is to keep an open mind for things. Always try to draw something in a different way. This is kinda hard to understand, but, sometimes you JUST can't get a certain thing to look right. In that case, try a different method or order of drawing what you want to draw. For instance, some people who love to draw manga NEVER deviate from the way they draw their faces. Some always draw hair, then mouth, then the same pointy chin, and then fill in teh facial features with HUGE DISHPLATE EYES. There are other ways to go about drawing a face. If you watch Jim Lee's style of doing things, he likes to draw the shape of the face first (ussually a cylinder, oval, or some simple shape), outline the details, and then fill in the details. Just try to keep your mind open to different approaches to things.crap, i typed up too much. I just can't stand it when people leave vague tips such as "keep practicing" and stuff, without giving any indication of what implementing such methods would lead to. If you keep practicing at something, you'll be good at it. But what are u practicing? And what will you truly be good at as an outcome? "drawing"? That's just too much of an area to be "good at". It's like saying you want to be good at football, where there are so many different positions. Don't underestimate drawing (but don't overestimate it and let it intimidate you). When you practice, you practice doing certain things (face, body, form, shapes, skeleton, shading, w/e). That is what practice means. *edit*btw, to answer your question "how long will it take". it will probably take you a couple of days or weeks even if your trying to draw something crazy 0.o. And you will have to take a lot of breaks and stuff while ur drawing. You will get tired of drawing, and you won't be doing as good as u used to. You might want to "jump around" on different areas in you drawing when you get tired, but I'd ussually take a complete break.If you're better, you can probably draw a character in 20 minutes out of reflexes and a background in another 20 minutes or so. If you're REALLy good from what I observe, you'd use foreshortening and perspective to almost cover up like half the page with something cool in almost 5 minutes, filling in most of the details with a lot of familiarity. then the small space left u'd draw in the background and stuff in 10 minutes.
