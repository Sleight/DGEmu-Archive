It's probably easier going from heat to cold.

I came in August, and at that point I think I was going from mildly cold, to reasonably pleasant (so maybe like 16 degrees to 26 degrees? Celcius. 60F to to 79F.), so that jump wasn't so bad. I had some time for the cold to settle in. Would have been worse if I went in January... looking at 35C to -10C (95 to 14F).

But it was pretty okay. As it got colder I just wore more and more layers. Undershirt, work shirt, sweater, winter coat. It's easier to keep warm than be able to cool off in terms of clothing. Actually, it's hovering about 27 degrees these days and I'm still wearing a long sleeve collared shirt and a sweater to work.

Fresh snow is nice. It's novel. Old snow and sludge is less fun, but apart from shovelling, it's not so much different from being stuck in a downpour; slow down, take it easy.

What was not fun was the windchill though. I've been in Toronto and Ottawa in Winter, and there are times where it just feels like the wind passes right through you no matter how much you're wearing. It stings. And that kills me a little inside. The past two winters here (i.e. the only Winters I have been here for) have allegedly been pretty bad; snowstorms in 2012/2013, ice storms in 2013/2014. "Oh it's not usually this bad" they say, but I suspect the WInter of 2014/2015 will bring polar bears roaming the streets.

And in that regard, I don't think I'll ever "get used to Canada". It took until the end of 2012/2013 Winter to just accept that it was cold and that was the way it is (because someone saying "it's cold" isn't really comparable to experiencing it), but those particularly bad days where it's supposed to feel like -30C/-22F, I still swear under my breath and I imagine I always will. When it's 30C/86F I'm happy, when it hits 42C/107F I manage, but anything under -10C/14F is unholy and unnatural in my books.

I also took a few good months to adjust to the idea of driving on the opposite side of the road. The first time is a bit wonky (I noticed myself drifting into the other lane), but you're right after that. The problem is the direction you instinctively look when you need to check for other vehicles. That takes time.
