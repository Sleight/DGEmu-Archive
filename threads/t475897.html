Hopefully everyones been training? if not, weathers getting nice so get out there!

Part of Parkour is getting fit, to be able to overcome obsticles and achieve your goals and that's what I want to touch onGetting fit doesn't mean going to the gym and benching 225lbs. (no way in hell I can bench that) but it does require that you work on your muscles, it's very important.So, if you don't have a routine that gets your muscles working, set something up, this is what I do when I don't go to the gym (I don't get to go very often)

Warm-Up Stretch
50 Push-Ups
50 Sit-Ups
50 Ankle/Calf Raises (full range of motion)
50 Lunges (25 each side) (or 3 sets each side holding for 30 sec)
50 Tricep-Dips
Relaxing Stretch

Feel free to adopt this, and adapt it to suit you and make it your own and add some feed back. If you do use this though, give it your all, and don't wuss out just because you're tired or it hurts, and don't just push out 50's (unless you can be my guest) but break 'em down into groups of ten, but if you lose count or forget where you are, start over! haha

Ankle/Calf raises - get on some stairs, balance on the balls of your feet on the edge of the step, and lower down from your ankles as low as you can, then come back up, that's one. Also do not bend your knees or back, try staying straight, because this is an excercise using only your body weight.
Lunges - step forward with one leg and lower down so its bent to 90 degrees and your back knee does not touch the ground, hold it for a second, then switch legs. The variation is just go into the lunge position and hold it for 30 seconds, then switch.
Tricep-Dips - this you will need two objects that are roughly the same hight and cannot be the ground. Simply stand in-between the objects, resting your legs on one and your hands on the other, you will be facing up, not the floor. Bend your elbows and sink down as low as you can, usually 90 degrees, and push up.
