ASRock are mostly entry line motherboards, but they do the job just fine. If you got a higher budget, I would recommend either a Gigabyte or an Asus mobo instead.

G.Skill Ripjaws are the best bang for the buck, so those are great.

Hard disk is good enough also.

DVD-RW of what brand? LG is garbage drives, Lite-On are passable, Asus are rebranded Lite-On. If you can still find, go with Pioneer drives, if not, Samsung is a good bet.

Also, instead of a Gainward, why not go for a Gigabyte? They offer a better warranty on most cases. Also, thanks for supporting my country, both Gainward and Gigabyte HQs are from my country. :)

Seasonic is the best choice of PSU out there with no competitors in line, but their prices are REALLY jacked up (got one on my HTPC, running 24/7 since about 2 years now, and not a single fault or issue. The best of their kind, but I paid premium also for it). You can probably get more performance for less money if you went with Corsair, Cooler Master, or XFX PSUs (I don't recall which, but some of those companies have rebranded Seasonic PSUs). If you're even tighter on budget, borderline Antec or OCZ power supplies will do the job also.

NZXT case you chose is pretty decent, though the facing of the hard disks will hinder long video cards (clearance of 230mm with HD installed, and your video card should be around 240mm in length, so it'll block 2 to 3 3.5" bays. If you ever plan on adding more hard disks, good luck.) For that price, go for a Fractal Design Core 3000: The orientation of the hard disk is different, thus allowing you clearance up to 270mm for video cards while having all 6 HD bays free (all ready for SSD installations if needed). You'll be lacking the frontal USB 3.0 port on this case and the screwless installation of your drives, but in revenge, you have a lot more options: PSU installation with fan facing down for direct air intake from outside of case, with air filter. You also for 1 frontal 120mm fan intaking fresh air straight to your hard disks, and possibility to install a second one. Again, air filter in the front to keep your tower clean from dust. You got a rear 120mm fan, and place to install up to 2 120mm fans at the top if required. You also got the possibility to install 1 bottom 120mm fan also.

For the monitor, can't comment as I never used anything Philips. I've dealt with LG, Samsung, Asus, HP, Acer screens, so I can recommend a few out of those if wanted.

KKirie MotobaDec 24 2012, 01:14 PM

Recommendations:

 

ASUS P8Z77-V Lk (same price after rebates in Canada):

- Z77 chipset enables overclocking vs the simple H77

- Only 2 USB2.0 ports, but in revenge, you have 4 USB 3.0 ports. Your board had 4 USB 2.0 ports, and 2 USB 3.0 ports

- 3-way Crossfire or 4-way SLI enabled if you want to go insane in the future (weird that it mentions that in specs, as I don't really see 2x 16x and 2x 8x PCI-E slots...)

- More SATA ports and positioned in a smarter way

 

Gigabyte Z77X-D3H (preferably go with the Asus one as this has the inferior VIA chipsets as to the Asus board uses Realtek):

- Z77 chipset enables overclocking vs the simple H77

- Only 2 USB2.0 ports, but 6 USB 3.0 ports

- Only 2-way Crossfire or SLI

- More PCI-E 1x ports

- More SATA ports and positioned in a smarter way

KKirie MotobaDec 24 2012, 04:52 PM

You want to downgrade?

H61 chipset is the previous gen stuff, and if I recall, you lose SATA 3 and USB 3.0 on it...

Pay a bit more for the motherboard, you shouldn't skimp.

 

Parts you definitely shouldn't skimp on are mobo and PSU, both of those are like the major life support on your computer:

- If your mobo breaks a few years from now, good luck trying to find another compatible one with your hardware unless you go into used stuff

- If the PSU dies in style, it'll take your other computer components with it

 

The rest of the parts aren't too bad, since it's they're just on a replace the part that is broken basis.
