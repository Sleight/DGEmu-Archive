DDouggyMar 18 2006, 10:07 PM
I was hooked by Astro Boy. I remember first seeing it on early morning cartoon lineups as a kid and even though it was quite modified from the original Tetsuwan Atom, the serious story caught my attention. Unlike the vast majority of "western" cartoons, Astro Boy/Tetsuwan Atom discussed topics many would not think of approaching in a generally youth oriented medium such as cartoons. Tezuka's approach to the story and characters is what truly made me a fan of the show as well as manga and anime in general. Treating the audience, regardless of perceived maturity level, with respect was what made Astro Boy groundbreaking to me as a kid. The character regularly dealt with "mature issues" like the mourning of his father's death, contemplating his existence in that he was a sentient being living within an artificially created body, being confronted by enemies who he had no wish to harm but had to at times in order to defend himself, and other modern "common" issues we see in the best of manga and anime, which the younger fans among us may take for granted.I also have to add that Macross/First chapter of the Robotech series was an important series that got me hooked. Similar to Astro Boy, Macross was a cartoon but not necessarily "just for kids". It dealt with the ugly side of our existence such as war and racism. Despite being played out before the fantastic backdrop of a science fiction setting, the series was essentially a study of people, human or Zentradi, showing the viewers that nothing is ever as simple as black and white in war. It's far easier and convenient to our conscious to consider the "enemy" as inhuman killers who are simply "wrong" while "we" are righteously and simply defending ourselves. Now I'm not saying that Macross was strictly a cartoon/animation that posed philosophical questions about war and such. There was humour and exciting action. However, it balanced those aspects of the story with deeper content that elevates the anime above basic entertainment where your brain can simply shut off while you enjoy a half-hour of mindless fun. There's nothing wrong with that. I'm all for kicking back and just going on "cruise control" but it's fairly rare to find "entertainment" that can both be fun and engaging on an intellectual level.
