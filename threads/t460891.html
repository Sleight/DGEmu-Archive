Ttedsb16Sep 12 2010, 04:02 AM
This statement probably couldn't be any more wrong, especially with the advent of netdecking these days. The reality is that most competitive decks are thought up by a very few select players consistently. These decks do well in tournaments, and then everyone just buys those cards and copies the deck which requires zero skill. All you need to do is to look at today's metagame environment compared with the past, where you see just a couple of very dominant archetypes that can be consistently successful at a tournament.Also, yes, luck is a large factor of the game but skill is arguably equally, if not more important. That's why although you can do many things during your turn, there are "correct" plays and "incorrect" plays. I can link a perfect example from a recent draft I saw if anyone wants. Common examples are recognizing to play things during the first or last main phase, and understanding/recognizing precedence and priority of many triggered abilities. Even though these are simple examples, many good players still make these mistakes.Ultimately, since there is relatively little skill in deckbuilding because at events, the great majority of people didn't;t come up with the deck they're playing, quite a bit actually does come down to skill.
