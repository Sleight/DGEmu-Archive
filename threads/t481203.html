They don't make money out of new games. In fact, companies make losses when a new game goes on sale. This is mainly the reason why Staples Canada is stepping down from the gaming market, and I had access to the systems to understand why...

A game, when shipped to the stores, their cost price per game is like 50$ or so, and when they sell it at 60$, it's a freaking 10$ profit... worthless. And this is because of licensing and everything.

 

What makes Gamestop survive this well is their used game market. Omfg, CoD is hot, pre-order today, regular edition, 60$, make 10$ profit...

HEY, PROMOTION GUYS, WE GIVE YOU 15$ instead of 10$ for that CoD that took you a few days to finish,, just in time to get a new pre-order of another game for 60$!

 

Meanwhile... Yes, we acquired a used copy for 15$, now we sell it for 55$, marked down 5$ from original price so that not only do the game companies get no benefit from it, but we're whoring out our clients with now a possible 300% profit on that same game we only did like 15% profit on new.

 

This also explains why our Best Buy and Future Shops here jumped on this bandwagon also...

And also explain why Microsoft pulled off all that lel used games shit on Xbox ONE, making Sony laugh at their faces.

 

It's been said that used game sales hurt game companies more than pirates, and this is thanks to this inflated trade-in used games ecosystem.

 

A fine example was my friend back in the days, traded in around 20 of his freaking really hard to find jrpgs (whole Shin Megami Tensei series on PS2, etc) along with his consoles to a Gamespot just to obtain the PS3 console 80GB model for 200$ instead of the MSRP (I think it was 500$). He was like uber glad.

Next day I go to that Gamestop her sold his games off... They were back on the shelves... for freaking 45$ each used.

A week later... all his JRPGs on that shelf were gone, meaning 20x45$... He could've gotten 900$ if he sold it himself, giving him enough cash to buy 1 brand new PS3 and a shitload of games with it.
