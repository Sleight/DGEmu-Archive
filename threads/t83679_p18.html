Although there's always the 'save the world' thing, they handle it in different ways each time. Digimon World: You raise a Digimon through care not level grind, and literally train it to go around saving the world they put you in. Sort of a True "monster raising" style. When you partiner dies you gain an egg to raise all over again, and you continue to learn new skills as you restore order and raise your partiner through different forms.Digimon World 2: You travel though boobytrapped mazes in upgradable tanks with a team of up to 3 digimon on the field when you literally 'run into' monsters. You also can try to 'befriend' new creature, provided you give them enough presents and are strong enough to convince them. There's alot of planning and preperation involved in getting through each map successfully. Digimon World 3: Alittle different from the other two games so far, this takes place inside the setting of a virtual reality game in which you get one of three teams of digimon to accompany the character through the game. YOuy can gain new partiners, but it requires some fairly difficult miniboss fights. The evolution system is interesting in that you can select prior to battle or in battle which digimon you want to use band at what evolution it should be. There's soem strategy in how to use digivolving, de-volving, and DNA Digivolving to the maximum effect. There is also the "blast meter" which fills gradually as you take damage, whne it maxes out your current partiner changes into a much stronger form for a short duration. You do have to go around and defeat different area leaders and bosses to progress through, but this plays out more like an RPG storyline.Digimon Rumble Arena: A fairly simple game, If you've ever played Mortal Kombat, Soul Caliber, or Mugen, you can probley pick this up fairly quick. When I was ten or so, my then-best friend and I fought like cats and dogs over this exact issue. I thought Digimon was stupid because all their names ended in '-mon', and she thought Pokemon was stupid because all they ever said were bits of their names. Two years later, I saw Digimon Tamers and loved it--and then I rabidly watched Frontier when it came on air, even the reruns.Nowadays, I like both, but I think Digimon actually has the better plot and character designs. Pokemon's anime is like a cheap monster-of-the-week series, with very little actual plot behind it--whether Ash is really journeying to the League or just wandering around aimlessly is quite uncertain despite what they say. But whereas Digimon may reuse the 'get sucked into another world and find out they're the chosen ones' plot in most of its seasons, it's portrayed differently each time, and the story is usually good.And for the characters--let's just say that Ash irritates me. I don't even watch the Pokemon anime anymore, but just thinking about his general state of cluelessness pisses me off. Digimon's characters are just more real and more likable than Pokemon's.The games are about the same in general quality; for example, DMW3 and DMW: Dusk are both fun, as are Pokemon Crystal and Pokemon Firered. The Pokemon games are all pretty much the same--which is both a good and bad thing. Good in that they have a good nostalgia factor, and bad in that those with little patience may find them stale. The Digimon games have a plot that is somewhat weak compared to most RPGs, but it's still pretty good. It seems to me that the Digimon games are a little harder than Pokemon, which is nice if you're looking for a small challenge.Personally, I like Digimon better. It's really all a matter of opinion, though. ...And I totally overthought the matter -_o;
