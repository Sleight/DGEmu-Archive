Bluetooth seems to becoming a more common standard issue for business or higher-end laptops. A lot more laptops/tablets I've seen are starting to come with Bluetooth as a standard package these days. Most common home users probably wouldn't have much of a use for it yet. Ie, people who use the computer for surfing, email, word processing and games probably wouldn't use Bluetooth. Business users, on the other hand, who want to sync their PDAs/phones or access the internet via their mobile phone probably find it more useful. The only things I could see an average home user using Bluetooth for would be a Bluetooth keyboard/mouse, or a pair of Bluetooth headphones. hoser' post='3910717 wrote on Jan 31 2008, 04:21 AM:
Bluetooth sucks, I can't see anyone would actually want to use it. Wifi has much larger range (200ft) , bandwidth (135 Mbps), and can actually run on the 5.7 Ghz band. Btooth gives you what, 10ft and 1Mbps tops?
I wouldn't say that Bluetooth sucks. I just wouldn't say that it's designed for the same purposes as WiFi was. Bluetooth isn't used exclusively for networking purposes like WiFi typically is. Rather, most Bluetooth products come in the form of computer peripherals (mouse, keyboard), remote controls (for cameras, computers), audio devices (wireless stereo speakers/headphones, headsets), etc. You might argue that these wireless applications are already fulfilled by other proprietary RF interfaces. However, imagine if you went that route, you'd have a dongle for your keyboard/mouse, dongle for your wireless speakers, dongle for your wireless headset, dongle for your wireless remote control. That's four dongles that you'd have to connect to your computer. With Bluetooth, you just connect your devices and go. The Bluetooth radio is typically integrated into most modern computers. Or even if not, you can just buy one single USB dongle and be set. Bluetooth is also commonly used as a general wireless serial link. Perhaps the most common use of this is to pair your mobile phone with your computer as a modem. With respect to network access, yes, WiFi gives you a range of 200 ft, but that's actually not much compared to what a Bluetooth enabled GSM phone can do. For example, I can surf the internet on my notebook computer while riding on a bus in the country side. WiFi typically doesn't provide for this type of usage. So Bluetooth by itself, probably not too useful. But its applications are quite good indeed. So, I'd actually beg to differ and say that Bluetooth does not suck. Rather, it's quite good and useful.
