I'm not too sure about these things.

 

I mean, sure, the Ouya is a cheap device featuring lots of "free-to-try" games, but forcibly make you pay for them later for features and such. Will the online support work properly? Will that little nVidia graphics chipset be enough to run "blockbuster" games as they're intending them on doing?

 

For Steambox: Remember, Steam on Windows has a large library of games. Steam on Linux, they only have the minority of games that were ported, and most large mainstream games aren't supported in Steam Linux. Same applies to the Mac version. Now Steambox is nice and all, but think of it this way: You're enjoying most of your game libraries on a Windows machine: Will all these games you own even run on that Steambox that will most probably be running a custom OS? Simulating Windows drivers might not be a possibility, and having a license of Windows installed is a costly alternative for them just to have every game avail.

 

And if the console is going to be like $200, I would really just save that money and invest it into a decent computer, that can do more than just playing a few Steam games.

If you like Steam Big Picture, any decent computer with even a low-end video card have HDMI outputs so that you can plug it into a TV.

 

Instead on relying on controllers that will probably be made specific to Ouya or Steambox, why not just have that Windows PC that supports any controller (Xbox 360, PS3, PC, adapters for classic PS2, SNES, etc).

 

The only way their project would work is probably if they can get their hands on console exclusive games (Not going to see any God of War, inFamous, Mario, Halo (the newer ones), Gears of War on your PC), it's not going to work too well.

 

My shallow POV though.
