ZZennalathasAug 07 2006, 06:27 AM
I think that, ultimately, the sheltering of a child will have adverse effects in the long run, but to let a child do as they please all the time would be letting said child commit suicide. I think games, video or otherwise, are an aspect of a child's life that should remain uncensored by parental guidance. Like music and film, games are an extension of a person's personality, allowing them to enjoy themselves within boundaries that suit their own preferences. If a child enjoys playing martial arts games, perhaps he will want to take up the martial arts. But for a parent to take away the game to strike against their child's will to do harm to others pre-emptively, would be irresponsible. In such a case, the parent labels the game inherently evil/harmful and the child will either pursue to play it more for such a reason, or their perception of the world will be altered slightly to lump such activities into the innapproriate side of life that said child attempts to avoid. On one hand, a parent has a child that learns a disrespect toward authority figures. On the other hand, they have a child too willing to adhere to the life plans of another and hand over independance. A parents should allow the child the freedom they wish, but at least show them the consequences of their choices, and aid them in making mature ones. If a child sees violence in a game, the parents should remember to assure their child that the game is ficticious and that real life doesn't allow such actions to occur without serious repercussions. Don't allow the child off of the leash...just buy a really long leash. Shelter a child for too long, and the "rebellion" at the onset of their puberty will be more drastic than if you allowed for freedom to begin with. Good parenting doesn't mean taking things away; good parenting is teaching what is and isn't mature decision making, and encouraging the child to learn from his/her mistakes.Of course, take my advice with a grain of salt. I haven't children, and I'm but 18 years old; however, I'd like to think I'd make a good father when I've the security to raise a child.
