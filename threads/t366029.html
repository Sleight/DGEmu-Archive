The difficulty is that when dealing with a creator, we can't restrict ourselves to the world we know when positing attributes of this creator. Simply put, if God is perfectly good, then the act of creation must have created the best world that could exist - even if we say that acts of free will might derail that later on. If we can demonstrate that there is some possible world where people retain free will, but some unnecessary sort of suffering is reduced, then we have an issue with the whole "absolutely good" thing.In other words, we can claim that free will is a very good thing, but things like punishments that occur after death have little bearing on people's decisions in life. One could have free will without the doctrine of hell, or even the doctrine of heaven. One could have free will without the Black Death. One could have people become closer to God without hurting, say, an animal that lives on a remote island and is never seen by human beings. What, then, does this mean for a God who chooses to create this world, rather than one of the many other possible ones?
