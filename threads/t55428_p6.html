Multiple favorites, overall. Naturally, the World is Square. >.<Honorable mentions: Robotrek, Final Fantasy IV, Tetris Attack, Tales of Phantasia, Bahamut Lagoon#5: EVO: A little known game, with its own appeal. Odd, yet interesting, your goal was to fight things to evolve, in a semi-RPG sort of sense--but the fighting was the key of the game...with some focus on the strategy of evolution. Overall? /Fun/.#4: Super Mario RPG: A really nice game which, again, blends RPG style with a great. The feel of the game was kooky, and yet it meant to be such...and made it so it was a game that I played through and beat multiple times, as opposed to some RPGs that you go through once, and never touch again.#3: Chrono Trigger: And then there are some RPGs that you go through once, and that's only the beginning. Chrono Trigger was revolutionary for its time, thanks to the idea that a RPG shouldn't have just one ending...but possibly a dozen. The New Game + was a wonderful feature, and an idea that's carried over to many successful RPGs today.#2: Final Fantasy VI: As influential as Chrono Trigger was, the heyday of RPGs was, in my opinion, Final Fantasy 6. The game was masterfully executed, the scenes touching...it defined the glory days of SNES RPGs, a time that many would like to go back to.#1: Secret of Mana: As wonderful as the other games are, for their depth, this game made up for it with its breadth--with the sheer amount to love about it. From its blending of adventure games with RPGs, to the fact that it was the only RPG you could pick up with a friend and actually play, it likely drew many more new players to RPGs than any of the other good RPGs of its time. With a compelling plotline, and beautiful graphics, it may have been underestimated or underappreciated by some, called shallow...but if it were not for SoM, I never would have been as much of a RPG player as I am now.
