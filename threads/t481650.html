KKirie MotobaMay 13 2015, 02:12 PM

Not exactly sure.

 

For 2007, you have:

- Retail key (Home and Student version is 1 key for 3 computers, Standard and above is 1 key for 1 user on 2 computers). Activation required online. License is transferable.

- OEM key (Original Equipment Manufacturer), 1 key for 1 machine, activation required online, and license is locked to that computer.

- VLK (volume license key), 1 single key activated for multiple machines as per the company's agreement with Microsoft. No activation for this. MS will audit companies to see if you're compliant.

 

For 2010 and 2013, you have (I may be off for the retail licensing part of 2013):

- Retail key (2010 Home and Student version is 1 key for 3 computers, 2010 Standard and above and anything 2013 is 1 key for 1 user on 2 computers). Activation required online. License is transferable.

- OEM key (Original Equipment Manufacturer), 1 key for 1 machine, activation required online, and license is locked to that computer.

- MAK key, 1 single key for X amount of activations, will be deducted from your license agreement with Microsoft, activation required.

- KMS key, 1 single key for all computers in a company, will connect to the company's KMS server and validate licenses there. If a computer is unable to connect to this KMS server within the 6 months, the product is deactivated and needs to get back on the network to validate. (this is the one they exploit now with the re-arm tactic, basically simulates a fake KMS server locally to reset the activation every 6 months)


Oh, and there's also Office 365, paid on a monthly subscription.
