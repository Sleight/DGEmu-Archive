Hey,I did a project not too long ago regarding organ and tissue donation and transplantation. Countries like Canada and the US have informed consent (where you must tell your health care professional you want to donate your organs when you die), and countries like Spain and Belgium have presumed consent (where you tell your health care professional if you dont want to donate). While the US has a much higher donation rate than Canada, both have significantly lower rates than Spain and Belgium. Do you think countries with informed consent should change their systems to be presumed consent?

SspiralbondJun 23 2010, 04:15 AM
Personally, I'm all for the presumed consent system. There are a bunch of fence sitters who are not opposed to having their organs used, and aren't really all for it either. So the rate of organ donations is significantly reduced by the number of these people if they are asked to volunteer for organ donations instead of having it presumed. Since there's no real harm making use of these organs (remember, these people are really neither for or against), I don't see why these... "resources" should be wasted.Of course the system could use some form of tweaking. For example, where I'm from, they send a letter to every single citizen who turns reaches adulthood at around 20 years, and the letter states our policy on the "presumed consent system". This gives people the necessary information to opt out of the system if they are opposed to donating their organs, they even make the process easy by providing all the relevant forms and contact information all neatly organised. In fact, they provide options for a person to specify the use of their organ donation, such as for scientific research or maybe just for surgeries.All a person needs to do to opt out is to fill in a form and send it through the mail (the form doubles up as a an envelope... it's hard to put it into exact words, but the address it's to be sent to is on it). So with a organised and systematic process like this, I don't see how people who are opposed to organ donation can find anything to complain about.
RRayadragonJun 25 2010, 04:10 AM
I'm for switching to a presumed consent situation. As other people have already stated, once you're dead, you don't exactly need your organs anymore. The physical body is not equivalent to the person who just died; it's a useful container for our physical life. After death, the body/organs either get burned to ash or buried to rot in the ground. Meanwhile, to those who have diseases that could be helped, and in some cases can only be helped by an organ transplant could benefit immensly from the physical donation of those who are no longer "here." If there were better alternatives avaliable, informed consent wouldn't be a problem, in part because we hopefully wouldn't need organ donation anymore. Until then, I can't see why we don't do something of a reverse of the current US system. Organ donation is tied into drivers licenses. If you've elected to be an organ donor it's marked on your license, and at the time of application and renewals your asked if you want to be an organ donor. Instead of that, have drivers asked if they wish to opt out of organ donation, then mark that it is not acceptable on their license. In case of people without a drivers license, assume the opposite, that consent has not been given and that organ donation is not acceptable.
