Okay, I tried to make this jokey and lighthearted, but the core concept of the game's plot irritates and confuses me. If this is Limbo, the boy is supposed to be dead, right? Does that mean everything is symbolic, or is this really what everyone has to go through to not go to hell? Also, how can you even die in Limbo if you're already a ghost? I found a bunch of forums with theories as to what some things could be interpreted as, but these all had holes in them For example, one thought white = life and black = death. If that were true, why are the zombie worms, which are a part of Limbo and cause death, white? Why is electricity, also a source of death, white? Why are literally all of the moving creatures in the game black? Same thing for the end, where you crash through the glass. Most say he crashed and died, but then why would he have to re-live that in Limbo? Some say he's coming back to life, hence his sister seeming to be startled by his presence. If that's the case, why put two rotting corpses (him and his sister) on the title screen in the same place he apparently returned from the grave? No one has an explanation for the factory or the zombie worms, or the zombie worm eaters, or the fact that his sister was there halfway in the game, then disappeared. And ending the game without any explanation whatsoever? Seems less artistic and more uncreative to me. Don't get me wrong, I loved the game, the puzzles and atmosphere were excellent. But take a game like Braid, where the plot is symbolic, but definitive (he's a scientist feeling regret for creating the atomic bomb), and compare it to this game, where the plot is full of weird images that could be symbols for a number of things, but has no definition to combat the rampant ambiguity. It bugs me that they chose to go for the "It's whatever you want it to be! Yay!" route instead of the artistic vision route. tl;dr Got Limbo today,Beat Limbo today,The game was okay,The ending was gay.
