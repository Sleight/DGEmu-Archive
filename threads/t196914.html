okay, so i've read some of the tutorials on pixel sigs out there, and they're mostly junk. mine's not much better, but i'll actually go through the process and explain how to do certain things instead of...step 1: draw a backgroundstep 2: draw people fightingstep 3: add stuff like fire and bloodi hope this help some of you. this is a BEGINNER'S TUTORIAL. that means, if you've never tried doing this before, or you have and you're just really really horrible at it, this will probably help you. if you need help on drawing perfectly proportioned people and advanced shading, this isn't the place to look. but by the end of this, if you have Paint and have ANY basic artistic ability, you should me able to make a simple sig like this.-------------------------------------------------------STEP 1: Laying out the settingthe first step is laying down the background. this is one of the easiest steps because all of the angles are the same. if you're drawing a room, just use the line tool and hold shift and draw out all your lines. the windows and spaces between the floor are all the same angles as the walls, so this took me about 3 minutes to draw up. the easiest way to draw a background is in an isometric view. this makes drawing shadows later and making the right angles a LOT easier. if you're just starting out, i recommend making the setting in the corner area of some kind of room such as this one.STEP 2: Adding to the backgroundthis is another easy step. just add some of the main components in the background. for this sig, it's just a kitchen countertop. if you notice, all of the angles are exactly the same as the room's. sometimes i copy a few of the angles i'm using and paste them to the side for reference later on. i recommend doing this because you can just copy those angles and paste them back in the drawing to make any kind of furniture or structures later on.STEP 3: Adding small background detailsthis part is pretty easy too, but it's time consuming. a lot of the same angles are used. for more natural objects, they're just basic shapes for the most part. the tomatoes are modified ovals, the cutting/cooling board thing is a rectangle, the pots and cake and cans on the shelf are cylinders. i also usually change the colors of gaps in the woodwork of the walls and floors in this step. it makes it easier to differentiate between them and parts of the cabinets and stuff later on.STEP 4: Adding charactersthis is the most difficult part you may think, because people aren't shapes. well, they are if you break them down. i always draw rough circles into the shape of a person, then clean up the lines later. you can make a decent looking person with a couple of elipses and circles, just make sure you keep everything in proportion. and when you're adding objects such as people, ALWAYS draw them outside of the main scene and paste them in later. if you don't, you're going to find yourself redrawing a lot of brackground linework later on.STEP 5: Clean up character lineartnot much to say here. just go through and erase all the jaggedy edges and double lines in your laneart of your characters. if you need to clean up any background lineart, this is also the time to do it.STEP 6: Insert charactersfill your characters with some color other than black and place them in the background. this part is easy and i probably should have included it in the previous step, hahaha.STEP 7: Coloring!this is my favorite part in the process because it means you're almost done. grab some basic colors and fill all your objects. i don't worry about shading unless it's big flat surfaces like the sides of the kitchen counter. i try to keep my colors pretty bright, because if you use colors that are too dark it sometimes makes the whole scene muddy and unappealing. also if you notice, i like to keep blocks of the colors i'm using for reference somewhere outside of the scene.STEP 8: Shading, shadows, and detailsthis is undoubtedly the most difficult and time consuming part. but it really isn't THAT difficult really. i use 2 or 3 shades at MOST per object - the main color, a darker shade, and a lighter shade. for shading, put some darker colors on the parts not facing your light source, and light colors on the parts facing it. for shadows, draw them in the opposite direction of where the light is coming from. the light source in this image is from the big window on the right side. i also add small details and stuff in this step like the cookies in the oven and the santa hat hanging on the wall. once you've finished this step, it's time for...STEP 9: Cropping and finishing touchesthis is the easiest part. all you do here is crop the image to the size you want and add finishing touches like your Runescape handle and any stats you want. for this i used my fishing stat, which isn't really impressive at all but it fits the sig.so there you have it. that's pretty much how i've done all the pixel sigs i've made. making pixel sigs isn't as hard as some make it seem - the big thing is time. this one didn't take too long because it's as simple as it gets. but, depending on the details and objects in the sig, it could take hours to create something that looks nice.hopefully that gives you some idea on how to do stuff like this if you didn't have a clue before. if you're already experienced in doing this kind of stuff, i hope i at least gave you some tips to make your job a little quicker and easier.
