KKirie MotobaFeb 24 2015, 05:14 PM

What model of laptop is it? HP?

 

HPs (and actually most makes anyways) often use thermal padding on some northbridge controllers / GPUs, and those heat too, causing the system to hang and die.

Thing is, after a few years, those paddings get "crushed" and wear out to be not effective anymore...

 

Solution?

Thermal paste as Milo suggested, and a piece of copper.

Open the laptop, take the thermal padding, throw them out, apply thermal paste on the chips, put in the copper, and then screw the heatsink back on top.


If you need to source for the piece of copper just look on eBay for the keywords "copper shim" and you should land on plenty of them.

KKirie MotobaFeb 25 2015, 09:35 PM

Well, the 5750G are ok.

 

Basically, preventive measures for user:

- Use laptop on flat surface. No naps, no covers, no laps.

- Room temperature also contributes to heat. Obviously I'm not telling you to turn off heating / use it in industrial freezer, but just know that the hotter the room is, the hotter the computer will be.

 

Checks for tech:

- Yes, battery does heat, but usually, shouldn`t cause the system to freak.

- Many laptops use thermal pads for cooling, not only limited to HP. Check those anyways.

- Yes, dusting is important, and fan must be in working order.

- Hard disk does overheat also. SSDs are a winner on all 3 aspects: save battery, less risk to damage, nearly no heat.
