I have. So, don't feel bad about wanting to be unique. I thought that is a positive thinking one should ever have once in their lifetime. It does lead me to a whole new adventure. Or, at least I took it as an adventure.Just in my family, I always want to be a special child my parent ever have (not that I have a lot of siblings). I had tried to do differently, think and act very different than my other sibling. In fact, I think I have sort of planted my image that way. But, it first started just because I want to be different. Then, I live the way it is now. I don't think when we try to be unique always means that we don't be ourselves. You still can be yourself, and be unique as your own. I think the term 'unique' is just meaning you have a character of your own. Furthermore, who can tell you to be who or what? None, unless you feel that you have to be your teacher's good student, or your parent's good child. The thing is we never have to feel 100% that we want or need to be someone for someone else. We just want to be ourselves, another cliche but it's true.Another thing is judgment. Although, we so wanted to be who want and to do what we want...we can't pass other people judgment. It is hard if you are both self-conscious and socially active. We just come to a never ending cycle. There is always personal and society matter. Nonetheless, I don't think that trying to be unique makes you lose yourself. It probably change the way you act and react, or change the way you think, but you can always change yourself, because you are yourself.

We all crave attention; for some of us, this drive is so pervasive to our viewpoint that even negative attention is better than none at all. Hence the people who claim to be "black sheep" in their families--this (in my experience) usually entails a contempt for the practices and views of others. But really they are superficial and do not contribute to the overall value of a person. The difference, in my mind, between mindless conformity and being a unique human being, is whether one is willing and able to embrace this fact.An example: I wear bright colors and mostly listen to David Bowie. I hang out with different crowds of people and have a zest for life. I love to argue but I also love to listen. People tell me I'm unique, but there are plenty of people who have similar tastes in movies, clothing, and music. Having something in common with others does not take away from my own personality at all. One last thing: even if every single person on the planet had the same style and musical taste and interests, we would still be vastly unique, if only for the way we arrived at our proclivities.
