KKlokinatorORIGINAL POSTJan 17 2011, 09:50 AM
After looking at the thread where the Dingoo was first mentioned, it got me really interested in comparing each of the Homebrew Handhelds worth looking at one by one.I'll start with the Dingoo, because it appears to be the cheapest, lowest quality one. It isn't a bad buy however, it has lots of features that make it very useful.Dingoo, Around $75 not counting shipping if you get it from Ebay. http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DingooFirstly, it supports a ****load of Video codecs that some of the others don't support. The same goes for audio files too. So importing a music and game library and even a few music videos (or porn) would be easy, and as far as I can tell they wouldn't need to be converted to a different compression ratio or anything like that. (Feel free to correct me)It supports emulation of all of these consoles. (Notice that it supports Playstation but not N64. That could be a big reason not to buy it.)
someone wrote:
* Atari 800 8-bit computers * Atari 2600 (Dingux only) * Atari 5200 * Atari 7800 * Atari Lynx * ColecoVision * Commodore 64 (Dingux only) * Commodore Amiga * Magnavox Odyssey 2 * MSX (openMSX Dingux) * Neo Geo * Neo Geo Pocket * Nintendo Game Boy and GameBoy Color * Nintendo Game Boy Advance * PC Engine * PlayStation (Dingux only) * Sega Genesis/Mega Drive/Mega-CD (Dingux only) * Sega Master System and Sega Game Gear (in progress, working for most games) * WonderSwan and WonderSwan Color (in progress, working for most games) * ZX Spectrum (GP2Xpectrum for Dingux, Unreal Speccy Portable for native OS)
I'm not going to quote all the specs, but an important part is its CPU clock rate is only about 133 MHZ. That's all right, but some of the others grossly outperform it in that area. Again, might be a turnoff point.7 Hour battery life is nothing to scoff at though. I'd assume that with time that diminishes to about 4 or 5, so don't think WOW 7 HOURS OMG because... well they all diminish over time lol.Overall, based on all the types and codecs it supports, the fact it uses a Dpad not a Analog stick, etc etc, I'd rate it a 7/10.Next up are the three GP2X devices. All three are interesting. I'll work from oldest to newest. (Excluding the GP32 which is very ancient)GP2X Original model, sells for around $185 according to Wikipedia. I was unable to find any on Ebay. Good luck if you want it lol. http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2XI think this would be a solid contender, except for a few... well I wouldn't call them flaws, but they're turnoffs for me.While it supports MP3 and AVI, many other formats supported by the Dingoo are not available. Still, AVI and MP3 are the most common, so this shouldn't be a problem for the average person.The design of it is pretty good. Watching videos of it on YT I got the impression it performed fairly well. While Dingoo had 32 MB RAM, the GP2X has 64MB RAM and an additional 64MB flash ram. Compare 133 MHZ iof the Dingoo to 200 MHZ of the GP2X. If you're looking for the fastest possible, this still isn't the best. It is pretty decent though.It's also worth noting that an updated version of the GP2X was released called the F-200, which replaced the analog stick with a D-pad and it included a touch screen.Apparently the GP2X has to use Emulators for its ROMs to play properly, and you can see exactly which ones those are on the Wikipedia page.Overall, I'd rate it a 6/10All right, now for the GP2X Wiz. Yeah that sounds delightful. "Whatcha doing bro?" "Playing with my wiz" "...... Um... I'll come back later..."Jokes aside, this thing looks ATROCIOUS. This is just my opinion, but two D-pads? REALLY? I hope it plays better than it looks because I wouldn't want it.GP2X Wiz. Selling point is about $160 on Ebay, not counting shipping. http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2X_WizFirst off, the massive speed increase from the last model to this one is mind blowing. 533 MHZ with an overclock of as high as 900 MHZ. WOW. The RAM is still 64 MB, but that MHZ is what I'm talking about.The amount of supported Video and audio codecs is again slightly reduced from the last model, but it still supports MP3 and AVI which are the most popular. As an added bonus it supports up to but no further than Flash Player 8. No idea on what that means its limitations are, but I thought you might want to know. As I said, the design of the console alone puts me off of buying it but hey, it might be someone elses cup of tea.Ignoring the awful design, I'd rate it a 7/10. It supports fewer codecs than the last model, but that speed is really insane.All right, now for the final model, the GP2X Caanoo. This one looks like it's my favorite, though the analog stick might put off other people who prefer D-pads.GP2X Caanoo, selling price on Ebay is around $170-$180 not counting shipping. http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP2X_Caanoo http://www.bwone.com/gameparks-gp2x-caanoo-review/It really does have great graphics, just look it up on youtube and see for yourself. It has 128 MB RAM whilst the last console had 64 MB. It has more processor speed period, so if you want speed you want this.What really stands out about this console though is the analog stick and the touch screen. The touch screen apparently isn't perfect because they were going for a tougher screen than a more sensitive screen, but it's not a main feature, so no worries. The analog stick recieves a lot of criticism, so that's worth looking into as well.The main thing I don't see anywhere is evidence that it can run either N64 or PSX or even SNES roms. I'm not saying it can't, but currently it would appear it's so new that people haven't really released any good homebrew for it yet. I'd give it a year and see how it performs.Overall, in the long run it might be a better buy, but the analog stick might be a bad thing, and though the touch screen and faster processor are cool, you might wanna go back one generation. 6/10.Well those are my opinions, and this has been an hour of nonstop typing. Hope my work helps somebody out.Edit: Could a mod please delete the other topic? Bad internet sorry. Thanks.
