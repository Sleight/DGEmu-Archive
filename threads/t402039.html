This is kind of a hard question to answer, but I think it would be alright, but only under the right circumstances. For example if your child is having nightmares about killing people, and saying that when there around someone they dislike they imagine hurting them, then your child should go to therapy. If they are seeing a therapist for a reason as serious as this, they will continue to go to therapy for years.On the other hand there are many parents who take their children to a therapist if they have been playing with an imaginary friend, playing tea party with stuffed animals, etc. Sending a child to therapy for something that simple could potentially cause future problems, because they are only in therapy long enough to solve the problem which could be as little as a month. Lets say the child was 5-6 when they went to therapy, and at like 10-12 they see something on TV or hear someone else say that if you go to therapy it means your "crazy" then that child is going to automatically assume that they're "crazy", which could lead to depression, paranoia, and could even lead them to actually go "crazy" Which could mean they have to go to a more vigorious therapy, or could lead to something even more severe.So my closing statement would have to be if the child is in danger of hurting his/her self or someone else then they should see a therapist because it could help with present, and future problems, but sending a child that doesn't really need therapy could fix the current "problem" but could potentially lead to new worse problems.
