FFhqwhgads.Aug 11 2008, 02:20 AM
Wait...The general consensus is Ocarina of Time? Ha!Majora's Mask is vastly superior to Ocarina of Time, just try and prove me wrong with your folly logic! Ocarina of Time was merely a basis. Majora's Mask built upon this basis and created a superior game with a refreshing story and a great atmosphere. The three day system did nothing other than enhance the game, which allowed for greater NPC interaction, improved difficulty, and intense side-quests. Each NPC had his or her own schedule, which permitted various intricate levels of emotional insight. Whereas seeing a static character, something you would otherwise see in Ocarina of Time, has become scarce. There was no moment to overlook, no point where the character will stand there for days unto no end, giving the same useless response as would be the next day. It is because of this that the side-quests were on a whole new intensity; they didn't just park themselves there and wait to be triggered by you. They had coherence to the story, too, where each character had connections. These links between each side-quest and character made Termina a living, breathing world, teeming and swarming with stuff to do, and much more evocative dialogue that's worth listening to. Wouldn't you rather have to think and plan for your side-quests, much like you should for your main quest? The largest criticism I see disgorged out of people's mouths would be that the three day system didn't allow for convenient saving or caused too great a difficulty. Half of these people didn't locate the song to slow down time, which if you didn't, then you just need to speak to people in the game more and stop focusing so much on plowing your way to the end of the game. Most everyone else didn't utilize the Owl's save-spots. "Save points" are nothing new, Metroid uses it and the series is praised for keeping itself a challenging game, due in part to the save point. I would much prefer a challenge than a tedious, monotonous game that holds your hand throughout (much like Twilight Princess or The Wind Waker did). There's also the possibility that they just suck at the game, but Majora's Mask is for the hardcore Zelda gamer, and not for naive first timers. If you couldn't handle the difficulty, then you are missing out on so many other aspects of the game that more than make up for it. Majora's Mask has a, to some extent, shorter main quest, yet I still fancy it over Ocarina's. Do you know why? It features some of the most compelling dungeons ever created in a Zelda game, including one of the greatest dungeons in the series' history: The Stone Tower. I'd desire a short yet sweet adventure rather than a tiresome and mundane rehash of previous adventures. These dungeons put a greater challenge ahead of me. They also draw a parallel to the sidequests and the whole game because you have to think before you advance. Even the things you have to accomplish before a dungeon is greater than that of Ocarina of Time. Hell, the Stone Tower had a dungeon you had to complete before the dungeon. Ikana Castle beats the Ice Cavern any day. It's ridiculous to say that Ocarina of time had more secrets or a more diverse world. The Great Bay is greater than the Water Temple, Zora Link is greater than the Iron boots, Odolwa is greater than Ghoma, etc. Majora's Mask also has a much more explorable world. The main field may not have been as large, but it was filled to the brim with stuff to do. Ocarina of Time's was an empty closet, forming hills to create an illusion to cover up the fact that it may as well have been a giant plane. Not so with Majora's Mask. Holes and treasure chests to behold, a variety of enemies, a wide range of terrain, and even a few NPCs to speak to. In fact, the entire game has a much wider range of terrain, including all four seasons, a much greater utilization of the weather, and an ocean. The villages and towns actually had depth to them. You can't list nearly as many unique and appealing aspects about Market town than you can Clock Town. OoT has more or less the same story as most in the series - fight Ganon, save princess, get Triforce. There was very little that set it apart from the stuff you got from A Link to the Past. None of it was on any level of emotion that radiates from Majora's Mask's story. The differences were: you had sheik, a physical lack of the Triforce, and a nap that lasted for seven years. Wait, here's something that Ocarina of Time has that Majora's Mask doesn't! Majora's Mask didn't have a one-dimensional, static antagonist that "does evil merely because he's evil". No, it had much, much more, for the villain actually had an effect on the people of Termina. You had a sense of urgency; the moon is falling, chaos and mistrust is inside of every mind you convene, lives have been ruined, and you only have three days to fix as much of it as you can. In OoT there was no urgency or sense of an evil looming over the world. It took seven years to take over one useless town, and everybody seemed to just move to the village next to them and not have a care in the world. Not only that, but there is no use trying to get attached to the characters that you're trying to save, yet another difference between OoT and MM. And to convince those of you who are graphic whores out there, MM, utilizing the expansion pack, had better graphics. More fluid animations, crisper textures, no cardboard cutouts for backgrounds, motion blur, superior lighting effects, greater draw distance, more NPCs/enemies on the screen at once, and an overall moodier and heavier atmosphere/art style. Albeit some of the NPCs were rehashed, but the characters were still enhanced. Not graphically (although some of them were indeed improved graphically), but in terms of dialogue and interactivity, so complaints of rehashed characters are only looking skin deep. Last, but not least, an imposing back-story. The history behind Majora's Mask, the Fierce Deity Mask, Ikana Canyon/Castle and the Garo, the paralleling reality between Termina and Hyrule, the power of the masks, the four Giants. There is so much that you can piece together and would otherwise miss if you grind right through the game. Do you believe that Ocarina of Time has a story that goes deeper than Majora's Mask does? Then maybe you should read this article. That only deals with one or two subjects, yet in great detail. Don't want to read it all? Maybe you shouldn't be so quick to judge how much imagery and meaning you can put behind a Zelda game such as Majora's Mask.I appreciate OoT for setting the foundational basis for the 3d adventure genre, I recognize it for its influences, and I acknowledge the achievements it made upon A Link to The Past, but this revolutionary basis that OoT made was vastly improved with its successor. There is no doubt about it.-Fhqwhgads
