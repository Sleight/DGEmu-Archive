Yeah, in the old game two-handing straight up gave you a 1.5x Strength modifier.

In Dark Souls 2 two-handing a weapon merely cuts the Strength requirement in half. It's a bigger reduction to the requirements, at the cost of losing additional attack scaling.

As for why you deal more damage in the two-handed stance with the same Attack Rating, every attack possible with each weapon in the game has a hidden attack modifier. Most of them are standardized and follow a general pattern.

In general the two-handed stance specifically confers the following modifications to a moveset: faster swing and recovery, less rebound from shields or armor, higher attack modifiers, while also increases the stamina cost per swing.

This is why, dating all the way to Demon's Souls, a weapon that has an identical moveset with an identical Attack Rating both one-handed and two-handed will still deal more damage when two-handed.
