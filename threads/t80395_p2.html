None of this information is relevant within a few minutes of posting because the technology is constantly changing. Right now AMD's APU's have a graphical advantage over Intel because they have the Radeon chips built into the processor. It's good for getting your rig up and running cheaper and quicker, but they are discreet graphics instead of being dedicated. This will give you an ability to start gaming at a cheaper starting cost but you will want to get a dedicated graphics card eventually. The intel Chips have the Intel GMA HD whatever they call it. which from my experience will only get you moving in productivity but not so much in gaming performance. so you will want to get a graphics card immediately to actually play any games that are modern.

As for HD Video performance, once again the AMD has the advantage from an out of the box experience as the Radeon chips handle HD video better than the Intel graphics.

So it's all a matter of what the current tech holds. If you have a computer that has an i7 but only Intel integrated graphics, a current AMD FX chip can and will be able to run circles around your i7. but if you have that same i7 and AMD systems with a dedicated graphics chips in them, then the I7's power can truly shine.

As I stated already though it's all a matter of how much money you plan on putting down on building your rig. the I7 is in a way overkill for most uses and an I5 can be just as good (especially the 2500k), but that aside between those two chips, weigh the pros and cons and what you plan to do graphics wise. If you expect to be able to jump right into using your computer with integrated graphics or if you are planning on getting a dedicated card. If you go integrated (more often in small form factors) I would use the AMD FX-8150 black edition, that way you have a strong system out the gate that can actually do something graphically. If you have a strong graphics card to put with the system (usually full desktops) then you will want to look at other details to decide but most likely the I7 would be a good bet, especially with a nice strong Nvidia or Radeon.

Considering your preference to put like parts together, you have to consider that any given system is going to need drivers. Even intel ones. The difference is that a lot of the pure intel systems tend to have basic drivers built in to the install disk. Though functional, they sometimes cause problems. So basically the best thing to do is find just the base required drivers (sometimes included with your Motherboard/graphics cards/etc. on a CD, just don't install the driver utility those are a pain to deal with.) to make the system function correctly instead of making it run with the basic drivers.
