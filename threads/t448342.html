KKirie MotobaNov 07 2009, 11:48 PM
In terms of the user interface, it has changed.Touchscreen support has always been there since Vista... though now they integrated multi-touch screen support to improve that feature.Basically, their new features are: Jump Lists, Aero Shake, Aero Snap, and other such.Jump Lists: Using the taskbar, right-clicking will give you lists of files often used and such.Aero shake: Grab a window, shake it with the mouse, look at all your other windows minimize. Yay...Aero Snap: Drag a window to the far right to have to maximize to half the screen, grab another window and drag it to the left to maximize it to the other half. Perfect for comparing documents and having say a site next to a word document to transcribe data.The whole interface is redesigned to have previews and such, and you can much more easily manage Windows components.Many more customization features, you can have a wallpaper changing at every say minute, and you can also change the background of the login screen without hacking anything.For anyone running a solid state hard drive, it is a recommended upgrade as it improves upon hard disk performance. I'm facing alot less stuttering with these SSDs on 7 compared to XP and Vista with full optimization.The differences between the versions that are purchasable, in quick version:Home Premium: Create a join Homegroups. Have access to the Media Center.Professional: Join domains, Windows XP mode.Ultimate: Multi-language UI, BitLocker capability.
