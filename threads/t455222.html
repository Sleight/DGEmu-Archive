*sigh* Duckworth Lewis. Hah! That reminds me of that match where the West Indies coach miscalculated the Duckworth-Lewis wrong and called back his team resulting in them losing the game. :mellow:Anyway...Basically, Duckworth-Lewis is a system that comes into play when the full game can not be completed due to reasons such as bad light, or rain etc and helps to decide a winner. If the first team has batted, and the second team come on to bat but play is stopped after a certain number of overs (One Day = 20 overs, T20 = 5 overs) then the Duckworth Lewis system can be used to call a winner. Duckworth-Lewis predicts whether the team batting second would have chased down the score the first team made based on certain things. For example, the number of runs that team has made at that point and their run-rate. Also, it would look at the number of wickets the second team have lost. Based on these factors, (and perhaps more factors as well) a calculation can be made as to whether that team would've won or lost had the game continued to completion. Duckworth Lewis is not used in Test matches. And it can not be used in One Days or T20s where the second team have not batted the minimum required overs for Duckworth-Lewis to take effect.And why is it called Duckworth-Lewis. *shrugs* Probably cos 2 guys called Duckworth and Lewis made the system. o.O
