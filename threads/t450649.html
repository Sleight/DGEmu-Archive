boy oh boy... here comes the age-old question: whether or not spanking is discipline or abuse (whether it be to a child or not).First, let's list the pros and cons of such an act:PROS- gives a nice little shock to the receipient, who probably thought it wouldn't hurt as much/you wouldn't go through with the threat.- lets them know that you're displease at their behaviour.- give you a chance to have a make-up session afterwards, whether it being you saying sorry for hitting them, or them apologizing for disappointing you.CONS- dangerous if not done properly.- can have the opposite effect if the situation wasn't fully understood.- CAN BE ABUSED!!!!!!!!!Now that I've listed them out, let's take a closer look at what I mean by those.First of all, the pros that are up there. Yes, it does more shock than hurt to the kids in question. If done properly, it will smart for a while, but leave no lasting physical scars, and will remain in their minds for a while. It will also serve as a reminder to not do it again. Also, it lets them acknowledge the gravity of the situation. Say, for example, your child had taken $2.00 out of your purse/wallet so they can buy the candy that you said that they can't have at the time. When you find out, obviously you'd be disappointed that they A) stole something from you, and B) disobeyed. So what's better than a little "shock therapy" to let them know exactly how disappointed you are? And afterwards, you can sit them down and while treating their wounds (and most likely wounded pride), talk it out with them why you had to do what you just did, and ask them to not do it again. Tell them sincerely, and they will take it to heart, learning not to do it ever again.Now comes the dreaded cons. If not done properly, it CAN have a lasting physical and possibly mental scar on the child. Especially if you didn't take the time to figure out exactly what was going on before you went ahead and "disciplined" the child in question. For example, I remember when I was little, my mom had lost a $20 bill. And because I had a notorious reputation in the house at the time for flinching money from her wallet to buy candy, she obviously thought that it was me. She didn't even think to ask whether or not it was me before she took me into the kitchen, took out the ruler (clear red plastic one, I remember clearly) and proceeded to spank my hand with it. It wasn't until my dad found it under the car seat that my mom realized that she was wrong. But it was already too late. 10 years later, I still remember that incident, and I doubt it'll leave me anytime soon.Anyways... enough with the memories, and back on topic now.As mentioned above and by many others, spanking can be abused if done for none other than spite. Many may argue that it's not spite, but taking out anger/frustration/stress on a child, but I say otherwise. The kid's done nothing wrong, and if they did, many didn't deserve to be spanked so hard/long that it leaves physical evidence, and possibly mental scars. It's the parents' responsibility to know when to stop, and if they don't, then they're the ones who's abusing the child, not the act.My conclusion? That spanking is good, done moderately. It won't always work, but it'll at least be a starting point for those parents who don't know what to do with their kids.

When it comes down to spanking, I personally feel that it's a legit form of discipline, but that it can be abused. If a parent is spanking a child in anger, that's never the right way to do it. A parent should communicate clearly to the child that he has done something wrong, and that as a consequence, the child will now be spanked. The point of punishment is to cause some level of pain on the punished, in order that they will hopefully not commit the same wrong again. When a child is young, spankings cause the most pain. As children grow up, groundings or the removal of privileges tend to work better, although a good spanking once in a while is still effective. The parent should never be spanking the child as a way to let out his frustration at the kid. When that happens, that's where potential abuse comes into the picture.
