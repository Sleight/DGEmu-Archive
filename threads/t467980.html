But the very funny thing about Descartes is his false predicate. "Cogito ergo sum," despite what Descartes thought, is a false statement, because it presupposes an "I" to do the thinking.Eventually, Descartes is attempting to reach the fact that there is an "I," the fact that "I exist." But the fact that he presupposes an "I" in his premise, "I think," means that his conclusion is false because he uses the conclusion in the premise in order to derive his conclusion. Consider the point of development of an infant: object permanence. The infant learns that an object exists, even if it cannot be seen. There is a difference between being measurable, and being sense-able. If there is a measurement of an object, it exists. Anything in existence can be measured either by an actual measurement or by a theoretical equation. This does not mean, if it can't be seen, it doesn't exist. The moon exists even when we don't see it. We can still measure it's effects on the Earth via gravity. And even if we were talking about a microscopic particle floating in space, which we do not know exists, it still exists. It is measurable, therefore it exists. The theory is not "it exists because we have measured it, and if we have no measurement it must not exist," but "we know it exists because we have measured it, and if we have no measurement then we do not know whether it exists or not."
