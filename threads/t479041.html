KKirie MotobaApr 09 2013, 05:35 AM

Just to let you know: Don't waste your time with an 8400GS. The performance is nearly identical to the 4200 HD onboard, you'll be throwing your money away:

http://www.videocardbenchmark.net/video_lookup.php?gpu=GeForce+8400+GS

 

The Passmark shows that the Radeon 4200 is 2 points above the Geforce 8400, but at that point, it makes no difference.

 

The GeForce 210 has a slightly better handle on pixel shading, so your games will run slightly better, but don't expect much at all.

 

Instead, I would personally recommend the ATI Radeon 6450, it can process more pixel shaders, supports DirectX 11, which the 210 can't.

This Asus model uses a passive cooling: http://www.amazon.com/Asus-Radeon-Silence-Profile-PCI-Express/dp/B004X8EO6Q

 

I've had many many bad experiences with many clients' desktops with small low profile video cards with puny fans that fails quickly: The smaller the fan, the more they turn to cool, the faster the bearing breaks, the faster your computer either becomes a jet engine or the video card overheats. So it's either: purchase passive cooling (silence, bliss), or upgrade with an after-market cooler.

 

As for the power supply, RAIDMax is not known at all for their quality. 730w basically means a computer that is consuming more electricity powering a computer that doesn't need all of it, and for sure your Phenom 840T doesn't need all that, considering that your video card is a low end card. Also, considering that I've seen comments about unstable rails on that power supply seems to be an issue, I would recommend it even less, unless you want to risk frying your whole computer when the PSU dies.

 

If you're attracted to the modular, check the Corsair Builder CX600M: http://www.newegg.com/Product/Product.aspx?Item=N82E16817139048

At regular price, it's in the same price range, offers a much more stable rail, and is modular. You'll miss out on the blue LED, 135mm fan, and wattage, but you're sacrificing it for a better known power supply that has less chance of encountering issues.

 

Btw, 600w is more than enough to power a beast like this: http://www.newegg.ca/Product/Product.aspx?Item=N82E16814150586

So the more reason why you don't need more.
