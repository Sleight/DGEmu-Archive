DRM can always be circumvented (with the possible exception of that Trusted Computing idea).But all in all, it is much too expensive to build a sufficiently powerful PC for playing games. The performance-to-price ratio of a console is still much higher than that of a PC, since consoles don't need to worry about anything other than playing games and so you can specialize and integrate the components in ways that aren't possible with a general-purpose PC. Plus there's a lot less overhead since you don't need to run a full OS in order to support the game to begin with.The upgrade argument also fails because you cannot continually upgrade your PC to the latest technology due to hardware changes. If you're paying for a new graphics card, new memory, and/or new processor, the cost of those components already exceeds the cost of a console (unless you get low-end parts, which wouldn't provide much of a comparison with the console anyway).
