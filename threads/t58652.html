DDoctor CthulhuforaheadFeb 21 2007, 03:33 AM
In my eyes, Final Fantasy IV and Breath of Fire II are clearly the top two RPGs on the system. They're top five for the system altogether, any way you slice it. Final Fantasy IV is pretty easily the pinnacle of the Final Fantasy series, especially the earlier half of it, which -- with the except of IV itself and IIIj -- is mostly mediocre. Breath of Fire II, similarly, is easily the peak of its series, and is probably the single most entertaining RPG on the SNES. The story, especially, was really creative and innovative for its time. (For all I know, it could be a direct ripoff of some obscure anime I've never seen, but by RPG standards, it was a first.) The corruption-of-the-church story has, of course, been done a thousand times since, but never nearly as good or creatively as Breath of Fire II.From there, it gets a little trickier. In retrospect, Chrono Trigger and Final Fantasy VI don't really belong on the list... Chrono Trigger is an anomaly to me, because it's generally viewed, even by hardcores, with a level of credibility that a) isn't generally given to Square games, especially popular Square games, and b) it, quite frankly, doesn't really deserve. It's not only a fairly standard time travel plot -- something that, while (mostly) new to RPGs at the time, is still a time travel plot, which hasn't really been a fresh idea since H. G. Wells -- but it's also probably the most basic and beginner-friendly RPG Square's ever made. It's essentially the RPG equivalent of training wheels. And no, this isn't an entirely bad thing. I'm sounding far more negative than I mean to. It's not an awful game by any means, but it's not really something that warrants being regarded as the Citizen Kane of RPGs.Similar is Final Fantasy VI... Final Fantasy VI, to me, is a game that had two major innovations going for it, and very little else. Every character could learn magic, and you had a whole bunch of them (characters, that is). Well, the first innovation's basically turned into a pox upon the entire genre, and the second one has been rendered obsolete by the Suikoden series. Then you have the latter half of the game, which is essentially ennui given form. Again, despite this, it's the closest thing the Final Fantasy series has to a sacred cow. Nostalgia is relevant to 'favorites,' not to 'best'... If this list was favorites, then the beautifully mediocre Paladin's Quest would be one of my first mentions. But it's supposed to be best, so PQ's not gonna cut it. Neither are CT or FFVI.Back to the topic at hand... I considered Earthbound, but while I had great fun playing Earthbound through the first time around, I've never been able to get through it more than once. The game is overflowing with icing, but there's very little cake, so to speak. Terranigma would probably be my pick for the third best RPG on the system. It takes the tired (not a typo) and true Zelda/Seiken system, and does it as well as it's ever been done outside of A Link to the Past (which I don't personally consider an RPG, and thus, won't be showing up on the list)... It also has a really good and unique story, and there just aren't very many faults I can find with it. Lufia II - Rise of the Sinistrials is another game I would consider, but I'm not so sure it belongs on a best RPGs list, so much as a favorite RPGs list. Its story and characters are pretty standard RPG fare, and the story scenes themselves are as rushed and inconsequential as Tales of Symphonia's.And that transitions us to Tales of Phantasia... Some people consider this one of the better (or best) games of the Tales series. The battle system, though, is so incredibly slow. Easily the worst battle system of the Tales series, which is a series that thrives on its battle system. And the time travel-based story is... Well, time travel. So no, I don't find it to be that great. Tales of Destiny improved on it in every conceivable manner. Super Mario RPG is essentially in the same boat as Earthbound. I loved it the first time through, but I've never wanted to play it again. Possibly my least favorite of the Mario RPGs. Final Fantasy V is, at best, the second worst game in the Final Fantasy series, I wouldn't even consider it. I adore Secret of Evermore, it's really the only Secret/Seiken game I like, but again, it's more 'favorite' material than 'best'... Ditto Live-A-Live, ditto Rudra no Hihou. They're both more 'fun' than 'good,' though Rudra's only real fault is in its near total level of customability (the zenith of the aforementioned 'pox.')I don't suppose you can consider ActRaiser an RPG. Half action and half strategy does not an RPG make. The only SNES RPGs I haven't played -- and this is including even the nigh-unplayable legions of mediocre Japanese-only RPGs (your Hercules no Eikous, your Elfarias, your Daikaiju Monogataris...) which I have suffered through -- are Dragon Quest V and VI, Far East of Eden Zero, and Record of the Lodoss War. So maybe one of those could belong in the top five, assuming I ever even get around to trying them... As is, though, I'd have to go with something like...1. Final Fantasy IV.2. Breath of Fire II.3. Terranigma.4. Lufia II - Rise of the Sinistrials.5. Secret of Evermore. (Which isn't nearly as bad as its reputation claims.)As usual, this was way bigger than I meant for it to be...
