Dubai is located in the United Arab Emirates and is, what we would call, successful in deterring most crime. My aunt and uncle, along with their 2 children, live in Dubai and have, to this day, not witnessed a single ‘major’ criminal act. You know why? It’s because they’re under the jurisdiction of Sharia Law (not wholly though.) Sharia Law is basically Islamic Law. It embraces Hammurabic laws and reform policies. Most of the punishment for crime is capital of some sort. While not usually fatal, it can result, and often does, in the losing of a limb and such. Needless to say, the crime rate is astonishingly low and everyone is happy. You can sleep with your doors unlocked and walk around at night, in the darkest of alleys without great fear of getting mugged, shanked, or killed (according to my relative and their friends.)My final decision? No, it would be a stupid decision. Instead of letting out criminals to walk on our streets freely, we should adopt a stricter form or justice. I’m not saying that we should adopt Sharia Law, but elements of it would be nice (I’m sure that we all want to retain religious, oratory, and a multitude of other freedoms.) Instead of letting rapists walk our streets free, we should deprive them of what they use to rape by castrating them. Yes, that seems very harsh, but it has its benefits.While some people may cry, “repression of the people!” you must understand, it only represses criminals and people with criminal thoughts. By utilizing capital punishment, we can coerce future criminals to not commit their heinous acts. While our society will never be crime free, we can at least stop a majority of crime from occurring.

Considering the statistics that have been discovered on recidivism in released convicts, parole and early release of a murderer can be construed as tantamount to negligent homicide. What everyone wants and what everyone is worthy of receiving are two completely different matters. Read on for recidivism rates pertaining to the issue in OP's post.As for the matter at hand, according to the 1994 U.S. Bureau of Justice Statistics study on Recidivism of Sex Offenders Released from Prison, just five percent of sex offenders followed for three years after their release from prison in 1994 were arrested for another sex crime. They later released another study in 2003 of 10,000 convicted sex offenders and they found that within three years, 3.3 percent of the released child molesters were arrested again for committing another sex crime against a child. That's a much lower rate of recidivism than criminals who are convicted of especially violent, life-ending crimes. This same 2003 study by the Bureau found that the sex offenders (those who have committed rape, sexual assault and child molestation) had a rearrest rate 25% lower than other criminals. Interestingly enough, part of the reason why their statistics are so much better is that serial sex offenders who pose an enormous risk to the public are most likely NEVER released from prison.Considering the issue at hand in this thread, I believe that if this rapist was released for good behavior than he was most likely deemed a minimal threat to society by the justice system. As a society, we have to put a certain measure of faith in the decisions made by the people who incarcerate our criminals. By doing so, we acknowledge that although we may feel a measure of discontent or fear with the decision to release certain convicts from prison, it is an inevitability that some of these people WILL be let out. Therefore, we as a society must understand that the decision to let someone out has been deliberated over greatly before it is made, and it (hopefully) will not be made carelessly as to endanger the lives of law-abiding citizens.
