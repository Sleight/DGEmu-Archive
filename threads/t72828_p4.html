If I am to base my measurements of storyline alone (main point of an RPG is that you play a role in a decent story), then...Well. Lunar's been done before - it's pretty much a remake - so its storyline loses a few points for originality. It's still very stable, nonetheless - guy wants to become a dragonmaster, trial and hardship await him - a working formula. A bit overdone, but that's because it works. People like us dig it, even though we've seen it for the nth time already.Now, Golden Sun.... Alone, those two don't have that much of a remarkable story, but together... Well, in the beginning Isaac's group is right - lighting lighthouses = destruction. In the end Felix's group is right - not lighting lighthouses = destruction. Who do you believe? Why should you? Which one is the greater danger? The GBA Fire Emblems - the first one had a very solid plot, I like. The second one was kind of a disappointment for me - I prefer secret societies more than all out war among nations. Great games, either way, but the story was fairly basic, unless you wished to add the extra little details which made them great.Pokemon? Story? What? There's one?Okay, that's a bit mean - Team Rocket, Team Aqua and Team Magma have been our solid basis of a story in the past few games. Sometimes it's new and refreshing... most of the time it's a remake of a previous enemy.And something else... Riviera is clearly the most story driven - I mean, come on, even the game-makers divided the game into chapter sections. Is that not a giveaway about how story-centric it is? That's a good thing, of course. Ditto with GS1&2, who's right? Why're they wrong? Stuff. If I was to rate games individually, Fire Emblem: Rekka no Ken and Riviera: the Promised Land would be among the top (such a close call, I'd just call it a tie.). But if we're to use collective power, the Golden Sun series would win.
