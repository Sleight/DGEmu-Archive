There may be other 2 or 3 alternative explanations to that nickname. The first one is about the bidding to 1933's World's Fair between Chicago and New York, where Chicago would eventually win. Around that time, the Chicago Tribune newspaper wrote an article titled "Chicago Dubbed Windy", which led to Charles Dana, an editor working for the New York Sun, mockingly referencing Chicago as the Windy City in their publications.The second one is related to a rivalry held between Chicago and Cincinatti, after Chicago surpassed Cincinatti in the meatpacking trade somewhere during the 1860's, with Cincinatti being very well known in that trade. This rivalry extended throughout the 1860's and the 1870's, eventually being extended to Baseball, where Cincinatti had pretty much the best team at that time, the Red Stockings. Chicago, hoping to defeat them, dubbed their team the White Stockings, which led to Cincinatti newspapers to also mockingly reference Chicago as "Windy City" in their sport sections.The third one relates to politics, due to the beliefs that Chicago's political style was referred to as a Spectator Sport, as in "not a really serious business". In that matter, Windy City was considered to be a reference to the city politicians that directly blew or did a poor job in their duties.
