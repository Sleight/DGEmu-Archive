SSleightblindDec 09 2011, 05:54 PM
Excellent. This is a long list, I know, but most of it is menu navigation. Click the windows icon in the bottom left. Click control panel. Click Network and Internet. Click Network and Sharing Center. Click change adapter settings (on the left) There should be a list of internet adapters you have, at least one of which should be wireless. Right click that, and click Properties at the bottom. A window should pop up with a big checkbox list in the middle. In that list are two items: internet Protocol version 6 and Internet Protocol version 4. Click one to highlight it, then click the properties button that lights up. Another window will pop up. The should be two sets of radio buttons (the little circle buttons), and both should be set to "Obtain ... address automatically." If they are, good, close that window, click the other Internet Protocol version, and do the same. If not, switch it. EDIT: Damn it Kui.

Cat in the Hat' timestamp='1323470763' post='4981264 wrote:

Was that thing an optic fiber switch or something? It's way overpriced if it's an ordinary ethernet switch. Hell you can find them for $20 that suits your needs perfectly by looking around a bit. On the subject hub or switch, for your needs there is little to no difference, but because the price of a switch is about the same of a hub, I would go with a switch. Hubs just repeats the network traffic from one signal to several devices, while a switch is a bit more careful with data and sorts it out before sending. It might sound paradoxal, but in the end the switch is faster. Overly simplified tech talk:To elaborate on that: because hubs mindlessly repeat the data, some data might be sent to the wrong device, which causes a lot more traffic, because the device and your router now have to sort out what data is whose. Add up that the data might also collide with other data, which causes corruption. Thus, hubs can make a mess of a network in no-time.
