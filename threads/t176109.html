<!--coloro:#654321--><span style="color:#654321"><!--/coloro-->Wasn't No$gbc called NO$GMB?If you're talking about the NO$GMB (windows or dos version), yes, it's one of the best GB/GBC emulators, but the GBC emulation is shareware (only works for 5 minutes, unless you register -or crack- it).At least that was what used to happen 10 years ago (when I used that emulator).It supports linking between two copies of the same game (rename one of the *.sav files to *.sa2, load the game, go to options, enable 2 GB emulation, enable linking, and there it goes).only Gameboy Color emulation was shareware.Most of old Gameboy Color games would run under Super Gameboy (a small cartridge that was attached to your SNES console to play GB games) emulation (supported by NO$GMB, freeware).TGB is nice cause it allows you to link between two different games (like, linking Pokemon Red to Pokemon Blue or The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time with The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Seasons), while No$gmb only allowed to link between two copies of the same game (Pokemon Red with Pokemon Red OR Pokemon Blue with Pokemon Blue).Again, I'm a but out-dated on those emulators (don't know about their current Compatibility list).And, like it was suggested before, I recommend linking to the Emulator's homepage instead of hosting the emulators themselves...Pro: not much need to worry about updates (newer versions)Con: noobs might not be able to understand how to surf through the Emu's Homepage to download the emulator (yeah... might be the reason why PlaySwiss want to host them here).A few other emulators I used to use:Genesis/Mega Drive:KGen98GenecystMeka (also has a good multi console support, similar to Gens)For Famicon/Nintendo Entertainment System I used to use a few emulators (two for *.nes files and the other for *.fam files).NES were called Nesticle and Nesten.
