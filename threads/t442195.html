We are all only human, and to be human is to be biased. Even people who act or attempt to act objectively will accidentally or purposely inject some of their own bias into it. We are flawed, imperfect, and as long as there's anything that can be opened up to interpretation, bias will exist. Let's put it another way. A teacher teaching World History won't be biased. There's nothing to debate (for the sake of argument), there were civilizations that existed, etc, and there can be no bias when reporting fact.Choice based lessons are more difficult. I wouldn't be so worried about bias as I would weak-mindedness. In a choice based scenario, only a complete idiot would swallow what a teacher tells hook, line and sinker. Indoctrination only works as far as the indoctrinated are willing to blindingly accept it. Did you make that distinction? Congratulations, you prevented indoctrination.Then again, I fear for the day when academia on a university level is filled with so many who are so easily indoctrinated.PS- Irony-- don't you think it's ironic that an article talking about indoctrination is written? You can choose to believe it or not. But if you buy it 100%, you've been indoctrinated, by an article that's anti-indoctrination.
