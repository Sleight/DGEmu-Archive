EEnhasaJan 01 2007, 09:54 PM
I really enjoy the real-time strategy games and the city-builders. Assuming that you have a decent computer, here are some of my top recommendations:Civilization IV: The best Civilization game to date, and that's saying a lot! Fans of the earlier games will notice many differences, such as an improved, more intuitive game-play. Graphics have also received a much-needed upgrade. Although Civilization games have always allowed players to customize the game mechanics, Civilization IV takes this ability to a new level.Pharaoh: This is an older game, so it might be difficult to find, but it's worth the hunt. These days, all of them come with the Cleopatra expansion pack. Pharaoh is an Egyptian city-builder. You begin by leading a small tribe in predynastic Egypt and progress to ever-increasing responsibility. Your main responsibilities are to provide food, industry, military defense, and to keep the gods happy. You will also need to build monuments for the dead. To top it all off, the game has an excellent soundtrack, which is rare for a city-builder.SimCity 4: I've been a fan of this series ever since the first game, and this one is no exception. Unlike the earlier games, which focused on the activity of a single city, this one allows entire regions of cities to interact. You can also interact with individual Sims to get feedback on their lives and neighborhoods. This game is a lot more challenging than the previous versions, because it not only costs money to build, but it costs money to maintain most things. So no more building a huge expanse of parks. :-) The only drawback is that you need a fairly powerful machine to run it effectively.The Sims 2: This is the one game on my list that really isn't about winning. Even in SimCity, you have a goal of building a thriving metropolis. In The Sims, the focus is on pure creativity. It really is a lot of fun to create families and watch their personalities collide. Novices will have enough to do with the items that are included in the game, while more experienced and artistic players will enjoy creating their own graphics and skins. Unlike the earlier games, there's really no limit to what you can do with the face customization tool. Since there is a huge community of players who like to swap lots and items, you might want to buy a new version so that you can register it.WarCraft III: With the advent of World of WarCraft, few people haven't heard of this series. This game takes place 15 years after the war between humans and Orcs and includes four playable civilizations: Alliance/humans, Orcs, Undead, and Night Elves. WarCraft III incorporates some RPG elements, such as an improved story and the need to gain experience. The game also has beautifully rendered maps and cutscenes. If you like this game, don't forget to buy the Frozen Throne Expansion Set.
