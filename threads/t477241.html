KKirie MotobaJul 27 2012, 11:26 PM
Not the same.

My desktop has 12GB of RAM, and I got 2 160GB Intel SSDs on RAID0...
Doesn't change anything. The method used to store data in normal memory, ReadyBoost memory, and virtual memory on hard disk is different.

RAM, being directly accessible and dedicated for that, will be fast, and especially since the data bus is right next to where your CPU fetches info, it's easier.
ReadyBoost is the same principle as virtual memory, except on a faster media device such as a USB stick.
Virtual memory is usually used to swap out data that doesn't fit into the RAM or doesn't require it to be stored there.

Also, when you have SSDs as your primary drive, it is not recommended to store TEMP files to it, not virtual memory. The constant writing shortens your SSD's life span, and if you have a lower end SSD, multiple threads of random writes will choke the drive and cause your system to stutter. Even on a RAID0 I notice it (very noticeable when I'm using a screen recorder on 1080p resolution, and record to my SSD, it doesn't keep up and system lags, even with a RAID0. Change to my secondary regular hard disk, everything is smooth)
