Go figure, they made a successful game, so now they have the money to hire outside talent. I could have just as easily listed the "Penumbra" series, which Frictional made before "Amnesia".

 

As for your last sentence, that's the point. Games made by non-AAA companies have the ability to be more innovative because they are not as likely to be beholden to corporate interests or other limitations on creativity. They don't need approval from publishers - if they're lucky, a publisher will pick up their game, but unlike with mainstream titles, they can try something new without first having to secure capital. Granted, this is often hard and often unsuccessful. In the case of "Amnesia", as evidenced by this blog post, Frictional was basically banking their continued existence on the game being successful. It clearly was, so now they have some freedom from the model of developing each game with the intent to earn enough to make the next one.

 

That's not to say that a non-AAA game will thus be innovative. But at the same time, when making a game with a limited budget, a developer has a better chance of striving for innovation than striving for cutting-edge graphics, which is why you get a lot of stellar indie titles that use a "retro" style (like Cave Story, Minecraft, Braid, Limbo, etc.).
