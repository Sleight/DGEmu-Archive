The question seems a little ambiguous. There are a few ways of reading it.A) "Three coins are flipped. They are then flipped again. Then they are, once again, flipped. What are the odds of getting at least two out of three coins showing heads on at least one of these sets of three flips?"First, we get the chance of getting 2 or 3 heads on one set of flips. We can get this quickly! There are 8 possibilities. 2 or 3 H is the same as 0 or 1 T. 1 way of getting 0 T, 3 ways of getting 1 T. So this gives us 4/8 = 50% chance of getting 2+ heads on one set of flips.To get the chance of getting 2+ heads AT LEAST ONCE in 3 repetitions, just find the probability of NEVER getting 2+ heads, and subtract that from 1. After all, those are the only two possibilities - either you see 2+ heads at least once, or you never do. So that's 1 - (1/2 * 1/2 * 1/2) = 7/8.So you'll see 2+ heads in at least one batch of 3 coins on seven out of eight repetitions of this scenario, on average.B) "Same as above, but what are the odds of getting at least two out of three coins showing heads on ALL of these sets of flips?"This is just like A, except in the last step, we use 1/2 * 1/2 * 1/2 - the chance of all 3 sets showing 2+ heads.
