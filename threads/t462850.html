Yeah, it's going to be a problem that we're going to have to deal with in our lifetime, but some of these problems may be averted, such as the energy crisis. I say this because solar power is getting more and more efficient every year, and many have said that within a decade solar power will become a viable, cost effective power source. Also, without sounding too optimistic, I think the ITER will also lead to a clean, stable source of power before our current resources used for power become too scarce to the point of inciting wars.Obviously there's the issue of cars. Even most hybrids are still reliant on gasoline, but with the increasing density of major cities and the ever increasing growth rate of our population, I can definitely see mass transit becoming a primary mode of transportation for a larger percentage of people within the next generation.For example, many people were I live use mass transportation almost exclusively, because driving a car is just too much of a hassle to get anywhere in a reasonable amount of time. So, my city is putting more and more focus on expanding the mass transit system to extend further into the suburban areas, since the road traffic here is nothing short of frustrating as it is.As far as not enough jobs, it's not further population growth that's doing the most harm, it's the mechanization of industrialized nations. Many factorizes are at least partially automated now, and even checkout lines at supermarkets are becoming automated. Machines are only going to continue to replace humans simply because they don't need to be paid, can work 24/7, and are more efficient. That, in turn, will expand certain hi-tech career fields, but will obsolete the more menial jobs of humans.As for schooling, why not just use home computers? Many legit colleges do so now, and kids are learning how to use computers as young as four years old, so it only makes sense that this is going to happen at some point.As for expansion, taller buildings and so-called arcology megastructures could be built, Japan has been toying with the idea for several years now. Hell, they've already built an artificial island, and of course there's Dubai. There was also an architect whose name I forget who proposed building underwater cities and oceanscrapers-- buildings where the top will act as a forest preserve/airport, while the rest is submerged and is powered by the water surrounding it.As far as pollution goes, like I said, when we eventually convert to fusion and solar power and utilize mass transit even moreso than now, that will be a great start. From there, eventually those power sources will be used in personal vehicles.Of course, by the time overpopulation becomes a real problem, 50-75 years from now, technology will have progressed so much that pretty much anything is possible including the beginning of space colonization, brain-computer interfacing which would eliminate the need for traditional education, and pocket sized cold fusion reactors, so eh, who really knows.tl;dr post is tl;dr
