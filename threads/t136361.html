Castlevania! Yessir!Wow... I've been a massive fan of the series since way back in '86.But yet I'm gonna go against the trend, and present the opinion that Symphony of the Night was awful.As a matter of fact, it marked the death of the series. Ever since that crap came out, Castlevania games have been lame.Games are all about challenge - and the bottom line is that SOTN doesn't provide any at all.It's an overwhelmingly easy / mainstream game, designed for casual gamers and boring RPG & Pokemon kinda people.That special kind of breed who believe that collecting pointless items and killing enemies for experience points is 'fun'.They are mistaken. It takes no skill whatsoever in looping an area over and over again and levelling up your character to near-invincibility.The whole game is a fraud: it fools you into believing that you are achieving something, whereas in fact you are doing nothing more than wasting your time.It true: you have to try REALLY hard to die in that game... It was made by, and for, beginners.It might be a little more interesting with the secret 'luck' code, but it still doesn't rescue it from monotony...You know the one: input your name as "X-X!V''Q" at the start of a game, and your energy, strength, and power are seriously reduced, and the 'luck' ratio is increased to 99. This not only makes the game a lot more challenging - it also makes a lot of those irritating 'Rare' items easier to get.Still, it doesn't rescue the game from abject tedium in the long run: all you have do is wonder around a set of predictable and repetitive environments at your leisure, picking up a series of pre-set items to unlock doors - this while occasionally being forced to listen to some laughably bad dialogue, like "Die Evil One! You Don't belong in this world!" etc. etc.Balls. If you claim to be even vaguely interested in games, then you know it's all about challenge and pure gameplay, NOT a series of stats and items.So stick to the arcade-stlye ones: the original Castlevania is a bona fide classic of course - and part III is easily the best one of the early series (even the director of SOTN names it as his personal favourite).The PC Engine Dracula X also gets full marks, despite also being a little too easy...And for the curious, I thought that the N64 ones weren't bad at all, while the Mega Drive 'Castlevania Bloodlines' is by far the most under-rated entry in the series.Forget the GBA ones - they are just cheap and predictable cash-ins on the mainstream success of SOTN, and are all nearly identical.The PS2 and XBOX ones are a complete sham also, and have little or nothing to do with what traditional 2D Castlevania is all about.So here we go: the no-nonsense 'Top 5' (RPG lovers need not apply)1) - Castlevania III - Dracula's Curse (NES) One for the experts... Just you try getting through the second loop - it's an extraordinary challenge!2) - Akumajo Dracula X - Chi no Rondo (PC Engine) A great package, and the hight of the PC Engine's career3) - Super Castlevania IV (SNES) A classic of its time - a little slow at times, but full marks in the atmosphere stakes4) - Castlevania Bloodlines (MD) Give this a go in EXPERT mode - turns out it's one of the best Mega Drive games ever made!5) - Castlevania - Legacy of Darkness (N64) Seriously, give it a try... There's more to the game than you think!Phew!
