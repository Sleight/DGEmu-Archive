The idea of an auxillary language is a good one and to unite everyone by having a single common language would be better for cultural values.
How do you believe imposing such an artificial barrier on the ability of peoples to express and communicate cultural ideas and thoughts would help enhance their cultural values in any way at all? Most research I have seen into the impacts of such an approach have expressed that it has highly negative impacts on the people involved. A good example would be the Maori in New Zealand, where based on research into the effects of forcing English upon them there is an initiative to restore the Maori language.

Ghassan Hage has done a lot of research on this very topic, in particular with his book White Nation: Fantasies of white supremacy in a multicultural society. His work is quite interesting and focusses on issues like multiculturalism, racism, nationalism and cultural identity. Living in Australia himself, a lot of his research is based on the debates there. One concept he uses is 'governmental citizenship', which he asserts the groups involved in the debate are fighting over. It is not as he points out primarily an issue of racism (although racism is part of it) but rather of nationalism with each group desiring the power and authority to shape and define the national identity of Australia. I would thus challenge your assertion that it is those coming to Australia who are the arrogant ones. When it is in fact those who are fighting over the position of 'governmental citizenship' who are actively depriving them of their status of person hood.
