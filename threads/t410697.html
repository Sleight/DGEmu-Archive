How to identify legitimate GBA cartridges

Don't be fooled by counterfeits!

Often times on eBay (and sometimes resell stores) you may, unfortunately, come across a GBA game that is fake. It may look legit, but fakes have a few ways that they can always be identified.

Surefire way:
Tilt up the cartridge and look at the circuit board (the label should be face up). You may need some light to help see in there. On the top of the circuit board, you should see NINTENDO in white letters. If you do, you're 100% legit. If you don't see it, then it's fake.

Alternate ways (hit-or-miss):
On fresh legit carts, you'll always see an imprint on the face of the cart. On GBA carts, there will be at least a 2 digit number on there, sometimes followed by a letter. The letter is important for in regards to telling you which version of a game you have (no letter is 1.0, A = 1.1, B = 1.2, etc). The lack of numbers doesn't mean though that it's fake - they can wear out. But if you do see it, you're more often then not fine.

Sometimes, the serial can give it away. I've seen some bad fakes that complete the serial incorrectly. First, all GBA serials are in the following format: AGB-XXXX-XXX. AGB is the code for Game Boy Advance. The XXXX in the center is the game's serial. You can look this up at a site like Pocketheaven to make sure this is correct. Mistakes I've seen include not only incorrect serials, but serials that don't have enough letters (I saw a copy of Lilo and Stitch that only had a 3 letter serial!). The XXX at the end is to indicate region. This part isn't important though, but you should at least see 3 letters, like USA, EUR, EUU, JPN, etc.

And last, if you have a triwing screwdriver, you can remove the screw in the back of the cart and open the cart. This will make the surefire way easier to see. You can also look to see if the game has what I dub the "BATTERY OF DOOM!" Most games on GBA don't require batteries, but a few older ones did (they used SRAM, which was battery backed up. This save type was later replaced with FeRAM and the battery was no longer needed). The only other thing that required a battery were games with Real Time Clocks, like the later Pokemon games. Most often the battery is encased in a yellow rubber ring in fake carts, but not always. But games that don't use SRAM or RTC shouldn't have a battery.

For images of bootlegs compared to real carts of various save types, check out this link, courtesy of Pocketheaven's Wiki.

I hope this is informative for you. If you have any questions or need help determining if your cart is legit, please feel free to contact me. But this guide, if properly followed, should be able to tell you whether or not your cart is legit.

Also, if you get a fake cart, don't lie there and take it, get your money back and report the seller! Feel free to let us here on DGEmu know too. Contact me of sellers that sold you a fake cart (please include an image proof if possible) and I'll add it to this thread. Kinda like a wall of shame.
