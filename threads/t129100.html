I have been the victim of an expensive mistake. Last night I tried to put FlashMe on my new DS Lite. As I started the SL1 connection part, it reached 1% and then shut off. I did nothing to the battery, AC adaptor, or the power switch. According to several boards, the problem lies with the way the DS Lite is manufactured. There are two known possibilities: you are tripping something you shouldn't or you are overloading the power thus triggering an automatic shutdown. The only way to fix this is with soldering. This problem has arisen in the latest batches of DS Lites so be aware of the risks associated with putting FlashMe on your DS Lite before attempting it.PreventionSome managed to flash their 3rd gen NDSLite by protecting a side of the SL1 hole with a piece of paper, covering parts that mustn't be shorten.That worked for them, but it's still risky.Once again, you should look at forums on supercard and flashme for the details.~~~Information provided by the user "mine".SolutionThere is a method. You can use a NDS Lite which had been flashme. Just like fix BIOS on PC motherboard.Example:A mean a good NDS Lite which had been flashme.B mean a bad NDS Lite which had been flashme fail.Step:1. Unplug the B.Bios from B. Unplug the A.Bios from A, and insert A.Bios to B.2. Open power of B. Run FlashMe program and waiting...3. Unplug the A.Bios from B, and insert B.Bios to B, keep power on.4. Press X B X B let FlashMe program continue.5. Yes. You fix the B.Bios now.Further information in regards to the solution: DS Lite Bricking Solution~~~Tip-off provided by the user "mine".

Don't panic!From what I have gathered after a lot of trawling for information, the flashing 'danger' is entirely a physical thing to watch for while shorting SL1 during the flashing process, not a "Nintendo has changed the firmware so we brick our lites" issue as some paranoid people would have you believe. To get a better idea of what to watch out for, take a good look at the pics on this Japanese site :http://higurashi.asablo.jp/blog/2006/04/16/This clearly shows there is a piece of metal on the back cover of the lite that is close to the edge of the SL1 hole. Apparently if you touch this, the DS resets. Not a good thing if you have just started flashing. Take a good look at the pics and just be aware what not to touch while flashing.Luckily the clever authors of flashme have ensured that even if your DS resets while flashing, there is a good chance you can re-flash because the very first thing written is a clever piece of recovery code. for details check this link : http://www.dspassme.com/FlashMe.shtmlor the dgemu post http://www.dgemu.com/forums/index.php?showtopic=94957Here's a snippet :> RECOVERY MODE> NOTE: Make sure there is NOTHING inserted into the DS slot. This includes a game or PassMe1/2!> First, write flashme.nds to your media and insert it into the GBA slot.> Next, hold SELECT+START+A+B all at the same time and turn your DS on.> The DS will boot directly to the GBA slot and from here, you can launch flashme.nds (If it isn't already ?> started) and install it again.If you reset it before the recovery code is written (really bad luck) you can still recover the lite by swapping the part-flashed firmware chip inside (it's socketed!) with another lite. I've read somewhere you can hot-swap the chip and re-flash the dead one. Sorry I can't find the link anymore, but I've read it in more than one place.In any case, unless it was an import, you could always just return a bricked unit to where you bought it if it's under warranty. Most places would have no idea this kind of stuff is possible and would just hand you another.Go on - what have you got to lose!FLASHME!!!BTW flashme is recommended if you're going to run homebrew in any case, 'cos badly written code can brick your DS anyway...Have fun! >=( R
