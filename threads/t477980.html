MmouseORIGINAL POSTOct 13 2012, 08:48 PM
At least, I'm enjoying it more than I did Diablo III. I haven't played online yet, though, primarily because I don't like playing with randoms and because NOBODY ELSE IS PLAYING IT. Well, almost nobody.

Sure, it suffers from a shortish campaign, much like Diablo III, but once you beat it for the first time, you unlock an extra area where you can play randomly generated maps with different buff/debuff rules for yourself and monsters, and with a random boss at the end of each one. It doesn't add anything to the story, but if you're hankering some lootin', it's pretty filling.

And sure, it doesn't use Diablo III's pioneering skill development method, where you unlock things as you level and can pick and choose what you want. Instead, it offers such a wide variety with just four classes that you can make a dozen characters and play each one differently. Each class has 21 active skills and 9 passive skills, and chances are you won't use more than a third of them with a given character. And even though you can't totally respec a character, you have to be trying pretty hard to break one.

All that aside, the game just feels more fun to me. The challenge level scales just right. And the characters are fun to play, whether I'm burninating everything with my embermage or mowing down mobs with my crit-tastic berserker. And to think, if I had saved the $60 I spent on Diablo III, I could have used it to buy four copies of Torchlight II and given three away to friends.

I started with a berserker and tried not to worry too much about wasted skill points. Turns out you can get by with just Savage Rush and Battle Standard with all the other points going into passive skills.

Next, I made an embermage, and I plotted out which skills I was going to use. Along the way, I found that some were not as awesome as I suspected, so I went with others. I doubt I have the optimal build, and I'm willing to bet that some of the skills I overlooked would be extremely useful, but I've gotten her to level 60 or so and I feel like I've got a viable survival strategy.

Now I'm working on an outlander, and while I did try to plan out his skills, I'm being a little more lenient than I was with the embermage. I've got a few I plan to focus on, but I might try out a few others as I go. Luckily, you can respec the last three skill points you spent, so if you unlock a new skill and quickly find you don't like it, you can get that point back.
