Sony Index What is an ISO?An ISO is an "image" of a compact disc's information.ISO stands for "International Standards Organization", and such files also come with other extensions and formats (bin/cue, img/ccd, mds/mdf).As simply stated as possible, an ISO is to games on compact discs, what a ROM is to games that come in cartridge format.What is a torrent, how does it work, and how can I use it to get ISOs?Definitions for the explanation.torrent = the actually file that you find on the tracker/website and use to download what you want in the clientBT Client = the program that is used to open up the .torrent file and download the final fileFinal File = the whole reason you are using BT, the file or files that you will ultimately have when all the pieces are downloaded.Pieces = in peer to peer (P2P) programs such as kazaa and limewire the entire final file is downloaded from a single person so you have to wait for that one person to get back on if they log off leaving without a complete final file, but in BT the final file has been broken down into pieces so that it can be downloaded from multiple people across the BT networks. The number and size of pieces can range based on the size of the final file.BT = BitTorrentP2P = peer to peer networking enabling regular people to directly connect and share files that they otherwise would not be able to.BT is a P2P style network that allows users to download files that other members have. In order to download off of the BT network you need to download one of the BT clients (refer to next question for clients), and install it. Then you can download the .torrent file on one of the many currently active torrent trackers (major ones listed in section III) to begin torrenting the games that you want. How fast the download finishes depends on 3 main factors; seeds, leeches, and your connection. The seeds are people that have the entire file downloaded and are "seeding" it so that other people can also finish torrenting the file, the more seeds, the more likely you are to receieve a complete final file. Leeches are the people that are also downloading and have not reached 100% of the final file, think of them as your competition for the pieces from the seeders. Therefore, the higher the ratio of seeders to leachers the better. The final factor is your connection. It does not matter if you are connected to 20 seeds, if you are on dial up you will most likely get a slower download than a person who has broadband and only 4 seeds.What are the Bit Torrent Clients and what do they do?All BT clients stem in one way or another from the original Bit Torrent and are the programs that allow you to download off the BT network. After you find the .torrent of the file you want to download you load it up into the client, the client will then create a placeholder file where you have specified (basically its goes ahead and creates the final file on the hard drive but you can't actually use it). It will then connect to the tracker and begin to swap information with the other computers that are currently active on that particular torrent file.All clients are different in one way or another (options and designs mostly) but you need to remember 3 things1. All clients are free2. All clients have the same connection and the same network, one is not any better than the next in capabilities.3. The client that you use should be the one that you like the best, not the one that some else says is the best, it is a matter of preference. Bbooyah_boyDec 30 2005, 10:20 AM
A "part" file is precisely what it sounds like: it is a part of the whole.This is a common enough practice when attempting to distribute large files, and one you should become accustomed to. In short, the single large file is split into smaller pieces, each of which is then archived, and thus, able to be sent in less time to various parties. Once you have downloaded all of the parts, simply highlight/select and dearchive all of them (WinRAR will do such automatically). Your parts will then be compiled into the single file you sought after.Best of luck...
