I decided, since the last "review" thread starter got banned, that I'd start giving some quick notes and reviews on the topics I've been posting every Monday in my signature. Plus it'll help me keep track of which ones I have and haven't posted.

This week, I've updated the links for my Fire Emblem Pack. This pack includes all ROMs for the Fire Emblem series and a pack of scans for the Japan-only Customizable Card Game based on the games.

The one thing that kills me about this series is the permanent death. If a character dies in battle, you cannot revive them. They remain dead for the rest of the game. This annoys some, but what it really does is force you to pay a lot more attention to the game and to be more careful who you use. Every level must be played with the knowledge that you may need to sacrifice a troop to get all of the objectives you want. You may need to play through levels repeatedly if you want to keep all of your troops alive. This was a new concept for me when I played Fire Emblem on GBA. I loved the game, but damn if I didn't have to play the levels over and over to keep from losing anyone.

Some people find that off putting and let it keep them from the series, but do not let that happen. The games are fun, challenging, and offer some decent stories too.

I suggest starting at the GBA game. There are translation patches for the earlier ROMs (and the pack includes some fan translated ROMs), but the GBA game is just polished enough to make it perfect in my opinion and the Sacred Stones game added even more with the ability to "grind" through a tower level repeatedly, allowing you to raise your characters outside of the storyline.

Of couse, I'm sure some of you will suggest otherwise and I'd love to hear it.

PPan Dimensional BeingSep 11 2012, 12:37 AM
This week I'm using an older ISO I posted a while back, the first Ape Escape game. Years later and this game is still fun.

If you've never played Ape Escape, where the **** have you been? The premise is some scientist creates a helmet to gives apes more intellect. An albino ape uses this new found intellect to travel through time and try to change the past so that apes rules the world over humans.

You use non-lethal means to capture all of the time traveling hench-monkeys and send them back to the present. As you go you get new gadgets to add to your arsenal.

This was the first game to require a dual shock controller and it uses the dual joysticks well. There was a PSP remake that worked okay enough, but without the second joystick, it just wasn't the same.

If you like silly platformers that get more challenging as they progress, download this and give it a shot.

I've played a couple of the sequels too. They add some extra gadgets and gimmicks, but they are really more of the same. It's still entertaining, but this first one is still the best.
