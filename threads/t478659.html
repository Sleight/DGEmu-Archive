How I make my asian style chicken

 

-use a boneless cut of chicken (breast or boneless thighs work. Keep in mind that white meat (breasts) has a different taste and texture than dark meat)

-cut into bite size pieces. Bigger is better for beginners because it's easier to avoid overcooking

-before you start cooking mix your sauce. I don't measure because it depends on your own tastes, but my main ingredients are:

 

 -salt (soy sauce, oyster sauce, or fish sauce. play it safe and go with soy. I prefer to use dark soy sauce as it gives the meat a nicer color and you use less since it's more concentrated than regular soy. make sure you're using a good soy sauce, don't use some cheap generic crap that's basically water + salt + black color)

-sour (I usually use a small amount of rice vinegar)

-spicy (My favorite hot sauce for asian cooking is Sriracha, but you can also use crushed sambal chilies or whatever pepper you want. please don't use shit like lousiana or frank's red hot sauce)

- sweet (I usually use brown sugar, you can use whatever. If you want to make teriyaki you can use fruit marmalade or juice)

Like I said, amounts are based on your preference, if you like sweet, use a lot of sugar, if you like spicy use a lot of pepper.

 

-Before you start cooking make sure your pan or wok is very hot. I like to throw in one piece of chicken and wait for it to start sizzling loudly in the oil as a temperature gauge. Make sure you use vegetable oil or something with a high smoke point, olive oil has a low burning temperature and burnt olive oil tastes nasty, stir fry type food is meant to be cooked on high heat and olive oil can't handle it. If you're using a frying pan make sure it's nonstick or else your sauce will burn. Make sure your chicken is completely dry too, If it's wet it won't brown properly.

 

-once your non-stick surface is hot and has heated oil in it, dump in your chicken. Make sure all of your chicken is touching the surface of the pan and spread out, no piggybacks or piled up chicken. spread your chicken along the outside of the pan, if it's all piled up in the middle it'll boil rather than brown.

-once your meat is spaced out properly don't touch them. let them sit and cook for a couple of minutes. flip one piece over and if its underside is nice and brown (brown, not black. black = burnt) then flip all of your chicken pieces. if you know how, just give your pan/wok a toss. if you're a noob use a spatula or something. Keep going until both sides of every piece is browned

-once your chicken is cooked about 85%-95% lower the heat then throw in your sauce. Make  sure all your chicken is coated nicely and let the sauce cook down. I like mine to be completely dry and to form a glaze on the chicken. If you like excess sauce wait for the sauce to cook down some then throw in some water.

 

there you go. I usually serve mine with fried or steamed rice or chow mein noodles cooked with vegetables. Now you can enjoy asian styled chicken without all the salt, MSG, and grease that you get with most chinese/japanese restaurants. This is just a quick run down and the basics. I could go into a lot more detail but I doubt any of you are actually going to try this. If you want to be a fat piece of crap then you can go the extra mile of deep frying your chicken in flour and then cooking it in sauce. Enjoy your fat.

Grape jelly meatballs, anyone?

 

Ingredients: 

 

1. 12 oz. of Heinz Chili Sauce

2. 16 oz. of Grape Jelly

3. Meatballs (About 40)

 

Directions:

1. Pour 12. oz of Heinz Chili Sauce and 16 oz. of Grape Jelly into a large crock pot.

2. Put the crock pot on high, and let the two settle, mixing occasionally until they do. (This takes approximately 20 minutes)

3. When the sauce is consistent, add the meatballs - continue to cook on high, stirring occasionally and coating the meatballs evenly. Do this until the meatballs are fully cooked - an hour to an hour and a half should do.

4. When finished, leave crock pot on warm and serve.
