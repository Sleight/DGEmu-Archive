SHOULD a propensity to commit rape be considered a mental illness? Debate has been raging, and now it seems that "paraphilic coercive disorder" will not make its debut in the next edition of psychiatry's diagnostic handbook.In February 2010, PCD was proposed for inclusion in the fifth edition of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5), scheduled for publication in 2013. An expert group convened by the American Psychiatric Association (APA) argued that PCD was distinct from sexual sadism, and applied to men who experienced intense fantasies or urges involving sexual coercion over a period of at least six months, if this caused them significant distress or impairment or had led them to force sex on "three or more non-consenting persons on separate occasions".Critics disputed evidence that PCD is a distinct mental disorder. It's a "wastebasket category", argues psychologist Raymond Knight at Brandeis University in Waltham, Massachusetts. The debate became highly charged, because many US states have laws that allow sex offenders who have served prison sentences to be detained indefinitely in a secure hospital if they are deemed dangerous "sexual predators".A ruling from the US Supreme Court states that this can be done only if they have a mental disorder that makes them likely to commit further sexually violent acts.At present, many of those held under these laws have a vague diagnosis of "paraphilia(not otherwise specified)", which is coming under legal challenge.The DSM-5 website now says PCD is being considered for inclusion in the manual's appendix, rather than as a new diagnosis - a designation intended for conditions that need more research.The APA had not responded to requests for a rationale behind the change of plan as New Scientist went to press.Source: http://www.newscientist.com/article/mg2102...ge-to-rape.html
So, who here thinks rapists should be medicated?Tricky situation here. On the one hand, you could keep "dangerous sexual predators" locked up in a secure hospital and medicate them. Treat them until they lose their urge to rape. (Yes, that's oversimplifying things, I know). The plus points are myriad. In the early stages, we might be able to learn more about the mind of a rapist when they're detained. Should we be able to "treat" rapists in the future, you have a situation where you can incarcerate, treat and release once the punishment is done with no fear of re-offending. Punishment is entirely optional.On the other hand, we're a society that likes to punish people for wrong doing. Doesn't matter if it inevitably ends up being the wrong thing to do, we like to punish. And I'm not being sarcastic there. Punishing people is a good thing. It's good for social morale, it sends a clear message to the offender and to those who may think about offending. Yes, the punishment could send their lives into a downward spiral that inevitably leads them to more crime, but as a society punishment is the thing we must do. On the plus side, we're not treating these people like guinea pigs. In the early stages of such a scheme, they'll be subjected to a lot of experiments and experimental drugs. In essence, if rapists are treated as people with mental disorders, we are inevitably taking away their human rights and turning them into human guinea pigs, detained indefinitely with no legal recourse to escape. All they can do is "get better", which is subject to a doctor's opinion.I personally don't know where to sit on this one. The current system has good things going for it, as does any potential new system where rapists are treated as "insane". Yet they both have potentially nasty cons.

Women can rape too, and there are serial rapists who are of the female gender out there.It seems to me that PCD is brushing the blame off of the rapists for raping. In essence, it is not their fault for raping because their urges and fantasies were too much to handle. Will they run tests on rapists to see how they react to certain things? What will these tests be?Over 90% of rape is committed by a person or people the victim knows, including family members. Mental health disorders do appear in the family history and can be passed on to the offspring. They can genuinely not be controlled and need medication to help ease the impact of whatever the disorder is causing. I don't think the decision to rape can be passed on to the offspring through genes. PCD could potentially be a symptom for another mental illness/disorder, possibly Sexual Sadism Disorder (or a variant of it). If the rapist is displaying increasing behaviors of sadism and there are valid fears that, if released, the rapist would commit more gruesome crimes involving some mutilation, then s/he should be institutionalized after serving a portion of the sentence.
