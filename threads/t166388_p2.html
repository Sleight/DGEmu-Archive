MMithrilFoxSep 15 2007, 12:08 PM
I thought I should clarify my comments a little.If you like tactical RPGs, FFXII: Reverent Wings is OK. The reason I was so horribly disappointed is because it had Square's name on it (oh, excuse me, "SquareEnix"). That and the fact that it wasn't labeled "tactics." I personally hate tactical RPGs in general, so it takes a serious special something to make me like it. In FFXIIRW's case, there is nothing very special here. The lack of control over what's going on during the battles makes it seem like you aren't even necessary. For several battles, I left my characters fighting, set the DS down, and came back several minutes later. It's really just too darn easy as long as you keep your levels up. It has a "mission" system. The screen pauses and flashes "Mission [whatever]" across the screen. Then, you're stuck wherever you are for a while playing that "mission." It really hampers any sense of freedom in the game. The music is nothing to write home about. In fact, it's just plain mediocre. And that's the problem. Square is not known for mediocre. If we wanted mediocre, we went for Enix (they were always producing "ok" RPGs). We go to Square for the really good stuff. FFXIIRW is clearly a product from the "Enix" side of "SquareEnix" (I'm only making a joke here, not referring to any programmers or teams). I think the increase in the gaming market, as well as the huge increase in the acceptance and fanbase of RPGs has lead Square to be a little lax in their quality. They know that as long as it's just "ok" and has their label on it, it'll sell. That's unfortunate. Square's fanbase should be careful and smart enough to say "yuck!" to the garbage and "yes!" to the good stuff. I think it all started with FFXII, which was clearly a product of the times and not of pure quality. The lame pre-rendered backgrounds that you were never sure where you could and couldn't go were just lame attempts to take "full advantage" of the hardware they had available to them. Same thing happens with some DS games, where programmers try to force the use of the stylus into some games where it clearly isn't necessary (or even good), all just to "take advantage" of all the features they have in the hardware they are using. If you don't need a stylus to play the game, don't try to force it into it! Goodness, after all, the comptetitor's handheld doesn't even feature a touch screen at all, and it's doing just fine (well, sort of). I highly, highly discourage anyone but the biggest of tactics fans to avoid this title. If you just loved Final Fantasy Tactics, then give this a try. You'll be disappointed with the lack of involvement you have with the battles (read: none), but it's a tactics RPG nonetheless. Hated it. Maybe you won't. Just my opinion.
