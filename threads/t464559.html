Well, I did post the Ten Commandments in my post above. My use of the terms, old, new, original and so forth aren't precisely right. The Ten Commandments that people know very well are usually called the Ethical Decalogue by scholars wishing to differentiate between the other set (the relatively newer set), which they call the Ritual Decalogue. This is primarily because the Ten Commandments that in theory survived were relating to ritual rites.You will notice that the "do not have other Gods before me" commandment in the Ritual set is a lot more violent, calling for the active destruction of other religions. It is not enough to worship God, you must destroy the altars of other gods. Furthermore, in this set, God explicitly states that he will drive out the people from the lands that Moses people wander into, so long as his people follow the commandments. It also actively calls for the sacrifice of all firstborn, excepting firstborn donkeys and sons. At least, that is what I presume he means by saying "The first offspring of every womb belongs to me, including all the firstborn males of your livestock, whether from herd or flock" Exodus 34:19.They are also very different, thus making it very difficult to presume (as traditional Jewish scholars do) that the Old Testament is a single narrative with a single author. Unless God is extremely senile or a liar, the safest bet is to presume that even Exodus itself was written by several different authors and the documents combined to form one. Hence, why the replacement set of Ten Commandments are so wildly different from the first. Judging from the character of the God that gave the Ritual Decalogue matches the wrathful God of the rest of the Old Testament, it is safe to presume that the Ethical Decalogue is a later invention that came after the Ritual Decalogue.However, there is no evidence relating to this viewpoint or to having it the other way round.What I've touched upon here is the Documentary Hypothesis. The Ritual Decalogue is commonly identified with scholars from the Kingdom of Judah. The Ethical Decalogue is commonly identified as having originated in the Kingdom of Israel to the north of Judah. It is thought by supporters of the Documentary Hypothesis that at a later point in time, a redactor combined the two to create the Exodus we know now containing these two sets of Ten Commandments.So this all begs the question of which set, if any, was passed down to Moses. God couldn't possibly pass down the Ethical set and then the Ritual set, saying that they're both the same when anyone who can read can tell they're different. Well, he could, but he couldn't do so without appearing as a liar or incredibly forgetful. If you're fine with having a God that lies to people, then fair enough. If you're fine with having a senile God that can't remember ten simple commandments but expects us to remember them, well, fine. However, if neither option appeals to you, then the only other option is to say that Exodus is the result of two or more different documents being combined as one and that it is primarily a synthetic (i.e. man-made) narrative.However, there is yet another set of Ten Commandments that I haven't even touched upon. They are not Biblical in origin but came from Solon the Athenian and go as follows:1. Trust good character more than promises.2. Do not speak falsely.3. Do good things.4. Do not be hasty in making friends, but do not abandon them once made.5. Learn to obey before you command.6. When giving advice, do not recommend what is most pleasing, but what is most useful.7. Make reason your supreme commander.8. Do not associate with people who do bad things.9. Honor the gods.10. Have regard for your parents.Unlike the Ethical or Ritual decalogue there is only one commandment in this set that is religious in nature and therefore incapable of being upheld in a secular society. Christians can't honour it, because it says "gods". The only people who could honour it are Hindus. However, everything else is fine and relevant to society today. If I could change things, I would make Solon's Commandments far more prevalent and well known than the Biblical set.
