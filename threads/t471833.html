EEndymion_MallornORIGINAL POSTMay 03 2011, 06:28 PM
Okay, I'm going to start off like this. I enjoy writing reviews. Moreover, I enjoy writing useful reviews, and getting rewarded for doing so. That's part of why I like DGEmu, in fact.However!The old system seemed to make sense, that we had per-system user DPs, and daily DPs. When we went to 100/day (thank you for that, btw!), we moved to a single user DP pool. This was, in a word, awesome. So thank you.The only problem is that most new games take more than 10 DP to download, which makes writing a really great review not feel like a great thing. It's a matter of diminishing returns, really. As games get larger and reviews maintain the same numerical value, the value of a review goes down, QED.So, how to solve that problem? Well, there are a few ways, I think. The first is fairly simple - increase the DP per good review. At this point, I suggest 20 as a good number, because, well, games that used to be 5-10Mb are now 16-32. Another way to increase the quality and number of reviews is to "feature" the best reviews on the frontpage, occasionally. It could definitely take DGEmu from just a ROMsite with a community into a thriving gaming website with real features and reviews. I think it would be very nice.So, what do you all think?

EEndymion_MallornMay 04 2011, 05:04 AM
Well, in all honesty, it was written as I was heading out the door - shame on me, I know, but I wanted to get the idea out there. It seemed more reasonable to have the basis out there longer than to wait for it and possibly never have time.As far as how to sort reviews, I'm not exactly sure. Obviously, there is a metric in place which determines which reviews are worth putting up, which are worth DP, and which just get forwarded to the circular file. I would base the front-page reviews on a possibly difficult metric, honestly. I will describe it by a series of "gates", but use whatever metaphor you like for it.The first gate, of course, is the eligibility for DP. If it's not worth DP, it's not worth showing off.The second gate is the game's vote rank. If the game is being reviewed, but other users are saying the game is a prime stinker, then, why show it off?And the third is the most awkward, and requires explanation. Increase in downloads; in short, was there a percentage growth in downloads between the week the review was posted, and the week before? For instance, let's take a good example. A lot of the games review sites loved Ōkami. Now, they made a sequel, Ōkamiden, for the DS and Wii. There's only one problem - it was released on 3/9. Four days later, Pokémon Black & White launched, some of the most looked-forward-to games of Q1 2011. Once again, Ōkami is overshadowed by Nintendo's release. It's a bad sign for the team, but... while I haven't played it, I'm sure it's great.Now, I will say that Pokemon White & Black only have a narrow lead over Okamiden here (lucky), but... if a review of Okamiden went up, that went through it and pointed out how great a game it was, it'd probably get a bunch of extra downloads. So what I'm saying is this.If a game has, say, 10 downloads in the week before a review hits; then a review comes up, and the game gets 50 downloads that week, you put the review on the front page as a featured review.Difficult, but not subjective. Agreed?
