DDarkgustMay 22 2007, 06:00 PM
*cough* The story of Romeo and Juliet is a portrayal of pride and prejudice. Although to the casual observer it simply seems to be an ordinary love story, one who examines closely can see traces of the pressure of society and family on a person, the effects of such pressure, and the alienation of the individual from the society due to such pressure. In Romeo & Juliet, the lovers were seperated due to the long-standing feud that the two families have. One need not look far to know that this feud causes great pressure and frustration to be placed on the two people in question; eventually, however, this pressure causes them to rebel, and as such "fight back" against those who seek to seperate them, in this case the two families. Their death, while romantic, can also be seen as a form of a final knockback, a refusal to bow down to the opression of the society. While the two families are indeed divided as such, the observation of the story being a representation of the divide in the society is quite unsupported. It is important to note that the city was in no way divided or seperated; it is only the families that are locked in a feud. The unrelenting hatred of the two families of each other, while indeed being the cause of the lovers' eventual end, simply does not represent represent the divide of the society as a whole. Instead, it is a one of the forms of pressure of society on the individual, which can be seen even today. ...Yes, I am 15.
