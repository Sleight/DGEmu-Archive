GGelmaxApr 06 2009, 11:55 PM
Well, I'm not sure which answer you're looking for, but there's a few ways. First, the obvious: since almost everything in the night sky is a number of light-years away, the night sky as we see it mostly represents what was there years, centuries, millenia, or even millions of years ago - as opposed to what's up there now. Additionally, though the atmosphere looks clear over short distances, it actually slightly limits how far you can see even when looking straight up, and both blurs and distorts the night sky; thus space telescopes are preferred over ground-based telescopes partially because they can see much farther without all that pesky air in the way. And, of course, there's much that can't be seen (dust clouds, dark matter, etc) and much that is hidden by other things (bright stars hide dimmer stars in the same direction, and large clouds of dust and gas often hide many more stars behind them. And, just in case, it's also because it's not possible to gauge the distances to various stars with the naked eye - two stars may appear to be right next to each other when in fact they're thousands of light-years apart, just because they happen to be in the same direction.
