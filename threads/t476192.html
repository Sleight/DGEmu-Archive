Reviews FAQ
Q: How do I write a Review?
A: From the details page of every game click . Look to the left and you will see a "Post Review" section. Click where is says "Click here" and begin your review.
Please be sure to delete the "Write your review here.." text first or it'll appear in your review
. When you're done, rate the game below and then simply click submit to send it for inspection.

Q: Oops! I made an error. Is there any way to fix/edit my review?
A: Not at the moment. Maybe a future version will allow review inspectors to edit reviews and correct errors for you. Do not worry, nobody demands perfection so if it is a decent review, it will still be accepted.

Q: What is in it for me?
A: If you write a highly exceptional review, you will receive 10 DPs for your efforts. If you don't get DPs, then your only reward is the satisfaction of contributing something to dgemu.

Q: How will I know if my review is approved?
A: Simple. It'll then appear in the reviews section of that particular game. Notice that you will not be informed on whether your review has been approved so check back periodically (typically if your review is not present after 48 hours, then it was probably declined).

Q: How can I check my review after submission?
A: If your review is accepted it'll appear amongst the others for that particular game. The only way to see if it's there is to navigate to the review page. Currently there is no system to track your reviews.

Q: My review was approved, but I did not get any DPs, why?
A: Not all approved reviews will earn you 10 DPs. As briefly mentioned above, only those which are of a highly professional standards are worthy of reward.

Q: I lost Dps!!!
A: Both plagiarism and spamming will not be tolerated. If our review inspectors find out that you've copied from an official review site like GameFaqs or see you just sent garbage, your review will not only get declined, but you will be penalized 10 DPs as well. Please only submit original and meaningful material.
NOTE: If you created a review for another site that you then wish to submit here as well, it may be best to mention this at the top of review so you are not mistakenly accused of plagiarism.

Q: If writing reviews copied from places like Gamefaqs is not allowed, then why do I see some there?
A: Either they were missed or they were submitted before that rule was made.

Q: Is there a limit to the total number of DPs I can earn from submitting reviews?
A: No. As long as your reviews are legitimate, written by you, and of high quality, you may earn 10 DPs for every review you submit, regardless of how many you have submitted in total. Note that any spamming or plagiarizing (as interpreted by the review inspectors) will earn you a deduction for each review falling under those categories.

Q: Who are the review inspectors?
A: Anyone who is support staff or higher as well as a few selected members can inspect your reviews. Do not badger them if you think that you deserved a reward, but did not receive it because there is no way for anybody to know who saw your review. If however, you have other types of questions, then feel free to ask (but most of them should have already been answered in this guide)

Q: So how do I write a review that is guaranteed to earn me DPs?
A: Please read the following guide: (written by ling99rp):Tutorial for making a review that is of quality.**First of all, I'd like to re-emphasize that your review must be written by you. It is easy to see if the review has been plagiarized or "ripped” from a different site. If you are caught plagiarizing or ripping you lose 10 DPs
**To start off, make sure you have played the game and understand something about it before making the review. This will ensure that your review is accurate and your chances of getting DPs are increased. Below is a fictitious review that will help you understand how and what a quality review is.

Wrong review-
USER1 wrote:
Pokemon: Sapphire version
This was the best game in my opinion. The gamming experience was the greatest. You should download it!! XD
The above review is "wrong" because it contains the following mistakes:
1. It is too short. Your review should be at least 50 words in length. Although this is just a suggested amount, the reason behind it is that it takes a considerable number of words to product a quality review.
2. Try to stay a way from using vague/generalized phrases or sentences like “this game is the best”.
3. Be sure to use proper spelling, grammar, and punctuation and try to stay away from odd symbols such as the above <3 . It deters the reader from your review.

Proper review-
USER2 wrote:
Pokemon: Sapphire version
The quality of this game was relatively high. I enjoyed the overall feel of the game and would suggest it as a download. Like the other games in the series it offers a wide range of pokemon and characters to encounter and battle. Since there is a co-game involved with this one, you are able to link up with a friend and trade pokemon which you can collect to fill in your pokedex. This game is strategic and will challenge you with puzzles to complete.

The above review is half way decent and guarantees acceptance, but the reviewer would still unlikely be awarded the 10 DP’s since it does not cover all aspects of the game.

Upgrading to a newer CFW.
Why update?
3.80 update added Internet Radio, using Shoutcast and Icecast.
3.90 added Skype (for PSP Slim models only).
4.01 added internet search.

First, you'll download an update package.
This will include the 3.90 M33 CFW, as well as the official Sony 3.90 install package.
The combination of the two will allow you to install 3.90 M33 on your PSP.
Both can be found atDark-AleX's website
Once you have the download package on your computer, extract the UPDATE folder and put it on the memory stick under PSP->GAME.
For the official firmware, Google for the following:

Do you have to do each one individually? No. If you are at CFW 3.52 M33-3, or higher, you can go to any base level of firmware. Incremental upgrades (M33-2, M33-3, etc) have to be added separately.
Alternate Update Method (starting with 3.80 M33)From 3.80M33, you can download new M33 firmwares updates via the Network Update feature released at 3.80M33. Otherwise, download it via this webpage as usual.
How to play PS1 games on your 3.90 M33 CFW PSP!Yes, I know you really want to know how to do this. I found some very simple tool to do this, called PSX2PSP.First, you need a PS1 ISO. If you need to download one, check out the PS1 Emulation forum here for links to such, or on how to convert your PS1 CD into an ISO...this guide won't cover it.
Load up PSX2PSP (I use Theme Mode) and go to the convert menu. The first box lists ISO/PBP file. Load up your ISO there (it can be in ISO, BIN/CUE, or IMG/CCD/SUB format). Most of the other stuff should list automatically, though you'll need to set the output folder. The fastest way to load it on your PSP is to set it to the PSP->GAME folder.Customize PBP will, after selecting your ISO, allow you to customize the background stuff, just like you see on a PSP game when on the UMD menu. This section is completely optional.
Icon Image - Box on the left to display the title (and disc) - Image size: 144 x 80 Pixels, 32 bit color, PNG formatBackground Images - Background of the entire display - 480 x 272 Pixels, 32 bit color, PNG format
Information image - Lower right corner, displays text about the game - 310x180, 8 or 32 bit color, PNG format, transparencies recommended.
Background image - Audio clip that plays when looking at the pretties. AT3 format (A WAV file encoded into ATTRAC3 format)
Icon Animation - Similar to image, but instead is an animation.
PMF format.Boot/Warning Image - The image that appears when your PSX ISO is loading (before the PS1 Boot Logo). Image size: 480 x 272 Pixels, 32 bit color, PNG format

When you're done, on the convert menu, click convert. Wait a while, and you'll have a PS1 game on your PSP!
This method isn't foolproof, but a number of games play just fine.

Here's a basic guide to PSP hacking and homebrew. If you want more I suggest you read around in websites like Wololo and PSP-Hacks and PSP Slim Hacks. If you just want to learn how to install a CFW, though, dive right in!

Why hack the PSP?
In this context "hacking" refers to using custom firmware (CFW) instead of official firmware (OFW), "firmware" being the software which runs the PSP. You can check what firmware you are running under Settings -> System Information on your PSP.
CFW allows you to do more with your PSP than usual. It lets you play homebrew (programs made by non-official developers), as well as ISOs (games), make backups of your PSP and your UMDs, and so on.
Sounds good to me! How do I do it?
First determine what model of PSP you have, using this image from a PSP Slim Hacks Tutorial:



If it's a PSP 100X, PSP 200X or PSP Go, the recommended CFW is 6.20 Pro permanent.
If it's a PSP-300X, use the PSP Module Checker to find out what module you have.
Just download it, extract it to obtain the "MCHECKER" folder, and place that inside the "/PSP/GAME" folder in your memory stick. Then put your mem stick back into your PSP, turn it on, and select the module checker under "Games".
If it is a 05g or below use 6.20 PRO permanent. If it is a 07g or 09g, use 6.60 PRO, because you cannot run 6.20 PRO on those models.
6.60 PRO is not a "permanent" CFW. This means that when you completely shut it down you will have to load it again (sleep mode is ok).
**If you are already running OFW from 6.
30 - 6.39, and have a PSP which can run 6.20 PRO, you will have to either downgrade your firmware first, or just use 6.60 PRO, the non-"permanent" CFW.

**Installing 6.20 PRO permanent (from the GBATemp Softmod guide)

If you are not already on Official Firmware 6.20... 1. Download 6.20 official firmware for PSP 100X/200X/300X or for PSP Go.
2. Copy the UPDATE folder to the /PSP/GAME/ folder on your memory stick (or the Go's internal storage).
3. Launch the 6.20 update on your PSP, and tell it to update. **If you are on CFW already DISABLE OR DELETE ALL YOUR PLUGINS FIRST**If you have older CFW already installed, e.
g. 5.50 Prome-4, you might see the "Firmware is already 9.90. There's no need to upgrade." error message. If so, just change its version.
txt.
**Now to install the 6.20 hack! 1. Download 6.20 PRO. 2. Copy the "620PRO_Permanent" and "PROUPDATE" folders to /PSP/GAME/. 3. Run the 6.20 PRO-B Updater (the icon has "UPDATE" faintly in the middle) on your PSP and when it runs press X to install and launch the temporary 6.20 hack.
4. After the PSP restarts, run the 6.20 PRO-B Permanent Patch (the icon says "PERMANENT BOOTLOADER").
5. Press X once to choose to install, then press X again to choose to test it.
6. If your PSP successfully rebooted after the test, then run the permanent patch again, but this time press X and then Circle to install.
(If your PSP did not successfully test, try removing your "/seplugins/" folder from your memory stick and try again, because not all 5.x plugins work on 6.xx)To uninstall it just run the Permanent Patch again.


Installing 6.60 PRO (also from the same GBATemp guide)
If you are not yet on 6.60...
1. Download 6.60 official firmware (for PSP 100X/200X/300X) or (for PSP Go).
2. Place the "UPDATE" folder into the /PSP/GAME/ folder on your memory stick (or the Go's internal storage).
3. Run the 6.60 update on your PSP and allow it to install. **If you are on CFW already DISABLE OR DELETE ALL YOUR PLUGINS FIRSTNow to install the hack!
1. Download 6.60 PRO 2. Copy the "660PROUpdate" and "660FastRecovery" folders to /PSP/GAME/ on your memory stick (or Go's internal storage).
3. Run 6.60 PRO Updater on your PSP, and press X to install.
4. Your PSP should restart into hacked mode. This will last until your PSP is shut fully off (sleep mode is okay). To hack it again quickly, run the Fast Recovery to get back into 6.60 PRO.
To uninstall it just shut it down fully.


So...now I can run PSP homebrew and games?
Yes. Homebrew comes in a folder, with an "EBOOT.
PBP" inside. Place this folder inside the /PSP/GAMES/ folder of your mem stick or PSP Go internal memory. Then run it from your PSP, under "Games".
Place your ISOs (games) inside the /ISO folder, and run it from your PSP under "Games".
How about CSOs? What's the difference between them and ISOs anyway?
CSOs are compressed ISOs (hence the 'C'). They should still work like ISOs, just that they might take longer to load or lag at cutscenes or so.
How about online play or the PSN?
You should be able to play online and access the PSN, if you follow this guide.

Here's a basic guide to PSP hacking and homebrew. If you want more I suggest you read around in websites like Wololo and PSP-Hacks and PSP Slim Hacks. If you just want to learn how to install a CFW, though, dive right in!

Why hack the PSP?
In this context "hacking" refers to using custom firmware (CFW) instead of official firmware (OFW), "firmware" being the software which runs the PSP. You can check what firmware you are running under Settings -> System Information on your PSP.

CFW allows you to do more with your PSP than usual. It lets you play homebrew (programs made by non-official developers), as well as ISOs (games), make backups of your PSP and your UMDs, and so on.

Sounds good to me! How do I do it?
