The market for Video Games is in some cases even more fickle than it was 10 years ago. It's almost as fickle as the Trading Card Market. The younger your fan base tends to be, the more fickle the market, because of supply and demand. Kids don't have a whole lot of money, so eventually prices must go down to actually sell a product. Within 3 months of release, most games are less than half as expensive as they once were. Some more popular games can take years to depriciate in value more than 20-25%. Games from a series that is no longer made or from a publisher that no longer produces games actually sell for MORE, because they are considered in some small part "rare", because they are no longer made. That part of the market is also fickle though. it just goes up and down, up and down. With the basic knowledge of the VG Market, I can tell you that if you want a more popular game like Halo 2 or something for cheap? You're going to have to try eBay or something, because nobody is going to sell it for less than $35-40 (here in the US) for at least the next 6 months, if not longer. I honestly had no idea they charged so much out of the US. I think it has alot to do with novelty, being that most games are created in the States or in Japan/Asia. They obviously have been making games directly for other countries for quite some time, but the fact is that this market could still be considered a Novelty where you live. Novelties tend to sell for alot more, and that would explain horrendous prices. Sounds like it's getting to a point now, however, where it has stopped being a novelty. Not suprising considering how historically fickle (yes there I go using that word again) this market is.
