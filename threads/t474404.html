Overview:Having worked as a retail store lead technician for quite a few years, I have experienced quite a variety of computer issues that I had to fix for clients. One of the recurring problems is well known as the fake scanners, taking form as an anti-virus, anti-spyware, privacy protector, hardware monitor, etc. What these little pieces of software do is take over the computer while starting up, popping to the user a window of the fake application performing a scan and finding all sorts of "errors" or "problems" related to their computer and asks the user to pay them to fix the issue (obvious scam attempt is obvious). Although there are many variations of this spyware, the cleanup steps are pretty much similar. This guide will pretty much show you the secret that will cleanup the issue for 80% of the cases out there. Note:This is the more up-to-date and simplified guide that should work on computers running Windows Vista and 7. I would like to thank Microsoft for the release of Windows Vista and later: The enhanced security that was introduced into the operating system has made viral infections less serious than their XP counterpart. This guide might work for Windows XP, but for the sake of a higher rate of success, I have included my older guide that had a greater probability to fix XP machines. Special thanks goes to GBATEST for digging back out the long lost guide. ^^ Before starting:This guide only applies to variations of the fake scanner spyware. They basically all have a similar look to this:The fake scanners might warn you about viruses, spyware, and trojans on your computer. The other variation will warn you about computer performance issues, hard drive failures, and other alarming hardware problems. If these condition reflect what you're currently experiencing, please do continue reading. A huge block of text can save you a virus cleanup fee at the store (They used to pay me $80 per visit, so I think it is quite worth it). What you will need:- CCleaner Free: http://www.piriform....leaner/download- Malwarebytes Free: http://www.malwareby...lwarebytes_free- USB Stick- Possibly a friend's computer if you don't have a second one. Preparing for the cleanup:Your computer might be crippled to the point of not being able to navigate the Internet to download what you need. In that case, this is where the USB stick and friend's computer come into play. :)Go ahead and download CCleaner and Malwarebytes from their respective sites (links are provided above). Makes sure to download the Free versions, they are sufficient to help you cleanup your computer. Once downloaded, put the installation files onto your USB stick and have it ready for your infected computer. Cleaning up your infected computer:1. Power on your infected computer.2. Right after the POST screen (company logo screen), hit 'F8' on the keyboard multiple times. You will then be presented a screen with multiple selections.3. Choose "Safe mode with networking" and let Windows boot up. It is very important to choose that setting as you will require Internet access.4. Login to an account that has administrative privileges.5. If you are now stuck with a black screen and cursor for some time, hit "Ctrl+Shift+Esc". That should bring up the task manager. Then choose "File > New Task (Run...)", type "explorer" into the box and click on "OK". Your Windows taskbar should start loading now.6. If you are presented with the fake scanner in safe mode, hit "Ctrl+Shift+Esc". In the list on the "Applications" tab, find an item that represent the fake scanner's name. Select the item, right-click it, and select "End Task" to kill the application.7. Insert your USB stick into the computer.8. First, let's install CCleaner. Run the installer, follow the wizard. Read carefully as CCleaner will propose to install a sponsor software at some point (either Google Toolbar or Google Chrome). Refuse to install those, unless you really want them on your computer, and complete the installation.9. Once you launch the application, we'll first start with a file cleanup. It is highly recommended to simply select all the items except for "Wipe Free Space" on the "Windows" tab, and also select all the items under the "Applications" tab. Click on "Run Cleaner" and accept the confirmation.10. Click on the "Registry" section button. Make sure all the items are checked, and click on "Scan for issues". Once the scan is complete, click on "Fix selected issues...". When prompted for a backup, it depends on your personal choice as it won't affect the procedure. I personally don't do one as I would not want to restore infected keys back into the registry.11. Once all that is complete, you can now quit CCleaner.12. Press on the "Windows Key" + R. In the "Run" dialog, type "cleanmgr" and click on "OK".13. In the new window, select the drive where Windows is installed and click on "OK". It is usually "C:"14. Click on "Clean up system files". Repeat step 13.15. Click on the "More Options" tab. In the "System Restore and Shadow Copies" section, click on the "Clean up..." button. Confirm the process, this might take a few minutes to complete, of which the "Disk Cleanup" dialog might seem to not respond, but that is normal. This is to avoid a system restore back to a point where your computer is infected. Once completed, come back to the "Disk Cleanup" tab.16. Select all the items in the list, click on "OK" and confirm the cleanup. This might take a few moments to complete.17. Once completed, come back to your USB drive and install "Malwarebytes". Towards the end of the installation, make sure the "Update Malwarebytes" and "Launch Malwarebytes" options are checked. This will ensure that you get the latest definitions while scanning.18. If ever the update fails, it is ok, we can still continue.19. Once Malwarebytes starts, decline the offer for the trial. This will let you continue with the free version instead.20. Select "Perform Full Scan" and let the scan complete. This might take between 30 minutes to 2 hours, depending on how many files are on the computer.21. Once the scan is completed, select all the problems, clean them up.22. Restart your computer.23. Windows should now boot into your normal mode. Login as you usually do, and your computer should now be fixed!24. If you want to be on the safe side, I would highly recommend doing steps 9 and 10 after logging back into Windows. If ever Malwarebytes did not update properly in safe mode for the scan, I would also recommend running Malwarebytes again, update the definitions, and re-run a full scan. All this is just simply to ensure that the virus is gone. Help, my files are all gone:This is usually the case with the variation of fake scanners that says you have a hard drive failure. You will mainly notice that all your desktop icons are gone, most of your shortcuts in the start menu gone, and all personal files in "My Documents" and such gone. This one is very easily fixable by following these steps:1. Open your "Start" menu, and click on "Computer"2. Browse to your "Local Disk (C:)" or the drive where Windows is installed.3. Locate your "Users" folder. You might not find it, or simply find it with absolutely nothing inside.4. Click on "Tools > Folder Options". If you are using Windows 7, you might need to press "Alt" on the keyboard to make the menu appear.5. Click on the "View" tab.6. In the list of options, you will notice a subsection called "Hidden files and folders". Change the option to "Show hidden files, folders, and drives", and then click "OK".7. Now, you should see a bunch of new "ghost" folder appear, which should include all your personal files, pictures, etc.8. Next, select the root folder to where all the files are hidden. Right-click it and click on "Properties".9. In the dialog, you will notice an option called "Hidden". If it is unchecked, check it once, click on "Apply" and choose "Apply changes to this folder only".10. Now uncheck the "Hidden" option, click on apply and choose "Apply changes to this folder, subfolders and files". This might take a while to complete.11. Once this is done on the "Users" folder, you should now see all your desktop items, start menu items, and personal files in your Documents and such. If not, simply restart your computer and it should be ok.12. Repeat step 8 to 10 as needed to any folder that have the issue. This didn't fix the issue:Try my older guide below. Like I said, it works 80% of the time~ Resume of what these steps do:Getting into Safe mode usually prevents most malware from loading. Running a scan with Malwarebytes and Spybot usually takes care of most malware files and registries. The sfc command will replace any system critical files that could have been patched by viruses back to original files. Running CCleaner and Disk Cleanup should eliminate all temporary files off the systems, usually where many malware are hiding. The registry cleanup should remove all keys pointing to viruses that are removed. Cleaning up the System restore will usually prevent the system from accidentally restoring back to a point where your system was infected, thus putting back the virus in place. As I mentioned, from personal experience, this works on 80% of the cases. People running Windows XP have slightly lower chances of recovery compared to users on Windows Vista and 7 because the way Windows manages the administrative privileges of applications.
