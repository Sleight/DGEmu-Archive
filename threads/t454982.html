Our store is a big reseller in terms of HP notebooks, and obviously, with the large amount of HPs going out with extended service plans, I've seen many returns of them as well for repairs.I've identified multiple problem models with HP, and have practically dealt with many of them quite a few times.Here's a little list of which I've told myself to be wary about:HP dv2000, dv6000, dv9000 series with nVidia chipsets: As mentioned, the GPU failure is very prominent on those, whether it's AMD or Intel based systems. HP has distributed bios updates that rev up the fan to temporarily solve the issue, but the failure will happen at some point. My dv9010ca is still living though, guessing I'm a lucky one. At the time, I have registered my notebook with HP, and they even extended my warranty for free because of that known issue.HP tx1000, tx2000 series, the worse tablet PCs on the market. Nearly all those models failed due to overheating, or suffer from constant fan spinning draining the battery completely, and running so hot the system starts to run sluggish. There was even a case where the system overheated so much the battery's plastic case cracked in two on the side. The newer TX2 models seem to be perfectly fine though.HP Mini 2133 and 2140, a nice aluminum casing holding pretty much the cheapest components out there. The 2133 based on VIA processors have been plagued with overheating issues and motherboard failure, same for the 2140.HP DV4-1000, DV6-1000, and DV7-1000 series, mostly plagued with video cable failure or LCD display failures. The DVX-2000 series are all fixed from those problems.All other consumer level notebooks from HP that are being sold today, meaning the CQXX, GXX, and DVX series seem to be working fine. Their low-end business line HP Compaq and ProBook models work perfectly fine also, but their HP ProtectTools software is very heavy on the system, is is mainly the cause of the performance issues on those models. I myself own a HP Compaq 2230s, and can confirm the ProtectTools issue, but the performance issue was gone by the time I installed an SSD to replace my old 5400rpm hard disk.
