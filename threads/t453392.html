RRickstilwellORIGINAL POSTJan 19 2010, 08:36 PM
Does anyone here seek out and look for NES systems and games to this day? Some people grew up with an NES in their home. Some households would always trade in their old systems when a new one came out and others would hold onto each system even though they bought a new one because they did not want to give up playing some of their favorite games. I fit the second category.Other people grew up with other systems and either passed up the NES due to being younger, or had a different system from the era or earlier in their home (ie Sega Master System, Atari or some other pre-NES system, or only played computer games). People who go after the NES in these situations tend to have a curiosity about what it was like to play it.As far as the collecting aspect goes, some people like to try and achieve the tedious goal of getting every game for the system. Myself I look at it as something that is only possible if I wish to pursue it all my life and spend a lot of time and money on it, sacrificing enjoying fun on many other game systems. So I choose to only collect the games that I can have fun with. My favorite styles are Action, Adventure and RPG, but the NES seems to have games from all other genres that are worth playing. Some people go as far as trying to only get games complete in box, or to collect all the odd controllers they can find. They can even hunt down the different system variants.So for anyone else who is still into the NES, what are your methods of looking for games and choosing what to buy? Are you picky about condition or just like to do it for the experience of classic gaming? Anyone here have the less common NES 2 (top loader) or an import Famicom?

RRickstilwellJun 01 2011, 07:31 AM
I decided to narrow my collecting of actual games down to complete in box copies with instruction books. Since this is the more expensive but more fulfilling experience, I decided to only do this for games I really like. Less can be more when you have limited space. I bought an NES powerpak and now there are very few games that don't work on it with the newest mappers. Once it gets here I'll have access to play roms on my real NES with the powerpak which is not emulation but actual hardware reading code.I am going to sell all my cartridge only games that end up working for me on the powerpak. So either it will be collected in the original box or it will be installed on my powerpak. I am doing the same thing with SNES.Any repros I will keep because cartridge is as complete as it gets and I paid good money to have these nice things custom made. Super Mario Bros./Duck Hunt and Super Mario Bros./Duck Hunt/World Class Track Meet also will be kept because complete is only game and manual as they were only released as pack-ins. The only boxes available are mockups that were created in 2008 or 2009.
