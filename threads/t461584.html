SShadowOct 07 2010, 12:10 AM
This is probably stupid, but could you take advantage of the fact that diodes have a forward voltage drop of approximately 0.7V? Set up 4 chains in parallel.Then for example the 3V "chain" could consist of some Schottky diodes to lower the V(f), a current-limiting resistor, and the LED. If the Schottky diodes lower V(f) below the required voltage for the diode to emit light, then it's below 3V and it won't light up, and if the voltage is enough to make V(f) above the required voltage for the LED to shine, you know it's above 3V because the LED lights up.Repeat for the 6V, 9V, and 12V chains using more and more Schottky diodes (which fortunately are pretty cheap off eBay).Does this work?
