Aakun20Sep 11 2006, 04:44 PM
I agree with Kittii. Most of the people bashing Dragon Quest 8 never played it, and those who do claim to hate and played it usually make poor decisions on what to bash. While I appreciated FFX, Dragon Quest 8 was still better in my opinion for a number of reasons.1) The Alchemy Pot - the fact that you can combine less useful items into a much more useful item instead of trudging back to town and selling the useless ones off made item finding a great more rewarding, regardless of the fact that monster gave you an item you didn't want. Plus, it was cool to see things like cheese play such a variety of roles.2) The sidequests for DQ8 were much better than Blitzball. I _HATE_ Blitzball. The fact that I could neither train prior to the playing it, and the fact that I hate losing, just pissed me off. At least with DQ8, I had a decent chance of completing the sidequests AND I was more often rewarded with better things for completing them. The sidequests for FFX seemed like an arbitrary tack-on and some were teeth-gnashingly hard (Lightning Plains, anyone? Dodge 200 lightning bolts for a SIGIL?).3) The storyline. Dragon Quest was FAR less linear once you got the ship and unlike the airship in FFX, you actually had to fight to get where you were going. Sure, you couldn't get to some places without completing certain tasks, but at least there was always a new place to find. If FFX had a new place to find, it was because of an incomprehensible riddle or because you just didn't notice that you could walk right instead of just going left. There was also the fact that you didn't have 'useless' characters that wound up neglected until you found you had an important battle that forced you to use them.4) The bosses were more of a challenge. Dragon Quest has always made getting insanely powerful a very difficult challenge, but every challenge was often met with at least worthwhile results.5) Balance. Depending on how you let them grow, virtually any character could replace any other. The only things they couldn't get were the Overdrives. On the other hand, in Dragon Quest 8, there were a plethora of skills that were character specific. While a few were shareable between the Hero and Angelo, no one could really replace anyone else. Yuna's summons, while awesome, made most of the other characters less useful and led to her being far too powerful all by her lonesome.6) Each of the characters had time to develop their background. In FFX, you're given a brief introduction to the character's history, just a few critical moments that influenced why they were where they happened to be that ultimately didn't seem to influence their overall growth. But DQ8 has numerous instances where the characters actually developed a personality and a history. Only Auron, Tidus and Yuna develop any real personality or significant history.

The characters in DQ8 are so much more lovable and colorful. I mean, what guy wouldn't want Yangus at his side? The world and gameplay in DQ8 is so much more open ended and each battle has to be fought much more strategically.One of my main gripes about the Final Fantasy series is how the render status effecting spells and abilities useless. In almost ever case, when fighting a group of monsters your best bet is to preform physical attacks and black magic spells. The only time I ever find status effecting spells usefull is in boss battles, and then it's only to improve my party (like with the haste or protect) because almost no status effecting spells actually have any effect on the bosses. Most field battles in Final Fantasy games can be just hack, slashed, and black magicked through.The field battles are where Dragon Quest titles shine. Most battles at your level are going to take more than one or two rounds, so to just concentrating on offensive abilities is suicide. In almost every battle one has to figure out how to properly deploy one's party to both do maximum damage and prevent loss of health. If you're not paying attention, a random battle could really wipe out your party. Status effecting spells and abilities are critical in defeating your oponents making each new spell and ability you aquire worth while. This is almost never the case in Final Fantasy games, I hope someday it is.I also like how Dragon Quest VIII even fleshes out the world's NPCs. Every town has immediatley recognizable characters in terms of personalities. They all have their quirks and stories which adds so much to the world.I really do enjoy the Final Fantasy series, though. Indeed, Final Fantasy X is one of the few games that actually brought me to tears at the end. As far as my favorite games on the PS2, I would actually rank FFX as second behind DQ8. However, in terms of gameplay, character likability, and overall attention to detail, I'm going with Dragon Quest VIII.
