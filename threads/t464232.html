Pphantoma4Jun 12 2011, 03:20 PM
Personally I believe a college education is a minimal requirement for any future employee/employer. Much more so if you intend to be a boss, instead of working for others. College provides a much higher level of learning and interaction between students and peers who are (hopefully) like-minded in terms of goals, dreams, future intentions, etc. A good college also provides avenues for students to interact with the real-world, such as internships, programs with outside businesses/organisations/institutes, and so on. These are invaluable experiences for anyone who is willing to undergo them.JCAM-CTZOG is right though, a college education does not equate to you being smarter than a school dropout. It still depends on the person him/herself. A college education or a university degree tends to mean a better paying job, but if you have no soft skills you're still pretty useless to any employer.Still, I think some work experience will also value-add to a student's life. Nonetheless, the work should be meaningful and purposeful, not simply a job you work to earn spare cash for whatever reasons. I'm working part-time now with a sales, and intend to continue doing so even after matriculating into university. My degree will be in Business anyway, so I think the experience will be useful for my studies.It isn't easy to balance work and school at the same time, but I think that would be the best way to go. A combination of applicable soft skills learnt from a real-life job coupled with the discipline, knowledge and critical skills learnt from tertiary education.

Right out of High school, I went into a program called Katimavik. It was an amazing thing to experience, and I learned so much. If I didn't go into it, I would've just worked until I was old enough to take a Correctional officer course. Currently, a month out of that program and old enough for that course, I am deciding that I may not want to take that course, and will most likely get into a trade or, eventually, open a business (Katimavik, the time away and trying new things, helped me make my decision).Now, I am waiting to hear from Canada World Youth about participating in that. I am working to save up money at a decent job until I get into that program (if something happens and I don't, I will merely volunteer where I live and try some different jobs).I've taken time from additional schooling to take these programs, which have helped shape my personality and outlook into the future greatly. This "gap year" (which is mandatory in some places) has helped save me 12 thousand dollars on a course (Corrections) that, now, I realize I most likely will not get into.In the end, I suggest taking a year to do things (try different things that you won't have time to do when you have a busy work or school week) and develop your mind to figure out what you'd like to do.Once you know what to do, then figure out if you need schooling for it. Trade? College or apprenticeship. Doctor or teacher? University, and lots of it.When it comes to BoA's and the like, again, figure out what you can get from it. Need it for a job you want? Take it. Want to just learn some things that interest you? Do it. Don't take it just to take it.Figure out what you want to do; figure out what you need for it; figure out what you *want* to do (photography classes, sculpting, do it!) And just do it!But, a final warning: test out what you think that job will be first, along with other things. I've volunteered in soup kitchens, Habitat for Humanity, a daycare (french, nonetheless!), and many other places, and have also worked for pay at a fast food restaurant and bakery. Try it out and make sure, you don't want to waste a bunch of money if you end up hating it (sometimes this is inevitable, but really, try your best to avoid this).I hope it works out for you (I feel this is more for insight than to start a real debate) and any others who worry about things like this.Edit: I'd just like to add, my father didn't stay past grade ten in school and today has a successful car garage running next to our house and loves it, and my mother dropped out after grade 11, for her grade 12 years later after a variety of jobs, and has a great government job now. Schooling wasn't a large thing to them (though to the half of my mother's family it was, they're doctors that took a lot of schooling).
