Back when I was younger my parents gave me a lot of crap for playing video games and being on the internet. They did buy me a computer though, which I convinced them to do because I built it myself as a "learning experience". Of course I loaded it with gaming hardware. I think a lot of the reason why parents hate video games is that they never had them as kids, and they don't understand. On the other hand, probably most of the people in this forum (myself included) play video games a LOT. So for parents it might be worrisome to see your kid always in front of the TV or computer. They don't want you to grow up to be a vegetable, you know? If you can prove to your parents and more importantly to yourself that your gaming might lead to something productive later in life (like being a computer programmer, or game journalist, or something), they'll probably be more accepting of what you do. And you might learn something about your own habits in the process. It wasn't until I forced myself to not play videogames for a month or something that I realized I played them way too often. Nowadays I've cut back on the gaming a bit. I'm never going to STOP gaming, but if you do it less compulsively and really make sure you're enjoying it and not just doing it because there's nothing else in your life, when you do actually pick up a controller you'll find you enjoy it a whole lot more. Videogaming in itself can get boring even if you're always playing new games.But to give a real answer to the original post: yeah, my parents are still very against games. But I've grown up, and it's not really up to them anymore. Just wait it out a few years, and learn to game in ways that doesn't piss off your parents. Plus if you go out and earn your own money to buy systems and games, they might just be impressed at your hard work. And talk to them. My parents may still dislike games, but they used to have an irrational fear of them. Once I started talking to them about games and how I felt about playing them, they stopped being afraid. Then it was just minor annoyance until I went off to school.Parents can be dealt with. I definitely had the craziest parents of anybody I knew. I was the kid who brought weird health food to lunch in elementary school. I wasn't allowed to get a driver's license until I was almost 18. Now that I'm 20 years old, I'm on summer vacation at my house and my mother still tries to set curfews and tell me who I can hang out with. But I just say no. If you act as though your parents were just some person your own age, and you treat them firmly but respectfully on the same level as them, chances are they'll just be surprised, and get off your back.
