mpvee' post='4842943 wrote on Jul 18 2010, 02:43 AM:
Personally, I believe sex should only be in games if the story truly warrants it. Not in the Grand Theft Auto sense, but much more towards a scene of intimacy between characters. I actually view this as a perfectly sensible view of the issue.Currently, on consoles, we allow increasingly ludicrously depraved levels of violence in our gaming... And very few could argue that sexual content is worse without resorting to fallacious arguments of one sort or another.Now, as some of the first comments on this thread have observed, there are indeed plenty of porn-based games out there - on the interwebs, in the inherent free-speech zone that the Internet represents. However, I suspect that the thread starter was speaking of console games.Currently, actual depictions of sex that could go into a PG-13 movie immediately upgrade a game to the "M" rating in the United States - putting the game (in most shops) behind an ID wall, where a person must prove that they are 17 to get it - such walls also exist for "R" movies usually, but never for T/PG-13.More significant depictions of sex tend to go unrated in the US - porn studios just plain do not bother, since they don't often sell to the same types of store (the biggest chain that does both general-audience and porn films, Movie Gallery, closed up last year). However, such films do have a rating for that rare porn studio that visits the MPAA - "NC-17" (which is occasionally pulled out for exceptionally ludicrous amounts of violence, as well). However, you never see this rating in practice, nor its gaming counterpart, "AO".Why is this, you ask? On consoles, all three current console manufacturers have declared that they will not allow games with an "AO" rating on their platform. The two cases where this got in the way of a game's release were the ultimately-cancelled Thrill Kill, and Manhunt 2, both for massive violence levels. Further, even if you limit your release to the PC, most retailers won't carry the games for the same reason they don't carry pornography (even if the game is "AO" for non-sexual reasons). Manhunt 2's true, as-authors-intended, AO-rated version may actually see release - but only on Steam or similar services, as a download only. (At least, people suggested it to Rockstar and they said they'd consider it. Valve has not stated if they would allow AO-rated games.)Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas had a sex minigame coded in, but disconnected from playability (see TVTropes: "Dummied Out" for other examples of this idea) - either the ESRB balked, or Rockstar's team decided to remove it in advance before even sending the game to the ESRB. However, the code was found - in every released version of the game at the time - and could be accessed with basic cheat devices (though this was trickier on the XBox, were few major cheat devices existed). This forced the ESRB to declare that all content on the game disc, accessible or not, would have to be considered in the rating - resulting in San Andreas being upgraded to AO rating, and subsequently recalled for all versions except PC. (A "Second Edition" that removed that part - and on the PC, disabled modding the game - was quickly released to fix this fiasco. First-edition copies are now quite valuable as a result.)I believe that it was correct to put the explicit, uncensored depictions of sex as something worthy of an "AO" rating - consistent with what the MPAA ratings board would do for a film with such content. However, I also believe that the AO rating needs to be put into use, and depictions of sex allowed onto consoles. Simply put, I don't view sex as worse than violence, and believe - firmly - that free speech should NOT stop at the doors of the walled gardens that are gaming consoles (and mobile devices - thus why my phone runs the open-source, nothing-banned-and-no-way-to-put-bans-in Android OS.)As to whether that content should be in the games - well, who are we to judge? That's up to the creators of the game. I wouldn't expect an AO version of, say, Guitar Hero anytime soon, for example. Let the developer decide, but don't block their choice, if you ask me!
