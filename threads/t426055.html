Piracy countermeasures also tend to adverse effects; they inconvenience the legitimate customer much more than they inconvenience pirates. Another factor tying in to the customer base for a particular producer is that media sales are not a zero sum game. The exchange of, let's say, food for money, obviously involves two parties transferring objects for their perceived value, one "loses" food and "gains" money, while the other "loses" money and "gains" food in the end, both food and money are conserved because they are merely transferred between two parties. In the case of media, the lack of piracy does not necessarily increase sales and an increase in piracy does not necessarily decrease sales. This is because marginal units of media has essentially zero value to the producer, all the cost is invested in creating the media, when a copy of the media is bought, the producer loses nothing and gains money, while the consumer loses money and gains a copy of the media (this is how CDSs were supposed to be for AIG until the housing market crashed). The only loss on the part of the producer that is possible is if a legitimate consumer instead decided to pirate the media. This hypothetical can only hurt the producer marginally, if at all. In simpler words, even someone who is homeless needs and wants food and shelter, while for a billionaire there is very little difference between literally burning $20 or putting it in the bank (putting it in the bank might actually take longer and count as a greater loss).
