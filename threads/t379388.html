The reason for most of the open endings of anime is because of the manga. About 95% (made-up statistic, but still close enough to get what I'm saying)---or almost all of anime, comes from the manga. That's right, the anime show you are watching is probably not an original plot line. Imagine making a movie about a novel series, and suddenly, the author dies. You have to make an open ending. Since anime usually has set budget or limited number of episodes (usually 13, 26, 51, or until forever ), and the manga hasn't ended. And it probably won't end for another 3-5 years. Most manga lasts for years and years, and the anime starts really early. So, most studios either can't fit the entire storyline into the limited number of episodes, or the manga hasn't finished and they have no clue how the story/plot will end in a few more years. It's not their fault; most mangakas barely know where their plot is heading either. So leaving the series's ending ambiguous is the only way to go. Which really sucks for the viewer. Imagine if Naruto had to stop at episode 26...yeah..exactly. Or they can go another way, and make an entirely weird, noncanon ending that swerves of from the story, like Full Metal Alchemist or Soul Eater. But really, if you liked the anime but hated the ending, just go online and read the real manga. Chances are, the manga is much, much better than the anime and makes more sense.
