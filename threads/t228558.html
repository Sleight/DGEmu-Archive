[center][/center]Name: Wii (Formerly known as the Revolution)Release Dates: North America- Sunday, November 19, 2006 Japan- Saturday, December 2, 2006 Europe- Friday, December 8, 2006 Australia/New Zealand- Thursday, December 7, 2006General SummaryThe Nintendo Wii is one of the latest video game consoles and is part of the seventh generation, a position it shares with Microsoft's Xbox360 and Sony's Playstation 3. The main feature of the Wii is the Wiimote, a unique controller that uses motion sensitivity to bring a whole new dimension to gaming. There are two other controllers in addition to the Wiimote: the Nunchuk and the Classic Controller, both of which attach to the Wiimote. Another of the important features of the Wii is that it is backwards compatible, meaning that the Wii is able to play the games of a preceding console, specifically, the GameCube. GameCube controllers, memory cards, and game disks are needed to play the GameCube games since the main parts of the Wii (controllers and internal flash memory) do not support GameCube functionality alone. In addition to GameCube compatibility, the Wii can also be used for wireless connection to the Nintendo DS. Through this, the NDS can be used as a kind of wireless controller to expand on the games of both consoles. The system also includes a built-in Parental Control program that reads the content warning of games so that the games of a higher rating than what was set will require a password. In the near future, Nintendo plans to release a version of the Wii that has DVD-video playback using CinePlayer CE DVD Navigator software engine by Sonic Solutions. However, it will require newer hardware to run.The Wii also has the power to connect through Nintendo Wi-Fi Connection, the Internet Channel, WiiConnect24, and the Virtual Console. The Nintendo Wi-Fi Connection allows free internet play between the compatible games. The Internet Channel is part of the Wii Channels (this will be explained later) and it uses a version of the Opera browser to connect to the internet depending on what connections are specified by the user. WiiConnect24 is basically a way for the the functions of the Wii as well as game data to be updated while the Wii is in stand-by mode allowing updates and patches to be downloaded during this time. The Virtual Console is like an online rom store where you pay for non-Wii-or-GameCube games so that they may be played through the Wii.Lastly, the Wii Channels are the system of features that allow users to play the Wii or GameCube games (Disk Channel); create avatars known as Miis (Mii Channel); send photos, music, and videos (Photo Channel); make use of your Wii Points (Wii Shop Channel); emulate old games (Virtual Console channels); read the latest news (News Channel); vote in various polls (Everybody Votes Channel); catch up on the weather (Forecast Channel); and surf the internet (Internet Channel). Recently added channels allow users to show off their Mii (Check Mii Out Channel), watch trailers or download demos/patches for the Nintendo DS (Everybody's Nintendo Channel), compare results and progress in Wii Fit (Wii Fit Channel), and check what shows are on the television (Television Friend Channel). The Television Friend Channel has yet to be launched in North America. A future channel that will launch soon is the Mario Kart Channel which will launch alongside the game.Also, this is what you should receive if you buy a Wii:
Wikipedia wrote on April 4, 2008:
The Wii launch package includes the console, a stand to allow the console to be placed vertically, a circular clear stabilizer for the main stand, one Wii Remote, one Nunchuk attachment, one Sensor Bar, a removable stand for the bar, one external main power adapter, two AA batteries, one composite AV cable with RCA connectors, a SCART adapter in European countries (component video and other types of cables are available separately), operation documentation, and, in all regions except Japan, a copy of the game Wii Sports.
