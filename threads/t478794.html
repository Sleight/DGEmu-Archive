Here's the funny thing, this is how "clear time" work in platinum game's games:

 

- Cut-scenes and time spent between sections(codec conversation for loading purpose) doesn't count only active game-play time from the start of the section to the score screen count.

- Time during unsuccessful attempts doesn't count(ie: you can spend one hour dying in a 10 minute section, but when you finally finish it, only the 10 minutes of the successful run will be counted).

- When you restart a section/chapter you have already finished before, it only count your best time in that section.

 

Basically, the timer isn't there to brag about how long the game is for marketing purpose, it's there to help players keep track of something called score, you know, something that is very important in good action games.

 

So yea, it's under 5 hours + all the time spent on failed attempts + cut scenes(a Metal Gear game cutscenes) + codec conversations + whatever time you spend restarting missions and not finishing them in a new record time.

A quick preliminary way of checking: Is there an HDMI port on your 360?

If it's pre-2008 and no HDMI, you probably have a Xenon.

If you got an HDMI port, it's either a Zephyr or Falcon model.

 

Anything Xenon and Zephyr are lousy, power hungry, RRoD prone machines.

The Falcon was the first gen with a redesigned 65nm CPU.

 

The best 360 phat model to get was a Jasper model (released after September 2008), which has a new reduced 65nm GPU also, making it less at risk of RRoD and lowers power consumption even further down. Doubt is that one you have though.

 

For modding a phat model, you can either:

- Jtag it (probably best solution for you since you're planning on never playing online)

- Mod the DVD firmware (learning this can easily earn you lots of easy cash once you acquire all the tools, newer games cannot be pirated easily)

- Install an Xk3y (most expensive option, but well worth it if you're planning on pirating the hell out of disc based games)

 

To ID your DVD drive, a quick way to do it without tearing apart your console:

 

There was 1 drive out of the batch that was a freaking pain to work with, I forgot which one it was... I've done flashing on Lite-On and BenQ drives and those went on smoothly.

Once that's done, when you download Xbox 360 games, if you don't want your console to be perma-banned from Live (if you don't want to lose resell value), you always need to pass your ISO through AGBX to make sure that the games are properly stealth patched and stuff.

Flashing your drive also brings in a new issue with physically pirating games: XGD3 protection.

To burn those games, you need a special firmware flashed Lite-On drive to be able to burn. XGD3 is now used on most games since May 2011, so if you're working with a firmware flash, good luck.

Guide to burning your games with XGD3 (assuming you have a Lite-On iHas flashed burner):
