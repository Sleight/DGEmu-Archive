It took you a week? It took me about twelve hours, not counting the extended (and very long) bonus dungeons.Anyway, it's already well known that FF1 and FF2 were both made much easier for the Dawn of Souls releases. On the PSX remakes, there was a choice between an easy mode and the original mode. On this they simply ran with the easy mode.Just a few examples:FF1 had the magic level system instead of magic points, so you could only cast maybe four or five spells to a level throughout an entire dungeon. Without any items to refill them (there were no ethers or anything of the sort) you'd have to return to an inn or use a house on the world map to regenerate them.There was only one healing expendable--the basic heal potion. They cost 60 gold each (which at the earlier levels was quite a lot). So between the limited number of those you could carry, the dozen or so cure spells that your white mage/wizard could cast, the few early level ones that whatever knights you had could cast, and whatever items could be used in battle to heal (heal staff, heal helm) that was all the HP regeneration you could get in dungeons.The GBA versions are far more generous with GP and EXP gained from battles. Things are also much cheaper throughout the game. The silver swords in Elfland are noticeably missing, since that was arguably the worst part of the game (they cost 4000 each and were quite necessary for all Fighters and Red Mages before going into the Marsh Cave).The maximum level for the original FF1 was 50, while in Dawn of Souls it's 99. And again, they're far easier and more rewarding than they originally were.As Incognegro mentioned, the presence of ineffective attacks was a major nuisance since you had to individually assign your party members to fight which enemies, otherwise they would do nothing.They also tinkered with the balance. It's nearly impossible to miss in Dawn of Souls, whereas in the NES original it was incredibly common (one of the reasons death was certain in the Marsh Cave if you didn't have silver swords). Running away from battles just to survive while making your way through a dungeon was a large part of playing, while in this remake you never find yourself needing to flee from battles. The undead have the natural ability to stun with their attacks, and in the original that happened nearly every time they hit a party member. It's the same with most status effects too like stone and poison throughout the game.The characters are all stronger now. The thief was particularly useless in the original (except for when running away, but that wasn't so bad to have back then since you had to run so often).More than anything, the fact that you can save anywhere now. The only way to save in the original NES version was to stay at an inn (yes, and you had to spend the inn fee every time too). So essentially you had to pay at least 40GP minimum (provided that you're able to reach Coneria town) in order to save your progress.
