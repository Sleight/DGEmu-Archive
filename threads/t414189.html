MMr HamAug 11 2008, 12:04 PM
Why bother? The alternator requires loads more time since the strain on the generator is larger. The lower fuel efficiency due to added weight and output decreases the optimal velocity. So at about 40km/h you're the most efficient. That's great for stop-start traffic but anything above that would drain the battery way too much. And if you're on the open highway, the alternator gets nothing from the engine and so you're screwed. Then you have to think about what energy you use to power your car. The alternator isn't enough; even further technology isn't going to rule out the need for external power to charge the battery(ies). If the power you use is sourced from coal or oil, you're left with a car that polluted probably just as much but has greatly reduced efficiency. Also, electric cars are crap for larger vehicles. You can't use an induction motor to start a truck; its just not feasible. Smaller cars also suffer because all that added weight isn't contributing any extra power. The only type of vehicle that electric motors are good for are medium vehicles where the added weight can be offset by having a larger engine, more starter-motor power or even an additional starter-motor.
