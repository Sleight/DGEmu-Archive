I somewhat agree with hikisun22. Although it is disappointing when you shell out 60 dollars for a game and it takes you 4 hours to beat it, it is what you take away from the game that matters the most. It will still leave a mark I suppose, and you might not ever trust the developer again, but it is a game, usually defined as something that is interactive.

 

On another note, usually I avoid playing games that are too short by instead playing RPGs and such. I don't play MMOs, but I do play JRPGs and those are usually long enough to last for a while even if you do know what to do. Take Breath of Fire, the first one in Capcom's series of games. I think I can beat that game in about 10 hours? It's pretty short and episodic, and there isn't much grinding that you need to do. Now as for more recent JRPGs, I still play Dragon Quest IX, and I haven't even done everything yet; I still have to beat the Legacy Bosses. When it comes to games, I generally want the most bang for my buck. I don't think I could be satisfied by paying full price for a game that is really short. In all fairness though, sometimes someone's idea of short is different from mine. Let's say you are playing a game from the 8-bit era. Karate Kid immediately springs to mind due to the AVGN review and the fact that I saw the whole game played through in around 20 minutes. Now it would be upsetting if you could beat that game immediately after all that money you payed, but the game developers foresaw this and decided to make it extremely hard. It's not a good game, or at least I wouldn't compare it to Super Mario Bros, but at least they have something going for it. A challenge if you will.
