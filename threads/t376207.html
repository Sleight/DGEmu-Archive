SSinkhanJan 25 2008, 01:49 AM
When you say "gaming PC", it depends on what games you intend to play.If you're wanting to play Crysis in DX10, "moderately priced" doesn't really exist.The nVidia 8800GT newly revised edition that was released not too long ago is the best valued card out there. It packs a greater punch that the 8800 GTS did when it first came out and almost rivals the first 8800 GTX. About $300As for CPU, the Intel Q6600 is also a fabulous balance of performance and speed. The next step up is almost triple the price, but like a 20% increase in performance. About $3801GB RAM is no longer able to cut it nowadays with most games. @GB should put you through for about another year or two at least. However, you really don't need super high end performance RAM to get good load-up times and low lag. The generic base level of RAM that companies offer like Kingston's ValueRam should suffice. As for clock speed, 667Mhz is cheap (like $40 for 2GB) and is tried-and-true, but you might want to look at 800Mhz if you've got a good processor and are looking to futureproof your system a little.Some people say that having 10k RPM W.D Raptor hard drives are a must when playing games to ensure good load up times, but again, it depends on what sort of game you plan to play. 7200 RPM should be good for the most part. Raptors are low-capacity and expensive.Just make sure you've got a good PSU and cooling system and you're good.
