DDoctor DirtyMar 27 2009, 08:51 PM
Halo is the most overrated game in existence. I don't see or feel anything special about the game at allI liked Halo 1 the most. The campaign was engaging, the combat was realistic in spite of the sci-fi atmosphere, and the story, although poorly written, actually MADE SENSE and kept me interested to the very end. What made this game a big deal was that it was one of the first console FPSs to implement a proper control scheme (as good as GoldenEye is, its controls suck in comparison to Halo's). That being said, Bungie's stupid decision to make players backtrack over half of the levels after the game's turning point really pissed me off. Lazy game design is inexcusableHalo 2 sucked. Campaign mode was left as an afterthought since Bungie added Xbox Live support and busied themselves with perfecting the multiplayer aspect. I made a conscious effort to watch a cutscene to figure out why I'm shooting aliens only to lose all of what little interest I had in the story. Halo's plot was a twisted, fragmented, convoluted mess to begin with and it became even more so in the later titles. Halo 2 failed to give me a reason to follow the story and instead gave me a reason to press A because my time is too precious to watch underdeveloped characters dig plot holes for 5 minutes. Even the ending delivers a cliche cliffhanger ending that served as a sloppy conclusion for the second installment. It seemed that the writers wanted the ending to spot for a sequel, but Halo 1's ending spotted well for this sequel while providing closure and without resorting to cliche cliffhanger garbage. Bad story aside, there was a noticeable change in the gameplay. The most obvious change was in the health system. The first game had a health bar and a regenerating flak jacket. This one had regenerating HEALTH which allowed people to take damage and make a full recovery by hiding under a table and crying a little. This health system dropped the game's skill level significantly and has (unfortunately, but predictably) since became ubiquitous in today's gaming industry. Also, the weapons were painfully unbalanced. The magnum in Halo 1 was equipped with a 2x scope and delivered instant death with headshots. Halo 2's magnum became useless as the scope was removed and its stopping power was decreased; not even headshots kill with one pistol bullet. Grenades became as powerful as bunny farts and overpowered energy swords were placed in the game. On many, many occasions, I've seen people emptying their dual-wield SMGs on a person wielding that stupid sword, only to be anally violated by said sword and sent flying across the landscape. "You CAN bring a sword to gunfight," says Halo 2. "After all, you can easily run through 56 bullets and still slice a guy in half." I don't care about how powerful Master Chief's MJOLNIR armor may be--in first-person shooters, headshots kill instantly. It's a basic fundamental of ALL shooters, and Halo broke it. That may be an aesthetic quibble, but I found it to be irksome nonethelessHalo 3 had more balanced gameplay and the campaign showed noticeable improvement. I'll admit that the level design is best in this Halo; the environments contain lots of variety that are open to various strategies in combat. Overall, it still wasn't impressive as it still had crappy storytelling. For the most part, it's the same as Halo 2 with 20% more bloom, so copy and paste my Halo 2 grievances to the end of this sentenceThroughout each of these games, I haven't picked out any elements that make it inspirational or revolutionary in the slightest. I only see three average FPSs that have inconsistent difficulty curves and pacing in their single-player modes and an unprecedented multiplayer mode that ultimately amounts to the games' only source of replay value. In fact, the only time I have fun playing a Halo game is when I play with others. A truly great game is consistent and delivers a good experience from all fronts. I would admire Halo more if there was another reason to play it other than multiplayer, but as it stands, it is an incredibly flawed game that has amassed an absurd amount of undue praise
