BBillDoorJan 01 2008, 10:11 AM
A draft is justifiable when it is applied fairly, without exceptions for wealth or privilege. Few people want to die, but somebody has to take the risk in some cases. It's the equivalent of drawing straws, or flipping a coin. Without forced conscription, many demanding wars just couldn't be fought. That inability could, in fact, increase the risk of war.Think about it this way. Suppose you are thinking about attacking a country, and you hear that as soon as morale fails and people stop wanting to fight, they'll surrender at once. That's pretty promising. But what if you know, in advance, that the people will be forced to fight as long as their government demands it? That's a bit more daunting. Better not to attack at all. So, by leaving open the option of a draft without constantly requiring one, the US system ensures that, in the most severe emergencies, the military will be able to keep fighting.I do not think the current situation justifies the label of "most severe emergency," though.
