How could these Mammoths have survived this great extinction of other continental Mammoths? The theory is that man killed them off. As always, this is just speculation, but when it comes to a topic such as this, it's always best to take everything into account and create an informed opinion as best you can.As for what you said about our current ecosystem and habitats as unable to properly emulate the living conditions of certain extinct animals, there is always a way around that. The first thing to point out is that, once again, the habitats of Woolly Mammoths are purely speculation, with no concrete proof. However, it is commonly accepted that they lived in a cold environment, similar to Siberia. Now, that's not to say they can't adapt to warmer climates, as is the case with other species of animals. There is currently a "park" in Russia called Sahka, where many reintroduced species found life again, and Russia has stated that they will gladly accept a Woolly Mammoth. Also, it has been proven that the food found in stomachs of Mammoths is unique, yet completely replicable, so a food source would not be of any problem. Like previously mentioned, they do not have to stay the course of this particular food forever because all creatures have the ability to adapt and micro-evolve to better live in their current environment.Woolly Mammoths would definitely not be a "lab rat." Mammoths are very closely related to elephants, so a potentially cloned Mammoth could be brought up and reared by an elephant. This would obviously be the best course of action if we have any hope of reintroducing Woolly Mammoths at all.In addition to that, concerning keeping it alive through reproduction, it is theorized that a Woolly Mammoth might possibly be able to interbreed with an Indian elephant. This is thought because in 1978, a hybrid was born to an Indian and Asian elephant. Granted, Motty didn't work out so well and died a little while later, but the fact that it occurred opens the door widely to various possibilities. So with this knowledge and all this potential at our fingertips, it could be entirely possible to successfully clone a Mammoth without it succumbing to our current environment or being confined to a laboratory.-Source
