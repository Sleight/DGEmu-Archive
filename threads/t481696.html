Okay I know I am late to the party, but I figure my 2 cents on the matter could be useful here.

 

Depending on what you plan to do with said laptop/tablet whatever, there are some things to consider.

  1. are you going to game on it?
  2. do you expect to be productive with it?
  3. how durable do you expect it to be?
  4. what sort of battery life are you expecting?
  5. Are you thinking upgrades in the future instead of just buying a new unit.
  6. are you expecting to have a butt load of storage onboard?

If you are just wanting a small tertiary computer for like skype while your main rig does all the heavy lifting, then by all means get a tablet.  That said tablets come in many shapes and style.  the ones that have "typing covers" typically break the typing part, but still function well enough.  The Two in One laptops (AKA convertibles) tend to be a little more durable depending on the plug connection between the tablet portion and the keyboard portion.  But the durability of the connection point is still debatable. 

 

If you are expecting storage space, (or powered USB ports for external use) go with a full laptop.  There would be no point in trying to power say an external hard drive from a USB port on a typical two in one or even a tablet.  Also Tablets tend to have small amounts of on board storage, maybe around 32 to 64 GB before OS install (unless Surface...) This is basically the big mistake for anybody thinking "I'll replace my laptop with this nifty tablet!" then start crying when they can't get all their files and games on it.

 

(please note that Not all two in one devices are the same, some are significantly more powerful than others, but they are still designed to be "low power consumption devices" due to the lack of cooling ability.  A laptop grade I7 is faster than a 2-in-1 i7 because there is an active cooling ability so it doesn't need to by held back as much as the 2-in-1)

 

Battery life and productivity are sort of hand in hand. If the damn thing can't go more than a few hours on battery then it is useless in the professional environment.  That said they aren't 100% related obviously as productivity can be related to how usable is that keyboard.

 

Gaming? Not on a tablet unless you call Solitaire or silly facebook like games, gaming.  I have a two in one that can barely play minecraft at usable speeds. (not exactly a fair test considering the processor intensive games and low power consumption setup) Graphics on tablets are typically limited to Intel Integrated or some form ripped from the Phone world, or if you are a risk taker there are the ARM tablets, but then you aren't in windows anymore (RT is dead)

 

If you have any ideas of upgrading at all from RAM to storage space, just stick with a legit Laptop.  There is nowhere to go once you get a tablet or two in one without 31337 solder-fu skills.

 

Long story short, unless you use a laptop as a tertiary computer (meaning it's usually the knock around, unimportant, 2nd screen for cheats/chat programs etc), a tablet is not the way to go.  Tablets are useful for handy quick lookups and such but not much beyond that. yeah blutooth keyboards exist, but that sort of defeats the purpose of smaller package of the tablet.
