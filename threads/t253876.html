None of them are going to drop out of the business altogether. Nintendo has a hot product in the Wii; a cheap-to-make device that has the world in an uproar over how awesome it is. Microsoft simply wants to have that feeling of having the #1 console in a generation in terms of support and sales, and are about to move out of the red on the 360 before 2007 is out (which, funny enough, will be a first for Microsoft's Xbox Division). Sony is pretty familiar with their PlayStation brand's arc: a slow start (the first year is always the slowest year, thanks to last-gen's popularity -- think about it: 7 years after the PS2 came out, and there are still big-name blockbuster titles like God of War?), followed by a spark of popularity once developers start to really use the system and use it in new and different ways (normally shot off by a new IP), coinciding with dropping costs on their bleeding-edge technology. In other words, Nintendo plays it humble, Microsoft screams from the rooftops, and Sony tries to blaze into uncharted territory, followers be damned. I dare say three companies that are that different can co-exist peacefully.What people don't do when they answer one of these things is look past the next three months or so. Nor do they look at the past and the patterns from before. I fear that Nintendo is doing too much of the former, Microsoft too much of the latter, and Sony trying too hard to do both at the same time.
