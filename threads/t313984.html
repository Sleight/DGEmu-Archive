Both have their place. GoldenEye was pure perfection for it's day. My first multi-player FPS experience. Halo is the here and now. Basically, you have to divide what makes the games into categories in order to rank them. Many will disagree with what I have to say, but it is what I have to say. I am keeping hardware limitations in mind. And I'm only counting Halo 1 for the purpose of this comparison.Graphics:GoldenEye: Low polygon models, not a true whit of what the 64 is capable of. All the character structures looked the same, but the animation was smooth. Level design and look was really well done. Background/wall providing scenery could have used some fleshing out.Halo: As a first gen X-Box game, a big wow! Character models were well structured, animation was smooth, weapon models were fantastic and functional. Level look was phenominal, buildings and scenery were pretty.Nod goes to: HaloSound:GoldenEye: To hear a Bond inspired soundtrack was just fantastic. Every bit of the music carried a bit of Bond into it somehow. The quality was astounding and sounded almost instrumentla over digital.Halo: A custom original composed score of brilliant proportions. A composistion who's brilliance can not be denied.Nod goes to: TieControls:GoldenEye: GoldenEye used the N64 controller perfectly to it's ability. Using the left and right C buttons or left and right on the D-Pad to strafe, Up and down on the D-Pad or C-buttons to look up and down, the analog stick to move around, and the Z button trigger as the trigger to fire your weapons. The only thing better would have been a dual-analog set up had the N64 controller had it. Though the auto-aim was sometimes as much hamper as harm, especially on the Cradle on the 00 Agent difficulty.Halo: Standard FPS dual-analog controls for looking and moving. While natural for the most part and smooth, no real comments either way.Nod goes to: GoldenEyeGameplay:GoldenEye: GoldenEye has a fair amount of weapons, though I think it was a tad limited on the kinds of each. And lacking reload animations, while making it quicker to reload, detached from the realism of these weapons in the game. The missions were pretty solid, though the in game character interaction was nothing to talk about.Halo: Just once you think you have the universe of Halo figured out, and what part the Master Chief has to play in it, a new threat is thrown into it and the whole game is changed in a blink. It's a big "holy crap" moment, and causes as much frustration as fun, that winds up leading to just more fun once you get this element figured out. A variance of weapons that could have used a bit of tweaking, but are functional and understandable.Nod goes to: TieMultiplayer:GoldenEye: Four player multiplayer in GoldenEye was the greatest thing of the time! Nothing like sitting around with four or more buddies blowing each other away until some greater thing compells you to get off the couch and turn off the game. All the weapons are there, all the game modes, and some cool maps.Halo: Plenty of maps, some familiar, some new. All the weapons are there, all the game modes. But where the hell is the music?!? And why is there no X-Box life?Nod goes to: HaloSo, while Halo may be the game of the new century, if GoldenEye were to come out re-made around the same time as Halo 3, I think that Halo would have some pretty sad sales numbers.And for those of you with Half-Life 2, check out the GoldenEye Source modification.
