someone wrote:
Go ahead and drink all the Kool-Aid you want, but cross-platform games do notlook worse on the Playstation 3 than on the Xbox 360 because PS3 developers areignorant or lazy or incompetent. They look worse because the PS3 is an inferiorplatform for game development. The PS3's CPU has a lot of raw power that is darnednear useless for game development. Of the sort of processing that games actually do-- you know, branch-intensive general-purpose logic -- the PS3 has one-third thecapacity as the Xbox 360. The PS3's CPU is essentially identical with previousgenerations, only with more capacity, while the Xbox 360's GPU is a truly innovativeadvance. The PS3 has less memory than the Xbox 360... yes, I said less. Not only doesthe PS3's divided memory architecture provide additional headaches to developers,but the PS3 reserves three times as much memory for use by the system than theXbox 360 does. It doesn't matter if a console has 32GB of RAM if it only makes416MB available to games. Of course, unlike the Xbox 360, the PS3 appears to dojust about nothing with that fat 96MB of reserved memory, seeing as how theirsystem software provides few services compared to the Xbox 360, but that's notreally important to game quality, just to user experience. Lacking EDRAM, the PS3finds itself bandwidth-bound on complex scenes as frame buffer reads and writesconsume precious bandwidth that could be going to texture fetches. That's why mostPS3 titles do not do the vaunted 1080p that Sony claimed was the only true HD, andwhy even those that stick with a 720p frame buffer frequently have frame rate issues.The PS3's mass storage medium is, I must admit, superior. That's the onlyadvantage it has as a platform. Over time, will PS3 games get better? Unquestionably, yes. Developers will findways to work around the platform's many shortcomings, and optimized middlewarewill do its best to save developers the pain. But the end result will be worse than thatwhich can be obtained on a more comfortable platform that lacks the shortcomings tobegin with. Right now, I have not been able to find one single review of a cross-platform title that has asserted that the PS3 version looked better than the Xbox 360version, and I've found many reviews that assert that the PS3 version looks worse. Iwould be happy to wager that five years from now, the situation will be similar: thevast majority of cross-platform titles will look better on the Xbox 360.
