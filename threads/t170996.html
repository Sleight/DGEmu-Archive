This is a guide I will be constantly updating. It's small now (as of 10/10/06), I know, but I'll put more info when I have more time.LAST UPDATED: 10/14/06Q: Can I play backups on my PS2 without doing anything??? Like, without a boot disc/mod chip or without having to buy anything?A: No. Not yet. You need either a mod chip, boot disc/Action Replay/Gameshark with a slide tool/flip top cover, or a memory card exploit. There is currently no way to just burn a PS2 backup, pop it in your PS2, and play, and there probably never will be, unless anyone finds a selfboot exploit type thing. Q: If I use Swap Magic discs, will it mess up my PS2???A: Well, it depends on what your swap method is. If you are using a flip-top lid (fat or slim PS2), then you probably won't have any problems, unless you installed the cover wrong. If you use the Swap Tool/Silde Tool (whatever you want to call it), there is a large chance it will screw up the PS2's disc tray. Using the Swap Magic discs, however, can supposedly screw up your PS2's laser, but I've been using Swap Magic 3 on my PS2 with a flip top lid for about almost a year, and I have no laser problems....yet :P Q: Can mod chips ruin my PS2A: Well, this is a pretty simple question to answer: If you install the mod chip incorrectly (ex.: you solder it into the wrong place, or, you drip solder accidentally on the PS2 board) then it will obviously screw some things up. As far as I know, if you are careful, and if you install them correcltly, mod chips will do nothing to your precious PS2 except let you play 'backup' games.Q: If I break my PS2 when installing a mod chip/flip top cover/etc., can I send it back to Sony to get fixed?? I still have a warranty....A: Unfortunatley, if you install a prepheral that lets you play backup games, you have to break open the PS2, thus breaking the warranty label, and without this label intact, Sony will not fix your PS2. If you are a n00b trying to install one of these items (as in, you've had no experience with soldering, technology, etc.), look for a friend who knows how to solder to help you along the way.Q: I just bought Swap Magic and I can't play my old PS1/PSX backups! Help!A: Sadly, Swap Magic cannot play PSX/PS1 backups, but that doesn't mean you can't play them at all. Using your method of swapping (flip top cover, slide tool, etc), there is a similar swap disc that is like Swap Magic, but can be used to play backups of PS1/PSX games instead! Breaker Pro is it's name, and it is a pretty 'nifty' device. It includes all the things you need to play backups of PS1 games on your PS2, or on your PS1, including a slide card! Super easy to use, and as mentioned before, you can also use it to play backups of PS1 games ON a PS1.SUPER-N00B QUESTIONS:Q: How do I play copies of games on my PS2!? I NEED ILEGANL COOPIES!A: Well, there are many options for this, depending on what you want to use:[center]SWAP DISCS[/center]Swap discs are quite possibly the easiest way to play backups of your PS2 games. What happens is you have a disc(s) and you put it in your game console, it loads, prompts you to swap the disc out with a backed-up game, and soon you're playing your backup. However, with the PS2, Sony has made it harder to use swap-discs (probably because it was so easy on the PS1 ^_^ ) The most versatile swap disc is Swap Magic.There are multiple ways to do this method to your console, depending on what model PS2 you have:Flip Top Lid (for old or new PS2) - The flip top lid is probably the best way to play backups on the old PS2. What it does, is that you dissasemble your PS2 (don't worry; you only take the screws out and open it up), disconnect a button or two, and switch out your old PS2 top cover with this new one, that can easily play backups with the help of a swap disc. Also manufactured is a flip top cover for new PS2s (the slim model; SCPH-7000X), but an easier method for playing backups on this model can be found below. For more info on this item, visit the Swap Magic website (scroll down to the bottom for this one)Slide Card/Slide Tool (for old PS2 only) - Most likely the easiest way to play backups, but not the best way, for it can damage your PS2 if you aren't careful and delicate. This tool is a small little piece of plastic that you insert into the disc tray of your PS2 and use it to manually pull out the tray and swap your Swap Magic disc with a backup. The only thing you need to modify on your PS2 to use this is to take the plastic bar with the Playstation logo off the front of your disc tray. However, if you aren't careful, this tool can mess around with your PS2's disc tray, thus making your eject button unusable (I have heard many reports of this). Included at the end of this bullet is a link for a template that you can use to make your own slide card with. For more info, once again, visit the Swap Magic website. (other useful links for this: Slide card/tool instruction/usage, Make your own slide card/tool - template (make out of an old credit card))Magic Switch (for new PS2 only) - This is one of the latest swap-disc tools and works on the brand new slim PStwo. What this one does, is that you place little plastic parts on certain places on the disc tray of your PStwo, which modifies what the PStwo is "seeing" and lets you play backups with Swap Magic. There are multiple versions; some in which you need to open up your PStwo. But the Magic Switch Pro gives you the chance so that you do not need to open your PStwo and so you can play backups without voiding your warrantee. It's easy to install, and is a great tool for you PStwo users. For more info, visit the Swap Magic website.[center]MOD CHIPS:[/center]Ahh, the 'old skool' way to play backups. With this technique, you need to have soldering skill, and risk a chance that you could break your PS2. However, once it is installed, you should have no problems. However, they are tricky to install, you need to open up your PS2 (thus voiding your warranty), they still aren't very cheap, but they are rewarding. Currently, the best PS2 mod chip is DMS4, in which is small, and has great compatibility. You can find everything you need about that chip on the link given. There are various other modchips for PS2, but they are mainly all harder to install and have less features. If you are an expert solderer, then go for a modchip; if not, then go for another technique or send out your PS2 to a company who will install one for you (for a hefty price).[center]Hard drive:[/center]You've seen them for X-Box, etc, but now, you can play your backups off of a hard drive, easily connected to your PS2 The tool is called HDAdvance. There are multiple versions of HDAdvance, but each are easily connected. For HDAdvance 3.0, you need to have a PS2 network modem and a hard drive, which you put in the back of your PS2. There is also USBeXtreme, in which you use your hard drive to play backups via the PS2's USB port. This option also works with the slim PStwo, in which HDAdvance doesn't. For more info, visit the HDAdvance website.[center]Game Shark/Action Replay:[/center]The least practiced technique; might be the easiest, for if you're new to the PS2 backup community, and you don't want to spend anything, this is for you. You'll need either a slide card/tool or a flip to cover to do this. What this does, is you can use your Action Replay or Gameshark cheat code disc to boot a backup copy of a PS2 game. I reccomend using Action Replay for this method, but use whichever you want. But, you can only play backup CD PS2 games, not DVD, so it isn't as great as it seems. Just posting this if anyone wants to know it.
