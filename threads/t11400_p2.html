DDoctor DirtyFeb 17 2011, 09:45 AM
I used to listen to a lot of mainstream rap, like T.I., Mike Jones, Slim Thug, and Pitbull. Then I saw The Boondocks episode "Let's Nab Oprah," where the played Madvillain's "All Caps," "Strange Ways," and "Raid."My taste in music changed completely. I was encouraged to look for music rather than settle for what the mainstream throws at my feet. I have several favorite artists; I can't name just one artist since their music appeals to me in different waysMF DOOM. Well, just DOOM now. He's sardonic yet menacing; he'd make a clever, humorous pop culture reference in one song and rap about launching "Rhyme-Propelled Grenades" in the next. His unique and unpredictable rhyming style keeps his songs in rotation on my iPod. I loved his collaborations with Madlib (the aforementioned Madvillain) and Danger Mouse (DANGERDOOM). His mask is cool, tooAesop Rock. Unorthodox in every sense of the word. His songs read like a stream of consciousness; it's the type of thing you'd have to ruminate upon before you can understand it. And by "understand" I mean form a personal interpretation of it; I believe his work is interpretive. My favorite Aesop Rock song is "I'll Be OK." What stood out to me was the chorus: "As long as I know right from wrong, I'll be OK, I'll sing my songs." After I listen to that song, I feel grateful to live another day; that song means a lot to meLittle Brother. Two skilled MCs commenting on the state of the hip-hop industry and the ghetto condition. Right up my alley. 9th Wonder's production leaves me in awe every time; he makes the kind of soulful beats I love to hear. Check out The Minstrel Show, their satirical concept album. Funny stuffWu-Tang Clan. Where do I begin. Nine awesome rappers in one place. I haven't seen anything like it, save for Army of the Pharaohs. RZA is dogmatic, channeling the Supreme Mathematics and Alphabet in his raps. GZA is precise and intelligent. Raekwon is a streetwise storyteller. Ghostface Killah is a soulful, braggadocious, self-proclaimed "slang therapist," capricious in his use of vocabulary (some may call it free association. He picks words by the way they sound). U-God is adaptive to ANY beat thrown at him. Method Man is brazen and impetuous, adept in moving the crowd. Inspectah Deck is confounding and poignant with his awe-striking adroit in poetry. Masta Killa is subdued with a great sense of flow. Ol' Dirty Bastard is simply bat s#!t, possessing a drunken, squalid grace all his own. But don't take my word for it: check these dudes out and you might be blown away. Their back catalog runs for miles, counting their solo work (in which their fellow members would participate in anyway). I give Only Built 4 Cuban Linx..., Liquid Swords, Blackout, Ironman and Supreme Clientele my highest recommendations not only as rap albums, but as albums in general. I'd say that some of RZA's best work is on the former four albums. As for the group albums, I could recommend any of them, really. Listening to 36 Chambers makes me reminiscent of the days when my sister would watch Yo! MTV Raps while I (more or less) patiently waited to play Super Mario WorldFelt. = Murs + Slug. An awesome collaboration; Murs's hubris and Slug's down-to-earth style make a great pair. I enjoyed each of their three albumsA Tribe Called Quest. They got soul. And raps. And awesome beats. Their integration of soul and rap keeps Midnight Marauders on repeatEsoteric. He's a member of Army of the Pharaohs, and he adds a nerdy angle somewhat. I find that to be inspiring; hearing him rap about Iron Man and Star Wars makes me want to write lyrics and cut anime samplesytcracker. Straight-up nerdcore. Apparently, this dude hacked into a NASA website when he was 17. He raps about video games and his days of sending spam for money, which greatly amuses and inspires meTo be fair, Wu-Tang counts as mainstream; they ruled the airwaves during the mid-90s. I find that hard to believe considering what mainstream rap is like nowadays
