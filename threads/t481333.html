The Canon

 

The Curious Case of the Uncuddled Kitten

 

The Mystery of Zombie Island

 

It was precisely 8:05 on a blustery morning when Stock stepped out of the storm and into his warehouse. A foreman, holding a clipboard and chewing on the on a pencil, stepped into time with him as Stock walked towards his office.

 

"Morning," the foreman said.

 

"Good morning," Stock said. "Anything to report?"

 

"Nah," he said, trying to match his boss's strides. "We're just making room for the, uh, merchandise, like you told us to."

 

"Have you an updated floor plan?" asked Stock.

 

"Yes sir, yes sir," he said, and handed a piece of paper to Stock. "We moved the medications 'n stuff over to the northwest corner, y'know, where there aren't windows."

 

"Excellent thinking," Stock said. He didn't look up from the new floor plan. "Less risk of moisture getting into the products. Where did you move the electronics?"

 

"We shipped those over to Warehouse Three," the foreman said. "Figured it was better if we got it closer to the supplier."

 

"Good work," Stock said. They had reached the door of his office. "Is there anything else?"

 

"No sir."

 

"Good. And now, what do you know about the break in?"

 

The foreman's mouth opened, then closed, then opened again. Stock sighed.

 

"My door is unlocked. I haven't given any of you a key, and I didn't leave it unlocked last night. Ergo, someone has broken into my office."

 

"Sir, we had no idea--"

 

"Yes, yes, I can see that. Go round up the security detail, and tell them it's time to participate in the lottery."

 

"Sir, please don't make me--"

 

"No, no, no. You won't do anything," Stock said. A deep, nigh imperceptible throb began to beat in his brain. It was going to be a bad day. "You're just the messenger. Tell them to draw straws, and to take care of the winner themselves. And make sure maintenance cleans the mess."

 

Stock liked to work with a specific kind of moron: someone without the intelligence or moral fiber to refuse an order, and enough deep-seated loyalty to his employer to see the work gets done. Of course, hiring any moron brings with it its own challenges. Like the inability to connect dots, or have even basic observation skills.

 

The foreman bustled away, sweating, yelling orders, and Stock opened the door and entered his office.

 

It was an unusual room. The structure was aesthetically spartan: the walls were corrugated steel and brick, with a large barred window looking out into the warehouse, the floor cold cement, and the ceiling a complicated network of piping and fluorescent lights. The furniture, however, was luxurious. A desk, made out of a single oak log, dominated the center of the room. Bookshelves held odd curious and rare volumes, and a liquor cabinet carved from a solid chunk of obsidian with a bottle of fifty year old scotch resting on top. And a coach, out from Paris, sitting under the large window.

 

Stock said, "It's a little early to be drinking, don't you think?"

 

"Good morning," said the Boss Regent, sitting on the coach and sipping on a fifth of scotch.

 

"BR," Stock said, sitting at his desk. "How may I help you?"

 

"Oh, I'm only interested in some information."

 

"Is that a fact? And what kind of information would that be?" Stock asked. "I don't exactly keep spies on the payroll, but perhaps I can accommodate you for the right price."

 

"That won't be necessary. I heard some scuttlebutt on the street that you are acquiring a vehicle, of sorts, for Mister Scratch."

 

Stock arched his eyebrows. "And you're interested in this because...?"

 

"How much would it take for you to turn it over to us?"

 

"I don't do that. Scratch is paying me for a service, and I will complete it."

 

"Of course. The old devil's got you in his pocket--"

 

"I am a business man first and foremost," Stock said. "You want the merchandise? Talk to Scratch. Maybe you and he can have a bidding war over it. Or maybe a real war. Either way, I don't care."

 

"Even if I offer... ten times what he's offering?" the Boss Regent asked.

 

"Even if. That's the rule. If I make an exception for you and your organization, I'll spend the rest of my life caught up in whatever street wars you and Scratch are fighting. I am strictly a supplier. Nothing more."

 

"Of course, of course," he said. "You sell everything but yourself. Everyone knows that."

 

"Yes. Now, if you're done wasting my time..."

 

"Not quite," the Boss Regent said. "I do have a job offer for you."

 

"Quit jerking me around, BR," Stock said.

 

"I would never do that to you, Stock," he said, standing up and draining his glass. "I can't go into details, at the moment. And besides, you have a busy day today. I'm just giving you a heads up. For the future."

 

"I'm await on pins and needles," Stock said.

 

"Say," the Boss Regent said, staring out of the window. "Is that you're security team? Why are they beating the shit out of their own man?"

 

"An object lesson," Stock said. "For future reference."

 

There was a bang, and Stock could hear a distant body drop.

 

"Good to know," the Boss Regent said, and left.
