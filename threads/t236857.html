No, they're not. Yes, it's true that the main reference to anime is because it's made in Japan, but there's also the fact of the characters appearing more realistic, and the cel-shading techniques that animators use in the process of creating an episode of anime-esque "cartoons". The cel-shading is called "hard-cel shading" and is used to create the effect of tone, or in other words, the effect of the characters being relatively 3D, as opposed to the 2D cartoons that the American "cartoon" style is known for. Avatar is an exception (American-made, but follows the style of Anime). It can be considered anime for its animation style, but many argue that it isn't, simply because it was made in America and not in Japan. Personally, I consider it anime, as in my mind, the difference between anime and cartoons are 1) better animation, and 2) an actual plotline. Avatar has both, thus I consider it anime.
