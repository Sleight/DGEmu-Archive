There are some programs.  P90X is supposedly pretty successful for some people.  You really only need some resistance bands to do it.  I think it's overkill if you're not going to do anything particularly athletic with your newfound ability to  jog in place indefinitely, but that's just me.

 

You don't need a specific program though.  Endurance training is all about doing natural movements for uncomfortably long periods of time.  Just focus on movements you would do as a human and then add some weights to them.  As examples to the contrary, there are lifts you would do to build some muscle or burn some fat that aren't natural motions (e.g. adductor and abductor machines), but you could increase endurance in those same muscles by adding resistance to a natural motion (e.g. wrapping a resistance band around your thighs and moving diagonally left or right).  Additionally, you could forget the resistance and just switch between a lot of different motions really rapidly, which is what P90X does.

 

Truthfully though, you're at a point where you can build strength and endurance.  You probably haven't plateaued yet, and until you do, you really won't have a clear idea of what's heavy and what's just added resistance.  For instance, I can do 4 sets of 20 on benchpress with 185 on the bar (a plate and a 25 on each side) and that's my relatively light lift.

Breakfast burrito (no cheese) ~ 300cal

Turkey and hummus wrap with salt and vinegar cracker chips (Special K brand) ~ 350cal

6oz Pork chop (no butter), green beans, 2x roll (no butter) ~ 750cal

Total ~ 1400cal
