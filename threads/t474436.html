VVictorique De BloisNov 28 2011, 02:02 AM
Well, it depends on the financing you take.Did you check the interest rate and the amount of time you got to pay it back? Usually, for 0% interest financing, they set you up on a certain amount of months to pay it back, and all you need to do is pay minimum per month, but by the time the financing plan comes to an end, you HAVE to pay off everything, or else they would slap you with the interest most probably gathered since the beginning (written often in fine prints where people don't read) Line of credit with the bank is basically the same as a credit card, except in another form with them and most probably a lower interest rate. Usually, you apply for one of those when you're in in a pinch and need quick money, but you can only obtain one if you got good credit history. The last solution would be a credit card, but that's not too awesome simply because the interest rate is high, usually around 20% here in Canada per card... But if you got a good credit history, there's also a hole in the system you can take advantage:Most cards will have a promotional APR when you open an account with them for say the first 6 months (2% instead of 20%), so you can purchase stuff stuff and pay minimum for the 6 months. But! On the 5th month, apply for a new card with another company and take advantage of their special 2$ APR transfer rate, and transfer most of your debt to the new card and completely pay off the old one. Once the old one is paid, call to cancel it. Repeat once this new card is about to come to an end.You can have probably about 1 year and a half of 2% interest if you use that tactic, but remember: only if you got a good credit line, or else, chances are you might get refused for the second time. Then your plan would backfire. :schmoll:
