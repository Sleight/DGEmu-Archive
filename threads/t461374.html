RRayadragonDec 17 2010, 05:43 AM
I have to play a bit of a devil's advocate. While I currently wouldn't consider having cosmetic surgery, there's been times in my life when I would've jumped at the chance if I'd had the money. It's not exactly a secret that there's a certain look that's considered attractive, and if you don't fit the sterotype then you're not good looking. If you're unattractive, you become an early target of teasing a bullying. That tends to leave a very negative impact on the psycological and emotional well-being of the person. If it's in someone's power to fix something that they percieve to be "wrong" with them, and if it would have a positive impact on their life, then I don't see why we should frown on someone's decision to do so. Ideally, we'd be able to accept someone for who they are regardless of their appearance. In reality I don't think it will ever quite happen.Others have already mentioned that cosmetic surgery would be acceptable for someone who's been severely disfigured, but there are more ways to be disfigured than in combat. The example I'm thinking of are women who have a mastectomy or double mastectomy because of breast cancer. The use of appropriate padding hides this disfigurement without resorting to surgery. However, it still doesn't address the issue that these women's physical bodies have been disfigured. Is it worth the risk of surgery to correct their appearance when a non-surgical method suffices? I think it's perfectly acceptable for them to have breast implants to restore them to how they feel they should look. Others may or may not agree with me.
