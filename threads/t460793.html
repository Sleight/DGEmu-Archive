Ok so this topic came to me after I myself was shut down on the sale of an item on Ebay, only a few hours before it was due to close for a quite nice sale price too. The item in question was a pocket knife of sorts, which are legal to sell on Ebay but the problem they had with mine was that it is hidden in a coin and apparently weapons disguised as something else are banned. It's only the size of a coin so if some terrorist was planning on holding up a plane with that he'd probably have more luck with the plastic knife. And looking through the list of banned items there are quite a few on there where you could say "well surely there should be exceptions to that?". Obviously there are straight up illegal items such as drugs and body parts but I mean for example, the sale of Nazi uniforms and anything with a swastika on is also banned. Surely there could be legitimate grounds for the sale of a piece of history, even if it's not such happy history? So my question is should Ebay police listings so aggressively or allow things to flow until something is brought to their attention? Is the responsibility on the site's owners to shepherd their flock? Is the line that they've drawn too heavy or maybe even too lenient? (ie should more things be banned?) What punishment should be dealt out to those who contravene the guidelines? How is the sale of a knife online any worse than from a department store?I will post my own thoughts on this matter properly in a bit but thought this first post should stand simply as the topic at hand.(As a comic aside, it's relieving to note that in the list of acceptable hand weapons, the wii's nunchuck is greenlighted! (Seriously)My thoughts on the subject are that regulations are understandable, they want to protect themselves from being sued by someone down the line because a vicious killer bought a coin knife on ebay and used it to slit their grannies throat. But at the same time they limit themselves by not at least giving people the options to do so. If somebody really wants something for nefarious purposes they'll find it, wether it's on ebay or from the shady guy on the street corner and in fact because an online record of the deed will be kept by the powers that be, serious criminal minds aren't going to leave the paper trail. And as for the non lethal stuff like offensive materials the onus should be on the buyer to be using it for legitimate purposes. If they really wanted surely they could ask all those who wanted to trade in such items to go through an extra registration step that qualifies them as a legitimate collector and waives any right for legal recourse or association with the company should said item get them in trouble. I once came across a copy of "mein kampf" in a big bundle of books my dad won at an auction, and because we were learning about history at the time, I read a bit of it.
