2bigpigs' post='4908737 wrote on Dec 30 2010, 01:15 AM:
Ok, Who is for when it's the subject like he, she, it; and whom is for when it's the object like him, her, it?That's pretty easy.
It's also important to keep in mind the context, particularly in which clause the "who" or "whom" resides.For instance, one might say, "I will vote for the politician who has the nicest political ads." In that case, "who" is the subject within the subordinate clause "who has the nicest ads", working as the subject of "has". In another example, "He is the one whom we chose," it is the object of the verb "chose" within the italicized subordinate clause.It can get more confusing if you have, say, a noun clause acting as an object. For instance, in "I will vote for whoever supports my causes," the italicized clause is a noun clause acting as the object of a preposition ("for"), but within the context of the clause, "whoever" is acting as the subject of the verb "supports" (it should also be noted that whoever/whomever acts the same way as who/whom).And sometimes it's a little trickier, like "Is she the one who they say got teen pregnant?" There, "they say" can be misleading, but the clause in mind is "who got teen pregnant" whereas "they say" is a separate clause by itself.
