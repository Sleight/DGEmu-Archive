MmouseORIGINAL POSTJun 27 2012, 04:40 AM
So, a few of you might remember that I had this cockamamie idea for a webcomic a while back, involving a genetically altered archer who serves as a member of an elite policing force for the ruling family in a bubble city set in a post-apocalyptic world in which scientists have discovered how to manipulate the genetics of all living things, a discovery that has led to biological advances as well as a whole new realm of biological warfare, all of it based loosely on the music of Melissa R. Kaplan... Anyway.

Though I haven't actually started it, and I still suck at drawing, I haven't forgotten about it. In fact, every once in a while, a snippet of something will get me thinking about it again, revising and refining the story in my head (and spread out on various digital and paper notes).

I just wanted to share with anyone interested about one such spark that hit me as I was sitting at work, waiting for the press to start on the last edition of tomorrow's paper.

I caught the news that Nora Ephron, acclaimed writer and filmmaker, had died, and I got to thinking that "Nora" might be a good name for the protagonist in the story. I hadn't really decided on a name up to that point. Then I thought, what if that name meant something? I also hadn't fully fleshed out an origin story for her, though I thought about incorporating some kind of laboratory experiments that made her who she has become.

Anyway, in the story I had worked out so far, the protagonist has vague memories of being raised like one of the children in the ruling family, though she becomes more like an attack dog as she gets older, given her abilities. So I thought, what if she had been stolen from a lab as a child, a lab where her genetic structure was being manipulated to turn her into a sort of supersoldier or something?

I'm also tossing around the idea that the great scientific genetic breakthrough has something to do with cancer - that scientists have somehow found a way to "control" cancer to foster beneficial cell growth to make people stronger or keener in some way or another. I'm not really sure on the details of that yet. Anyway, matching those bits up, I figured that the name "Nora" could come from a shortening of the name of the facility where she was stolen - maybe something with "radiology" because of the cancer aspect. The first thing that came to my mind was "Northern Radiology" - or "NoRa" for short. Maybe the shortened form was on some item that was taken along with her, or the name comes from some vague memory she has where she can only recall a portion of a logo or something.

Anyway, out of curiosity, I googled "Northern Radiology" and found out that a place with that name exists in Watertown, New York. I thought, wouldn't it be neat if the places in the webcomic are based on real-world places? Up until now, I hadn't really considered setting the story in a real place, just some nondescript or fictional location. Anyway, I did a bit more research on Watertown (read: Wikipedia), and I found some interesting details. For one thing, Watertown has a hydrowater plant (go figure), which could provide energy to the town. Also, there's a military base nearby - Fort Drum - that houses the historic LeRay Mansion.

Perfect! In the event of an impending apocalypse, a military fort would seem like a safe place to go (right?), so it would make sense that one might become a post-war bastion. On top of that, it's got a mansion! In my head, I imagined the ruling family living in a prominent building within the city. Though, judging from pictures of the mansion and the base, it would conflict with what I had originally envisioned - a rundown city block, close quarters, no sign of vegetation. The mansion is surrounded by greenery, and the base is built in a fairly wooded, mountainous region. Still, it might serve as inspiration. Plus, it would work well with the narrative I have planned out so far. I imagine it playing out in three acts - the escape from the city (Fort Drum), survival in the wilderness and eventual return to the city, and the search for a cure to the negative effects of genetic manipulation (into Rivertown to the point of origin, Northern Radiology).

TL;DR: Sometimes, my mind goes off on tangents, and those tangents spawn more tangents in a rather rewarding way.

I do the same thing. The tangents thing, that is.

My two cents:


I like how you derived the name. Sounds kind of like what happened in V or Hancock. Worked well there.

The cancer thing has been the subject of quite a few sci-fi plots, I Am Legend being the most prominent one I can recall. It's still a relatively novel plot device, but a twist might be welcome.

Matching the comic to real world places is a nice idea. Looks like it's even given you some inspiration you might not have had otherwise. I say keep with it. As for vegetation, or lack thereof, as the author you can bend that to your will. Maybe the plants have all since died from lack of water, lack of light, lowered temperatures, chemical contamination, etc. Maybe the plants were all cut and used for fuel. Maybe you could work the greenery into your dystopia and break away from the concrete jungles that most dystopian stories boil down to.

What kind of negative effects were you thinking about? Madness, like in Bioshock? Zombification, like in I Am Legend? Mutation, like in Fallout? It's another often visited, but still valid, plot device.

All in all, liking the idea, but until it gets more fleshed out, it could use some more distinction to make it stand out from the crowd of dystopian stories out there.
