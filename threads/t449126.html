Prologue In the quiet forest, Kayla dashed through the winter flakes that had fallen onto the ground, breaking them as the wind played with the young wolf’s furry light gray coat. After running for miles, she came to a stop. Slyly crawling on the ground was a little white mouse that was almost impossible to see in the snow, but Kayla kept a sharp eye out for prey since it was so scarce in winter. She crouched down as low as she could, her eyes gazing at the mouse stiller than a statue making sure it wouldn’t get away. Suddenly she pounced toward the mouse. She caught it at the edge of its tiny body. She dug her claw deep into the mouse, killing it. Kayla slowly ate it, savoring every small bit knowing it would take many more hours to find more prey. Even though she was ravenous wanting to gulp the whole mouse in one bite, she didn’t since she knew she would need it again soon. “Prey is rare in winter, that’s why I must savor every smidgen of kill.” She whispered to herself as she always did when she found prey. Kayla heard something once say that long ago, but she couldn't remember what. She ate half of the mouse, and then she dug a small hole in the snow. Kayla banked the mouse in the hole. After that she covered it with the winter flurry. She placed her paw on the spot where she buried it making a paw print, so she would know just where it was when she needed it again. As Kayla raced through the forest, the taste of her prey stayed in her mouth as if she was still eating it. But when her stomach growled the taste seemed to disappear. She stopped at the edge of a cliff, and then looked up at the moon in the shadowy sky. She howled loudly toward the giant lamp far away from her, and then she walked down the cliff. She yawned loudly as she strolled toward her green cradle. She cuddled with leaves on the floor of her den and quickly fell asleep; she was incredibly weary after another long day of hunting. Baby rays of sunshine asked Kayla to wake up, so she unhurriedly got up from her bed of leaves. She yawned quietly and then walked to her prey. She recognized her paw print in an instant, so she ripped the white chips away and ate the rest of her mouse. Kayla stretched and then darted through the forest in search of fresh, new prey. Kayla quickly stopped after running for only a while; in front of her was a large bear. Her eyes became clouded in terror, she tried to run but the bear bit her back. She screamed furiously as her back turned into a river of blood. Her eyes flashed with anger. Once the bear pulled his teeth off of her back, she turned around and then showed the bear her sharp fangs, which was a signal she was ready to fight back. She jumped high into the air, and then bit the bear’s neck with rage. The bear cried with furry, then flung Kayla off of his neck. The bear clawed Kayla’s head, but Kayla quickly flung his claw off. She swiftly clawed the bear’s gigantic stomach. The bear yelled loudly with ire, and then tightly grasped Kayla’s back and threw her off his stomach, pounding her onto the lake that ice shielded. The ice broke, and before Kayla knew it she was drowning. “I guess this is the end…” Kayla sighed softly. She closed her eyes tightly; a few moments later she remembered she was able to talk underwater. Suddenly her eyes flashed open. Could it be possible those weren’t her final words? She then she looked down, there were what looked like thousands of other wolves to Kayla. She saw a wolf with birch black fur and shining yellow eyes standing beside a wolf that looked like an older Kayla. She saw a wolf with fur the color of blackberries and the eye color of a red autumn leaf; he was staring at Kayla without blinking. He tilted his head in confusion; all the wolves including him were staring at Kayla who was gently floating down. A wolf with puffy white fur sat alone, far away behind the crowd of wolves. There were wolves with the same fur color Kayla had, some with black fur, some with white, light orange, and others even had blood red fur. Kayla heard murmurs throughout the crowd, but they were too soft for her to hear what they were saying. Kayla gently landed on the lake’s floor. “I know this is sudden, after all," she smirked in a jokingly way, "you just landed here. But, who are you?” the wolf who looked like an older Kayla asked politly. “My name is Kayla.” Kayla replied respectfully and a bit nervously as well, she wanted to show admiration to the wolf older than her, but it was also kind of frightening because she had never seen another wolf besides herself in her life. “Greetings Kayla, my name is Daylight. The wolf sitting beside me is named Midnight. What Pack are you from? I haven't seen you around yet, are you just now starting to train for battle?" “Wait, I don’t understand… What are Packs? A bear just threw me on to the river, and then the ice covering it shattered and I fell into the river. I don’t know how, but I am a alive even though I cannot swim, or breathe underwater. Erm, so I thought." “Let me explain." Midnight said sounding a bit suprised. "There are four wolf Packs within this forest, Aqua Pack, Flame Pack, Breeze Pack, and Dim Pack. Right now you are in Aqua Pack’s territory. Daylight,” he said looking at her sternly, “you remember the time prey was so uncommon in all the Packs because of the foxes that were stealing our prey and hunting in our ground, right? The Packs left where they originally were and had to find new land so they could hunt without anyone stealing from them, some Packs went looking for new territory so long many Packs had to abondon pups because they didn't have enough food to feed them. Maybe Kayla is one of the abandon pups. But she somehow surivied." “That’s most likely the case.” Daylight agreed, nodding her head. “My name is Blackberry.” The wolf with the fur color of his name said. “Can you swim, Kayla?” “No.” She said slowly shaking her head, just realising that she would not be able to swim to the surface. “Then would you like to join Aqua Pack?” Daylight asked. “We need more wolves in our pack, and you don’t seem to be a threat. Or we could quickly teach you how to swim and you could swim back to the surface.” “May I have some time to decide?” “Yes, you may.” Daylight replied. Kayla lay down beside Blackberry; she was extremely weary from the battle with the bear. “Hiya!” Blackberry said cheerfully. “Hi…” Kayla replied nervously, she felt uneasy with Blackberry since she didn't know him very well. Soon after that she closed her eyes and quickly fell asleep. Kayla raced through the forest, running from dark figures behind her. She fell down; she had tripped over a rock. She looked behind her, a dark creature held a spiky club high into the air, and then he thrashed it down hitting Kayla. Kayla awoke screaming loudly and fearfully. All the wolves of Aqua Pack hurried toward her. “What’s wrong?” Daylight asked. “Nothing, I just had a nightmare.” “Okay, Kayla. Have you decided if you want to join Aqua Pack yet?” “No…” Kayla admitted. She had been so weary she fell asleep and forgot about deciding. “We will give you until sundown to decide, if by that time you have not decided we will teach you how to swim; once you know how to do that we will order you to swim to the surface. Understood?” “I understand.” “Stay with us! We need more wolves in our Pack, and I bet your life would be much easier for you here. Everyone hunts for prey and we all share it, there are fish in our river and sometimes we go above our lake to get mice, deer, and other yummy animals. “Just let me think alone for a little while longer.” “Whatever; have fun thinking.” Blackberry said walking away to give Kayla time alone to think about it. If I join them, it would be easier to find prey during winter, she thought. But then again, I don’t know about everything they do. What if they do some really hard things too, or are really strict about how to catch prey or what is eaten. I’m going to ask what else they do. Kayla ambled to Daylight, only to find her talking with Midnight. “Is this a bad time to ask a question?” She asked. “No, it’s fine. What is it?” Daylight asked. “What else do you do in this Pack besides hunt?” “Well, one thing we do is train for battles.” “What battles?” “Sometimes we are attacked by rival Packs, they want to steal our hunting grounds. We must defend our territory though, if we lose our rival gets that part of our land. We also must try and take our rival’s subject.” “So it’s like a game of survival?” Kayla asked. “Yes.” “I’m joining.” Kayla said smiling. “Alright, you can leave the Pack anytime.” Daylight’s reply was a swift nod from Kayla’s head. “All wolves of Aqua Pack come! It’s time for a meeting.” Those words made all wolves stop what they were doing, and hustle to their Alpha. “You have all met Kayla, and she has decided that she wants to join our Pack. I have told her she can leave anytime she wants. Now, I shall rename her. Kayla is not the kind of name any Pack wolf would have, it’s an odd name that almost sounds like a human’s. Her new name will be…” Daylight paused. Blackberry whispered telling Kayla that was her ancestors telling her what her new name should be. Daylight’s suddenly flashed open, and then she spoke. “Kayla’s new name is Dawn.” Dawn heard thousands of wolves howl her new name. Others screamed “Hooray!” or simply howled happily. Dawn felt proud, she smiled with her mouth wide open and then she proudly stood up straighter. Still needs Editing. (A lot), but is still pretty good now. Moving..then I'll answer you..this doesn't belong here...In all honesty, this doesn't really belong here either, but this is the closest I can get...There we go, now..first off...posting this on the internet is not a good idea...any person can come along and steal all your hard work..best to chose a trusted person and PM them or e-mail them, which is even better....or ask a relative/friend to look over your work. That said...I, being a writer for a hobby, will..critique your work a little. You have a rushed style. You display details in flashes and then go straight to the story bare boned. This can best be displayed by looking at the encounter of the bear as compared to the encounter with the mouse. Look at the detail you placed in the mouse encounter, yet, even though you try to re-establish detail later on, the encounter with the bear is quick paced and lacking. You then jump right into the packs and their abilities, pushing everything frontward on the reader. It's sensory overload, and when one is overloaded, they shut down and lose interest. When information comes to fast, we simply glide right over it all... It's best to have some steady flow, don't give brilliant flashes and then dull it out to near nothing...be consistent. Now, besides some grammar mistakes and a couple or awkward sentences..it's a nice concept...You could also benefit from using a different array of words.."unhurriedly got up from her bed" for example, sounds sloppy. "Languidly rose from her pallet might be a better, but you can leave the "bed" if you wish. As for whispering the prey is precious, it might be better to have her thinking that, as some readers might be confused as to how she might accomplish both eating and whispering. You don't need to necessarily change that though...Now, while calling the moon a giant lamp may be creative, it's distracting. Think of something less obscure and more linking to the story. As this is one revolving around a more natural environment...Backtracking again, I just realized how much "Baby rays of sunshine asked Kayla to wake up" bothers me. While it can be looked at as the bond between Nature and beast, and some may actually take it literally as some mythical or magical meaning, It is awkward and cuts the flow. It's sticking out like one of those delightful rocks on a river bed, but you should not have phrases and words that jump out at a reader like this one does..it draws attention away from the story and makes them stare at it alone. There are other points, but I believe I would be inflicting my own style upon you if I went further. Work on what you think is best...and don't be afraid to change things up....
