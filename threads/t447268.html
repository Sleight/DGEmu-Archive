I am of course not going to talk about typos as those are entirely accidental:Using Grammatical Errors:- When you are talking to a person you know who is already familiar with your style of communication, such errors can speed up communication because you save a lot of time having to format and even proof read your messages before sending. I noticed just from just instant messaging and e-mail experience that everyone varies in what shortcuts in the English language they tend to use so perhaps it is a personal matter too.- Let's say English is not your first language and you MUST get your message across. Throughout my time on Dgemu and other web boards, I have seen so many non-english speaking people who are actually familiar with at least the short forms used but are not well versed enough in the language to type out complete sentences. I have even chatted on MSN with a couple people like this. By making these errors, I am actually putting my message in a form that is more readable to them than a complete grammatically correct paragraph/sentence. I am of course not talking about people that use a literal translation but rather those that make their best attempt to speak english.Not Using them:- Sometimes these errors can send the wrong message making you out to be less intelligent than you probably are. This isn't an opinion of mine, I have actually seen on multiple web boards, people automatically mistreat others because of the way they type. It would be safe to say that it is unwise to use errors when you are introducing yourself and trying to make a positive impression on others. Of course, if you can't speak English well, then specifying so alleviates this issue.- Burnout above makes another good point to which I will expand upon. Using grammatical errors outside the scope of communicating with friends or on message boards (or to quote him "speak in internet acronyms in the real world"). There are some that make the mistake of putting them into school essays or even professional e-mails. This is especially so if you tend to use this type of language often and it becomes second nature (much like slang words).Conclusion:As shown above, whether to use or not use spelling/grammatical errors is entirely situational and also depends on the person and who they are communicating with. I can understand why it may be annoying for outside viewers to read error ridden paragraphs, but considering the above points (namely the ones about people with English as their second/third/etc language) makes it easier to be more sensitive towards others whom may have no choice by to communicate in this manner.
