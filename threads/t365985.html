DJ Night Force 9' post='3770315 wrote on Dec 13 2007, 11:16 PM:
1. Members can choose the view the ads if they want as opposed to it being randomly slapped in their face.3. If members shut off sigs, they can't see the ads. Plain and simple.
Do you realize that the way that they can avoid having ads "randomly slapped in their face" is by shutting off sigs? Granted, they won't see the good sigs, but any member can view the specialized forum, and if the user does or does not want ads in sigs, they may disable them or ignore them.And, it's rather simple to have ads not being "slapped in their face": don't look at them. I mean, any sig image can be slapped in someone's face; why does it matter if it's a link or image advertising something or an image not advertising something?
someone wrote:
2. It's WAAAAY more organized allowing for far better publicity. For example, a member doesn't have to be "lucky enough" to see another member's ad which interests them (because not all members post in EVERY forum). They can simply browse through all the approved ads and pick what they want.
If the member wants to see ads, they use the forum. If an advertiser want to have more publicity, they use the forum. Before they are able to use the forum, they have limited publicity with their signature. Since not every user can post ads in the forum, anyway, it gives members who would be interested in the link a chance to find the link, instead of not having any chance of finding it before the member can advertise in the forum.
someone wrote:
4. It allows for far easier moderator control. Moderators don't have to second guess whether a member has permission nor do they have to put up with the possibility that a member could lie and state they have permission when they don't. All ads outside this designated forum can just be removed accordingly
By allowing all members to advertise in their sigs, permission doesn't matter; the only thing that matters is the content of the link, which is the same thing that matters in the forum, anyway.
someone wrote:
5. It's easier to maintain an ad in a thread and leaves the signature free for other content (besides a link to that thread of course).
What's the problem with allowing both signature and thread ads? A member who has access to the forum doesn't need to use their sig (although it would give more publicity), but a member who doesn't have access wouldn't be allowed to advertise anywhere, even their own sig. Yes, the system is meant to restrict permissions, but when a new member wants to casually advertise a link, they shouldn't have to post so much to have permission.My suggestion is that we allow members (under post #/moderation acceptance criteria) to post in the Advertising Board, allow all members to post a link in their signature, and disallow any topics advertising in other forums. This solves the problem of publicity. If you have a link in your signature, the more you post, the more publicity your site has. You can't take an easy way out by posting a topic in the spam center: in either instance, you must work to gain publicity for your site. When you reach a point, you have the added benefit of being able to make a thread. Since the signature advertisements aren't very prominent, it isn't an annoyance to members, and if it is, then they can disable sigs. The ad forum is for the select group that has properly gained rights to post links, so that a member looking for a site can look through that forum without seeing the sites of the members that haven't gained permission. This allows members to advertise, which plenty may want, but does not restrict permission to people with an ungodly (to them) number of posts. Banning advertising altogether would restrict members and would force moderators to remove practically all signature links, which is more difficult than allowing them. A member doesn't really have an incentive to having viruses/porn in their sigs, anyway, since they aren't gaining points. Yes, moderation of one forum is much easier than moderating an entire body of members, but even if advertising were banned in sigs, moderation of sigs would still take place. You must also consider that not that large of a group of people want to advertise their sites: not everyone has a site. Whether the number of members that want to advertise is so small that a forum is not necessary is your choice.I hope that I've sufficiently voiced my opinion.
