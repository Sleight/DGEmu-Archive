GGraavORIGINAL POSTJun 05 2012, 07:11 AM
So this teacher in the US decided to set up a real-life role-playing game with his class. He set things up so that students get XP for getting up and answering questions in class, joining in class discussions, helping classmates, and a bunch of other things like that. He also set it up so people can get ACHIEVEMENTS (yay!) by doing extra unassigned work, like reading an extra chapter or writing a paragraph. And then there's 'Random Encounter Friday'. The students then use their XP to level up. And then here's the great thing, the kids can group together in ALLIANCES and the alliance with the highest level at the end of the year gets a free pizza party.

Turns out, all those schoolkids in Indiana think this is the greatest DLC to hit school in years. Apparently they're doing all that 'getting involved' stuff that teachers like to see, and they're making up their own class/race system. Who else thinks that this would have nudged them to study more? Because if they'd had this in primary school, I'd have been a level 72 ninja velociraptor by mid-term break.
