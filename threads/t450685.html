IidazruNov 29 2009, 06:17 AM
I would say that it depends on the situation. Going by what was outlined in your post, it appears that you are referring to what might be called "casual" listening, in other words, a person pops their cans on, and listens to the type of stuff they enjoy. However, there are other reasons why people listen to music, no? Consider patriotic songs, played on independence days (from experience in my own area, and in quite a few societies). Certainly, one could think of these as "something people do for enjoyment", but there is also a purpose behind such songs. Writers of this sort of music may have the intention of encouraging citizens to love the country and its leaders. In decades past, when war was more common, it would be even more so. The national anthem of China, for instance, was based on a song composed to stir up emotion against the Japanese during wartime. When a person, especially so-called "impressionable youngsters" (I question this term, since anyone could be impressionable), listen to songs like these under certain conditions -- like for an extended period of time, or during independence days as mentioned -- then it is possible that they would be influenced and may fulfill the composers' intentions of becoming more patriotic.
