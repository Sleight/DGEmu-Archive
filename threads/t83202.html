I'm torn between Full Metal Jacket and GloryFull Metal Jacket found a balance between the anguish and contempt we feel for our troops; that is very uncommon for its genre, since it's usually an either-or case. I was surprised to see how the film quickly shifted its focus from Pyle's bullying during the famous training sequence (which, may I add, was presented as a desensitizing experience) to the sheer douchebaggery of the troops in Vietnam, and trust me, Kubrick was exceedingly poignant in both areas. Full Metal Jacket avoided the overused jingoism presented in such films to show what assholes some soldiers are regardless of the amount of support and "best wishes" postcards that they receive from their fellow countrymen. This film did a damn good job of shaking the war genre to its foundations. Now only if they made a deconstruction film about cops...Glory went past the typical themes of racism and prejudice that exists in these types of films and delves into self-identity in a way that black people can relate to today. This was evident in the tension between the educated black man and an escaped slave. It made for an engaging sideplot; I believe that this aspect of the film served as a catalyst in my growth as a person.
