Umm, well, if you just take an intro non-PDE course, you will mostly learn about Differential Graphs and solution curves. How to spot ODE solutions and families of solutions. Then you'll spend the rest of the course talking about different methods to solve different Ordinary DEs (ODEs), which mainly revolve around Separation of variables, Cauchy-Euler method, u-substitution, variation of parameters, integrating factors, using auxiliary equations, and LaPlace integrals (my favorite of the class). Some places teach other methods, like the Annihilator method, but the ones I mentioned are the most useful and the ones that crop up again in PDEs. Though, PDEs are a whole other beast.

Basically, in Calculus you learn methods that work for all systems (derivatives, integrals). In ODE you learn methods that work for specific types of problems (2nd order ODE with leading a(x)=1), and then in PDE you learn methods that only work on one specific problem. (like PDE dealing with a vibrating membrane)
