In 1949 U.S president Truman delivered an important speech which changed the way we view the world. In this speech he talked about a great need for us to share our knowledge with them. By us he referred to what is now known as the first or developed world and by them he was talking about the third or undeveloped world. He expressed that it was of great importance that we share our scientific and industrial knowledge so that they too would be capable of growing their economies and thus becoming developed nations just like us. Perhaps he truly sought to make a positive difference and to help those he saw as marginalized and vulnerable on the world scale. There have been accusations made that the theory of developed and underdeveloped world served as a means of justifying colonization and providing the smoke and mirror needed in order for the continued exploitation of small scale cultures and the so called 'developing' world. This is not surprising because if we inspect these values being presented we suddenly find ourselves face to face with the same arguments which were being presented in order to justify just that, the colonization of other countries by Europe. Although at this stage it was argued on the basis of divisions such as social evolution and civilization. In both instances we have Large scale societies professing a desire to supposedly elevate and help this undeveloped world, yet in both they use their privileged position to decide what these people need. Furthermore because the 'undeveloped' people did not get much (if any) input into the policies in regards to how this 'progress' and 'development' was to be achieved it resulted in the policies being highly insensitive to local cultures and religions and through this insensitivity it became damaging. These problems can be predicted quite clearly by a simple look at the work of Franz Boas, who argued so strongly that each culture was formed from its own unique history and how this prevents their from being a universal path of evolution (read as development). Although unfortunately it seems the world has still not learned this lesson, as we still see international organizations such as the IMF prescribing the tired "us big people know best, you little people should listen" approach.So what is 'progress' and 'development' and how should they be achieved?

Another important point to realize is there are only two strands of social evolutionism by and large. Unilineal which holds that all societies follow the exact same evolutionary path. This was dismantled utterly by Franz Boas and the Historical particularists, by pointing out that culture comes from its own unique history. Multilinear on the other hand holds that there are several evolutionary paths which societies can follow based on their evnironmental surrondings. Evolution here was seen as a response to the environment people found themselves in as opposed to something which just happened because we are human.However similar flaws which plagued Unilineal theory of social evolution were still present. One of these being the flaw of applying irrelevant cultural norms and values to that which was being studied. It would seem some people were still to learn the lesson of studying cultural traits and behaviours in their own context.
