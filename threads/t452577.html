Knights in the NightmareGenre : Doesn't really fit inside a bubble nicely, but Tactical Strategy is pretty close.PremiseAfter watching the opening scene/prologue, I was kinda unnerved. After starting the game, definitely unnerved. The storyline is given to you VERY slowly, scattered piece by piece. Takes some time to appreciate the game as it starts pretty slow. You play as a wisp/spirit who is on a mission. LOTS of people are eager to stop you. The only 'people' who help you are dead guys, except ONE. Seriously. The 'glorious' kingdom has fallen, chaos going everywhere, and there you are, smack in the middle of it. Winning battles gives little satisfaction in the beginning. Definitely not an easy game to get into. However, as time goes on, when more and more pieces of the story are revealed, things start clicking and gets a lot more fun.Score : 8/10GameplayYou play as a wisp and you move with the stylus. Wherever the stylus goes, the wisp goes. You are able to posses fallen knights and by giving them weapons, they fight for you. There isn't a 'world map' to explore, each battle is just one after another but you get an intermission inbetween battles to distribute exp, upgrade weapons, enhance characters and all that good stuff. As a Sting/Atlus game, this game is unique in many things. Units don't have attack/defense or even hp. They have something similar to hp but it can't go down from anything an enemy does. Every time a unit attacks, they lose hp. This might make the game sound dumb, but the hp lost is very little. So what happens when you get hit? You lose time. Each battle has a set number of turns and each turn is 60 seconds of player movement. Enemies don't really target you. Their attacks are just 'Bullets' that just spray around the map that might hit your wisp. Although your wisp is about the size of the tip of the stylus and it might sound easy to dodge, it is a lot more difficult than it sounds. Because the battles are real time, dodging and finding chances to attack while getting items becomes a pain in the butt later in the game. Definitely NOT the game for a casual gamer who just wants to have some easy fun. Key ItemsKey Items get their own little corner of this because they make up a large corner of the game. On every battle field, there are numerous destroyable items (rocks, crates, boxes, plants, signs, etc.). These items drop two kinds of items - weapons, and KEY ITEMS. Weapons are unimportant as you get an abundance in the game by killing enemies. Key Items are unique items that can only be given to one character. What happens when you give it to a character? They join your team. What happens if you don't? They don't join your team. The AMAZINGLY FRUSTRATING thing about this is that you usually get Key Items 3-4 battlefields BEFORE you even meet the character they apply to. So what happens when you miss a Key Item? QQ. You can't go back to get them, and there is only one save file for the game. PAIN IN A BUTT!!! Well, pain in a butt only for those of us perfectionists who can't stand missing a character. Score : 9/10VisualArt is good, each playable character has a unique portrait except for filler units who join and leave your party after one mission. Enemy units are recycled but that doesn't make too much of a difference, just a little less variety. The battle fields, however, get a little more complicated. The screen is cluttered to a point that the game is border line unplayable, but I personally found the battle fields to be pretty chaotic visually. Unlike games such as Pokemon/Final Fantasy/Mario RPG/Golden Sun/STUFF, these battle fields just seem to have too much in them and many objects begin to become distracting. There are always numerous destroyable items on the field and (because getting items out of each of these objects is almost a must,) all the items begin to give you a sense of dread, frustration, and helplessness all at once. I personally don't really like the lighting of the game. It 'fits' the game as it is always pretty dark and moody, but these colors take away from the effect of the battles. Everything just begins to seem monotonous which might be a comfort for some of you, but for me it was frustrating after a while.Score : 7/10AudioI honestly don't have much time to focus on the music when I'm playing the game. It fits nicely (considering I barely notice it), but with all the things going on in battle, it is very hard to find time to listen to what is going on musically. When a unit attacks, they always say something. The most amusing line for me was the voice of one unit saying, "How could I miss?" Half the time, that's the same thing I'm thinking. The voices that accompany the attacks make the game somewhat less monotonous but after 30 battles, they begin to annoy you. Score : 7/10AppealThis is a very good game but it isn't very easy to get into. I started this game, quit really soon, but after coming back to it and giving it more time, I found that I truly like this game. One thing that both takes away from its appeal as well as enhance its appeal is the difficulty level. This game is ridiculously frustrating at times (for the [evidently] novice player I am). Many things are not very easy to figure out without outside help so I HIGHLY recommend using Gamefaqs.com as a supplement to playing this game. Key Items will make you cry. This game really doesn't have an in between. I think those people who enjoy it will REALLY enjoy it likewise with those who don't like the game will never come to like it. The game does take time but no one wants to beat the game and still not have fulfilled that time. Score : 8/10Overall Score : 7.8/10One-liner A game filled with frustration that leaves a lasting impression - for good or for worse.
