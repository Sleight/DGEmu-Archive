[font="Trebuchet MS"]The difference between cartoons and anime?Cartoons are caricatures or characters who are then put into series of short stories that can be watched (on tv) or read (in comicbooks) in a random order. They are also rather short and usually have some kind of moral woven into it. There is often a hero and a villain in a cartoon, good and bad are clearly separated because cartoons are often targeted at children. However, there are also cartoons for a slightly older public (teens) which often shows longer episodes and a less clear line between 'good & evil'.Anime is short for 'animation'. It goes hand in hand with manga, which are comics originally produced in Japan. Most anime are the animated version of their manga-counterparts. The difference between manga/anime and cartoons, is that manga/anime is often in a specific order. In the latter, the character often stays the same age for the whole duration of the series. The former, on the contrary, often has their characters 'grow up' and evolve through the episodes. The biggest difference is probably the fact that there is an actual plotline, most of the time. Manga/anime can be targeted at children, but also at teens & adults. There is manga/anime that is specifically made for girls and boys (just like with books).And yes, if you use the term manga then it really has to have been produced in Japan originally. So if it's an English version but the original came from Japan, it's still a manga & not a cartoon. There is also Manhua, which is made in China, Hong Kong & Taiwan, and manhwa which comes from Korea. The origins are important because these influence the art style of the manga.[/font][font="Trebuchet MS"]

As such, and until such a time as this no longer is so, because most non-cartoon use of animation comes from Japan(at least to the US market this has been so) Anime as been viewed as Japanese Animation. The clear difference between Anime and cartoons is down to cultural roots. Our Western society (with the exception of Belgium) seems to be so obsessed with cartoons/comics being for kids. Why is this?I don't know about our society but I read a piece about Chinese cartoons (can't remember where it was) that lamented the decline in Chinese animation. The problem, the author pointed out, was that in Chinese animation, the plot was too prescriptive. It was didactic about what to do and what not to do. The cartoon treated its watchers like children, clearly making sure that the evil is obvious and constantly ramming home the message that the evil is something you shouldn't do and that you should aspire to be the heroes. Basically, it insults the watcher's intelligence. The watcher takes on look and thinks it's too simplistic, that's it's childish in its execution and considers all subsequent animation to be of similar nature.In Japanese anime, there's a clear difference. Yes, there are those that are clearly aimed at children, but there are those that aren't. I do believe it has something to do with the fact that in Japan, it is somewhat cheaper to do animation than live film. So we get stuff aimed at adults also in the mix. This stuff isn't simplistic. The villains aren't necessarily obvious and the heroes aren't necessarily people we'd aspire to be. Messages aren't necessarily simplistic. They can be deep or overtly philosophical like say... Ghost in the Shell (any of them, literally any one of them that you can care to name). Even some of the ones aimed at children do not necessarily treat them the viewers as children.Let's give an example of Digimon Tamers, the third season of the Digimon franchise: It's not as twee and light as the previous two. (Heck even the second season had a tyrant child whose Digimon partner suffers in an abusive relationship). The third one introduces viewers to the concept of death (one character dies and never comes back). Its primary villain is actually not so bad after all. Its main villain isn't really evil, just doing what it was programmed to do. Um, I can't remember much of the rest, actually, but it's not as condescending as other cartoons of Western nature.Now some have said that Anime is violent. Well, Tom and Jerry is violent too. The Simpsons parodies it with 'Itchy and Scratchy'. The difference of course, is that this particular cartoon is nothing but mindless violence and it's sanitised violence. There's nothing there except cheap sight gags. You don't need much intelligence to get Tom and Jerry. In Anime, the violence isn't sanitised. They treat you to the consequences, even if they are focused exclusively on the victim. The best Anime is that where the violence is counterbalanced with material that makes you think.Cowboy Bebop has its fair share of violence, but it sometimes does ask big questions that make you wonder about life and the Universe. That show even has comedic moments and rude moments and heart-warming moments. Most importantly, it doesn't treat you like an idiot.Perhaps, that is the key to the success of some of Pixar's cartoons. They don't necessarily treat you like an idiot. Wall E for example has a very science fiction feel to it; exploring the future of a purely consumerist society, it paints a bleak future of what happens if we do nothing to curb our excesses. That particular movie doesn't treat you like an idiot. It shows everything as it is. Unfortunately, it gets a bit twee near the end, but it still works.
