KKirie MotobaApr 19 2014, 07:13 AM

Though it's not really exaggerated, the expenses are quite in for the typical people here. If you notice, there's a lot of luxuries you can get rid of in that video, but the message it's trying to pass: Our government taxes us really hard. Oh, federal lowers their tax to 5%? Well, we'll augment our provincial tax to 9.5% to compensate!

Oh, you bought a new house? Welcome to Quebec! Have a welcome tax!

Oh, you decided to move to a new bigger house within the same city? Welcome to Quebec again! Have another welcome tax!

Oh, you work and make around 35k per year? Let us tax you for all the services, leaving at hand about 27k of what you earned.

Oh, you work and make more money, around 50k per year? Well buddy, you're in the new bracket and get more taxes, leaving you around 37k per year.

 

1.30$ per liter for gas is not applicable, right now, our gas prices fluctuate around 1.45$ per liter.

And yes, there's the yearly fee for the licenses, the car insurance (which you get taxed on also), license plate fees, etc.

I'm one of the rarer exceptions that actually got a cellphone with no data plan. That's 35$ per month. People around me all have data plans, that's about 50$ per month for Android, and 60$ per month for hipsters with iPhones. This is all because our phone services are provided by like 4 giant telecom companies, and they have this agreement where prices are averaged around the same, and without a lot of competition, and the prices keep going up every year. You're locked in a contract for 2 to 3 years, and once comes the time to change your phone, they change your contract to have the "preferential" price for phone upgrades and lock you in another 2 to 3 years.

 

200k$ house? That's a small shitty condo in the suburbs. an average condo in Montreal starts at 240k$. My place I bought in the suburbs cost nearly 300k$.

 

Travel expenses, I don't really believe in the needs to go out everywhere in the world every year. My brother, on the other hand, travels every year, his friends also.

 

Unless you're 2 people earning an average income of 40k in a household here in Quebec, forget living with the luxuries shown in that video. You'll be living the "home poor" way, or with luxuries and in debt forever.
