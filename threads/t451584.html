So, this summer I had to read a book called The Bluest Eye, by some chick called Tori something or another, for a class. It was a pretty crappy class, in that it lacked a lot of books full of explosions and jokes and focused more on why men are evil and how all women are secretly lesbians. (It's to my credit that I stuck with the class... I guess.)Anyway, to make a long story short, The Bluest Eye is about a little black girl who's oppressed by THE MAN and WHITE CULTURE'S way of judging beauty. You see, she's an ugly black girl, and she thinks that all she needs to have to be beautiful is a pair of nice blue eyes, just like Shirley Temple. Blah blah blah, yadadada. The middle section of the book talks about how hypocritical Americans are, true love, a dead cat, the word **** gets used a fair bit, the ugly girl's bat**** insane father, the time said father impregnated the ugly girl (who was about twelve at the time) while she was doing something in the kitchen because she reminded him of her mother, the author uses the phrase "fucked her gently" or "lovingly" or something like that to describe it, I'm dropping this thing right now because I hate thinking about this book.Okay, point is, this book was about racial beauty, and took that to a creepy high I've never seen before. And my prof made me read it, because it was a literary, or intellectual, or whatever fancy word you want to use, work of art.In the meant time, I also read a book called The Green Mile, by Stephen King. I'm not going to tell you about it, because it was an awesome book and you should read it if you get the chance, but not only did I like it better, I also felt that it was written better. There was no stupid-ass attempt to repeat a phrase over and over and over and over and over and over and over and over and over and over and over again in between each chapter. The narrator didn't try to capture the scene with flower prose. There was no incest, which is always a plus. And we all learned a special lesson.I'm going to be an English teacher in a couple years. Guess which one of these books I'd give to my students. The over drawn, cumberson, preachy tale that can only apply to a select group of people? Or a book with actual characters, doing stuff that doesn't involve sex with family members, that actually contains a certain part of human nature in it?What I want to say is this: fuck literature. Fuck the writers who'd rather make a point with a story than tell a story that maybe contains a point. Fuck the pretentious assholes who congratulate themselves on writing Art, who discount any author that writes in such a way that the average Joe could understand or even enjoy. Fuck you. The art of writing is too precious to waste on your self-important scribblings.
