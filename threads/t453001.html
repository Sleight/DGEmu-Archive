Just a little anecdote for you.My gaming rig:Core 2 Duo E6320 (1.86GHz OCed to 2.6GHz)2GB DDR2-800 RAMNVIDIA GeForce 8800GTS 320MBClearly, this machine is a little lacking in the memory department. But it can play most games out there at my monitor's cap of 60FPS (which most monitors cap at anyway, that whole 60Hz limit) on mid settings, and around 40-50FPS on high settings. I play Crysis on mid settings, but that's mainly because I play all of my games at 1680x1050 and my video card could use some more VRAM for that high of a resolution.Lesson: More than 4GB of RAM is unnecessary. I haven't even been able to get above about 1600MB usage when I leave Firefox, Pidgin, and Word open while I play Crysis.What I would do is invest in maybe 3GB of DDR3 if you want a future-proofed gaming rig? Go for speed and low latencies, because you're not going to be using more than that much capacity in most cases. Then, in a few years, DDR3 will be cheap and you can get 6GB or 9GB. You should really focus on your video card. RAM is not that crucial in terms of buying the best of the best and a huge amount of it, and if you get a Core 2 Duo, you can overclock the crap out of it. My E6320 was a lot cheaper than the E6600 and it runs at the same clock speed, so it's essentially the same thing. (How well do the i3/5/7/9 overclock? I'm assuming it's an improvement, but still).
