KKirie MotobaFeb 13 2014, 03:17 AM

My thoughts while still owning both:

 

3DS:

- Small and light-weight, holding it up in the air in bed for long periods of time is great

- Accustomed to the DS Lite? This one should feel perfect for you

- The stylus: Being a telescopic stylus and being positioned at the top is EXTREMELY annoying. Not only that, since it's a metal finish, it tends to gradually slide between your fingers as you use it. I just tend to take spare DS styluses and use them.

- The 3D switch does not lock itself at off. Very easy to accidentally tap it to on.

- The console is more "square". It may hurt your palms a bit depending on how you hold it.

- The hinge feels loose. I know, it's to prevent the old DS Lite hinge breaking catastrophe, but I personally don't like a semi-wobbly top screen.

 

3DS XL:

- Big, not so heavy, really nice to see more, but holding it up in the air for long times is tiring.

- Accustomed to the DSi XL? This one is actually more comfortable than its older counterpart.

- The stylus: On the side, already the perfect length, and made of plastic. All the cons of the regular 3DS fixed.

- The 3D does lock itself to off! No accidental flipping it on.

- The console is more rounded. More comfortable to hold. Sweaty palm people beware though: The material this console uses may slide out of your palms since it's rounded.

- Hinge feels really solid and locks in 2 position: table top play and hand held play.

 

Personal choice: 3DS XL for sure

 

The 3D feature? Completely useless. It's as useful as having a 3D TV. Slightly shifting the console makes it hard to distinguish stuff on the screen, long exposure leads to headache.

Fun fact: Turn the 3D on to minimal, and wear 3D glasses that you might have stolen a pair from a theater. It works much better! ...but you look stupid doing so.

Just because they're both based on RPG battle systems doesn't mean that they're the same. Mario and Luigi puts a bigger emphasis on the standard RPG battle format. Most of the abilities, items and attacks are based on purely doing high amounts of damage. Paper Mario puts a lot less emphasis on doing pure damage (which is why everything from damage done to maximum health in the games are on a small scale i.e. the final boss would have like 100hp vs something like the final boss of superstar saga who has thousands of HP) and puts more emphasis on unique, gimmicky play styles using things like number buffs, number debuffs, status ailments, status improvements and most notably party member attacks. The fact that there's party members at all should be evidence enough to show that there's a lot more variety in both the game's unique damage mechanics and the status buffs/debuffs. What do I mean by unique damage mechanics? It means that you can't just use the same attack on every enemy in the game. Now, to be fair, mario and luigi has the same type of unique mechanics where some enemies can only be hurt by jumps, hammer or fireball/lightning (based off of SS since that's the most recent one I played), however, paper mario's unique damage mechanics go way more in depth i.e. some enemies in paper mario 1 that can only be damaged by watt's electric attacks or bombette's explosions.
