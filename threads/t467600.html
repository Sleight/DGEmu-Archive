http://www.sportssafety.org/sports-injury-facts/I'm at an ends here personally. Years of psychological studies have shown that playtime is vital to proper development, and sports are a form of play. On the other hand it is true that sports lead to a great many injuries for children: "Injuries associated with participation in sports and recreational activities account for 21 percent of all traumatic brain injuries among children in the United States." We cannot just leave it here though, for it is not the playing of the sports, but improper training that causes a vast number of the injuries, "Overuse injury, which occurs over time from repeated motion, is responsible for nearly half of all sports injuries to middle-and high-school students. Immature bones, insufficient rest after an injury and poor training or conditioning contribute to overuse injuries among children." It seems that the reason behind the injuries caused by, and the dangers of youth sports are not the sports themselves, but improper training, which is easily correctable. Thus, because of the psychological evidence supporting sports combined with the fact the many injuries are preventable, I still believe youth sports to be a good thing.Now, as for fighting sports, you believe the should be limited to post-pubescent people because of the dangers of injury. My first inclination is to agree, for I also dislike fighting and do not like the idea of teaching children to fight. Consider this, however: From 1982-2002, the total numbers of direct and indirect fatalities among high school athletes were: Baseball — 17 Basketball — 88 Cheerleading — 21 Cross Country — 14 Football — 22 Soccer — 31 Track & Field — 47 Wrestling — 16 Wrestling has the LEAST number of fatalities, with basketball, one of the nations most popular sports, on top! Perhaps fighting sports, although the website does not list boxing, are not so bad after all.This, just like my first paragraph, boil back down to safety. Why do certain sports have more injuries? Well, let us think about the safety practices. In football, safety is paramount. Pads and helmets for all. In cross country, coaches carefully train their runners, for runners have gotten so good that the only way to win is to have good training, which includes good safety. In wrestling, safety is practiced. Take pro wrestling for instance. We all know there is a large amount of fake wrestling on television (not all is fake, for example, MMA is quite legit). After all, it isn't legal to beat people over the head with folding chairs or clamp jumper cables to a man's testicles (yes, I saw a wrestling match where one wrestler pretended to do this while the other screamed in pain, but it was quite clear the cables were clipped to the edge of the shorts - they were acting). These "wrestlers" appear to beat each other up in a violent manner but actually are being very careful. Now, take basketball. Perhaps the reason basketball is on top is because of it the safety practices: Football is played in pads on cushioned turf. Basketball is played with no pads on a hard floor. Basically, I think all youth sports are good, we just need to improve our safety practices.
