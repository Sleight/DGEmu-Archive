Look, it's supposed to be science fiction. So there has to be some element of science in it. Now, I'm willing to overlook a few violations of basic scientific principles. (Apparently, according to a Radio 4 programme I was listening to, by rights, in the movie Sunshine, we shouldn't have been able to see the stars move in relation to the ship which would have made the ship look as if it was standing still. This would have looked wrong, even though it was right, so the movie producers decided to do otherwise for the sake of keeping a good narrative going on).However, when you look at every episode and see so much that violates basic scientific principles, you can't help but wonder whether the writers are taking us for complete fools. I mean, it's not just scientific principles. Take the one where Gallifrey makes its come back. The Master manages to do *that* to the Gallifreian President. Really? What was the point of having those guards behind him, then? Guards, that, I might add, didn't even react when the Master shot lightning at their boss. They just stood there like ornaments. And that's presuming they actually were guards. If they weren't, then that beggars even more disbelief. If they weren't guards, then what on Earth were they doing there?And the series seems to rely an awful lot on deus ex machina. Like, every single series finale?I don't know about you, but I expect some scientific principles to be obeyed when making a science fiction story. Therefore, I prefer to see artificial gravity being created through means that don't invoke magic materials. I prefer explosions in space to have no sound, because sound can't travel through a vacuum. And I prefer it when the villains have some sense of realism. What villain expects to build a giant robot and expect the people to rejoice upon seeing it? Can't they see that people would naturally run away, terrified, from a monstrosity they've never seen before, with feet so large it could crush hundreds of them into flat mush with one step?You can only ask the audience to suspend their disbelief so far, before the entire thing descends into absolute farce.
