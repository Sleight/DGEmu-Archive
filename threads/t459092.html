The parralax barrier works by making each of your eyes view different images as they would in the real world.(duh) The best example to see what i mean is to hold up your thumb and look at the different angles of it that you see with each of your eyes. If you hold it at a distance and slowly bring it closer, all the while changing which eye you look through, You will notice that the closer it gets, The more your thumb jumps from side to side.(So we go to the converse of this. If the image in the 2 eyes get farther apart then it should be closer. Thats the third dimension)Now for the parallax display, If you read the link at the bottom of the page, There is this nice image.So the position and size of the holes of the barrier has to be in such a way that if your left eye sees one pixel, Your right sees the next, Then the left sees one and so on. The 3DS has a resolution of 800*240 pixels. This isn't super wide screen. Half of the 800 (400 pixels) is for each eye so that you can see 3D images.Now The problem with the parallax barrier (also the reason we haven't seen it before on a commercial product which everyone wants) is that your eyes need to be in a certain 'sweet spot' to get the 3D effect. And if your eyes or the screen aren't horizontal (OR parrallel to each other;I mean, If your eyes are at an angle then you need the screen to be at the same angle) to get 3D. It's my understanding that a part of the strain from the glasses is that there are two different image sources that the eyes have to navigate: the original image and the surface of the glasses. Ultimately, you're not looking at the original image at all, but rather the light as split and interpreted by the glasses.Parallax barriers, on the other hand, allow you to look directly at the source without any weird intermediary barriers like glasses lenses. Ideally, this *should* lessen eye strain...but, as you've said, for folks who are staring at a screen for hours daily, who knows what the impact will be. At best, a case of tech scare (anyone else remember when NES caused brain damage?); at worst, some serious physiological problems and unending lawsuits. Yikes!
