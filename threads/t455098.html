Rayadragon' post='4810960 wrote on Mar 8 2010, 01:07 AM:
I'm going to take the initial post at face value and stay out of the arguments for or against homeopathic treatments. I'm going to interprit the question as "is it alright to give a placebo in place of a medically approved drug?" I have to add in the medically approved part, since most drug testing includes drug versus placebo testing in order to determine efficacy.When thinking of placebo versus approved drug, I find it somewhat morally reprehensible to prescribe a placebo. This would be a situation in which a known, effective treatment is avaliable, and the doctor decides to give the patient something that has no physiological effect just in case the patient might will themself to be well. It reminds me a bit of the Tuskeegee syphilis experiment in which poor, black Americans with syphilis weren't told of their exact condition and of existing treatments in the name of science. The doctor is bypassing the patient's right to informed consent by not indicating that the prescribed medicine is a placebo.I'd also think there'd be a risk of following misdiagnoses because of a placebo prescription. Think about all the drugs (prescription and over the counter) that are out there and all the side effects there are to taking them. I'd assume that a placebo would have to include a similar list to ensure the illusion of efficacy is maintained. My concern would be that a patient would ignore these side effects assuming that they're just caused by the medicine, when they could be indicating a more serious medical problem. For example, a stomach ulcer could be mistaken as side effect indigestion, or bronchitis/pnemonia could be mistaken as an expected shortness of breath.I can understand why a doctor might be tempted to prescribe a placebo in place of an antibiotic or the like if a patient insists on it, but I don't think it's the responsible thing to do. A patient deserves the true explanation of what's going on. While people may be insistent on the course of treatment, it's up to the doctor to explain why that course of action will not work, no matter how long it takes. Furthermore, the cynic in me has to point out that prescribing a placebo with its price markup would be a heck of a moneymaker for doctors, and legitimate healthcare is expensive enough.
