BbeekeeperSep 27 2006, 12:53 AM
I'm indifferent to dubs, it's a hit or miss kind of thing. For example, Tiffany Grant as Asuka in Evangelion couldn't *be* more perfect of a role. I'd say ADV has got the best dubs. Although, I do enjoy the work Anime Works has done, and add the fact the outtakes they often throw in the DVDs are hilarious. A good dub consists of, various voice actors (if you can close your eyes and tell who is who), convincing emotion (which is harder to detect in Japanese voice actors if you're not Japanese or are familiar with Japanese dialogue), and most importantly minor if any changes in story dialogue (though changes are almost impossible to avoid, since the script needs to fit the mouthflaps and sometimes there will be many mouthflaps for a Japanese word that translates out to a simple 1-3 syllable word in English). Anime dubs are tolerable, and sometimes preferable. However in live-action films... Dubs are ALWAYS a mistake. Live-action mouthing of words is far more complex and can not be faked into another language unlike the simple movements of an animated mouth (and sometimes the mouth doesn't move at all). All in all, I say "kudos" to dubs. They provide a vital service of bringing anime to the masses. And a big "screw you" to the elitist ****s who think watching anime strictly subbed makes them somehow superior. Someone mentioned in another thread that watching anime in anything other than subs makes one less of an anime fan. That simply is not true, people have their reasons to not watch it subbed, from a simple preference to physical/mental issues that prevent or make difficult the reading of subtitles. In fact the only anime viewers I wouldn't call a fan, are the ones who merely pirate anime and don't legally own any. If you want to impress someone, learn Japanese and watch it raw. Anime is for everyone.And besides... subtitles ruin anime music videos.
