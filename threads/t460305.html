In the real world, they are two completely different things. Arguing is something like "Damnit, Janet! I thought we were going to watch Family Guy tonight! We watch Everybody Loves Raymond every night!" "Oh yeah, Paul? If you don't like it, why don't you go home? This is my apartment and I'm entitled to watch whatever I want!" "Fine, whatever. WHAT DID YOU JUST SAY?!" etc. etc. Debate is a structured disagreement involving factual data which is used to support one's opening statement. This is kind of weird because this is one of those words whose meaning and connotation have been lost through misuse.  As pretty much any speaking professor will tell you, A debate is merely the forum while an argument is the specific action.  You don't posit a debate against someone, you posit an argument, and sometimes this is in a structured turn taking manner that constitutes a formal event known as a debate.That seems like a pedantic differentiation but in reality it's not because theres a distinction to be made.  An argument constitutes itself of one idea supported by bits evidence that goes to prove a point (usually that you're right).  Its singular and one sided by nature because it's designed to say something specific.  The mentality between the two is different in scale.  An argument is short term, and the objective is simply to put forth your idea, irrespective of what the other side would ever say unless it could undermine your argument. In a debate you take multiple turns, and you're still making one-sided arguments, but you're listening to parts of what the other side has said into consideration, if only in order to formulate your own counter-argument and maybe make another one to support your first.  The objective then becomes to win and not necessarily be right.  There are debates whose purpose is not to win, but to be honest I would consider those Discussions (eh, there's probably a better term I can't remember atm, but I'll stick with that for now).So to summarize:An argument is one-sided action taken to prove a point or be rightA debate is a formal set of one-sided arguments taken in turns by two sides to win in the eyes of a third party.A discussion is a two-way endeavor taken to reach an understanding.Edit: Mmm... Editing eats up my spacing :3Oh, and I think that's the fundamental reason why you guys who say you're trying to have "debates" end up in "arguments" a.k.a. fights.  Debates only work when the setting is formal because each side has agreed to a set amount of arguments with a party for arbitration to whom they're trying to win in the eyes of.  There is a set time limit, and set rules to the event.  However it goes each side is assured they'll be treated fairly and get to make their arguments.  In real life, such limits and assurances don't exist.  You can make an argument, but nothing forces you or them to listen to them because they don't know that you'll do the same.  The mentality is completely different.  That's why the only way you can function in the real world is to hold open minded discussions where the conversation is back and forth so that each side knows they can have their say.  The mentality of a debate or argument is completely wrong for that because the trust isn't established by some third party body whose sole purpose is to arbitrate fairly. You're directly trying to win over the individual, and in trying to win, you lose sight of what they're saying. Final edit I hope, getting tired of re-adding all these br tags haha: I suppose you could argue that this forum doesn't follow the debate format and destroys my logic, but if you think about it, the mods and readers are an audience that serve as the third party arbitration and the set limit is until all arguments and thoughts have been made (if you repeat a thought, the mods can dock you).
