A new DS itself is not a bad idea, and I’ve kind of expected one for a little while now. A game-console’s lifespan is traditionally 4-5 years as technology is constantly improving. This is partially spurred on by PC games as they are constantly improving as the strength of the platform is constantly growing. To “catch up” with the PC, a company’s console is updated to the latest (or near latest) hardware and then that market’s computing power essentially freezes for the next 5 years. The handheld market hasn’t followed this pattern however. Nintendo’s original Game Boy went uncontested for over 10 years. There were other contenders that entered the market, but none could beat this 8-bit machine in popularity and Nintendo felt little reason to update the line as it has its home consoles. Recently there has been an explosion in the portable entertainment market from mp3 players to cell phones that all can play games. Portable consoles are now starting to feel the pressure given by these devices similar to the PC’s effect on the home console market.Nintendo has apparently felt this pressure as in the past it has released three versions of the Game Boy Advance, and two versions of the Nintendo DS, each an effort to improve the image of the product in contrast to other devices. The last update was the DS lite, a cosmetic update to the admittedly dull looking DS. This update helped the DS as it made it a device that people weren’t embarrassed to carry around. The DSi is not this kind of update.The DSi is a functionality update. It adds abilities to the DS that didn’t previously exist. The first of which is the ability to take pictures. There are two cameras on the device, one on the outer-shell that acts much like a traditional camera, and one on the inside hinge that can act like a web-camera. The second addition is a SD card slot and internal storage. This allows for the capability of music and movies to be played on the device. Nintendo is going a little further though and inserting a browser and app store much like the one on the Wii. The rest of the hardware (processor, ram, etc…) is the same functionality wise. These updates create a divide in the Nintendo DS market.This divide can only hurt the DS. As the main hardware and firmware is the same, it runs the same games and any new games written for the new systems are theoretically compatible with the old devices, which have a huge install base. People with the current DS lite will not want to go and spend an additional $180 for a mediocre camera and music player when they already have their 8 MP camera and iPod. This becomes a premium version of a product they already own and many people won’t buy them.This gives developers a hard decision: develop a new kind of game that fully integrates these features and play to a portion of the market, make these capabilities optional and unnecessary to actual gameplay, or ignore them altogether. The latter two options are going to be the more popular choices. This makes the DSi a doomed device, joining the ranks of the Sega 32x and Nintendo Expansion Pak.What I imagine Nintendo should have done is do a full update of the device. While the DS is not dead, it is approaching the end of its lifespan. The graphics and functionality of the DS feel dated compared to that of the PSP and iTouch which are becoming more and more enticing to the gaming community. Throw in faster processors. Give it more RAM. Make both screens multi touch and increase the resolution. I wouldn’t mind spending $200 on a DSi that can compete with the iPhone in specs. The DS is approaching the end of its 5 year lifespan and it needs a real update.
