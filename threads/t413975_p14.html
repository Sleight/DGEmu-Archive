Also, tubby!ISO is basically how much light the film/sensor is taking in.An ISO of 100 will take in little light, but an ISO of 1600 will take in 4 or 5 times more light (if I remember correctly ISOs increase camera speed by a stop by being multiplied by 2... 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200... and usually many stops inbetween those.) The lower the ISO, the less grain and the better the image looks. Most DSLRs can shoot in the 1000 ISO range without too much of a loss in quality. The D3 can shoot in the 3000s and still look amazing... but it costs $4000. With film, the ISO literally refers to crystals in the film, ISO 50 film has crystals twice the since of 25 film... if that example helps :XThey're called F-Stops, not D-stops. The F stop refers to the aperture, the aperture is what controls how much light enters the camera... a smaller number means the aperture is bigger for a semi-pro camera, an aperture of 2.8 is the normal... If you see a telephoto lens with a moving aperture, say a 24-120 f/3.5-5.6 then that means as you zoom in the smallest (number) you can get will begin to increase. From what I understand, this has more to do with Depth of Focus then anything. And DoF basically means what you have in focus. I know you've seen a camera focus and how it only focus' on one area, the larger (number) the aperture, the sharper the image and more area that will be in focus. At f/64 everything is in focus, but you will only find that on a large format camera.If you want to do photography as a hobby, I would recommend buying a fixed focal length lens as they allow you to grasp the concepts quicker and better.If you just buy a camera, I would look at resolution and ISO. If you just want it for quick snap shots and stuff like that, get a Canon. If you want to spend a lot of money, go Nikon.
