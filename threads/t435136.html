Ultimately, Smash Bros. is a party game. An awesome party game. Putting Mario and Link into an arena and demanding the two to pummel the hell out of each other sounds like a winning scenario to me, and I'm sure many others will agree since it's working out well for Nintendo. It wasn't meant to be taken seriously, but I do play Brawl competitively and it's really fun. Mechanically, the three games are radically different from each other (number of characters, movesets, controls, items, stages, unlockables) but it isn't hard to understand why one would dismiss the three games as "being the same;" you do the same thing in all of the games, but in different ways. I will admit that while Brawl was the purest form of fanservice since Sailor Moon, in summary, games like Smash Bros. encourage developers to take risks (it didn't use a traditional fighting game scheme, either); it's far from overrated. It deserves most of the praise it gets
